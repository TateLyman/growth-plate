# DRAFT — not sent

**To:** the corresponding author of *"Near Final Height in Males treated with Aromatase
Inhibitors"* (PMID 41537284, 2026)
**Subject:** Two further analyses your near-final-height cohort could support

---

Dear Dr [NAME],

I'm a student building a quantitative, source-graded map of the mechanisms that set human
height. Your paper is, as far as I can establish, the only cohort followed to near-final height
in which age at start, treatment duration and concurrent GH are all measured alongside the
outcome — and it answers a question I had been treating as unanswerable.

The reason I'm writing is that I think two further analyses on the same data would settle
something the field has been arguing about with surrogates.

**1. Pubertal stage at start, as a continuous exposure.** You report that earlier pubertal
stage predicts greater gain (P=0.012). The comparison that matters for mechanism is whether
that gradient is *monotonic within* a single diagnosis — because "started earlier" and "had a
different underlying disorder" are confounded across your diagnostic groups (GHD 31%, early
puberty/premature adrenarche 18%, ISS 15%, overweight 14%). Within the GH-deficient subgroup
alone, does gain still scale with Tanner stage at initiation?

This matters because the only prepubertal-start randomised trial with a true adult-height
endpoint — Merke et al. 2025 in CAH — is null, and its authors recommend against the regimen.
If your gradient holds within a diagnosis, that is evidence the null is about the indication
rather than about the timing.

**2. Did anyone start before puberty?** Your cohort is described by pubertal stage, which
implies all patients were pubertal. If even a handful started prepubertally, their outcomes
would be the only data in existence on aromatase inhibition begun before puberty in children
with normal steroidogenesis — which is the single comparison separating the pharmacological
duration lever from the genetic one. Men with ESR1 or CYP19A1 loss of function reach ~204 cm
having lacked oestrogen signalling from conception; the pharmacological version, started in
puberty, delivers your median +1.2 cm.

**One observation you may find useful, and which I have not seen stated.** Neely et al. (2014,
JCEM) compared the same two drugs at one year on *predicted* adult height and found the
opposite ranking to yours: anastrozole +4.2 vs letrozole +1.4 cm (P=0.03), against your
letrozole +4.2 vs anastrozole +0.8 cm at near-final height (P=0.013). Different cohorts and
different designs, so it is not a paired comparison — but two studies of the same two drugs
ranking them oppositely on a surrogate and on the real endpoint is a fairly direct argument
that predicted adult height should not be used to rank agents in this class. Much of the
literature does.

analysis has already been done, I would be glad to be told so.
