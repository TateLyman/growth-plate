**The question.** Does higher cumulative GH exposure shorten the remaining growth period —
earlier attainment of near-adult height, at the same or lower final height?

**Why now.** Chu et al. (PNAS 2025) showed in mouse that GH depletes the growth-plate stem pool
by shifting divisions from self-renewing to lineage-restricted, cell-autonomously. If that
transfers, GH trades duration for velocity. The published KIGS aggregates have the right shape
— a 43% higher US dose gives more first-year gain (0.75 vs 0.69 ΔHt-SDS) and identical
near-adult height (1.96 vs 1.97) — but they are equally consistent with catch-up to a genetic
target, and 31% vs 9.5% differential follow-through could produce the null on its own. Only
individual participant data separates them.

**Why it matters clinically.** If the association exists, it is an argument for changing GH
dosing schedules — the same total exposure spent differently. If it doesn't, that is worth
knowing too, and the proposal commits to publishing a null as prominently as a positive.

The proposal is attached: objectives, prespecified falsification condition, variable list,
publication plan, and — the part I think matters most — an explicit account of the
confounding-by-indication problem with four planned mitigations and a commitment to report
"not answerable in these data" if they fail.
