# Claim vs counter-claim ledger

Two sections:
1. **Contradictions** - two credible primary sources disagree, unresolved.
2. **X-grade claims** - repeated in reviews but not traceable to primary data.
   Per the confidence scale these are high-value findings, not filler.

| C-L3-03 | **NPR2/GC-B is expressed in the prehypertrophic chondrocyte layer**, with CNP in the non-hypertrophic layers — the localisation used to argue for a short-range local CNP/GC-B system (`nakao2015`, citing the same group's earlier IHC) | **NPR2 transcript is present at similar levels in all three zones**; what is zone-restricted is the effector, PKG-I and PKG-II being expressed at much higher levels in hypertrophic cells, which the authors offer as the explanation for the spatial distribution of CNP effects (`agoston2007`, zonal microarray of microdissected mouse tibia) | `nakao2015` vs `agoston2007` | `npr2_receptor`, `pkg2_kinase`, `cnp_protein`, `pde_isoform_inventory`; whether the zonal partition of CNP action is set at the receptor or at the effector | **Unresolved, and consequential.** If the receptor is uniform and the effector is zoned, then CNP-analogue dosing acts on a tissue whose response is gated downstream of the drug target, and PKG/PDE distribution rather than receptor distribution sets where the drug works. | C-L1-01 | **Chondrocyte columns are the architecture that channels hypertrophic volume increase into longitudinal growth, and column formation is required for elongation.** A century of 2D histology plus the mutant literature (Itgb1, Cdh2, alpha-parvin) treats columnar order as the elongation-enabling structure | **Columns are rare precisely when elongation is fastest.** 3D multicolour clonal analysis of the E18.5 mouse growth plate found only 17.6% (distal femur, n=1044 doublets) and 19.4% (proximal tibia, n=805) of clonal doublets stacked at the 60-90 degree elevation angle that indicates a completed division-plane rotation; most embryonic clones are ellipsoidal clusters oriented **orthogonal** to the growth axis. | C-L1-02 | **Linear growth is saltatory**: infant length accrues in 0.5-2.5 cm bursts separated by 2-63 day intervals of true stasis, with 90-95% of days growth-free (`lampl1992`); canine proximal tibial growth measured with implanted tantalum markers is likewise reported to follow saltation and stasis (`mcbrien2011`) | **Linear growth is continuous.** Rabbit proximal tibial elongation measured directly with about 15-fold better precision than human anthropometry gave a single Gaussian distribution of daily velocities, not the bimodal distribution the saltation model predicts (`klein1994`); a further Science critique followed (`heinrichs1995`). | C-L7-01 | **Terminal growth plate chondrocytes are cleared by apoptosis**, the account carried into fusion by default from the rodent hypertrophic-chondrocyte literature | **The one human growth plate ever examined at the moment of fusion contained zero TUNEL-positive cells**, no detectable cleaved caspase-3 or Bax (only Bad among pro-apoptotic proteins), and no ultrastructural apoptosis or autophagy - instead showing hypoxia and necrosis (`emons2009`). | C-L7-02 | **"Rodents do not fuse their growth plates"** - the standard statement used to explain why fusion cannot be studied in mice (`emons2011mech`) | **Mice do form countable transphyseal bony bridges**, hundreds per tibial plate in wild types (495, SD 45; density 1.2, SD 0.5), rising ~12-fold in density in Socs2-/- animals, which the authors describe as *accelerated growth plate fusion* (`samvelyan2022`); yet the same plate is still calcified cartilage at 55 weeks, having LOST rather than gained type X collagen and MMP-13 (`yu2025`) | `emons2011mech` vs `samvelyan2022`, `yu2025` | `mouse_does_not_fuse`, `bony_bridge_formation`, `species_transfer_risk_fusion`, and the translation_risk grade on every murine node in the atlas | **Terminological, but consequential.** Both are true at their own level: mice bridge but never complete cartilage-to-bone replacement.
