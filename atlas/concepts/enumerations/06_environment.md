## R436 full-concept-space enumeration

**Method.** Everything below was found by EXTERNAL search only (Europe PMC REST API, NCBI eutils
efetch, WebSearch/WebFetch). No file in `/home/user/growth-plate` was read except the two briefs.
Species is stated on every row. Where a number could not be verified from a primary abstract it is
written `UNVERIFIED`. Reviews are marked as index-only.

**Reading the table.** `DIRECTION` = sign of the exposure's effect on attained stature / linear
growth. `HUMAN EFFECT (cm)` is in centimetres of attained or attained-equivalent height wherever a
primary source supplied one; otherwise SD/Z units, odds ratios, or `UNVERIFIED`. `MODIFIABLE IN AN
INDIVIDUAL NOW?` is judged for a person, not a population. `OBSCURE?` = rarely discussed in the
mainstream growth literature — these are the highest-value rows.

## LARGEST ENVIRONMENTAL EFFECT SIZES, RANKED

Ranked by the size of the *measured* human height effect, not by how much attention the exposure gets.
Only effects with a primary number are ranked; qualitative rows are noted separately at the end.

1. **The secular trend itself — up to +20.2 cm in one century.** NCD-RisC (PMID 27458798) is the ceiling
   observation of this entire domain: South Korean women gained **20.2 cm (95% CrI 17.5–22.7)** and Iranian
   men **16.5 cm (13.3–19.7)** across birth cohorts 1896→1996. No single named exposure in this table comes
   close; the secular trend is the *integral* of most of the other rows. It also bounds what is achievable:
   whatever the growth plate's mechanism, the environment demonstrably moves human stature by ~1.5 SD
   within four generations. The counterpoint is row 68 — in several high-income populations the trend has
   **stopped**, which implies the accessible environmental headroom in those populations is now small.

2. **Adoption out of deprivation — +3.0 cm, closing 77% of the deficit.** (PMID 39993655.) This is the
   single cleanest demonstration in the human literature that a *comprehensive* environmental improvement
   produces real catch-up in linear growth, and it still works when applied after 24 months (+2.2 cm).
   The paired negative is that head-circumference catch-up is much less complete (PMID 17700087) — growth
   plasticity is **tissue-specific**, not global.

3. **Prenatal/childhood famine — −0.41 to −1.12 cm, and it crosses a generation.** (PMID 38309830.)
   Exposure in childhood costs more than in utero exposure (−1.12 vs −0.41 cm), and the children of exposed
   parents are **−1.07 to −1.29 cm shorter** despite never experiencing the famine. This is the largest
   quantified *transgenerational* environmental effect on stature in the table.

4. **Consanguinity — −1.2 cm for offspring of first cousins.** (PMID 26131930.) Not strictly an "exposure",
   but it is the best-quantified demographic determinant, and its direction (directional dominance) means
   the *inverse* — outbreeding — is a real positive.

5. **Very preterm birth — −1.7 cm in adult women.** (PMID 27941067.) Sharpened by row 53: preterm birth that
   is *appropriate-for-gestational-age* gives **no** final-height deficit (PMID 27998884). The cost belongs
   to fetal growth restriction, not to prematurity.

6. **Maternal smoking in pregnancy — −0.8 to −1.0 cm in adult daughters.** (PMID 30862963, n=22,421.)
   Dose-graded (light vs heavier smoking), measured on attained adult height, in a very large cohort.

7. **Infection burden, expressed as growth velocity: −0.26 cm/month (diarrhoea), −0.14 (malaria), −0.10
   (respiratory infection).** (PMID 41946349.) These are per-episode-month rates against a background
   velocity of 1.58 cm/month, so a heavily infected child loses on the order of 15–20% of velocity — and
   the effects are additive across pathogens. *H. pylori* separately costs **HAZ −0.41 SD** (PMID 34997950).

8. **Birth order — −0.4/−0.7/−0.8 cm for birth order 3/4/5+.** (PMID 23645856, n=652,518.) Small but
   extraordinarily well powered, and the mechanism (resource dilution vs intrauterine) is still open.

9. **Endocrine disruptors — BPA −0.49 height-Z in boys at the top exposure decile.** (PMID 30185943.) In Z
   units this is the largest chemical effect in the table; PFAS (PMID 40446474) and phthalates
   (PMID 42503950) point the same way with smaller or less-quantified effects.

10. **Childhood tonsillectomy — +0.34 cm adult height.** (PMID 40909198.) Small in absolute terms but it is
    one of only two *positive*, *individually actionable* effects with a hard adult-height number, and the
    effect was **larger the earlier the surgery** and **larger in people with a low height polygenic score**.

**Large but not quantified in cm:** migration to a high-income setting within one generation (Maya-American
children "much taller with longer legs", PMID 15463970) probably belongs in the top three but I could not
extract the magnitude; institutional care (d ≈ −2.4 to −2.6 at placement, PMID 17700087) is of similar
order to famine but expressed in SD.

**Reversible, non-osseous, and worth naming separately:** microgravity increases stature by **up to 3%**
(PMID 34674563) and reverses on return. That is ~5 cm at 170 cm, larger than every drug effect and almost
every environmental effect in this table — and it is entirely intervertebral disc and spinal unloading. Any
measurement protocol that ignores this is noise-limited below the effects it is trying to detect.

**Effects that are famous and turn out to be small or null:**
- **Mass deworming** — Cochrane: little or no effect on height (PMID 31508807).
- **Household WASH** — cluster-randomised: null on linear growth (PMIDs 30554749, 29396217).
- **Removing household air pollution by RCT (LPG cookstove)** — null on linear growth trajectory
  (PMID 41474812), despite observational PM2.5–stunting associations (PMID 36503479).
- **Breastfeeding promotion by RCT** — height difference at 16 y **0.05 cm** (PMID 28459932).
- **Aircraft noise** — essentially null (PMIDs 6624891, 3993764).
- **Ambient temperature / Bergmann's rule in humans** — argued to have virtually no effect (PMID 35414036).
- **Dioxins/PCBs** — no effect on near-adult height, only a ~6-month delay in reaching it (PMID 31466867).

The pattern across those six is consistent and worth stating plainly: **single-component environmental
interventions almost never move linear growth; the interventions that work are the ones that change many
things at once** (adoption, migration, the secular trend) **or that remove a specific ongoing biological
insult in an individual** (OSA, *H. pylori*, coeliac disease, lead source removal).

## EXPOSURES A PERSON COULD ACTUALLY CHANGE

Ordered by expected value to an individual with an open growth plate. Nothing here is a recommendation;
each item is a lead with its evidence grade attached.

**Tier 1 — remove a specific ongoing insult (best evidence, individually actionable)**
1. **Diagnose and treat obstructive sleep apnoea / adenotonsillar hypertrophy.** Untreated paediatric OSA
   carries growth restriction as a recognised complication (PMID 41731114), and childhood tonsillectomy is
   associated with **+0.34 cm attained adult height**, with a larger effect the younger the surgery
   (PMID 40909198). This is the only exposure in the whole domain with a *positive* adult-height number
   attached to a routine, already-indicated clinical procedure.
2. **Test for and eradicate *Helicobacter pylori*.** **HAZ SMD −0.41** (95% CI −0.69 to −0.13) across 29
   studies and 9,384 subjects, with a linear-growth-disorder OR of 1.76 (PMID 34997950). Curable in a week.
3. **Screen for subclinical coeliac disease.** Growth diverges *before* diagnosis in screen-detected cases
   (PMID 32941083) — i.e. the exposure is silent and the test is serological.
4. **Identify and remove lead / cadmium / arsenic sources.** Consistent direction across cohorts
   (PMIDs 36372750, 36287875, 38666332, 40015175). Actionability depends entirely on whether a source
   exists — which is a measurement question (blood lead), not an argument.
5. **Treat maternal depression where a child is still growing** (PMID 41409430).

**Tier 2 — avoidances that are free and where the direction is established**
6. **Do not use anabolic-androgenic steroids while a plate is open.** The magnitude is unverified (row 77)
   but the mechanism — androgen-driven epiphyseal fusion — is not in doubt, and this is the one exposure
   in the domain that a motivated adolescent is likely to *add* deliberately.
7. **Avoid BPA / phthalate / PFAS-heavy exposure routes** (packaging, non-stick, contaminated water).
   BPA −0.49 height-Z in boys at the top decile (PMID 30185943); PFAS reduce linear growth, lean mass and
   BMD at age 3 (PMID 40446474).
8. **Avoid smoking, and — for anyone who may become a parent — avoid smoking and alcohol in pregnancy**
   (PMIDs 30862963, 37612658). The daughters-of-smokers effect is −0.8 to −1.0 cm on *attained adult
   height*, which is larger than several drugs in the wider atlas.
9. **Reduce infection exposure where it is reducible** — the per-episode velocity costs are real
   (PMID 41946349) even though the population-level WASH RCTs are null.

**Tier 3 — plausible, cheap, weakly evidenced**
10. **Stay physically active.** The COVID natural experiment found the lowest growth rate during lockdown
    and a significant post-lockdown rebound in boys (p=0.04) (PMID 41799032). n=72, retrospective — grade D.
11. **Protect sleep quality and duration.** Bone stiffness (not length) is lower with short-or-long plus
    poor-quality sleep, and **daytime napping was positively associated** (PMID 33245373).
12. **Do not over-specialise in a compressive, small-joint, high-volume, low-energy-availability sport.**
    Artistic gymnasts finish below target height while rhythmic gymnasts exceed it (PMID 22450345) — but
    the causality has explicitly *not* been established (PMID 23743792), so this is a caution, not a claim.

**Tier 4 — measurement, not intervention (but it gates everything above)**
13. **Standardise the time of day of every height measurement, and measure sitting height as well as
    standing.** Spinal unloading changes stature by up to 3% (PMID 34674563), and growth velocity is
    seasonal, with **summer accounting for ≥67% of the boy's annual growth** (PMID 28561389) and spring
    lower-leg velocity 8.15 vs 5.06 cm/y in autumn (PMID 7734863). A six-month growth measurement taken
    across the wrong half of the year, at the wrong hour, can invent or erase a centimetre.

**What is NOT modifiable, listed so nobody re-derives it:** altitude of residence (row 1), latitude and
effective day length (row 61), month of birth (row 11), birth order (row 22), consanguinity of one's own
parents (row 69), famine exposure of a grandparent (row 51), preterm birth (row 52).

**The one thing an individual can do that has the largest documented effect is not on the list because it
is not available to an individual: change country, or be born 50 years later.** That is the honest reading
of rows 67 and 72, and it is the reason this domain contributes a ceiling estimate rather than a lever.

## WHAT I COULD NOT VERIFY

- **Anabolic-androgenic steroids and adult height (row 77).** A title-level Europe PMC query for
  *anabolic steroid / androgen abuse* combined with *epiphyseal / growth plate / final height / premature
  closure* returned **one** record, and it was about pulmonary haemorrhage. A broader query for
  *androgens AND "premature epiphyseal closure"* returned only congenital adrenal hyperplasia, McCune-Albright
  and precocious-puberty case material. **I could not find a primary human study measuring final height in
  AAS users.** The row is recorded on mechanism only.
- **Bogin's Maya-American migration magnitude (row 72).** The abstract of PMID 15463970 states the children
  are "much taller" with "longer legs" but gives no centimetre figure in the retrievable text. The
  commonly-quoted magnitude is not verified here and is deliberately omitted.
- **Austrian conscript birth-month effect (row 11).** PMID 40162595 was retrievable in Europe PMC but not
  via NCBI efetch, so I have the direction (June–December births shorter) and the authors' own statement
  that the effect is "very small", but **no cm figure**.
- **Four records with no PMID.** Rows 28 (*Shigella* IPD meta-analysis), 57 (Benin birth-interval study),
  71 (two assortative-mating preprints) and 64 (a 2021 Chinese screen-time/growth record) appear in Europe
  PMC without a PubMed identifier. They are marked `NA` / UNVERIFIED and should not be cited until a PMID
  or DOI is confirmed.
- **Lead: no cm figure.** Every lead–growth study I retrieved reports HAZ/Z-scores or odds ratios, not
  centimetres. The direction is consistent; the magnitude in cm is UNVERIFIED.
- **Altitude: no cm figure.** The India NFHS analysis gives adjusted odds of stunting (OR 1.40 above 2000 m)
  rather than a height difference, and the Tibetan study argues the association may be socioeconomic rather
  than hypoxic. **The causal altitude effect on human stature, in cm, is not established by anything I found.**
- **Fluoride.** The clearest height finding (PMID 1398632) is a 1992 Chinese-language study of 140 children;
  I read it in translation via the Europe PMC abstract only. Treat as grade D.
- **Cannabis (row 76) and noise (row 78)** are recorded as evidence vacuum and near-null respectively; for
  cannabis the systematic review excluded **all 29** full texts it assessed (PMID 30349416), so the vacuum
  is documented rather than assumed.
- **Paywalled / not retrieved in full:** every row is based on abstract-level text plus, where available,
  the structured RESULTS section from NCBI efetch. I did not read any full text. Numbers quoted from
  abstracts have not been checked against tables, and CORR-318-type "figure-only" values are absent by
  construction — where an abstract gave no number I wrote UNVERIFIED rather than estimating.
- **Domains deliberately not enumerated here because they belong elsewhere:** micronutrients and
  macronutrients (nutrition domain), glucocorticoids and other drugs (pharmacology domain), altitude
  *adaptation* genotypes such as EPAS1 (genetics domain), and chronic organ disease (disease domain). Where
  a row touches those — rows 33 (nutrition arm of the WASH trials), 34 (HIV/ART), 85 (coeliac) — it is
  included only because the trial or cohort was the environmental control condition.
