# THE UNFOUND — shared brief (R444)

You are NOT hunting anomalies in the bone-growth literature. Two previous rounds did that and hit
diminishing returns. **You are being sent into a DIFFERENT FIELD**, one that has solved — or at least
deeply characterised — a problem structurally identical to one in human longitudinal bone growth, and
which has **never been connected to it**.

## WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT MOVE

The project this brief serves has spent ~440 rounds searching a literature that was never trying to
make anyone taller. Every compound sweep it ran was shaped `query X AND bone length`, and since nobody
measured bone length except people studying dwarfism, that query returns near-nothing **by
construction**. It cannot find a mechanism that exists under another name in another field.

One concrete example of what we want, which the project found and never followed:

> The terminal chondrocyte enlarges ~10× in volume but only ~1.6× in width — it expands
> **anisotropically**, against a mould of collagen fibrils running longitudinally in the septa. That
> is *precisely* the problem plant biology solved decades ago: a turgid cell expanding against an
> anisotropic wall, with direction set by cellulose microfibril orientation, controlled by cortical
> microtubules, and loosened by expansins and apoplastic pH. **Two mature literatures, identical
> physics, zero citations between them.**

Find more of those.

## HARD RULES

- **DO NOT search the growth plate, physis, epiphysis, chondrocyte or skeletal-dysplasia literature
  as your primary source.** You may check at the END whether your finding has already been connected
  to bone — and reporting "it has been connected, here is the paper" is a *useful* result, not a
  failure. But your search must START outside.
- **NEVER invent a citation, author, year, journal, or number.** If unsure of a PMID write
  `PMID unverified` plus title and first author. A fabricated identifier is worse than a missing one
  and it will be checked.
- **Every claim carries its species/system and, for organisms, the developmental stage.**
- **Prefer measurements to mechanisms and mechanisms to opinions.** A number with an n and a p beats
  a review.
- **Reviews are an index, not a source.** Cite the primary.
- **Paraphrase. Do not reproduce article text.** Short quotes only where the exact wording carries the
  claim, under fifteen words, one per source.
- Search the whole internet — Europe PMC, Google Scholar, bioRxiv, patents, theses, regulatory
  documents, agricultural and veterinary databases, non-English literature.

## WHAT COUNTS AS A HIT

A hit has all four:
1. **A REAL MECHANISM OR MEASUREMENT** in your assigned field, with a primary citation.
2. **A STRUCTURAL MATCH** to a named term of human bone elongation. The terms are:
   - **N** — the number of progenitors in the reserve pool, and how long it lasts
   - **A** — amplification: how many progeny each progenitor yields before it commits
   - **h_term** — how much each terminal cell enlarges, and **IN WHICH DIRECTION**
   - **MATRIX** — how much matrix each cell secretes. *This term is 32–49% of elongation and the
     project has never worked it. Hits here are worth double.*
   - **DISCHARGE** — clearance of the terminal cell so the column advances
   - **PERIOD** — how long the whole thing runs before it stops
3. **A REASON IT MIGHT TRANSFER** — shared physics, a conserved gene family, a homologous cell
   behaviour, or an equivalent constraint.
4. **A NAMED NEXT STEP** — the molecule, gene, parameter or experiment that would test the transfer.

## WHAT IS *NOT* A HIT

- "Pathway X is also present in cartilage." Everything is present in cartilage.
- A gene with a height GWAS hit. The project has 207 with signed effect sizes in centimetres.
- Anything whose only claim is that it makes cells proliferate.
- A drug that damages growth, unless the *mechanism* is both unexplained and instructive.

## OUTPUT

A markdown table, then prose on your best three.

| # | FINDING (one line) | FIELD IT COMES FROM | WHAT IS MEASURED | TERM IT MAPS TO | WHY IT TRANSFERS | HAS ANYONE CONNECTED IT TO BONE? | SOURCE | CONFIDENCE |

Then 200–300 words each on your three strongest: what exactly was measured, in what system, by whom;
what the mechanism is; the precise structural match; **what would have to be true for it to transfer,
and what would falsify it**; and the named next step.

Finish with **"WHAT I LOOKED FOR AND DID NOT FIND"** — a real result, and the section that stops the
next round repeating your search.

Aim for 20–40 rows. A row that is merely interesting is not a hit.
