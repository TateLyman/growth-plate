# DOMAIN 13 — THE AXIAL SKELETON AND TRUNK (complete inventory)
The subject's residual growth is trunk-dominant and nearly every agent in the literature was
characterised on long bones. Enumerate everything specific to axial height.
Cover: vertebral body growth and the endplate physes (superior and inferior); the neurocentral
synchondrosis and its closure timing; the ring apophysis and its maturation stages; the intervertebral
disc as a height component (nucleus pulposus, annulus, endplate cartilage, disc height by level and by
age, notochordal cells, fixed charge density and osmotic loading, diurnal disc height change, disc
growth cessation age); the sacrum and coccyx; the skull base synchondroses (spheno-occipital,
spheno-ethmoidal) and their closure; craniofacial height contribution; cervical, thoracic and lumbar
segment contributions to sitting height; sitting height vs subischial leg length and the upper:lower
segment ratio; spinal curvature (kyphosis, lordosis, scoliosis) and its effect on measured height;
vertebral wedging; Scheuermann disease; the somite and sclerotome origin of the axial skeleton;
HOX code along the axis and axial identity genes (PAX1, PAX9, TBX6, MEOX, NKX3-2, UNCX, MSGN1,
segmentation clock HES7/LFNG/DLL3); the axial vs appendicular difference in signalling response
(what works in a limb and not in a vertebra, and why); vertebral-specific dysplasias
(spondylo- prefixed conditions, brachyolmia, SEDC, SEMD, Kniest, dyssegmental, spondylocostal and
spondylothoracic dysostosis); vertebral growth modulation devices and tethering; growing rods;
vertebral body stapling; the axial response to GH, to CNP analogues, to FGFR inhibitors, to
oestrogen and to glucocorticoid, treated SEPARATELY from the limb response; whether any agent has
ever been given with a vertebral length endpoint; and the measurement of trunk growth itself.
