# DOMAIN 20 — THE QUANTITATIVE STRUCTURE OF GROWTH (models, arithmetic, measurement)
Height is an arithmetic outcome of a small number of measurable terms, and this domain enumerates the
terms, the models and the measurements rather than the biology.
Cover: the kinetic decomposition of longitudinal growth (chondrocyte production rate, cell cycle time,
clonal amplification / cells produced per progenitor, proliferative zone residence time, hypertrophic
cell height and volume, matrix synthesis per cell, and their measured values by species, bone and age);
stereological and kinetic methods for measuring them (Hunziker's method, tritiated thymidine and BrdU/
EdU, calcein/xylenol double labelling, tetracycline labelling, fluorochrome interlabel distance,
confocal disector); growth rate by bone and by physis in humans (which physis contributes what, distal
femur vs proximal tibia vs vertebrae vs humerus); the ICP and other growth models (Preece-Baines,
Jolicoeur, JPA, SITAR, Karlberg, Gompertz, logistic); saltation and stasis; growth velocity curves and
their derivatives; peak height velocity and its determinants; allometry and scaling; segment
proportions and their normal ranges by age and ancestry; adult height prediction (Bayley-Pinneau,
Roche-Wainer-Thissen, Tanner-Whitehouse, Khamis-Roche, BoneXpert-based) and the published error of each;
mid-parental target height and its regression to the mean; the heritability arithmetic; measurement
methodology (stadiometry technique, sitting height and subischial length, knee height, arm span,
segmental measures, diurnal variation, measurement error and SE, digital and imaging-based stature,
EOS, DXA-derived length); growth charts and reference populations; SDS/Z-score computation and
LMS; catch-up and catch-down quantification; how much height each remaining bone-age year is worth;
and any published quantitative or computational model of the growth plate (finite element,
agent-based, reaction-diffusion, continuum growth mechanics, systems models of the GH-IGF axis).
