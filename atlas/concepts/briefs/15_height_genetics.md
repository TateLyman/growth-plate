# DOMAIN 15 — HUMAN HEIGHT GENETICS AT SCALE (complete inventory)
Enumerate the genetic architecture of human stature and the instruments used to measure it — including
each instrument's design decisions, because CORR-358 says those decisions are inherited by every
conclusion drawn from them.
Cover: the major height GWAS in sequence and their sizes (Weedon, Lango Allen, Lettre, Wood 2014,
Yengo 2018, Yengo 2022 5.4M, and any 2023-2026 successor); exome and whole-genome burden studies
(Marouli 2017 rare variants, Akbari, Kosmicki 2026 and its 207 genes); the saturation claim and the
12,111 variants / 21% of genome result; heritability estimates from twins, siblings, pedigrees, SNPs
and whole genomes and the missing-heritability gap; polygenic scores for height and their portability;
Mendelian randomisation using height; the recessive and biallelic architecture invisible to burden tests;
copy number variants and structural variation; the pseudoautosomal region and SHOX dosage; X and Y
chromosome effects and sex-chromosome aneuploidy; mitochondrial DNA effects; parent-of-origin effects;
gene-environment interaction on height; assortative mating; ancestry differences and population-specific
variants; consanguinity effects; the specific gene families the GWAS keeps returning (ECM, chondrocyte
TFs, chromatin, IGF/GH, hedgehog, TGF-beta/BMP, cell cycle, nuclear hormone receptors); functional
follow-up of height loci (CRISPR screens, MPRA, enhancer maps, chondrocyte eQTL and caQTL, Hi-C in
cartilage); tissue enrichment analyses of height loci; the constraint metrics (pLI, LOEUF, s_het,
MisZ) and what they say about which height genes are drug targets; databases and resources (GWAS
Catalog, OpenTargets, GTEx, IMPC, MGI, HPO, OMIM, ClinVar, gnomAD, DECIPHER, Monarch, Human Cell Atlas,
UK Biobank, All of Us, FinnGen, Biobank Japan, China Kadoorie, Million Veteran Program) and what each
would return for height that this atlas may never have queried.
