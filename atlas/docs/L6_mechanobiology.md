That is the correct shape for a layer whose central coefficient has never been measured in the

---

## 1. The settled core

**Load modulates physeal growth rate, and the relationship is approximately linear over the
range tested.** Sustained compression reduces and distraction increases longitudinal growth:
compressed rat tail vertebra grows at **52%** of control (p = 0.002), distracted at **113%**
(n.s.) after four weeks at nominally 60% body weight (`stokes2002`). Pooled across rat, rabbit
and calf caudal vertebrae and proximal tibiae over 8-day windows, the sensitivity is
**17.1% growth change per 0.1 MPa** (range across plates 9.2–23.9%; 15.0% for vertebral, 18.6%
for proximal tibial plates), against baseline unloaded rates spanning 30–366 µm/day
(`stokes2006`). Maximum achievable growth-rate change across the loading range tested: **53%**
(`stokes2007`). Grade C, and every animal in it.

**The cellular route is hypertrophy, not proliferation.** Under sustained compression,
hypertrophic cell height falls to **85%** of control, zone height to 87%, and the per-cell
height increment to 78% (`stokes2002`). Multiple regression across pooled loaded and control
plates gives a normalised coefficient of **1.39** for final hypertrophic cell height (r = 0.56)
against **0.72** for proliferative cells per unit width (r = 0.38) (`stokes2007`). Load acts
where L1 says elongation is generated.

**Guided growth works in children, and the effect size is human and large-n.** 654 growth
modulations in 313 children: distal femoral correction **0.67°/month (SD 0.55)**, proximal
tibial **0.43°/month (SD 0.38)**, **68.7%** achieving a neutral mechanical axis (idiopathic
78.2% vs non-idiopathic 66.3%, p = 0.02) (`tolk2026`). Grade A, not_applicable translation risk.
`epiphysiodesis` and `distraction_osteogenesis` are likewise grade A human interventions.

**Diurnal stature loss is human, replicated and large relative to daily growth.** 19.3 mm mean
circadian variation in adults (1.1% of stature, 54% lost in the first hour after rising, 70%
regained in the first half of the night, n = 8, precision 1 mm, `tyrrell1985`); 2.0–4.6 mm over
a working day in 12–14-year-old boys (`whitehouse1974`); 7 mm (SD 7) in 98 children with
idiopathic scoliosis (p < 0.001, `czaprowski2019`). Against a mean daily height increment of
~0.02 mm, **the diurnal artefact exceeds one year of growth**, which is why L1's saltation
dispute (C-L1-02) cannot be settled by surface anthropometry.

**Unloading raises stature acutely and reversibly.** Nine ISS crew gained up to **3%** of
preflight stature with segment circumferences falling up to 11%, fully reversed postflight
(`young2023`). This is spinal column decompression, not physeal growth — and no measurement in
an **open** physis in microgravity exists (`g_l6l5_009`).

## 2. The live disagreements

**"Dynamic loading is growth-preserving" is a conflation of two different experiments, and the
atlas separates them.** This is the layer's most consequential correction and it is entirely a
reading error the field has institutionalised.

- `stokes2005` varied **duty cycle**: load removed for 12 h/day. Rat vertebral growth 82% (24 h)
  vs 90–93% (12 h) of within-animal control; proximal tibia 70% vs 84–86% (n = 5/group, sham
  100% and 89%). Growth is spared when the load is *off* part of the time.
- `valteau2011` varied **waveform about a non-zero mean**, with the load never removed: 0.2 MPa
  sustained vs 0.2 MPa ± 30% at 0.1 Hz for two weeks gave **19% vs 19%** growth suppression,
  both p < 0.001 vs sham, the static–dynamic difference **not significant**. What *was* spared
  was histomorphometry: plate thickness and proliferative cells per column fell under static
  only (p < 0.01 static vs dynamic; hypertrophic cell height p = 0.014).

Reviews cite both under "dynamic loading is protective". The atlas's reading:
**mean stress integrated over time sets growth rate; waveform sets tissue damage**
(c-L6-01, `g_l6mech_004`, tract 5). The orthogonal 2×3 factorial that would settle it —
duty cycle × waveform at matched mean stress — has never been run.

**And the architectural sign inverts between in vivo and explant** (c-L6-02, `g_l6l5_002`).
`sergerie2011` loaded 4-week swine ulnar physeal explants at matched mean strain (10% static
vs 7–13% dynamic at 0.1 Hz, 48 h) and found **DYNAMIC** compression caused severe loss of
columnar arrangement while **STATIC** preserved it — the opposite of `valteau2011` — while
dynamic simultaneously *increased* aggrecan and collagen II which static depleted. **The same
paper therefore sits on both sides of the ledger depending on which endpoint is scored**, and
part of the explant phenotype may be zonal cell death rather than architecture:
`kaviani2015` shows explant chondrocyte viability falls with frequency and amplitude, with
proliferative and hypertrophic zones most sensitive. `zimmermann2017` adds that hypertrophic
lateral and volumetric strain are *higher* under static (p < 0.001, p ≤ 0.015), and that
high-frequency dynamic gives lower volumetric strain than low-frequency (p = 0.002).

**Does gymnastics remove adult height, or select short late-maturing girls?** (c-L6-02 second
entry, `g_l6mech_010`). For causation: peak height velocity 5.48 vs 8.0 cm/yr against swimmers
at ~22 vs ~8 h/week (SEM 0.32/0.50, p < 0.05 at bone ages 11–13), sitting-height/leg-length
ratio 1.054 vs 1.100 (p < 0.001), height SDS falling with time at **r = −0.747** (p < 0.001,
vs r = −0.165 in swimmers) and **no change in the chronological-age/bone-age ratio**
(`theintz1993`); a within-sport dose gradient of peripubertal sitting-height velocity 2.3 vs
3.1 cm/yr by training volume, with >35% of pre- and peripubertal gymnasts showing velocity
<4.5 cm/yr (`daly2005`). For selection: spurts present and ~1 year late, matching short
late-maturing girls with short parents (`thomis2005`), and the FIG review finding
epidemiological criteria for causality unmet (`malina2013`). **Both sides are right about
different things**: attenuation *during* training is real and dose-related; removal of *adult*
height is not established. Neither side has separated mechanical loading of the physis from
energy deficit and hypothalamic suppression. `gymnastics_stature_effect` and
`sport_specific_loading_human` carry reciprocal `CONTRADICTS`.

**The mechanotransducer is unassigned, and one candidate is grade X.** `piezo1_channel` and
`piezo2_channel` carry reciprocal `CONTRADICTS`; PIEZO protein has never been localised in
**human** growth plate (`g_l6mech_007`). x-L6-01: "YAP/TAZ mediates chondrocyte
mechanotransduction" is the default pathway of growth-plate mechanobiology reviews and of
stiffness-tuned scaffold design — a Europe PMC search combining YAP/TAZ/WWTR1/Hippo with growth
plate/physis and loading and in vivo/knockout returned **461 records, all screened, none
qualifying**. No chondrocyte-specific *Yap1*/*Wwtr1* deletion has ever been combined with a
physeal mechanical challenge and a growth readout, **in any species**. `yap_taz_chondrocyte` is
held at grade **E** deliberately (`g_l6mech_009`).

## 3. The load-bearing assumption

**That the Hueter-Volkmann coefficient — 17.1% growth change per 0.1 MPa — describes the human
physis, and that it can be multiplied by a human physeal stress.**

Both factors of that product fail, and they fail differently.

The **coefficient** was measured in rat, rabbit and calf caudal vertebrae and proximal tibiae
over 8-day loading windows, at applied stresses of roughly **0.02–0.2 MPa chosen for the
loading apparatus, not for physiology** (`stokes2006`, `physeal_stress_in_vivo`). It pools
species whose baseline growth rates differ by more than tenfold (30–366 µm/day) into a single
linear fit whose confidence interval is not reported, and its per-plate range is 9.2–23.9% —
a 2.6-fold spread inside the pooled number.

The **multiplicand does not exist**. In vivo human physeal stress has never been measured, in
any age group. A Europe PMC search returned **232 records** on human physeal stress-growth
sensitivity, every quantitative human statement in them being model output *using the animal
coefficient as input*; a second search screened **2,851 records** for a direct human physeal
stress measurement and found none (`g_l6mech_001`, `g_l6mech_003`, x-L6-02).

So: `hueter_volkmann_law` is grade **C**, species rat/rabbit/bovine, `human_evidence: indirect`
— and it is the quantitative basis on which guided growth, vertebral body tethering and every
finite-element scoliosis-progression model is planned. `scoliosis_vertebral_growth` explicitly
records that the 15.0%/0.1 MPa vertebral coefficient applied to human frontal-plane geometry
**is an extrapolation**.

The honest position is a split verdict, and it is the only interesting thing to say about this
layer: **the clinical practice is validated and the model underneath it is not.** `tolk2026`'s
654 modulations in 313 children establish that tension-band growth modulation works and at what
rate. Nothing in that dataset validates 17.1%/0.1 MPa, because the applied stress was never
measured.

## 4. What would change everything

**An instrumented tension-band implant.** Guided-growth plates are already implanted in
children by the hundreds; adding a strain gauge and telemetry supplies the missing multiplicand
directly, in the exact clinical situation the coefficient is used to plan. Paired with the
measured per-month angular correction that the same implant produces (`tolk2026` gives the
population values), it yields a **human** stress–growth coefficient in one prospective cohort.

Three outcomes, each a rewrite. If the human coefficient lands near 17.1%/0.1 MPa, the animal
transfer is vindicated and forty years of modelling becomes quantitative rather than
illustrative. If it is materially different, every FE model of scoliosis progression and every
tethering dose calculation is recalibrated. If growth response proves **non-linear** across the
physiological range — which `g_l6mech_002` asks and no experiment has bounded, since the animal
work spans only 0.02–0.2 MPa — then the linear coefficient is a local tangent and its
extrapolation to gait-level stresses is invalid regardless of species.

A second result would be nearly as destabilising: resolving why mechanically modulated growth
becomes **load-independent after two to four weeks** of sustained tethering (`g_l6mech_005`).
If that escape is adaptation of the physis, the linear model has no steady state and every
long-horizon prediction built on it is wrong in a direction nobody has characterised.

## 6. Top gaps and their discriminating experiments

1. **`g_l6mech_003`** (method_blocked, tract 2) — measure human physeal stress. Instrumented
   tension-band implant, or an instrumented external fixator across a physis during
   distraction. This is the multiplicand the whole layer is missing; everything else is
   secondary to it.
2. **`g_l6mech_004`** (contradiction, tract 5) — the orthogonal 2×3 factorial: duty cycle
   (continuous / 12 h / 6 h) × waveform (sustained / ±30% at 0.1 Hz / ±30% at 1 Hz) at matched
   *mean* stress, one species, one plate, with growth rate **and** histomorphometry read
   together. "Mean stress sets rate, waveform sets damage" predicts main effect of duty cycle on
   rate and of waveform on architecture, with no interaction.
3. **`g_l6mech_002`** (quantitative_gap, tract 5) — where does the linear stress–growth relation
   saturate? Ten stress levels from 0.005 to 1.0 MPa in one species. A tangent that is quoted
   without its bounds is being extrapolated blind.
4. **`g_l6mech_005`** (known_unknown, tract 4) — why does modulation become load-independent
   after 2–4 weeks? Distinguish physeal adaptation from implant/soft-tissue creep by measuring
   applied force continuously (same instrumented implant as gap 1) while tracking growth.
5. **`g_l6mech_008`** (known_unknown, tract 4) — which transducer is *required* for the
   Hueter-Volkmann response, as opposed to being merely present? Chondrocyte-specific deletion
   of PIEZO1, TRPV4, ITGB1, IFT88 and YAP/TAZ, each combined with the **same** tail-compression
   protocol and growth readout. Currently none has been tested with a load.
6. **`g_l6mech_010`** (known_unknown, tract 3) — separate mechanics from energetics in athletes.
   The within-athlete segment contrast: heavily loaded distal radius vs lightly loaded lower
   limb, with energy availability measured. Mechanical suppression predicts segment-specific
   attenuation; energy deficit predicts uniform attenuation.
7. **`g_l6mech_011`** (search_established, tract 3) — the 1 mm/day, 4 increments/day distraction
   prescription has never been dose-optimised in humans. `ilizarov1989` tested 1, 4 and 60
   increments/day in dogs and outcome improved **monotonically with frequency** — so the
   clinical standard of 4 is a compromise, not the measured optimum, and has stood unexamined
   for four decades.

## 7. Human-translation status

**10 of 31 nodes (32%) carry direct human evidence, and all ten are replicated** — the only
layer where those two numbers coincide. But the human and animal halves of L6 are not about the
same thing, and the split is clean.

The **human** nodes are outcomes: `epiphysiodesis`, `guided_growth_tension_band`,
`distraction_osteogenesis`, `diurnal_stature_variation`, `spaceflight_microgravity_growth`,
`gymnastics_stature_effect`, `blount_disease`, `scoliosis_vertebral_growth`,
`gravity_posture_spinal_loading`. They tell you what happens to a child's skeleton under a
clinical or behavioural load. **None of them includes a measurement of the load.**

The **animal** nodes are the mechanism: `hueter_volkmann_law`, `static_vs_dynamic_loading`,
`strain_magnitude_dependence`, `strain_rate_dependence`, `strain_frequency_dependence`,
`loading_effect_plate_height`, `mechanical_modulation_growth` — rat, rabbit, bovine, porcine,
14 of 31 nodes at high translation risk. They tell you the dose–response and the cellular
route, in species whose baseline growth rates differ from human by an order of magnitude and
whose plates were loaded through an apparatus at stresses chosen for the apparatus.

The molecular nodes are worse than either: `yap_taz_chondrocyte` (E),
`focal_adhesion_kinase` (E), `piezo1_channel` (C, mouse), `piezo2_channel` (D),
`primary_cilium_mechanosensing` (C, mouse), `cytoskeletal_tension_chondrocyte` (D) — six
candidate transducers, none tested with a mechanical load in vivo, none localised in human
tissue.

So the layer joins at neither end. The correct statement of any L6 answer: **the human
outcome data are strong, the animal dose–response is strong, and there is no measurement
anywhere that connects them.**
