---

## 1. The settled core

**Height is the most responsive human trait to environment on a century scale, and the ceiling
is known.** NCD-RisC pooled 1,472 adult studies (18.6 million measured adults, 200 countries,
birth cohorts 1896–1996) and 2,181 child/adolescent studies (65 million, ages 5–19, 1985–2019).
The largest century-scale gain is **+20.2 cm in South Korean women** (95% CrI 17.5–22.7), and
**+16.5 cm in Iranian men** (95% CrI 13.3–19.7) — an implied maximum sustained rate of
**2.0 cm/decade** (1.75–2.27) (`ncd2016`). The lowest recorded national mean is **140.3 cm**
(Guatemalan women, 1896 cohort, 95% CrI 135.8–144.8).

**And the between-country spread has not narrowed.** Tallest minus shortest national mean was
19–20 cm in the 1896 birth cohort and **19–20 cm in the 1996 cohort**, unchanged for women
(`ncd2016`); at age 19 in 2019 the spread is still **≥20 cm** (`ncd2020`). A century of
20-centimetre gains left the inequality exactly where it was.

**The trend is bidirectional.** Mean adult height **fell by ~5 cm from its cohort peak** in
Niger, Rwanda, Sierra Leone and Uganda (`ncd2016`, `secular_trend_reversal`, grade A). Whatever
raises height can be withdrawn.

**Rate is set by conditions, sharply and locally.** German male conscripts: **0.45 mm/yr** before
1916, **2.15 mm/yr** 1916–1933, and **4 mm/yr** in East Germans after 1989 reunification
(`scheffler2025`) — a nearly tenfold swing in secular rate within one population.

**Famine leaves a permanent, measurable and modest deficit.** Forty years after the 1984–85
Great Ethiopian Famine, adult height is **−1.4 cm** (95% CI −2.4 to −0.3) against matched
controls and **−2.4 cm** (−3.7 to −1.1) against post-famine-born controls (`abera2026`). Grade A.

**Being born short is the strongest single predictor of being short.** Relative risk of short
final stature for birth length < −2 SDS is **7.1** (95% CI 3.7–13.6) in 3,650 healthy full-term
Swedish singletons; 22% of short 18-year-olds were short at birth (14% by birth-weight
definition) (`karlberg1995`). SGA catch-up: 82.9% within 6 months, 94.3% reaching a normal-range
final height; of infants still short at 6 months, **15.2%** remained short into adulthood
(`karlberg2002`).

## 2. The live disagreements

**The Dutch plateau is population-specific, not environmental — and the evidence is two papers
from the same national surveys.** Dutch-origin children showed **0 cm change in height-by-age
between 1997 and 2009** (`schnbeck2013`, n = 5,811 boys / 6,194 girls; mean final height 183.8 cm
SD 7.1 and 170.7 cm SD 6.3). In the *same surveys, the same country and the same period*,
Turkish- and Moroccan-origin children **gained 2–3 cm** (`schnbeck2015`, n = 2,548 and 2,594),
reaching 177/163 cm at 18 — still **5.5–7 cm below Dutch-origin peers**.

The Dutch environment therefore did not stop delivering growth. It stopped delivering it to one
population while continuing to deliver it to another living in it. Any reading of the plateau as
"the Netherlands has reached the environmental ceiling" is contradicted by children in the
Netherlands still climbing toward it. Gap `g_l10env_005` states the alternative explicitly:
attained genetic ceiling, or persistence of an unmeasured growth-limiting exposure that affects
the two groups differently?

**Nutrient supplementation closes almost none of the stunting deficit.** Small-quantity
lipid-based nutrient supplements, pooled across 15 RCTs in 10 low- and middle-income countries,
n = 36,970: **+0.15 length-for-age Z** (95% CI 0.12–0.17). Expressed as attainable benefit,
that is **11%** (95% CI 9.4–12, 12 comparisons, n = 19,768, **I² = 90%**) — and in height-age
terms, **12 days** (95% CI 9–14) (`watson2025`). Zinc does slightly better on the same scale:
+0.350 SD height increment (95% CI 0.189–0.511) across 33 RCTs, with serum zinc rising 0.820 SD
confirming exposure (`brown2002`). Vitamin D3 does nothing for height: no significant
height-for-age effect (Z = 0.43, P = 0.67) while improving weight-for-age (Z = 2.72, P = 0.007),
across 12 RCTs (`peng2025`).

So the field's dominant intervention closes **~11%** of a deficit that the secular record shows
is ~20 cm in magnitude and fully reversible over a century. **`g_l10env_006` asks where the
other 89% is**, and nominates inflammation and infection burden. The atlas has partial support:
in 740 stunted Ugandan children (mean HAZ −3.02, median IGF-1 **37.4 µg/L**, IQR 24.2–53.3), a
positive malaria test is associated with **−4.7 µg/L** IGF-1 (95% CI −7.8 to −1.6) — and that
association **attenuates after adjustment for inflammation** (`madigan2026`), which puts
inflammation upstream of infection in the causal chain rather than beside it. Environmental
enteric dysfunction markers are elevated in **>80%** of Indonesian infants at 6 months
(`htet2026`), across a literature of 81 studies and 31,996 children (`lowe2025`).

**Milk raises IGF-1 and isonitrogenous meat does not, so it is not the protein.** Seven days of
1.5 L/day skimmed milk in 8-year-old boys: **+19%** serum IGF-I (P = 0.001) and +13%
IGF-I/IGFBP-3 (P < 0.0001), at 4.0 g protein/kg/day. Isonitrogenous lean meat raising protein
intake 54%: **0%** IGF-I change (`hoppe2004`). The 2×2 factorial resolves the fraction:
**casein +15%** IGF-1 (P < 0.0001), whey **+21% fasting insulin** (P = 0.006) with IGF-1
unchanged (P = 0.27), and milk minerals (Ca, P) with **no independent effect** on either and no
protein × mineral interaction (`hoppe2009`, n = 57). This is one of the cleanest dose-fractionation
experiments in the whole atlas and it makes the "protein intake raises height" claim
substantially narrower than it is usually stated.

**DOHaD's genetic and environmental components are separable, and famine erases the genetic
signal.** In the Dutch Hunger Winter Families Study, a birthweight polygenic index explains
**14%** of birthweight variance in unexposed controls (r = 0.38, P < 0.001, n = 161) but
correlates at **r = 0.10** — indistinguishable from zero — in those exposed in mid-to-late
gestation (n = 283) (`taeubert2026`). Severe prenatal constraint overrides genotype at the
birthweight endpoint.

**Five nodes carry `translation_risk: unknown`** — `altitude_hypoxia_growth`,
`endocrine_disrupting_chemicals`, `iron_deficiency_growth`, `placental_function_growth`,
`vitamin_a_growth` — all grade D or B on human epidemiology with no established mechanism at the
plate. Two searches make the shape explicit: does high-altitude residence change oxygen tension
inside the human growth plate (`g_l10env_010`)? Do BPA or phthalate metabolites, at
concentrations measured in children's urine, act on chondrocytes via ERα or aromatase
(`g_l10env_011`)? Neither has ever been asked experimentally. Meanwhile the Tibetan secular
trend at ~3,600 m is **increasing** across four national survey waves 2005–2019 (`zhang2026`),
which is hard to reconcile with altitude as a fixed growth constraint.

## 3. The load-bearing assumption

**That the secular trend measures the environmental component of height, so a plateau in an
affluent population marks the genetic ceiling.**

This is the licence for everything L10 is used for: reading population mean height as a health
and nutrition indicator (NCD-RisC's explicit premise, 18.6 million people), bounding the
achievable effect of any intervention, and interpreting the Dutch and Scandinavian plateaus as
biology rather than as an unfinished exposure.

The evidence for it is strong in one direction: the trend is real, huge (+20.2 cm/century),
bidirectional (−5 cm in four sub-Saharan countries), and rate-responsive to identifiable events
(0.45 → 2.15 → 4 mm/yr in German conscripts around two wars and a reunification). Height is
demonstrably environmentally responsive.

The evidence against the **ceiling** reading is §2's migrant contrast, and it is decisive as far
as it goes: Turkish- and Moroccan-origin children in the Netherlands gained 2–3 cm over precisely
the period Dutch-origin children gained 0, and remain 5.5–7 cm shorter. Either the two groups
have different genetic ceilings — in which case "the environmental ceiling" is not a population-independent
quantity and the plateau tells you nothing generalisable — or the Dutch-origin plateau reflects
something other than exhaustion of environmental headroom.

And the decomposition that would settle it does not exist. **`g_l10env_008` (tract 5): of the
~20 cm per century, how much is attributable to protein and energy intake, how much to infection
and inflammation, how much to each remaining candidate?** Never partitioned. The largest
environmental effect on any human trait has never been apportioned to its causes. That gap sits
directly beneath the SQ-LNS result: if nutrient supplementation buys 11% and the total available
is 20 cm, the other 89% is attributed to a set of exposures that has never been quantitatively
enumerated.

## 4. What would change everything

**A migrant-generation series with measured exposures.** First-, second- and third-generation
children of one origin population in one high-income host country, with measured dietary intake,
infection burden, inflammatory markers (CRP, AGP), EED markers and socioeconomic position at each
generation, against host-origin controls in the same surveys.

If height converges on host-origin means by generation three while exposures converge, the
plateau is environmental, the Dutch ceiling is not a ceiling, and the 5.5–7 cm gap is a
measurable and closable exposure deficit. If height plateaus below the host-origin mean while
exposures have converged, the ceiling reading survives, "genetic potential" becomes an estimable
population parameter, and NCD-RisC's use of mean height as a pure health indicator needs an
ancestry adjustment it currently does not carry.

Either result changes how 18.6 million measurements are interpreted. The data infrastructure
already exists — `schnbeck2015` did the height half of this in the Dutch surveys and simply did
not measure the exposures.

The second-order rewrite would come from `g_l10env_006`: partition the 89% of the stunting
deficit that SQ-LNS does not close. A factorial trial of nutrient supplementation × infection
control (azithromycin or WASH) × anti-inflammatory intervention in one stunted cohort would do
it, and would tell the field whether its dominant intervention modality is 11% effective because
the dose is wrong or because the target is wrong.

## 6. Top gaps and their discriminating experiments

1. **`g_l10env_005`** (known_unknown, tract 4) — genetic ceiling or unmeasured exposure? See §4.
   The migrant-generation series with measured exposures is the discriminator, and the Dutch
   surveys have already done half of it.
2. **`g_l10env_008`** (quantitative_gap, tract 5) — decompose the ~20 cm/century. Discriminator:
   structural decomposition across the NCD-RisC country-year panel using contemporaneous
   protein/energy supply, under-5 mortality, sanitation coverage and income, with the German
   conscript rate discontinuities (0.45 → 2.15 → 4 mm/yr) as natural experiments for validation.
3. **`g_l10env_006`** (quantitative_gap, tract 3) — where is the other 89% of the stunting
   deficit? Factorial trial: nutrient supplementation × infection control × anti-inflammatory,
   with LAZ as the endpoint and EED/CRP/AGP as mediators. `madigan2026`'s finding that the malaria
   association attenuates on inflammation adjustment predicts inflammation is the larger arm.
4. **`g_l10env_004`** (quantitative_gap, tract 2) — measure IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α **in human
   growth plate interstitium** during chronic inflammatory disease, and compare to the 10–100
   ng/mL concentrations used in `choukair2014` and `macrae2006`. The entire mechanistic account of
   inflammatory growth failure is built on cell-line and explant doses that have never been shown
   to occur in a child.
5. **`g_l10env_009`** (species_gap, tract 2) — are SOCS2, SOCS3 and phospho-STAT5B expressed in
   human growth plate, and does abundance differ with chronic inflammation? This is the proposed
   mechanism of inflammatory GH resistance and it has never been looked for in the target tissue.
6. **`g_l10env_010` / `g_l10env_011`** (search_established, tract 3) — altitude and endocrine
   disruptors. Both nodes are `translation_risk: unknown` because the exposure–tissue link has
   never been tested. For altitude, the Tibetan **increasing** secular trend at 3,600 m
   (`zhang2026`) already argues against a simple hypoxic constraint and nobody has followed it up.
7. **`g_l0l9_009`** (species_gap, tract 4) — does human undernutrition raise FGF21 to
   growth-suppressing concentrations, or is the FGF21 route mouse-specific? Measure FGF21 in
   stunted children against the concentrations that suppress murine chondrocytes; pair with
   `g_l0l9_001`, which asks whether β-Klotho is even expressed in human physis.

## 7. Human-translation status

**29 of 34 nodes (85%) carry direct human evidence, 18 replicated, and 17 carry
`translation_risk: not_applicable`.** For the *epidemiology*, this layer is as strong as the
atlas gets: NCD-RisC alone is 18.6 million measured adults and 65 million children, and the
supplementation evidence is meta-analysed randomised trials in tens of thousands.

The translation problem in L10 is not species — it is **the missing middle**. The exposure is
measured in humans at population scale; the outcome is measured in humans at population scale;
and the step in between, where an exposure acts on a growth plate, is either mouse, cell line, or
nothing at all.

Three nodes carry the mechanism and none is human-physeal.
`inflammation_growth_suppression` (B, `he=indirect`, `tr=high`) rests on a **rat chondrogenic cell
line** at 10–100 ng/mL cytokine (`choukair2014`) and **mouse metatarsal explants**
(`macrae2006`); `g_l10env_004` records that the corresponding human physeal concentrations have
never been measured, so it is not known whether the doses are physiological.
`microbiome_igf1_axis` (C, `he=absent`, `tr=high`) is entirely mouse — germ-free conventionalisation
and SCFA rescue (`yan2016`) — and `g_l10env_001` records that the human test has not been done.
`gut_microbiome_growth` (C, `tr=high`) is the same evidence with a human correlative layer.

And **five nodes carry `translation_risk: unknown`**, which in this atlas's vocabulary means the
mechanism is not merely unproven but unlocatable: altitude, endocrine-disrupting chemicals, iron,
placental function and vitamin A. Each has a human epidemiological association (some strong —
`frisancho1991`'s inverse lead–stature relation across 0.14–1.92 µmol/L in 1,454 children) and no
identified target in the growth plate. `g_l10env_007` asks the question for four of them at once:
**which specific protein in the growth plate is the rate-limiting target for zinc, iron, vitamin A
and lead?** Unanswered for all four.

The honest summary: **L10 knows, at a precision no other layer approaches, that environment moves
human height by up to 20 cm per century — and cannot say through which molecule.**
