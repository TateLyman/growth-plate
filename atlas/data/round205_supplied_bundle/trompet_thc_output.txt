WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND IT IS NOT WHAT ROUND 204 EXPECTED
============================================================================================

[1] THE h_term EFFECT IS REAL AND IT IS TRANSIENT.
    femur  1 week 1.00x  ->  1 month 1.18x  ->  2 months 0.93x
    tibia  1 week 1.07x  ->  1 month 1.25x  ->  2 months 1.09x
    Nothing at one week. A clear rise at ONE MONTH, in both bones, with every
    animal moving the same way in the tibia. GONE BY TWO MONTHS - and in the
    femur it goes BELOW control.

[2] WHY THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE PEAK VALUE.
    trompet2024's LENGTH gain persists to SIX MONTHS and is the only
    length-endpoint proof in this arm of the atlas. The h_term gain does not
    last two. SO THE SUSTAINED LENGTH GAIN IS NOT CARRIED BY TERMINAL CELL
    HEIGHT. The authors' 'probably due to an elevation in the height of the
    terminal hypertrophic chondrocytes' is true at one timepoint and false at
    the timepoint where the length actually accumulates.

[3] AND IT PUTS A NUMBER ON HOW BIG AN h_term EFFECT CAN GET.
    The largest terminal-cell-height ratio anywhere in this dataset is 1.25x.
    Set against weber2025's +20 per cent under NPR3 loss and hunziker1994's
    1.36x under growth hormone, THE WHOLE OBSERVED RANGE OF h_term ACROSS
    EVERY AGENT THIS ATLAS HOLDS IS ROUGHLY 1.0 TO 1.4x. That is the ceiling
    the free axis is worth, and it is far smaller than the 5x exchange rate
    of round 202 made it sound.

[4] THE GROWTH PLATE HEIGHT AND THE CELL HEIGHT DISAGREE, WHICH IS CORR-197
    IN LIVE ACTION.
    femur 1 week    zone 1.001x   cell height 1.000x
    femur 1 month   zone 1.158x   cell height 1.177x
    femur 2 months  zone 1.052x   cell height 0.928x
    At two months the zone is slightly UP while the cells are DOWN. A zone
    height was never going to report the cell term, and here the two move in
    opposite directions in the same animals.
