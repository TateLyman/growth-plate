## ★★★ TIER A — the numbers that decide reachability

### A1. **Vahle 2002, FIGURE 1** — per-dose femoral length values
`vahle2002` (Toxicol Pathol 30:312–321) reports "+5% males, +6% females, dose-dependent" in prose. The
per-group means ± SD and which doses carry the asterisk are **only in Figure 1**. I have the text, not
the plot values.

**Why it decides everything:** if 5 µg/kg is already significant, the effect sits ~4× above the approved
abaloparatide per-kg dose and is plausibly reachable. If only 75 µg/kg is significant, it's ~56× and
isn't. *Right now I cannot tell you which.*

**How:** high-resolution scan/photo of Figure 1 (p.313), or the underlying group means. Eli Lilly may
have the tabulated data.

### A2. **Vahle 2004 femoral length data — measured, never published**
Methods state: *"Femoral length was measured using calipers (Mitotoyo, Japan)."* It appears in **no**
result, figure, or table. The arms that matter:

| group | dose µg/kg | age during treatment | why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H5 / H30 satellites** | 5 / 30 | **2–8 mo, killed at 8 mo** | growing-animal, 2-dose length result |
| H5 / H30 main | 5 / 30 | 2–8 mo, then vehicle to 26 mo | **does length gain persist after stopping?** |
| E5 / E30 | 5 / 30 | 6–12 mo | late initiation comparison |
| I5 / I30 | 5 / 30 | 6–26 mo | long late exposure |

60/group, plus 30/satellite group. **This experiment was run and its length endpoint was never
reported.** Email Dr John L. Vahle (Eli Lilly, Indianapolis) — the data almost certainly exists in the
study report.

### A3. **Is the caliper length physeal or periarticular?**
These rats had marked trabecular hypertrophy and focal proliferative lesions. Caliper length is
end-to-end and would include periarticular new bone. **No growth plate histomorphometry is reported in
either paper** — but `vahle2004` Methods describe taking *"a longitudinal section of 1 femur that
contained diaphysis and distal metaphysis and epiphysis."* **The slides exist.** Growth plate height,
zone heights, cells/column from those sections would settle it.

## ★★ TIER C — the amplification question, still completely untouched

### C1. No resting-zone cycle time under any PTH1R agonist, in any species.
Confirmed by enumeration (round 191 Tier 2 = 0 papers). **This is the one thing that decides whether the
drug raises yield or spends the pool**, and it has never been measured.

### C2. The Hunziker five under a PTH1R agonist
Calcein-labelled elongation rate, terminal hypertrophic cell height, resting cells/column, resting-zone
cycle time, cells/column by zone — all in the same animals. Nobody has done it. The Vahle slides could
give three of the five retrospectively.

### C3. **Growth plate histomorphometry from any long-term PTH study.** Vahle 2002/2004, Jolette
2006/2017 all took longitudinal femoral sections. None report zone heights.

## ★★ TIER D — the human side

### D1. **Winer's source protocols** — refs 1, 2, 6, 23 of `winer2025`
Specifically: the **per-patient growth curves**, the **µg/kg doses per patient**, and **ref 23** (the case
of the 6-year-old treated a decade to normal final adult height). NIH protocols 92-CH-0011 and
11-I-0187 — the data may be obtainable from Dr Karen Winer (NICHD).

### D2. **Bone age under PTH.** Nobody has ever reported skeletal maturation under a PTH1R agonist. If it
advances bone age faster than height, the whole thing is self-defeating. Not one datum exists.

### D3. **`zygmunt2026`** (PMID 42388864) full text — 16-year-old on **palopegteriparatide** (long-acting =
the *continuous* arm) with an open plate. Abstract is biochemistry only. **Any height/growth velocity in
the full text** would be the only human observation of continuous PTH1R agonism in a growing skeleton.

### D4. **Natural history of stature in ADH1 (CASR gain-of-function), untreated.** `benson2025` says short
stature is an ADH2 feature, ~42%, not ADH1. I'd like a proper ADH1 cohort with heights to confirm the
comparator assumption round 192 rests on.
