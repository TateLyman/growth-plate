TITLE: Nf2 orchestrates β-arrestin2-biased PTH1R signaling to couple bone mass with skeletal integrity
======================================================================
The skeleton constitutes a dynamic organ system critically responsible for structural integrity, mineral homeostasis, and endocrine regulation. Embryonic bone formation proceeds via two principal pathways: intramembranous ossification, where skeletal mesenchymal stem cells directly differentiate into osteoblasts, and endochondral ossification, which proceeds through a cartilage intermediate that is progressively replaced by bone (1, 2). The endochondral process is characterized by a meticulously orchestrated sequence of chondrocyte maturation stages, beginning with mesenchymal condensation, progressing through proliferative expansion, and culminating in hypertrophic differentiation. Notably, recent lineage-tracing studies have revealed a remarkable plasticity of terminally differentiated chondrocytes, which possess the capacity to transdifferentiate into osteoblasts or revert to precursor states (3, 4). Dysregulation of this tightly coordinated differentiation cascade contributes to a wide array of skeletal dysplasia and metabolic bone disorders (1, 2). Thus, elucidating the spatiotemporal regulation of chondrogenesis and molecular mechanisms underlying chondrocyte phenotypic plasticity remains essential for understanding skeletal development and homeostasis, and for developing targeted therapeutic strategies.
G protein–coupled receptors (GPCRs) represent the largest class of druggable targets in human physiology. The parathyroid hormone type 1 receptor (PTH1R), a class B GPCR, transduces signals from both parathyroid hormone (PTH) and PTH-related peptide (PTHrP). Upon activation, PTH1R couples to Gαs and Gαq subunits, triggering cAMP-PKA and phospholipase C (PLC)-PKC signaling cascades, followed by GPCR kinases (GRKs)-mediated phosphorylation (5) and β-arrestin-dependent receptor desensitization (6). Beyond these well-established roles, PTH1R signaling contributes to dental development and longitudinal bone growth (7, 8), with its spatial enrichment in the hypertrophic zone of the growth plate enabling precise regulation of chondrocyte differentiation via SOX9 phosphorylation (9, 10). On the other hand, the PTH1R signaling axis is a key regulator of hypertrophic chondrocyte trans differentiation into osteoblasts during trabecular bone formation, and its overactivation disrupts this process, leading to a pathological increase in trabecular bone mass (11). Pathologically, PTH1R dysregulation underlies Jansen’s metaphyseal chondrodysplasia (7, 8), a rare genetic disorder in humans, while targeted engagement of PTH1R/β-arrestin axis has emerged as a therapeutic breakthrough for osteoporosis with reduced adverse effects (12, 13). Nevertheless, the cell-autonomous mechanisms that spatially constrain PTH1R signaling within the growth plate remains poorly defined.
The Neurofibromin 2 (Nf2) gene encodes Merlin, a 70-kDa tumor suppressor protein belonging to the Ezrin-Radixin-Moesin (ERM) family within the band 4.1 superfamily. Germline mutations in Nf2 cause neurofibromatosis type 2 (14, 15), and the protein’s activity is modulated by conformational changes, a closed state (mediated by FERM-CTD intramolecular interaction) suppresses tumor growth, whereas an open state promotes proliferation. Although Nf2 is widely recognized for its tumor suppressive functions across various tissues (14, 15), Nf2 also plays critical roles in embryonic development, with expression initiating as early as E6.5 and knockout models exhibiting gastrulation defects (16, 17). Notably, Nf2 deficiency in hematopoietic stem cells increases trabecular bone mass and VEGF-dependent vascularization (18), while its deletion in skeletal mesenchymal stem cells disrupts craniofacial morphogenesis (19) and limb patterning (20). Despite these advances, a direct role for Nf2 in chondrocyte-lineage and skeletal homeostasis has not been established.
To address this gap, we investigated Nf2 function in chondrogenesis using Col2a1-Cre (chondrocyte and its progenitor-specific) (21, 22). We reveal Nf2 as a chondrocyte-intrinsic brake of PTH1R signaling that orchestrates growth plate organization, endochondral ossification, and systemic bone homeostasis via selective control of β-arrestin2-dependent receptor trafficking. We delineate an Nf2–PTH1R–β-arrestin2 axis operating within chondrocytes and unveil its therapeutic potential for metabolic bone diseases such as osteoporosis.

##  Results

##  Elimination of Nf2 Driven by Col2a1-Cre Results in a Short-Limbed Dwarfism.
To investigate the regulatory role of Nf2 in chondrocyte differentiation and endochondral bone development, we generated mice with conditional knockout of Nf2 (Nf2-cKO) (23) specifically in Col2a1-expressing cells, which include skeletal stem/progenitor cells and committed chondrocytes of the growth plate (21, 22). Although our previous studies implicated Nf2 in craniofacial (19, 24) and limb morphogenesis (20), its cell-autonomous function in chondrogenesis remained unclear. Nf2 mutant mice were viable and fertile but exhibited significant postnatal growth retardation, evident as reduced body size (Fig. 1A) and weight (Fig. 1D) compared to the Nf2-floxp controls (NC). Efficient Nf2 ablation was confirmed in cartilage tissues, with no compensatory expression changes observed in liver, brain, lung, skin, or skull (Fig. 1B). Immunofluorescence further revealed strong Nf2 expression in prehypertrophic and hypertrophic chondrocytes of control animals, which was specifically abolished in SOX9+ chondrocytes of mutants (Fig. 1C).

Embryonic analysis indicated comparable body weights between Nf2-cKO (n = 7) and control (n = 5) mice at E15.5 (SI Appendix, Fig. S1B), but a significant reduction in mutants (n = 3) relative to controls (NC, n = 4) by E18.5 (SI Appendix, Fig. S1C), suggesting that Nf2 critically regulates late-gestational skeletal expansion. Alizarin red/alcian blue staining revealed uniform shortening of all long bones, including the femur, tibia, humerus, ulna, and radius, in E18.5 Nf2 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 A and D), accompanied by reduced length of primary ossification centers (POC) (SI Appendix, Figs. S1A and S2E). However, the POC-to-bone length ratio remained unchanged (SI Appendix, Fig. S1F), indicating that Nf2 loss primarily impairs prenatal growth plate elongation.
Quantitative analysis of H&E and EdU staining in embryonic mouse tibiae indicated that Nf2 knockout subtly but significantly impaired chondrocyte hypertrophy and primary ossification center formation beginning at E14.5 (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 A and D). Chondrocyte proliferation, however, remained unaffected at this stage (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 A and B). By E16.5 and E18.5, a slight but significant reduction in the length of the hypertrophic zone (HZ) was observed (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 B and E–H). Intriguingly, although proliferation in the resting zone (RZ) was unchanged, a notable decrease occurred specifically within the proliferative zone (PZ) (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 A, C, and D). This reduced proliferative capacity persisted postnatally, as EdU staining of the P6 growth plate (GP) confirmed diminished chondrocyte proliferation (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 A and E).
Postnatally, Nf2 deficiency caused severe disorganization of the growth plate architecture. We observed progressive shortening of growth plate zones, with dramatical reductions in the overall growth plate, proliferative zone, hypertrophic zone, and resting zone lengths by P5 (Fig. 1F, n = 8). This was accompanied by disrupted zonal proportions relative to total GP length (Fig. 1G, n = 8). By P14, Nf2 mutants continued to display reductions in GP and HZ length (Fig. 1H, n = 6), perturbed HZ/GP ratio (Fig. 1J, n = 6), and diminished area of the secondary ossification center (SOC) (Fig. 1 I and K, n = 5). EdU incorporation assays of P14 revealed a marked decrease in the percentage of proliferating chondrocytes within the growth plate of Nf2 mutants (Fig. 1 K and L, n = 6). These proliferative defects (consistent with SI Appendix, Fig. S3) identify an Nf2-dependent chondrogenic bottleneck that disrupts the differentiation cascade and ultimately drives the dwarfism phenotype. In summary, our findings establish Nf2 within Col2a1+ lineages as an essential regulator of endochondral ossification, governing prenatal growth plate formation, and postnatal skeletal elongation.

##  Transcriptomic Profiling of Nf2-Deficient Cartilage Implicates PTH1R, cAMP, and VEGF Signaling in Chondrodysplasia.
To elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which Nf2 regulates cartilage biology, we performed RNA sequencing on tibial growth plate cartilage tissues isolated from E18.5 embryos of E18.5 Nf2-cKO/Col2a1-Cre embryos and Nf2-floxed littermate controls. Comparative transcriptomic analysis between control and Nf2 mutant mice identified 200 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 47 up-regulated and 153 down-regulated genes (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that Nf2 deletion significantly affected biological processes including angiogenesis (GO:0001525), bone mineralization (GO:0030282), cell migration (GO:0002042), and regulation of bone development (GO:0030500), underscoring a critical role for Nf2 in cartilage development (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1B). KEGG pathway analysis further indicated enrichment in several signaling pathways implicated in skeletal biology, including cAMP signaling (mmu04024), parathyroid hormone synthesis, secretion and action (mmu04928), VEGF signaling (mmu04370), focal adhesion (mmu04510), ECM–receptor interaction (mmu04512), Hedgehog signaling, PI3K-Akt signaling (mmu04151), and GnRH signaling (mmu04912) (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1C).
To validate these findings in a cell-autonomous context, we isolated primary chondrocytes from neonatal mice and cultured them for differentiation for 7 d prior to RNA extraction and sequencing and qPCR analysis. The qPCR results confirmed a consistent decrease in the expression of key cartilage activity markers in Nf2-cKO cells, aligning with the tissue-levels findings (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-2 A and B). RNA-seq analysis of these chondrocytes identified 574 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in Nf2-cKO versus control cells, comprising 179 up-regulated and 395 down-regulated genes (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1D). A subset of these DEGs was independently verified by qPCR, confirming that the expression differences observed in vitro were consistent with the transcriptomic data (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-2). GO terms significantly altered included multicellular organism development (GO:0007275), collagen-containing extracellular matrix (GO:0062023), cell adhesion (GO:0007155), extracellular matrix (GO:0031012), cartilage development (GO:0051216), and G protein–coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186) (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1E). KEGG pathway enrichment was highly consistent with the tissue-level data, showing pronounced effects on cAMP signaling, Hedgehog signaling, calcium signaling (mmu04020), PI3K-Akt signaling, focal adhesion, ECM–receptor interaction, and GnRH signaling pathways (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1F). Heatmaps illustrating the top differentially expressed genes in both cartilage tissue and primary chondrocytes further confirmed the reproducibility of the transcriptional changes (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1 G and H). Collectively, these transcriptomic profiles indicate that Nf2 deletion markedly disrupts chondrocyte function, particularly affecting programs related to bone and blood vessel morphogenesis. Key pathways involving focal adhesion, ECM–receptor crosstalk, VEGF, and cAMP signaling were consistently altered, establishing Nf2 as a central transcriptional regulator of chondrocyte differentiation and cartilage homeostasis.

##  Nf2 Loss Causes Aberrant Activation of PTH1R Signaling in Chondrocytes.
Given the enrichment of PTH1R-related signaling pathways in Nf2 mutant tissues and primary chondrocytes (SI Appendix, Fig. S4-1), we sought to determine whether Nf2 loss directly affects downstream PTH1R signaling activity in the growth plate. Immunoblot analysis of E18.5 growth plates revealed moderately reduced levels of type 2 collagen (Col2a1) in Nf2 mutants, while SOX9, Runx2, and ColX protein levels remained largely unchanged (Fig. 2 A and B, n = 4; SI Appendix, Fig. S5 K and L, n = 3). However, immunohistochemistry at P5 showed a pronounced reduction in the zonal distribution and intensity of ColX immunostaining (SI Appendix, Fig. S5J, n = 4), a finding consistent with the observed shortening of the hypertrophic cartilage area in H&E-stained sections (Fig. 1E).

Notably, phosphorylation of SOX9 at serine 181 (p-SOX9 S181) was markedly increased in Nf2-deficient cartilage at E18.5 (Fig. 2 A and B, n = 4). Spatial analysis revealed abundant p-SOX9 (S181) accumulation specifically within prehypertrophic and hypertrophic chondrocytes of Nf2 mutant tibiae (Fig. 2C), with a significantly expanded p-SOX9–positive area in the hypertrophic zone (Fig. 2E, n = 3). Given that PKA, a canonical effector of PTH1R, specifically phosphorylates SOX9 at S181 in these chondrocyte populations (9, 10), and that p-SOX9 (S181) colocalized with PTH1R in prehypertrophic and hypertrophic zones (Fig. 2C), these results suggest that Nf2 loss leads to hyperactivation of PTH1R signaling and pathological SOX9 phosphorylation.
We also observed a substantial increase in phosphorylation of the transcription factor CREB, another well-established readout of PTH1R activity (25), in Nf2-deficient cartilage (Fig. 2 A and B, n = 4). Immunofluorescence confirmed robust accumulation of p-CREB specifically within prehypertrophic and hypertrophic zones (Fig. 2 D and F, n = 6), mirroring the spatial pattern of p-SOX9 elevation.
Furthermore, Vegf-A, a critical angiogenic mediator downstream of PTH1R signaling (26), was dramatically upregulated in Nf2-deficient prehypertrophic and hypertrophic chondrocytes, as shown by immunohistochemistry (Fig. 2 G and I, n = 3) and immunoblotting (Fig. 2 H and J, n = 4). This elevation was consistently observed in P5 Nf2 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 H and I). Secreted Vegf-A was also significantly increased in culture media from Nf2-deficient primary osteoblasts (Fig. 2K, n = 8). Together, these data demonstrate sustained hyperactivation of the PTH1R–pCREB–pSOX9–Vegf-A axis in Nf2 mutants, establishing Nf2 as an essential chondrocyte-intrinsic regulator that constrains PTH1R signaling output during endochondral ossification.
Concurrently, we examined whether the previously reported Nf2–FAK axis (19) contributes to the cartilage phenotype. Although Nf2 mutation similarly reduced the activity of FAK and its downstream effectors Akt and ERK1/2 in cartilage tissue (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 A and B), comparative analysis revealed that both Nf2 and FAK are expressed more strongly in osteoblasts than in chondrocytes, and their interaction is more pronounced in the osteoblast lineage (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 C and D). Furthermore, no direct interaction was detected between PTH1R and FAK (SI Appendix, Fig. S6E), indicating that FAK modulation likely represents a parallel, lineage-enriched regulatory circuit distinct from the primary Nf2 mediated PTH1R–pCREB–pSOX9–Vegf-A axis in cartilage.

##  Nf2 Binds PTH1R C-Terminal Domain and Inhibits Its Signaling Independent of the IHH-PTHrP Loop.
Building upon the observed hyperactivation of PTH1R signaling in Nf2 mutants, we sought to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanism. The Ihh-PTH/PTHrP feedback loop poses a crucial regulatory mechanism in the growth plate (27, 28). Given that the key components of the Ihh-PTH/PTHrP feedback loop remained unaltered (SI Appendix, Figs. S4-2B and S5 A and B), we hypothesized that Nf2 regulates PTH1R activity directly at the signal transduction level, rather than through ligand or receptor expression.
As a FERM-domain protein, Nf2 mediates key protein–protein interactions. Coimmunoprecipitation assays in primary chondrocytes confirmed an interaction between Nf2 and PTH1R (Fig. 3A). This interaction was further validated in HEK293T cells, which demonstrated a direct association between HA-tagged Nf2 and Flag-tagged PTH1R (SI Appendix, Fig. S6E). To map the binding interface, we generated a series of PTH1R truncation mutants and identified the C-terminal cytoplasmic tail (464–591 aa) as both necessary and sufficient for interaction with Nf2 (Fig. 3B). Given that Gαs and β-arrestin2 were established downstream efforts of PTH1R (29, 30), we confirmed that PTH1R interacts with Gαs and β-arrestin2, but not β-arrestin1 (SI Appendix, Fig. S6F). Notably, Nf2 did not interact with these canonical PTH1R effectors, Gαs, β-arrestin1, or β-arrestin2 (Fig. 3 C and D and SI Appendix, Fig. S6G), suggesting it regulates PTH1R through a distinct, effector-independent mechanism. The physiological relevance of the Nf2–PTH1R interaction was further supported by their endogenous colocalization in prehypertrophic and hypertrophic chondrocytes (Fig. 3E).

To assess the functional consequence of Nf2–PTH1R binding, we stimulated primary chondrocytes with the PTH1R agonist abaloparatide (ABL) (31). Nf2 deficiency resulted in hypersensitive signaling responses, as evidenced by enhanced CREB phosphorylation at low ABL concentrations (1 to 5 nM) (Fig. 3F and SI Appendix, Fig. S6 J and K). A similar potentiation of p-CREB was observed upon beta-arrestin2 knockdown in ATDC5 cells stimulated with at 10 nM ABL (SI Appendix, Fig. S6M). This effect was confirmed in Nf2-knockdown chondrocytes using siRNA (SI Appendix, Fig. S6K) and reversed upon Nf2 overexpression in ATDC5 chondrocytes (SI Appendix, Fig. S6L). Forskolin, by increasing cAMP levels and activating the PKA pathway, can lead to the phosphorylation of CREB (32, 33). Similarly, forskolin-induced activation of the cAMP–PKA pathway led to amplified p-CREB and p-SOX9 responses in Nf2-deficient cells and dampened responses in Nf2-overexpressing cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 M–O).
PTH1R canonically activates adenylyl cyclase (AC) to stimulate cAMP production (34, 35). Consistent with these findings, Nf2 loss significantly enhanced ABL-induced cAMP production in both ATDC5 and primary chondrocytes by 26.88% and 36.10%, respectively (Fig. 3G and SI Appendix, Fig. S6N), whereas Nf2 overexpression suppressed cAMP accumulation by 19.98% (SI Appendix, Fig. S6L, 3L). Together, these results establish Nf2 as a chondrocyte-intrinsic negative regulator of PTH1R that binds directly to its C-terminal domain and restrains receptor signaling output, including cAMP production, CREB phosphorylation, and SOX9 activation, independent of both the Ihh-PTHrP loop and downstream effector recruitment.

##  Nf2 Deletion Results in a PTH1R Activation-Like Bone Phenotype through Cell-Autonomous Mechanisms.
Nf2 mutant mice are viable and fertile, enabling analysis of their postnatal skeletal phenotype. Serum biochemistry revealed elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and phosphorus (PHOS) levels, while calcium, PTH, and tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) remained unchanged in Nf2 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 C–G). In tibial sections from 6-mo-old Nf2-cKO mice, total ALP staining intensity increased in trabecular bone but decreased average intensity; cortical bone displayed unchanged total ALP intensity but a slight increase in average intensity, likely due to the extensive interwoven trabeculae invading the inner cortical surface (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 D and E, red arrows). Lumbar vertebrae exhibited increased trabecular ALP total intensity without a significant change in average intensity (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 D and F). Although TRAP staining revealed more TRAP-positive cells in both tibial and vertebral trabecular bone in Nf2 mutant mice, the osteoclast surface per bone surface (Oc.S/BS) remained statistically unchanged (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 D and G). Thus, histological and serological data consistently demonstrate elevated osteoblastic activity without altered osteoclast function in Nf2-cKO mice, suggesting a chondrocyte-localized dysregulation of PTH1R signaling rather than systemic endocrine disturbance, consistent with intact Ihh-PTHrP loop activity (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 A and B).
Longitudinal micro-CT analyses uncovered consistent and sex-independent alterations in bone microstructure. Adult Nf2 mutants displayed a distinctive dichotomy: increased cancellous bone density accompanied by cortical bone loss, a phenotype persistent throughout adulthood (1, 6, 12, and 18 mo) (Fig. 4 A–E and SI Appendix, Figs. S7 A and B, S8, and S9). Cancellous bone exhibited pronounced trabecular hyperplasia, with significant increases in bone volume fraction (BV/TV), bone mineral density (BMD), trabecular number (Tb. N), connectivity (Conn), and connectivity density (Conn. D), along with decreased trabecular thickness (Tb. Th) and separation (Tb. Sp) (Fig. 4 A–C and SI Appendix, Figs. S7A, S8-1, and S9). Marrow cavities showed extensive trabecular infilling (Fig. 4 A–C and SI Appendix, Figs. S8-1A and S9A), with phenotypes worsening over time, BV/TV increased by 83.22% at 6 mo and 198.52% at 18 mo, while BMD rose by 25.64% and 14.71%, respectively (Fig. 4 A–C and SI Appendix, Tables S5 and S7). Conversely, cortical bone progressively deteriorated, with notable reductions in BV/TV (−16.74% at 6 m; −28.65% at 18 m), BMD (−5.76% at 6 m; −31.67% at 18 m), and cortical thickness (Cort. Th) (Fig. 4 D and E and SI Appendix, Figs. S8-2, S9, and S11).

Given these structural alterations, we assessed whether they translate into compromised mechanical properties using three-point bending, a classic method for evaluating bone biomechanical competence. The tibiae and femurs from 4-mo-old Nf2-cKO mice showed a pronounced reduction in biomechanical performance. In male mice, maximum load (F-max) decreased by 33.45% in tibiae and 27.20% in femurs, while elastic modulus (E, Mpa) declined by 59.37% and 10.56%, respectively (SI Appendix, Fig. S9 N and O). In female mice, F-max dropped by 47.51% (tibia) and 47.21% (femur), and E decreased by 64.80% and 33.88%, respectively (SI Appendix, Fig. S9 N and O). Thus, despite enhanced trabecular bone formation, Nf2 mutants exhibit substantially reduced overall bone strength, particularly in the tibia.
Lineage tracing using R26-tdTomato reporter mice under the control of Col2a1-Cre and Gli1-CreER (36) revealed abundant tdTomato+ progeny within subchondral and metaphyseal regions of Nf2 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S10 A and C), indicating an enhanced contribution of chondrocyte-derived cells to the osteoblast lineage. Quantification of EdU incorporation in these tdTomato+ cells showed only slight, nonsignificant differences in proliferation rates within both the growth plate and trabecular bone between genotypes (SI Appendix, Fig. S10D), suggesting that the expanded population of chondrocyte-derived cells in the mutant bone is not primarily driven by increased local proliferation of this progeny. Correspondingly, Nf2 mutants also exhibited elevated cancellous BV/TV and reduced Tb. Th at 3 mo (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 D–F). Collectively, these results demonstrate that Nf2 deficiency disrupts skeletal homeostasis by promoting aberrant transdifferentiation of chondrogenic lineages into trabecular osteoblasts, producing a unique phenotype of hyperplastic yet structurally compromised cancellous bone coupled with progressive cortical thinning, mimicking sustained PTH1R activation in a cell-autonomous manner.

##  Nf2 Genotype Dictates Compartment-Specific Responses to PTH1R Modulation in Bone.
To determine whether the skeletal abnormalities in Nf2 mutant mice were driven by PTH1R hyperactivation, we employed targeted pharmacological interventions. The observed phenotype, elevated trabecular bone volume (37–39), reduced cortical mass (40, 41), and increased serum ALP (38), closely mirrors clinical hyperparathyroidism, where cortical deficits are often reversible upon PTH normalization (38). Since constitutive PTH1R activation is known to increase trabecular but decrease cortical density (7, 11, 42), we hypothesized that modulating PTH1R activity could rescue the Nf2-related bone imbalance.
We treated 1-mo-old mice with either cinacalcet (30 mg/kg/d for 30 d), a calcimimetic that suppresses PTH secretion, or the PTH1R agonist abaloparatide (ABL, 5 µg/kg/d for 30 d). Micro-CT analysis revealed that cinacalcet significantly reduced trabecular overgrowth in Nf2 mutants, whereas ABL exacerbated it (Fig. 4 F and G and SI Appendix, Fig. S7C). Notably, Nf2 mutants exhibited heightened sensitivity to cinacalcet. Trabecular bone volume (BV/TV) decreased by 31.74% in mutants (from 15.259 ± 1.591% to 10.415 ± 2.077%, P < 0.01) compared to an 18.89% reduction in controls (10.051 ± 1.397% to 8.152 ± 1.107%, P < 0.05) (Fig. 4G). Similarly, trabecular BMD declined by 5.40% in Nf2 mutants (0.817 ± 0.061% to 0.772 ± 0.073%, P < 0.05) versus 2.07% in controls (0.734 ± 0.054% to 0.719 ± 0.034%) (Fig. 4G). Cortical bone parameters (BV/TV and BMD) decreased comparably in both genotypes following cinacalcet treatment (Fig. 4H), indicating a genotype-independent response in cortical compartments.
Due to that Nf2 negatively regulates PTH1R-mediated intracellular signaling, we further explored potential role of the Nf2–PTH1R axis in bone loss. In aged Nf2-mutant mice (18 mo), we found a 2.98-fold increase in trabecular BV/TV (5.642% vs. 1.892% in controls, P < 0.0001) with significantly elevated bone mineral density, despite marked cortical bone loss (Fig. 4 A, D, and E), suggesting that Nf2 deficiency protects against age-related bone loss specifically in the trabecular compartment. In the ovariectomy (OVX) model of postmenopausal osteoporosis, Nf2 mutant mice showed ameliorated OVX-induced trabecular bone loss, reflected by changes in BV/TV (−26.18% in mutants vs. −37.92% in controls), trabecular thickness (Tb. Th), trabecular number (Tb. N), trabecular separation (Tb. Sp), connectivity (Conn), and connectivity density (Conn. Dn) (Fig. 4 I and J). In contrast, Nf2 deficiency failed to rescue OVX-induced cortical bone loss (Fig. 4J). Thus, the PTH1R activation resulting from Nf2 loss produces a bone mineral density profile reminiscent of hyperparathyroidism.

##  Nf2 Controls an Unconventional Arm of PTH1R Endocytosis That Bypasses GRKs to Enforce β-Arrestin2-Dependent Internalization.
To dissect the mechanism by which Nf2 regulates PTH1R signaling output, we performed lentivirus-mediated Nf2 knockdown in HEK293T cells cotransfected with PTH1R and β-arrestin2 or Gnas expression plasmids. Coimmunoprecipitation assays revealed a pronounced impairment in PTH1R–β-arrestin2 complex formation upon Nf2 depletion (Fig. 5A, n = 3), whereas PTH1R–Gnas interaction remained intact (Fig. 5B, n = 3), indicating that Nf2 selectively regulates β-arrestin2 recruitment.

Because the PTH1R–β-arrestin2 complex initiates receptor internalization upon activation, we assessed membrane trafficking using cell surface biotinylation using Sulfo-NHS-LC-Biotin. In ATDC chondrocytes, Nf2 knockdown leads to significant retention of PTH1R on the plasma membrane following stimulation with 20 nM ABL for 15 min (Fig. 5C, n = 6).
In parallel, shCtrl and shNf2 HEK293T cells were transfected with a PTH1R-copGFP overexpression plasmid, treated with 20 nM ABL for 15 min (Fig. 5 E–G), and analyzed for receptor internalization. Colocalization imaging of PTH1R-copGFP (Fig. 5F, green) with the membrane dye DiI (Fig. 5F, red) confirms significantly impaired ligand-induced receptor internalization in Nf2-deficient cells (Fig. 5I, n = 8, P = 0.0368). Quantification of cytoplasmic PTH1R-copGFP puncta (Fig. 5E, internalized vesicles) shows significantly more internalized receptor in shCtrl controls after ABL treatment (Fig. 5H, n = 10, P = 0.0384). Furthermore, Live-cell superresolution imaging (HIS-SIM) of PTH1R-copGFP trafficking over 5 to 10 min after ABL treatment revealed a robust, dynamic internalization in control cells (Fig. 5G, arrow), which was markedly attenuated upon Nf2 deficiency (Fig. 5G, arrow).
Since GRK-mediated phosphorylation typically precedes β-arrestin recruitment and internalization, we asked whether Nf2 loss affects PTH1R phosphorylation. Coexpression of Flag-PTH1R and HA-GRK2 in control and shNf2 cells showed no difference in GRK2-mediated receptor phosphorylation (Fig. 5D). Phosphorylation status of PTH1R was assessed using Phos-tag gel electrophoresis in ATDC5 chondrogenic cells, and the results showed that Nf2 deletion did not alter the phosphorylation of PTH1R (SI Appendix, Fig. S6H), ruling out a defect in canonical phosphorylation-dependent desensitization. These results define a noncanonical regulatory mechanism in which Nf2 allosterically facilitates β-arrestin2 coupling to PTH1R and promotes receptor endocytosis, independent of GRK activity, likely through a specialized Nf2-scaffolded protein complex (Fig. 5J).

##  Discussion

##  Nf2 Serves as a Master Regulator of Endochondral Ossification and Skeletal Homeostasis.
The precise coordination of skeletal development requires stringent regulation of cellular crosstalk, whose disruption often results in severe developmental anomalies. Our work identifies Nf2 as a central player in this process, significantly advancing our understanding of endochondral bone formation and its pathologies. Beyond its well-established role as a tumor suppressor (14, 15), Nf2 exhibits pleiotropic functions during development, with expression initiating as early as E5.5 (17, 43) and contributing to the morphogenesis of multiple systems including the skin (44), embryonic axis (45), neural tube (46), and tongue (47), limb patterning (20), and cranial structures (48).
In this study, chondrocyte-specific deletion of Nf2 leads to growth retardation, disrupted growth plate organization, and suppressed chondrocyte proliferation and hypertrophy (Fig. 1). Remarkably, Nf2 deficiency produces a unique skeletal phenotype characterized by excessive trabecular bone expansion accompanied by cortical thinning (Fig. 4), a pattern closely resembling the effects of PTH1R gain-of-function mutations (49–51) seen in Jansen’s metaphyseal chondrodysplasia (7, 8). These findings establish Nf2 as a regulator that spatiotemporally controls chondrogenesis, endochondral ossification, and bone homeostasis, providing a promising therapeutic target for disorders related to skeletal growth and PTH1R signaling.

##  Nf2 Is a Chondrocyte-Intrinsic Brake on PTH1R-Dependent Skeletal Development.
While PTH1R canonically transduces signals through Gαs/cAMP and is modulated by the IHH-PTHrP feedback loop (27, 28) to coordinate chondrocyte proliferation, hypertrophy, and differentiation, we identify Nf2 as a spatially restricted negative modulator that binds directly to the C-terminal domain of PTH1R. This interaction occurs independently of Gαs or β-arrestin2 binding and is enriched in prehypertrophic and hypertrophic zones, enabling precise spatial control of PTH1R signaling output. Loss of Nf2 leads to elevated phosphorylation of CREB and SOX9 (S181), increased Vegf-A expression, and a distinctive skeletal phenotype characterized by trabecular hyperostosis and cortical thinning, mimicking PTH1R gain-of-function models (49–51).
Conversely, PTH1R deficiency results in opposing phenotypes, including elevated cortical density, reduced trabecular bone (52), impaired mineralization (53), and disrupted SOX9 phosphorylation (9), underscoring the role of Nf2 as a molecular rheostat fine-tuning PTH1R activity within chondrocytes. Notably, the absence of elevated serum TRAP and TRAP histological staining in Nf2 mutants indicates that its actions are independent of osteoclast-mediated remodeling, distinguishing it from classical PTH1R activation (50) and highlighting its potential for targeted therapeutic intervention without systemic bone remodeling effects. This work provides a foundation for further structural studies (e.g., Cryo-EM of the Nf2–PTH1R complex) and mechanistic investigations into related skeletal disorders such as Jansen’s metaphyseal chondrodysplasia.

##  Nf2 Potentially Modulates Chondrocyte-to-Osteoblast Transdifferentiation.
Chondrocyte-to-osteoblast transdifferentiation is a well-documented biological process supported by multiple lineage-tracing models, including Col10a1-Cre/tdTomato, Agc1-CreERT2, Col2a1-CreERT, and PTHrP-CreER systems (54). These studies confirm that hypertrophic and resting zone chondrocytes can undergo fate transition and contribute substantially to the formation of the primary ossification center and trabecular osteoblasts (3, 4).
Several signaling pathways and transcription factors, including β-catenin (55, 56), Wnt7b (57), BMPR1a (48), Runx2 (58), Hgs (59), Piezo1 (60), and PTH (11), have been identified as regulators of this transdifferentiation process. Notably, PTH not only promotes this conversion but also drives pathological trabecular hyperplasia (11). Lineage tracing using R26-tdTomato reporter mice under the control of Col2a1-Cre and Gli1-CreER revealed a marked increase in tdTomato+ progeny within the trabecular bone of Nf2 deficient mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S10), suggesting a potential role for Nf2 in negatively regulating chondrocyte-to-osteoblast transition. These findings position Nf2 as a modulator of skeletal cell fate determination, warranting further in-depth investigation to elucidate its precise mechanistic role.

##  Nf2 Directs β-Arrestin-Biased PTH1R Trafficking as a Noncanonical GPCR Mechanism.
Osteoporosis, a major cause of fragility fractures, severely impacts health and quality of life in the aging population. Current therapies, including bisphosphonates (61), zoledronic acid (62, 63), and human PTH (1-34) (teriparatide) (64, 65), are limited by suboptimal efficacy and side effects such as hypercalcemia. Unlike conventional PTH1R agonists, β-arrestin-biased ligands selectively activate β-arrestin-mediated signaling without inducing catabolic or hypercalcemic effects (12, 13). Genetic and pharmacological studies confirm that β-arrestin2 deletion abolishes PTH-induced bone loss (66), and biased agonists promote bone formation exclusively through β-arrestin-coupled internalization (29, 30) and Gβγ-sustained cAMP signaling (34, 35). Here, we identify Nf2 as a direct interactor of PTH1R that facilitates β-arrestin2 recruitment and receptor internalization, independently of Gαs or GRKs, thus fine-tuning pathway specificity and signal duration. This noncanonical regulatory pathway highlights the existence of alternative scaffolding systems capable of biasing receptor trafficking independently of classical phosphorylation-dependent paradigm, adding a new dimension to understand GPCR regulation. Although further investigation is required to determine whether this mechanism extends to other GPCRs. Our work suggests that directly targeting the intracellular trafficking, specifically Nf2-mediated β-arrestin2 recruitment, offers a potential strategy for developing PTH1R pathway modulators.
Taken together, our work identifies Nf2 as a chondrocyte-intrinsic regulator of PTH1R signaling that specifically controls β-arrestin2-dependent receptor trafficking. The Nf2–PTH1R–β-arrestin2 axis acts as a molecular rheostat that orchestrates chondrocyte differentiation, growth plate organization, endochondral ossification, and skeletal homeostasis, providing a mechanistic framework for understanding skeletal homeostasis and a potential target for future pathway-specific interventions.

##  Materials and Methods

##  Generation of Nf2 cKO Mice.
The use of animals was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the Zhejiang SCI-TECH University. The study was performed in compliance with the guidelines for the care and use of animals in research. The animals were maintained in the animal facilities in the Experimental Animal Center at the Hangzhou Institute of Medicine, Chinese Academy of Sciences. Animal experiments were performed in accordance with Zhejiang Sci-Tech University Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines. All the animal experiments were approved by the animal committee of Zhejiang Sci-Tech University (#201911001).
The Nf2 floxed allele (f/f) was provided by Inserm (23). Col2a1-Cre was kindly provided by Xiao Yang (Academy of Military Medical Science), Gli1-CreER by Jianquan Chen (Suzhou University), and ROSA-R26 tdTomato (R26 tomato) mice by Yanmei Tao (Hangzhou Normal University) to genetically label derived cells under Cre transgenic mouse line. Genotyping primers are listed in SI Appendix, Table S1-1.

##  Micro-CT Analysis and the Three-Point Bending Test.
Bone microarchitecture was assessed using a Bruker Skyscan 1272 system. Samples were scanned at 70 keV, 140 μA, and 12 μm isotropic resolution. Trabecular bone was evaluated in a region extending from the growth plate, and cortical bone was analyzed at the mid-diaphysis. Three-dimensional reconstruction and quantification were performed using Mimics and Bruker CT-Analyser software. Complete datasets are provided in SI Appendix, Tables S5–S24. For biomechanical testing, the tibiae and femurs were cleaned of adherent soft tissues and stored at –80 °C. During the three-point bending test, a span of 11 mm was used for the tibia and 8 mm for the femur, with the load cell advancing at a rate of 0.5 mm/min.

##  Lentivirus and Cell Lines.
Nf2 and β-arrestin2 shRNAs were obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (sc-36053-SH, sc-29743-SH). The Nf2 cDNA was cloned into the pLentiPGK-puro vector using Gateway Cloning (Invitrogen). Lentivirus was produced by cotransfecting HEK293T cells with the lentiviral vector and second-generation packaging plasmids (psPAX2 and pMD2.G) using Lipo8000 reagent (Beyotime, #C0533) (67). Virus-containing supernatant was collected at 48 and 72 h posttransfection.

##  Western Blot Assay.
Protein lysates were prepared from cultured osteoblasts at various differentiation stages using RIPA buffer, with concentrations determined by the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with 3% BSA in TBST, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (SI Appendix, Tables S2 and S3) overnight at 4 °C, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Signal was detected using a ChemiDoc MP system as described (19).

##  Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR).
Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using TRIzol (Life Technologies, #15596018). cDNA was synthesized from 5 μg RNA with Hifair® III First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (Yeasen, #11141ES10). qPCR was performed using Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, #11202ES03) on an ABI 7500 instrument, with expression normalized to Hprt1. Primer sequences are listed in SI Appendix, Table S1-S2.

##  Primary Chondrocyte Extraction and Culture.
Growth plate tissues were dissected and digested in 0.3% collagenase D at 37 °C, with two 30 min predigestion steps followed by a 3 h final digestion (68). Cells were pelleted, resuspended in low-glucose DMEM with 10% FBS and antibiotics, filtered through a 70 µm strainer, and plated at 8 × 103 cells/cm2. For hypertrophic differentiation (20), cells were cultured for 7 d in medium containing 1 nM dexamethasone, 20 mM β-glycerophosphate, 50 µg/mL ascorbic acid, 50 ng/mL L-thyroxine, and 1% ITS. For micromass cultures, 1 × 105 cells were seeded in a 10 µL drop, allowed to adhere for 2 h, and then gently overlaid with 500 µL of differentiation medium.

##  Transcriptomic Profiling and Bioinformatics Analysis.
RNA sequencing was performed on E18.5 tibial growth-plate cartilage and primary chondrocytes. Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol, and poly(A)+ RNA was enriched, fragmented, and reverse-transcribed. Libraries were prepared and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (PE150). Reads were aligned to the mouse genome (GRCm39/mm39) using TopHat2. Differential expression analysis was conducted with Cufflinks (67, 69). Genes with an adjusted P < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed and were subjected to functional enrichment analysis (GO and KEGG) using DAVID. The complete dataset is available as Dataset S2, and original data have been deposited at GEO (GSE319151).

##  Coimmunoprecipitation (co-IP) Assay.
Cells from one 10-cm dish were lysed in 500 μL co-IP buffer supplemented with protease/phosphatase inhibitors. Lysates were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific antibodies or control IgG, followed by capture with protein A/G magnetic beads (Yeasen). After washing, bound proteins were eluted in SDS buffer and analyzed by immunoblotting as described (19).

##  Pharmaceutical Drug Rescue Assay.
Nf2 control and mutant animals were treated with ABL (50 μg/kg, intraperitoneally) in normal saline (0.9% NaCl) and Cinacalcet (30 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) in sterile oil for 30 d when they were 1-m old, and the Nf2 f/f control were treated with normal saline. Nf2-cKO/Col2a1-Cre mice were treated with Cinacalcet for 30 d when they were 1-m old, and the sham controls with Nf2-cKO were treated with normal saline.

##  Elisa Detection Assay.
Serum PTH concentrations were measured using a high-sensitivity ELISA kit (Elabscience, E-EL-M0709; n = 14). Intracellular cAMP levels were quantified in cell lysates with a cAMP ELISA kit (Elabscience, E-EL-0056; n = 12 to 16). For extracellular VEGF-A detection, primary osteoblasts from control and Nf2-mutant tibiae were grown to confluence in α-MEM with 10% FBS. After switching to serum-free medium, conditioned supernatants were collected at 12 h and 24 h, and VEGF-A levels were determined using a mouse VEGF-A ELISA kit (Abclonal, RK00028) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

##  Live-Cell Imaging.
HEK293T cells stably expressing control (shCtrl) or Nf2-targeting (shNf2) shRNA were transfected with a PTH1R-copGFP plasmid. At 96 h posttransfection, live-cell imaging was performed on a Nikon HIS-SIM superresolution microscope (37 °C, 5% CO2). Following addition of 20 nM ABL, images were acquired every 20 s for 5 to 10 min using 488 nm excitation and processed with NIS-Elements software. Corresponding movies are provided as Movies S1 and S2.

##  Quantitative Analysis of the PTH1R Internalization Assay.
Cell-surface biotinylation assay: Control and Nf2-knockdown HEK293T cells were treated with 20 nM ABL for 15 min. Surface proteins were labeled with 1 mg/mL Sulfo-NHS-LC-Biotin (MedChemExpress, HY-D0799) in PBS for 30 min at 37 °C. The reaction was quenched with 100 mM glycine, and cells were lysed. PTH1R was immunoprecipitated from lysates, and biotinylated receptor was detected by immunoblotting using an anti-PTH1R antibody and HRP-conjugated streptavidin.

##  Fluorescence-based internalization assay.
Control and Nf2-knockdown HEK293T cells were transfected with PTH1R-copGFP. After 48 h, cells were stimulated with 20 nM ABL for 15 min and the membrane was labeled with DiI dye (Beyotime, C1991S). Dual-channel fluorescence images were acquired, and membrane-localized PTH1R was quantified by measuring GFP-DiI colocalization using ImageJ.

##  Ovariectomy-Induced (OVX) Osteoporosis Mouse Model.
Three-month-old virgin female mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (dose adjusted by body weight). Bilateral ovariectomy was performed via a dorsal approach; sham-operated controls underwent the same procedure without ovary removal to control for surgical stress. Postoperatively, antibiotics were administered to prevent infection. Bone mass was assessed by micro-CT 2 mo after surgery in both Nf2-mutant and control mice.

##  Data Analysis.
All data are expressed as mean ± SD with at least three biological replicates and processed using GraphPad Prism Version 9.0 software. Quantitative image analysis was performed using Fiji (x86-64). Data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA statistical analysis or Students’ t tests were used to evaluate the significance of the experimental data. A P-value less than 0.05 (P < 0.05) is taken as statistical significance. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001.
