Findings This randomized clinical trial including 84 participants demonstrated superiority of navepegritide in annualized growth velocity at week 52 vs placebo in children with achondroplasia and improvements in tibial-femoral angle, mechanical axis deviation, fibula-to-tibia length ratio, and Achondroplasia Child Experience Measure–Physical Functioning. Meaning Navepegritide treatment resulted in significantly greater linear growth and similar safety and tolerability compared to placebo in children with achondroplasia, with additional benefits beyond growth, including improved skeletal alignment and body proportionality, and positive changes in health-related quality of life. This randomized clinical trial evaluates the use of navepegritide vs placebo for the treatment of pediatric achondroplasia. Importance Historically considered a skeletal dysplasia characterized by disproportionate short stature, achondroplasia is a condition with multisystemic effects due to the widespread expression of the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 variant throughout the body, impacting muscle, neurological function, cardiorespiratory health, and health-related quality of life. Objective To evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of once-weekly navepegritide, an investigational prodrug of C-type natriuretic peptide, while assessing benefits beyond growth that may have important implications for complications and health-related quality of life in children with achondroplasia. Design, Setting, and Participants Enrollment for this pivotal phase 2b, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (APPROACH) was conducted between March and August 2023 at 10 hospitals in Australia, Canada, Denmark, Ireland, New Zealand, Spain, and the US with randomized, blind treatment through 52 weeks and an open-label extension (ongoing). Eligible participants aged 2 to 11 years had achondroplasia confirmed by genetic testing, were naive to treatment with growth-promoting agents, and had their height recorded at least 6 months prior to randomization. Enrolled participants were stratified by age and sex. Those with radiographic evidence of closed growth plates, planned bone surgery, severe untreated sleep apnea, or medical conditions known to affect growth were excluded (n = 2 of 86); of 84 participants enrolled, all were analyzed for safety and efficacy outcomes, including 2 who discontinued treatment. Interventions Navepegritide (100 μg/kg/wk) or placebo administered by once-weekly subcutaneous injection. Main Outcomes and Measures The primary end point was annualized growth velocity at week 52. Other clinically important secondary measures included radiographically assessed skeletal outcomes and health-related quality of life, evaluated using Achondroplasia Child Experience Measures. Safety assessments included adverse events, clinical laboratory assessments, bone age, and immunogenicity. Results Eighty-four participants were enrolled and assigned randomly in a 2:1 ratio to receive navepegritide (n = 57; mean [SD] age, 5.6 [2.6] years; 31 [54%] male) or placebo (n = 27; mean [SD] age, 6.0 [2.7] years; 14 [52%] male). All randomized participants were included in efficacy and safety analyses, although 2 patients in the navepegritide group discontinued treatment (one at week 26 and the other at week 34). The trial met its primary end point, demonstrating superiority of navepegritide in annualized growth velocity at week 52 vs placebo (least-squares mean treatment difference of 1.49 cm/y; 95% CI, 1.05 to 1.93; P &lt; .001). Treatment resulted in improvements (least-squares mean treatment difference [95% CI]) in tibial-femoral angle (−1.81° [−3.16 to −0.47]), mechanical axis deviation (−2.78 mm [−4.71 to −0.86]), fibula to tibia length ratio (−0.016 [−0.024 to −0.008]), and Achondroplasia Child Experience Measures–Physical Functioning (−11.1 [−21.5 to −0.80] in children younger than 5 years). No serious adverse events were treatment-related, and no deaths occurred. Injection site reaction rates were low, and no symptomatic hypotension or fractures were observed. Conclusions In this randomized clinical trial, navepegritide treatment resulted in statistically significantly higher annualized growth velocity in children with achondroplasia, with a similar safety and tolerability profile vs placebo. Moreover, navepegritide demonstrated additional potential health benefits beyond growth. In the lower limbs, fibular overgrowth relative to the shorter tibia often results in progressive leg bowing, exacerbating mechanical strain on the joints and reducing physical functioning. 7 C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) is a positive regulator of endochondral bone growth, promoting chondrocyte differentiation through binding and activating the natriuretic peptide receptor-B. Activation of natriuretic peptide receptor-B stimulates production of cyclic guanosine monophosphate, which inhibits the mitogen-activated protein kinase–signaling pathway downstream of FGFR3 . 8 , 9 , 10 Vosoritide, an analog of human CNP with a modified N-terminal domain, is a daily injection providing intermittent exposure with high peak plasma concentrations. 11 , 12 In randomized clinical trials, vosoritide demonstrated efficacy in increasing linear growth; however, these published results have not demonstrated additional benefits beyond growth, such as improvements in HRQoL. 12 Navepegritide is an investigational prodrug of CNP that leverages the TransCon technology platform, designed to transiently link an inert carrier to a parent drug with known biology. The CNP moiety in navepegritide is identical to the amino acid sequence 89-126 of endogenous CNP. Following once-weekly subcutaneous administration, autocleavage of the linker occurs under physiologic pH and temperature, providing predictable, sustained release and continuous systemic exposure to active CNP. This pharmacokinetic profile is designed to counteract the constitutively active FGFR3 -signaling pathway through natriuretic peptide receptor-B activation in tissues throughout the body while avoiding high peak concentration. 10 A Clinical Trial to Evaluate Efficacy and Safety of TransCon CNP Compared With Placebo in Children With Achondroplasia (APPROACH) aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of navepegritide while assessing benefits beyond growth that may have important implications for achondroplasia-related complications and HRQoL. Methods Study Design APPROACH was a pivotal phase 2b randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial conducted at 10 academic hospitals across 7 countries (Australia, Canada, Denmark, Ireland, New Zealand, Spain, and the US) comparing once-weekly, subcutaneously administered navepegritide (100 μg/kg/wk) with placebo for 52 weeks in children aged 2 to 11 years (eFigure 1 in Supplement 2 ). The total study duration was 104 weeks: a 52-week double-blind randomized period followed by a 52-week open-label extension period to evaluate long-term safety and efficacy, which is currently underway. The trial was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines as defined by the International Council for Harmonization E6(R2), and all applicable regional regulations. The trial protocol was approved by the relevant ethics board or institutional review board (IRB) at each site: Comité de Ética de la Investigación con medicamentos, Advarra IRB, or Children’s Minnesota IRB. Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials ( CONSORT ) reporting guideline was followed. Prior to enrollment, written informed consent was obtained from the parent or legal guardian. Written assents were obtained from participants appropriate to age. Participants Children aged 2 to 11 years with a genetically confirmed diagnosis of achondroplasia, naive to treatment with growth-promoting agents, and height recorded at least 6 months prior to randomization were eligible. Participants had to be able to stand without assistance and have a caregiver willing and able to administer subcutaneous injections. Participants with radiographic evidence of closed growth plates, planned bone surgery, severe untreated sleep apnea, or medical conditions known to affect growth were excluded. Full eligibility criteria are provided in the protocol ( Supplement 1 ). Interview data included sex and race (as identified by the participant and/or caregiver and based on the following provided classifications: American Indian or Alaska Native, Asian, Black or African American, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander, White, or other [those reporting more than 1 race]) and were recorded to comply with regulatory requirements for collection and reporting of race and ethnicity data. Randomization and Masking Participants were randomly assigned at a 2:1 ratio, using an Interactive Web Response System, to receive navepegritide or visually identical placebo. Stratification factors were age and sex (&lt;5 years of age, ≥5 years of age and female, ≥5 years of age and male). Participants, investigators, and caregivers were blinded to group assignment. Procedures Participants received weekly subcutaneous injections of navepegritide (100 μg/kg/wk) or placebo, as assigned. Caregivers were trained by dedicated site staff to store, prepare, and administer the trial drug according to the protocol. Participants had scheduled visits at screening, first administration (visit 1), and weeks 4, 12, 26, 39, and 52. Scheduled trial activities included anthropometric measurements (eg, standing height and upper and lower body segment), annual radiographic assessments with central reading, and safety assessments. Further details are included in the protocol ( Supplement 1 ). Outcomes The primary efficacy end point was annualized growth velocity (AGV) at week 52 (planned visit window ±7 days). Protocol-specified secondary end points included: change from baseline in achondroplasia-specific height z score at week 52; change from baseline in Short-Form–10 Physical Summary Score (SF-10 PHS) at week 52 (only relevant for participants 5 years and older at baseline); change from baseline in Achondroplasia Child Experience Measure–Physical Functioning (ACEM-PF) at week 52; change from baseline in in ACEM-Daily Living Functioning (ACEM-DF) at week 52; and change from baseline in ACEM-Observable Signs Measure (ACEM-OSM) at week 52. Achondroplasia-specific height z scores were derived from reference data on children with achondroplasia. 13 Height z scores were calculated relative to an average-stature population based on US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reference data. 14 Exploratory end points included measures of skeletal proportionality, as assessed by physical landmarking assessments (upper to lower body segment ratio) and radiographic measurements (fibula to tibia length ratio assessed from standing radiography), and the degree of lower limb malalignment as evaluated radiographically on standing radiography using standardized measurements: tibial-femoral angle (TFA), the angle between the mechanical axes of the tibia and femur in the frontal plane, and mechanical axis deviation (MAD), the distance between the center of the knee and the weight-bearing axis of the leg. Positive TFA and MAD values reflected outward bowing of the leg (genu varum), while negative values indicated inward bowing (genu valgum). Greater positive or negative values reflected more severe lower limb malalignment. TFA z scores were derived based on established age-specific reference values. 15 HRQoL was assessed via the ACEM and the SF-10 PHS. ACEM domains included ACEM-PF, ACEM-DF, and ACEM-OSM (eFigures 3 and 4 in Supplement 2 ). 16 , 17 , 18 Achondroplasia experience measures were observer (parent/caregiver) reported, scored on a scale from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating greater burden. 16 , 17 , 18 Safety was evaluated based on adverse events (AEs), laboratory results, vital signs, physical examination, electrocardiogram results, and immunogenicity. Bone maturation and integrity were assessed through bone age (section 3 in the eResults in Supplement 2 ) and bone-related safety events. AEs of special interest were defined as injection site reactions, symptomatic hypotension, and fractures. The severity of all identified AEs were determined by the investigators. Statistical Analysis An estimated sample size of 78 (52 participants in the navepegritide treatment group and 26 in the placebo group) was determined to provide approximately 90% power to detect an AGV treatment difference of 1.2 cm/y at a 2-sided significance level of .05, assuming a standard deviation of 1.5 cm/y. All randomized participants were included in the efficacy analyses (full analysis set). All participants who received at least 1 dose of trial treatment were included in the safety analysis. Data from clinical assessments were summarized using descriptive statistics. End points were tested using a multiple testing procedure based on a graphical approach 19 to control for multiplicity and type I error across the primary efficacy end point (AGV at week 52) and 5 secondary end points, as detailed in section 2 in the eMethods in Supplement 2 and eFigure 2 in Supplement 2 . For participants with missing height data at week 52, missing height measurements (cm) were imputed by the multiple imputation method. Further details are included in section 2 in the eMethods in Supplement 2 . APPROACH is registered in the European Union Drug Regulating Authorities Clinical Trial Database (EudraCT: 2022-002954-25) and National Institutes of Health (ClinicalTrials.gov; NCT05598320 ). The significance level was controlled at .05 for a 2-tailed test. Baseline characteristics were similar between groups ( Table 1 ). Mean adherence rates were similar between groups (navepegritide: 99.7%, placebo: 100.1%). The mean achondroplasia-specific height z score was close to 0 at baseline, indicating that the trial population was comparable in height to the broader population of untreated children with achondroplasia ( Table 1 ). Participants’ baseline height deviated from that of an average-stature population as expected, with mean CDC-based height z score markedly below 0 (the mean across both groups was −5.0). The trial met its prespecified primary efficacy objective, demonstrating superiority of navepegritide compared with placebo in AGV at week 52 (least-squares [LS] mean difference, 1.49 cm/y; 95% CI, 1.05 to 1.93; P &lt; .001). Children treated with navepegritide achieved an LS mean AGV of 5.89 vs 4.41 cm/y for those treated with placebo. A summary of AGV data is presented in Figure 2 A. A difference in AGV between the navepegritide and placebo groups was observed by week 12 and was maintained throughout the double-blind period. The treatment effect was observed across age subgroups. In children younger than 5 years (n = 31), the LS mean difference in AGV at week 52 between navepegritide and placebo was 1.02 cm/y (95% CI, 0.29-1.74). In children 5 years and older (n = 53), the LS mean difference was 1.78 cm/y (95% CI, 1.22-2.33) (eTable 1 in Supplement 2 ). For children 5 years and older, the treatment difference was 1.41 cm/y in those aged 5 to 7 years (n = 36) (95% CI, 0.65-2.17) and 2.48 cm/y in those 8 years and older (n = 17; 95% CI, 1.39-3.57). A prespecified subgroup analysis in children younger than 5 years treated with navepegritide showed greater improvement in ACEM-PF compared with placebo (LS mean treatment difference, −11.1; 95% CI, −21.5 to −0.8). To prevent reporter bias from observer variability, the protocol recommended the same caregiver respond to ACEM questionnaires at baseline and at week 52. A post hoc analysis showed notable treatment effect for ACEM-PF when the same caregiver responded (LS mean difference, −8.61; 95% CI, −16.14 to −1.08) (eFigure 5 in Supplement 2 ), which was more pronounced in participants with greater baseline ACEM burden (score ≥20) (LS mean difference, −15.76; 95% CI, −29.39 to −2.12) (eFigure 5 in Supplement 2 ). No difference between groups was identified in SF-10 PHS at week 52. No AEs led to discontinuation of treatment or withdrawal from trial. AEs occurred in a similar proportion of children receiving navepegritide (n = 52/57 [91.2%]) and placebo (n = 26/27 [96.3%]) ( Table 2 ). The most common AEs are listed in Table 2 . Most AEs were grade 1 or 2. Five AEs were grade 3: 4 in the navepegritide group (localized infection, otitis media, vertebral foraminal stenosis, and cerebral ventricle dilatation) and 1 in the placebo group (sleep apnea) ( Table 2 ). Treatment-related AEs occurred in 21.1% (n = 12/57) of participants on navepegritide and 25.9% (n = 7/27) of participants on placebo. Serious AEs were reported in 6 participants: 3 in the navepegritide group (torticollis, localized infection, and vertebral foraminal stenosis) and 3 in the placebo group (muscle spasms, mesenteric lymphadenitis, and sleep apnea). Discussion In this randomized clinical trial, children randomized to treatment with navepegritide (100 μg/kg/wk) had significantly better growth and other improvements in important health outcomes compared with those receiving placebo. These results underscore the efficacy of navepegritide in promoting linear growth in children with achondroplasia. Achondroplasia-specific height z score showed a significant improvement from baseline to week 52, with an LS mean treatment difference of 0.28 between navepegritide and placebo. The mean baseline achondroplasia-specific height z score based on reference data from the Achondroplasia Natural History Study (CLARITY) 13 was near 0, further validating the baseline comparability of the trial population to other treatment-naive children with achondroplasia. These findings highlight the potential of navepegritide to provide clinically meaningful growth benefits across the population of children with achondroplasia. Cross-trial comparisons between navepegritide and vosoritide must be interpreted with caution, given differences in study design, study end points, and patient populations. In the phase 3 placebo-controlled trial of daily vosoritide, 11 which enrolled children aged 5 to 17 years, the difference in change from baseline to week 52 in AGV between the vosoritide group and the placebo group was 1.57 cm/y (95% CI, 1.22-1.93; P &lt; .001). This result is not directly comparable to the result of the APPROACH trial, which enrolled younger children (aged 2 to 11 years) and assessed a different primary end point (AGV at 52 weeks). However, analysis of the subset of APPROACH participants aged 5 years and older showed a difference in mean AGV at week 52 between navepegritide and placebo of 1.78 cm/y (95% CI, 1.22 to 2.33). Given the importance of overall health and wellness, analyses were conducted to identify the effects of navepegritide beyond increasing height. Normalization of the relative rates of fibular and tibial growth, along with improvements in lower limb alignment (characterized by reductions in both TFA and MAD), were observed in the navepegritide group, suggesting that navepegritide helped correct lower limb malalignment. This may reduce the mechanical strain on joints in the lower limbs and contribute to long-term reductions in joint dysfunction, pain, and the need for corrective orthopedic surgery. These are novel findings from a randomized clinical trial in children with achondroplasia, demonstrating observed improvements in skeletal alignment. Beyond growth and skeletal alignment, navepegritide also showed positive effects on HRQoL, particularly in physical functioning. These benefits were more pronounced in children with greater baseline burden, as assessed with ACEM, highlighting that those with more severe functional limitations may benefit most from navepegritide treatment. Importantly, the benefits of navepegritide were achieved without accelerating bone age or negatively affecting spinal curvature (section 3 in the eResults in Supplement 2 ). Navepegritide demonstrated a safety and tolerability profile similar to placebo, with most AEs being mild or moderate. Anti-CNP antibodies were infrequent, transient, and low in titer, likely because the active CNP moiety in navepegritide is identical to the amino acid sequence 89-126 of endogenous CNP and is shielded by the TransCon carrier until it is released. Disadvantages of vosoritide treatment include relatively high rates of injection site reactions and challenges associated with daily injections. 11 , 21 , 22 , 23 , 24 Navepegritide mitigates the risk for injection site reactions through once-weekly administration, absence of foreign or modified amino acids, and its administration as an inactive prodrug, limiting local exposure to active CNP at the injection site. 25 The high peak concentration following daily injections of a short-acting CNP analog like vosoritide can lead to vasodilation and potential safety concerns pertaining to hypotension. 26 Navepegritide was designed to provide steady release of active CNP to minimize the risk of peak concentration–related side effects, enhancing tolerability and reducing related side effects such as transient hypotension. Symptomatic hypotension was not observed with navepegritide. Limitations Several limitations of this trial should be acknowledged. This trial did not evaluate the long-term effects of navepegritide on adult height nor how efficacy may differ based on longer-term treatment duration. The analyses also do not directly evaluate functional improvements, medical complications, or the need for surgical interventions. Investigations are underway to assess the long-term effects of navepegritide, efficacy and safety in additional age groups (&lt;2 years and 12-18 years), and benefits beyond growth, including orthopedic outcomes and HRQoL. Conclusions The results reported from this 52-week trial suggest navepegritide represents a promising new option to treat children with achondroplasia, with the potential to reduce the medical burden of this condition, particularly with regard to fewer injections and reduced injection-related complications. By providing continuous exposure to active CNP, navepegritide offers a convenient once-weekly dosing regimen that promotes growth and improves lower limb alignment and HRQoL. To further assess the long-term balance between benefit and safety of navepegritide, the AttaCH trial will continue to follow up with these participants until the end of their growth period. Collectively, the results of this trial suggest navepegritide could become a transformative treatment option for children with achondroplasia, offering meaningful improvements in height and health outcomes beyond height.
