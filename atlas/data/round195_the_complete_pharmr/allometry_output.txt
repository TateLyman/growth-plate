==============================================================================
FEMORAL LENGTH GAIN VERSUS BODY-WEIGHT GAIN, FEMALE RATS
==============================================================================

Reference line: d(length)/length = k * d(mass)/mass, k = 1/3 for
isometric scaling. Sensitivity k = 0.25 and 0.40 alongside.

--- 26-week rat toxicity study, females ---
    femur length reported as 1.02x, 1.03x, 1.04x; BW +7/+10/+10%. Males in the same study: BW -3.8%, length change not seen.

     dose    dBW%   dLen%   k=0.25 pred   k=0.33 pred   k=0.40 pred   excess @1/3
      low     7.0     2.0          1.75          2.33          2.80         -0.33
      mid    10.0     3.0          2.50          3.33          4.00         -0.33
     high    10.0     4.0          2.50          3.33          4.00         +0.67

--- first carcinogenicity study, females (2 y) ---
    only the high-dose length is stated (up to 6%); BW +4.5/+9.0/+10.4% after 1 year. Length is by CALIPER in animals whose femoral width rose 33%.

     dose    dBW%   dLen%   k=0.25 pred   k=0.33 pred   k=0.40 pred   excess @1/3
      low     4.5     n/r          1.12          1.50          1.80             -
      mid     9.0     n/r          2.25          3.00          3.60             -
     high    10.4     6.0          2.60          3.47          4.16         +2.53

[1] READING
    26-week study. Isometric scaling predicts +2.3%, +3.3%, +3.3%
    length from the observed weight gains. Observed: +2.0%, +3.0%,
    +4.0%. Two of three doses fall BELOW the isometric line and the
    third exceeds it by 0.7 percentage points. There is no residual
    length effect left over once body size is accounted for.

    Two-year study. Isometric predicts +3.5% from +10.4% BW;
    observed +6% by caliper, an excess of +2.5 points. But that
    measurement is the one whose femoral WIDTH rose 33%, and the
    regulator's own reading of it is 'effect of LY mainly on
    periosteal expansion'. The excess is the size of the artefact
    the method cannot exclude.

[2] WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
    It does NOT prove the length gain is only body size - allometry
    is a reference line, the exponent is not measured in these
    animals, and body weight and femur length could both be
    downstream of one plate effect.
    It DOES remove the reason to think otherwise. The female length
    gains are the size that the concurrent weight gains predict; the
    males, who lost weight, gained no length; and the one arm that
    exceeds the line is the one measured by a method that counts
    periosteal bone as length.

[3] THE DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT, WHICH NOBODY HAS RUN
    Dose intact males and females at matched exposure with a
    PAIR-FED control arm, so body-weight trajectory is equalised
    across drug and vehicle. Measure femur length by microCT and
    growth plate height and cells per column in the same animals.
    If the female length gain survives pair-feeding, it is a plate
    effect. If it disappears, the entire femoral-length literature
    for this drug class is a body-weight readout.
