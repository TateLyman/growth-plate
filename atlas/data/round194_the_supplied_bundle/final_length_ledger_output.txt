[1] THE SPLIT IS BY MEASUREMENT METHOD, NOT BY STUDY
    QCT, interim (6 months of treatment): POSITIVE, dose-dependent,
      in BOTH the 2-8 month and the 6-12 month window.
    QCT, terminal (26 months of age): NULL in every arm, including
      the two arms dosed continuously to the end (2-26, 6-26).
    Caliper, terminal: +5%/+6% (vahle2002).
    BMA-inferred, terminal: +5%/+3% (jolette2006).
    The two positives at terminal both use methods that cannot
    separate longitudinal growth from radial and periarticular new
    bone. vahle2002 measured femoral WIDTH +33%/+32% in the same
    animals; jolette2006's BMA is length x width by construction.
    CORR-189 applies to jolette2006 by name: a projected AREA cannot
    be assigned to the length term.

[2] AGE GATE -- ANSWERED, AND THE ANSWER IS 'NO GATE'
    2-8 mo window (rapid growth):  +1.5% @5, +3.4% @30
    6-12 mo window (slowing):      +1.5% @5, +2.9% @30
    The two age windows are within 0.5 percentage points of each
    other at both doses. Whatever the drug does to femoral
    elongation, it does not require the rapid-growth phase.
    This retires the age-gate objection raised in round 185.

[3] UNTREATED GROWTH FOR SCALE
    Control femur 8 mo  = 32.8 mm; control 12 mo = 34.2 mm
    -> 4 months of untreated growth at this age = 1.4 mm
    -> 6 months of 30 ug/kg/d in the 6-12 mo window = 1.0 mm  (DERIVED)
    The drug added 0.71x what unaided growth
    delivered over the preceding four months. The effect is real and
    it is not small -- at the interim timepoint.

[4] WHAT THE TERMINAL NULL CAN EXCLUDE
    No SD is reported anywhere. Recover one from the significance
    boundary: +0.5 mm at n=8/group was flagged significant (p<0.05).
    two-sided t crit, df=14        : 2.145
    implied SD                     : 0.466 mm     [INFERRED, value_unverified]
    implied CV at 35 mm            : 1.33%
    min detectable diff at 50% power: 0.50 mm = 1.43% of final length
    min detectable diff at 80% power: 0.70 mm = 1.99% of final length
    So the terminal null excludes a final-length gain of ~1.5% or
    more at 80% power. It does NOT exclude a gain of ~1%.
    It DOES exclude the +5%/+6% caliper claim: 5% of 35 mm is
    1.75 mm, which would have printed as 37, not 35.

[5] THE ONE READING THAT SURVIVES BOTH TABLES
    Interim positive + terminal null = the drug ACCELERATES femoral
    elongation and the animal gives it back. Controls caught up
    (32.8 -> 34.2 -> 35.0 mm) while treated animals arrived at the
    same asymptote earlier. This is a RATE effect on an unchanged
    total, i.e. exactly the rate-yield trade-off fitted from
    hunziker1994 in round 183 (A proportional to GR^-0.146).
    PTH1R agonism is therefore a rate agent, not a yield agent, in
    the only species where final bone length has been measured.

[6] WHY THIS IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY FATAL FOR THE HUMAN CASE
    The rat gives it back because the rat plate never closes and the
    control catches up over 14 further months. A human at bone age
    16+ has a plate that FUSES on its own schedule. If the schedule
    is set by time or by hormonal milieu, an accelerated 6 months is
    banked. If the schedule is set by exhaustion of a fixed
    proliferative reserve, accelerating consumption fuses earlier and
    banks nothing.
    THE RAT DATA CANNOT DISTINGUISH THESE. This is now the single
    open question on which the whole line turns.

[7] SIZE OF THE PRIZE IF -- AND ONLY IF -- IT IS BANKED
    ASSUMPTION, NOT DATA: the interim rat femoral response (+2.9% to
    +3.4% over 6 months) transfers to human femur at the same
    fractional rate. There is no human observation supporting this;
    it is an order-of-magnitude sanity check only.
      at 2.9%: femur +1.4 cm, femur+tibia +2.5 cm  [ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY]
      at 3.4%: femur +1.6 cm, femur+tibia +3.0 cm  [ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY]
    Spine is excluded from this arithmetic: no vertebral HEIGHT was
    measured in any of these studies. L6 vertebral X-AREA rose
    10.6-16% (young) and 4.2-11.5% (older) -- a cross-section, which
    by CORR-189 says nothing about vertebral height.

[8] WHAT THE REGULATORY PACKAGE DOES NOT CONTAIN
    NDA 208743 (abaloparatide, Kuijpers): zero bone-length endpoints,
      zero juvenile animal studies, one passing mention of the growth
      plate in background prose.
    NDA 21-318 monkey study: animals were deliberately selected as
      'skeletally mature adults with no open growth plates' (9-11 y).
    So no PTH1R agonist has ever been given to a growing non-rodent
    in a regulatory study, by design.
