## 3. ⛔⛔ BUT VinSpinIn IS NOT THE AGENT — TWO HARD STOPS, NEITHER OF THEM MY RULE

### Stop 1: its own negative control is toxic
From the probe's characterisation: **"Both the active and inactive compounds displayed toxicity, implying
off-target effects."** VinSpinIC — the purpose-built inactive control — is toxic too. **The toxicity is
off-target chemistry, not SPIN inhibition.** A probe whose inactive twin is equally toxic cannot be dosed
into an animal and attributed to its target.

### Stop 2: the selectivity runs exactly backwards, and the off-target is lethal
| | SPIN1 | SPIN4 |
|---|---|---|
| **VinSpinIn** | **9.9 nM — highest affinity** | binds (thermal shift); family KDs 10–130 nM |
| compound 18 | 30 nM | 0.71 µM — **24-fold SPIN1-selective** |
| EML631–633 | selective | ⛔ **no interaction** |

**Every existing Spindlin compound is SPIN1-first.** And:

> **SPIN1 knockout mice DIE SHORTLY AFTER BIRTH.** SPIN1 is essential for postnatal survival and controls
> skeletal muscle development — *"the first histone code reader for which ablation in skeletal muscle has
> fatal consequences."*
> **SPIN4 knockout mice are VIABLE, LARGER, and have MORE resting-zone progenitors.**

**The safety asymmetry is perfect and it points away from every available molecule.** We need SPIN4 *without*
SPIN1; the pharmacopoeia offers SPIN1 *without* SPIN4. **Dosing VinSpinIn hits the essential gene first, at
the tightest affinity, with a compound whose control is already toxic.**

## 4. ⭐ SO HOW DO WE ACTUALLY HIT IT

| route | selectivity | delivery | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASO / siRNA vs SPIN4** | ⭐ **free — sequence-based; SPIN1 vs SPIN4 discrimination is trivial** | the hard part | **the right answer** |
| SPIN4-selective Tudor binder | **provably feasible** — the field made SPIN1-selective compounds, so the family IS chemically separable | systemic | **does not exist; nobody wants it** |
| PROTAC / degrader | needs a selective binder first | — | blocked by the above |
| pan-SPIN small molecule | ⛔ SPIN1-first, and SPIN1 is essential | — | **rejected** |

**The oligonucleotide route inverts the problem in our favour.** The reason small molecules fail here is
selectivity between two ~60%-identical Tudor proteins. **An ASO or siRNA gets that selectivity for free** —
SPIN1 and SPIN4 are different sequences. The hard part becomes delivery to cartilage.

> ⭐ **And this programme already solved that delivery problem for a different agent.** The atlas's
> intraosseous fibrin-depot route into the secondary ossification centre (R373/388/393) — designed for SAG —
> is exactly the delivery format a cartilage-targeted oligo needs. **The two halves were developed eleven
> rounds apart and have never been put together.**

Also relevant: **SPIN4 is X-linked**, so a male subject is **hemizygous** — one allele, not two, which
halves the knockdown burden.
