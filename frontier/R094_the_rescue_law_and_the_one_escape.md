## 1. First, the correction to F-R093

I wrote that CD2665 given alone *"expands the plate and delays maturation"* and treated that as a
never-closing lever. **I had only the abstract. The full text says the length endpoint was flat.**

`nihms-1846840` — Koyama et al., JBMR 2021, full text, §"companion mice":

> *"companion mice receiving RARγ antagonist alone displayed an appreciable delay in chondrocyte
> maturation and ossification indicated by a **slight increase in overall growth plate height**… and an
> expansion of Col II-expressing upper zones and Col X-expressing hypertrophic zone."*

Then, three paragraphs later:

> *"**All the above skeletal parameters were essentially unaffected in companion mice receiving RARγ
> antagonist alone. Given the paucity of responses, this treatment regimen was not included in subsequent
> experiments.**"*

**A thicker growth plate and no longer bone.** The arm was dropped for lack of effect. What CD2665 *did*
do was real and large — LDE225 caused a 10–15% deficit in body length and tibia length by P31, and
co-administration **largely rescued it**, with the plate visibly open on X-ray. But that is rescue.

**F-R093's claim that RARγ antagonism is a standalone never-closing lever is withdrawn.**

## 4. Trompet's systemic length result is not negative. It is underpowered and measured far too early.

I have been carrying "Trompet's systemic arm showed pool +61% but no length change" as if it were
evidence against systemic dosing. Reading the actual numbers:

> *"this **short exposure** did not cause differences in bone length (tibia length **15.2 ± 0.4** and
> **14.5 ± 1.7**, P = 0.29, **n = 7/5**, femur length **12.7 ± 0.4** and **14.8 ± 4.7**, P = 0.247,
> n = 7/5, for control and SAG-treated mice, respectively)."*

Three things:

1. **The femur mean was HIGHER in the SAG group** — 14.8 vs 12.7 mm.
2. **The SD was 4.7 mm on a 12.7 mm bone.** That is a 37% coefficient of variation on a skeletal
   measurement, at **n = 5**. This measurement cannot detect anything.
3. **They killed the animals at P38 — eight days after the last dose.** Trompet's own bead experiment
   did not show a length difference until **one month**, and it kept widening at two and six.

**Nobody has ever given systemic Smoothened agonist to a normal animal and measured length at a
timepoint where length could have appeared.** Li/Yang did systemic-with-length but in a deficient animal.
Trompet did normal-animal-systemic but looked at 8 days with n=5.

**That is a gap, not a refutation, and it is the second decisive missing experiment.**

## 5. The escape, and it is the only one the data supports

Why did the bead work when nothing else did?

- Gli1 reporter signal **gone by 3 weeks**
- femur longer at **1 month**, more at **2**, more at **6**
- no osteoarthritis at 6 months
- contralateral control in the same animal

**The exposure ended and the divergence kept widening for five more months.** A rate agent cannot do that.
Counter-regulation cannot undo it either — which means the pulse changed something **structural** that the
set-point machinery does not re-regulate. Column number, or pool size, or niche occupancy: a quantity that
once changed, stays changed.

**That is the escape from the rescue law: a transient pulse whose structural gain outlives the
counter-regulation that would otherwise erase it.** Push and hold, and you get Sufu-cKO. Push, release,
consolidate, repeat — and nobody has ever tried it.

### And the combination that follows, stated as the hypothesis it is

F-R093 established that sustained Hedgehog closes the plate (Xiu) and that Hedgehog-driven closure runs
through the retinoid circuit — Cyp26b1 down, Raldh3 up (Koyama).

**CD2665 alone does nothing because there is nothing to block.** Its entire measured value is as a
counter-agent to a drive toward maturation. Given alone in a normal mouse, there is no accelerated
maturation, so there is no effect — exactly what Koyama observed.

**SAG supplies that drive.** So the untested combination is:

> **pulsed Smoothened agonist to expand the compartment, plus RARγ antagonism to block the maturation
> acceleration that is how the system closes the plate in response.**

Neither paper tested it. Koyama's closure came from an Hh *inhibitor*, not an agonist, so whether
over-activation closes through the same Cyp26b1/Raldh3 circuit is unknown and is the load-bearing
assumption. **I am flagging this as a hypothesis with one clear untested link, not a result.**

### The receptor is clean, which is why this is worth testing

`zjaf018` — Matsuoka et al., JBMR 2025 — settles the retinoid axis with unusual precision:

- RARγ agonist nanoparticles implanted **medially and laterally** at the proximal tibial physis → *"the GP
  underwent involution and closure. Overall tibia length was **shortened** compared to the contralateral
  element."*
- Implanted **laterally only** → *"the adjacent epiphysis **tilted** toward the lateral side, leading to
  apical angulation of the tibia."* **Directional, sub-physeal spatial control.**
- **Systemic** RARγ agonist → *"GP closure at **many sites**, inhibiting skeletal growth over time"* —
  which is the mechanism of palovarotene's boxed warning, reproduced deliberately in mice.
- **"Agonists for RARα and RARβ elicited no obvious responses over parallel regimens."**

**RARγ specifically, not retinoid signalling generally.** Combined with palovarotene's label — premature
physeal closure in **21 of 57** children under 14, **36.8%**, boxed — this axis has cleaner
direction-and-receptor evidence in humans than anything else in the file. What it does not have is any
demonstration that antagonism alone adds length.
