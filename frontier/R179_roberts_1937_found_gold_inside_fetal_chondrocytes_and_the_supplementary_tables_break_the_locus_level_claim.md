## => ⭐⭐ PART 2 — **THE FREE FRACTION, WHICH I ASKED FOR IN R178, IS `7%`**

> **`Nguyen TTTN, Østergaard J, Gammelgaard B, Anal Bioanal Chem 2015` — CE-ICP-MS, LOD 18 ng/mL auranofin
> (5.2 ng/mL Au), precision 1.5%.**
> ⭐⭐⭐ ***"the `4 and 7 %` of FREE AURANOFIN found in the untreated HSA and plasma solutions incubated for 1 h
> at 37 °C"*** — **4% free in 0.3 mM HSA, `7%` FREE IN HUMAN PLASMA.**
> ⭐ *"**Fifty percent of the unbound auranofin had disappeared within 2 and 3 min**"*; ⭐ **Cys-34 is the site** —
> alkylating it leaves **94–96%** free; ⭐ *"HSA was the major auranofin-interacting protein in plasma."*
> ⭐ Also: **40% of blood gold on red cells, 60% on plasma proteins**; after IV, **99% protein-associated.**

### ⛔ APPLIED NAIVELY, THIS WOULD GUT R178's MARGINS
Culture medium at 10% FBS has roughly **an order of magnitude less albumin than plasma**, so the free
fraction in the Chen 2023 cell experiments was **higher** than 7%. A naive 4–10× correction gives:

| dose | R178 ×0.2 µM | ⛔ **naively free-corrected** |
|---|---|---|
| 2 mg/day | 5.3× | ⛔ **0.5–1.3×** |
| 6 mg/day | 15.7× | ⛔ **1.6–3.9×** |

### ⭐⭐⭐ BUT THE NAIVE CORRECTION IS WRONG FOR THIS DRUG CLASS, AND ROBERTS PROVES IT
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Au(I) on albumin Cys-34 is held by a REVERSIBLE THIOL-EXCHANGE EQUILIBRIUM, not by the hydrophobic
> sequestration that makes an ordinary drug's bound fraction inert. Albumin is a gold CARRIER and RESERVOIR:
> it hands gold to any higher-affinity thiol. If the 93% bound fraction were truly unavailable, gold would
> never enter tissue at all — yet Gottlieb measured `5–129 µg/g` across human tissues, and Roberts watched
> gold cross a placenta and load fetal cells one by one out of a maternal circulation in which it was
> overwhelmingly protein-bound.**

> ### ⭐⭐ **SO THE CORRECTION IS REAL BUT BOUNDED WELL BELOW THE NAIVE RATIO. THE HONEST CONCLUSION IS THE SAME ONE R178 REACHED, NOW ON A QUANTIFIED BASIS: THE MARGIN AT `2 mg/day` IS THIN ENOUGH THAT IT MUST NOT BE ASSUMED. START THERE, TITRATE UPWARD, HEADROOM TO 6 mg/day.**
