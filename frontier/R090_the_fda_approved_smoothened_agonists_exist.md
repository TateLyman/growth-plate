## 1. The paper

> **Wang JC, Chen JK, … Chen W, Lefkowitz RJ et al. *Identification of select glucocorticoids as
> Smoothened agonists: potential utility for regenerative medicine.* PNAS 2010;107(20):9323–9328.
> PMID 20439738 · PMC2889058 · Duke University.**
>
> *"The development of Hedgehog-related therapies has been impeded by **a lack of US Food and Drug
> Administration (FDA)-approved Smo agonists**. Using a high-content screen with cells expressing Smo
> receptors and a β-arrestin2-GFP reporter, we identified **four FDA-approved drugs, halcinonide,
> fluticasone, clobetasol, and fluocinonide, as Smo agonists that activate Hedgehog signaling**. These
> drugs demonstrated an ability to **bind Smo, promote Smo internalization, activate Gli**, and stimulate
> the proliferation of primary neuronal precursor cells alone and **synergistically in the presence of
> Sonic Hedgehog protein**."*

Duke filed a provisional patent on the four for tissue repair and regeneration. The authors' own closing
line:

> *"we propose that FGSAs could be used **immediately orally or i.v.** on an acute or chronic basis…
> the well-known pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of these **FDA-approved steroid Smo
> agonists provide a significant jumpstart in the process of beginning human studies**."*

### Potency — direct SMO engagement (β-arrestin2-GFP translocation, EC50)

| compound | EC50 | formulation | route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fluticasone propionate** | **0.099 µM** | Flovent, Flonase, Advair, Trelegy | **inhaled / intranasal — routine, paediatric** |
| **halcinonide** | 1.1 µM | Halog 0.1% | topical |
| **clobetasol propionate** | 1.5 µM | Temovate, Dermovate 0.05% | topical (superpotent) |
| fluocinonide | >5 µM | Lidex 0.05% | topical |

### Functional output — cerebellar granule precursor proliferation vs DMSO

| compound | alone | with 2% Shh |
|---|---|---|
| **halcinonide** | **40–50×** (≈ purmorphamine's maximum) | strong synergism |
| **fluticasone, clobetasol** | **5–6×** | strong synergism |
| fluocinonide | none alone | 30× with Shh |
| SAG (positive control) | — | 95× |
| **cortisone, dexamethasone, prednisolone, corticosterone** | **none** | — |

**Fluticasone is the most potent at the receptor. Halcinonide produces the largest cellular response.
Both facts stand; they are different assays.**

## 2. This corrects F-R089's Tier 2. Dexamethasone is the wrong glucocorticoid.

F-R089 nominated **dexamethasone** as the banking agent, on Schrier's P=0.016 increase in resting-zone
chondrocyte number. **Dexamethasone is not a Smoothened agonist. It is the opposite.**

> *"the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) agonist **dexamethasone had a tendency to inhibit the activity of
> 2% Shh**… **dexamethasone inhibited Shh-activated GCP proliferation in a dose-dependent manner**…
> **No** such growth-enabling responses were observed in GCPs treated with cortisone, dexamethasone,
> prednisolone, or corticosterone."*

The screen covered **68 glucocorticoids and related steroids** from the Prestwick FDA-approved library,
including cortisone and dexamethasone. Four came out.

**So Schrier's dexamethasone result is a GR effect — slow the divisions, spend the pool more slowly —
and it is not the Hedgehog mechanism.** It survives as *conservation*. But **the FGSAs would do the
Trompet thing instead: actively expand.** Conservation and expansion are not the same buy, and I had
picked the weaker one and attributed it to the wrong receptor.

### The structure–activity rule, which explains why

> *"each of the active Smo agonists contains a hydroxyl group in the beta configuration at C-11… These
> inactive Smo agonists often possess a **C-17 substituent that is more hydrophilic, less branched and
> generally smaller.** Some of these compounds also have a **fluorine atom in the C-9 alpha position**."*

Active = 11β-OH **plus a large, branched, hydrophobic C-17**. Fluticasone propionate carries a 17α-
propionate *and* a 17β-fluoromethyl-carbothioate — the bulkiest C-17 in the clinical glucocorticoid
class. Dexamethasone has a small C-17 and a 9α-fluorine. **The SAR predicts the split, and it is not
"fluorinated = active".**

## 3. The finding that makes this usable: SMO agonism is separable from GR

This is the part that matters most, because the GR arm is what suppresses growth.

> *"Experiments repeated in the presence of **5 µM mifepristone (RU-486)**, a glucocorticoid nuclear
> receptor antagonist, **gave similar results**."*
>
> *"Although all the fluorinated glucocorticoid smoothened agonists from our study possess the ability,
> **like dexamethasone, to activate GR** as assessed by a GR-GFP nuclear translocation assay… The
> opposite response of dexamethasone in the GCP proliferation assay and its **similar** response in the
> nuclear receptor assay compared to the FGSAs suggest that the signal for the GCP proliferative response
> is **independent of glucocorticoid nuclear receptor signaling and is most probably attributable
> directly to activation of Smo**."*

Two drugs, identical GR activation, **opposite** proliferative outcomes. The proliferation survives GR
blockade. **Therefore the two arms can be pulled apart pharmacologically, and mifepristone is an
approved drug with a chronic systemic dosing precedent (Korlym, 300–1200 mg/day for Cushing's).**

That is the extrapolation Tate asked for: **fluticasone or halcinonide for the Smoothened arm, with a GR
antagonist to strip the growth-suppressive arm the glucocorticoid class otherwise carries.**

## 4. Chu 2026, read properly — and the nuance we had wrong

`Chu TL et al. Sci Transl Med 2026;18:eadw3590` (on disk). Early-pubertal human growth plate, surgical
specimens from growth-restricting procedures.

**The human resting zone contains TWO transcriptionally distinct clusters, and PTHrP is not the root.**

| | **GP1** | **GP2** |
|---|---|---|
| identity | *"parental, dormant stem cell population"* | PTHrP-positive |
| WNT | **low** | partially activated |
| **TGF-β** | **low — and actively repressed** | partially activated |
| soluble inhibitors | **high** (SFRP5; **THBS1, THBS2, DCN** the top TGF-β repressors) | lower |
| proliferation | **minimal — lowest index of all clusters** | **higher** |

> *"GP1 is characterized by low WNT and TGF-β signaling, high expression of soluble inhibitors, and
> minimal proliferation, suggesting a stable homeostatic or stemlike state. In contrast, **PTHrP-positive
> GP2 cells showed partial activation of these pathways and higher proliferative activity**… it is
> plausible to anticipate that **GP1 represents a parental, dormant stem cell population**."*

**Tate was right that we may have had the nuance wrong, and here is exactly where.**

Every mouse pool number this branch has ever quoted is measured on a **PTHrP reporter**:

- Trompet's **+61%** — `Pthrp-mCherry`
- Trompet's **65.5 → 139.8 cells/mm²** — `Pthrp-creER`
- the PNAS GH depletion, **P<0.0001** — `Pthrp-mCherry`
- Newton's niche, Mizuhashi's niche — PTHrP

**In human terms, all of that is measured on GP2 — the tier that is already partially activated — not on
GP1, the dormant root.** So Trompet's +61% is consistent with two different stories:

1. genuine expansion of the stem compartment, or
2. **recruitment of GP1 into GP2** — filling the second tier by spending the root.

**Nobody has measured GP1 number under any intervention, in any species.** This is the deciding
measurement restated in human terms, and it is now a sharper question than F-R088's "empty or asleep":
**it is a question about which of two tiers a drug moves.** I am not going to paper over it.

The one piece of evidence that leans toward (1): Trompet's bead exposure was **gone by 3 weeks** and the
length gap **kept widening through 6 months**. Pure recruitment spends a fixed root and should
decelerate. It did not. That is suggestive, not decisive.

### And Chu hands us a second axis that is obtainable

GP1's niche is **low-WNT *and* low-TGF-β**, held there by **THBS1, THBS2, DCN**. F-R088 and F-R089 only
ever had the WNT half. The preprint version states GH activates TGF-β **autocrine** in these cells — so
**TGF-β is a route by which GP1 is pushed out of dormancy, and blocking it should preserve the root.**

Obtainable TGF-β-axis agents, in descending order of realness: **galunisertib (LY2157299)**, Lilly,
ALK5 inhibitor, phase II in humans; **vactosertib (TEW-7197)**, clinical-stage; **fresolimumab**,
clinical-stage antibody; **pirfenidone**, FDA-approved oral (crude, indirect); **losartan**,
FDA-approved oral generic, a weak but genuine TGF-β suppressor with a long paediatric record in Marfan
syndrome.

**Losartan is the cheap probe and pirfenidone the approved one; galunisertib is the clean one.**

### Chu's GH explant, for completeness

Human growth plate explants, 2 months, vehicle vs GH: cartilage expanded, **PZ proliferation up,
P=0.013**, *"although not all patient samples responded."* Note the effect is in the **proliferative
zone**. This is GH as a rate agent in human tissue — it does not contradict the PNAS depletion result,
which was about fate in the stem tier.

## 6. Adapting it to humans

**The counterweight, stated first.** Fluticasone at asthma doses **reduces growth velocity in children** —
this is among the best-documented paediatric drug effects there is (~0.5–1 cm/yr early; ~1.2 cm final
adult height in the CAMP cohort with budesonide). Two things follow, and they point opposite ways:

- **against us:** at the doses in use, the **GR arm dominates the rate term**;
- **for us:** it is direct proof that **inhaled fluticasone reaches the human growth plate at
  pharmacologically active concentrations.** The delivery question is answered by the side effect.

And the shape of that clinical signal is itself informative: velocity falls a lot, **final height falls
very little**. That is a pool-preserved, rate-suppressed signature — the same shape as Schrier's
dexamethasone arm. It is what you would expect if the plate banked what it did not spend.

### The protocol as I would build it

| arm | agent | rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **pool — SMO** | **fluticasone propionate** (EC50 0.099 µM, the only one with a routine systemic-ish route and paediatric PK) — or **halcinonide** topical over large surface area for maximum cellular response | the only FDA-approved way to push the axis Trompet proved |
| **uncouple** | **mifepristone** — strip the GR-mediated rate suppression, keep the SMO agonism | the PNAS data say the two are separable; mifepristone has a chronic systemic precedent |
| **pool — root preservation** | **losartan** (obtainable, paediatric record) → **pirfenidone** → **galunisertib** | Chu: GP1 is TGF-β-low and actively repressed; GH pushes TGF-β autocrine |
| **pool — Wnt half of the niche** | as F-R089: niclosamide / pyrvinium / PORCN inhibitor, with the anti-osteoanabolic cost | Hallett + Chu both name low-WNT |
| **pool — do no harm** | **somatropin down toward physiological + intermittent** | unchanged from F-R089; the current dose is pool-negative |
| **downstream of Hh** | **abaloparatide** (PTHrP analogue = GP2 tier) | Trompet: SAG raises Pthlh⁺ cells; this is the tier below |
| **rate / deadline** | erdafitinib, mecasermin, anastrozole, ascorbate | unchanged |
| **dropped** | **dexamethasone** | wrong receptor arm — it *inhibits* Shh signalling. F-R089 Tier 2 withdrawn |

### The honest risk sentence

Sustained Hedgehog agonism is the mechanism of basal cell carcinoma and medulloblastoma; PTCH1 loss is
Gorlin syndrome. Tate has ruled risk out of scope and I am taking him at his word, but it goes in the
record in plain language rather than a footnote. The mitigating datum is that **Trompet saw the Gli1
signal vanish by three weeks with the length benefit still compounding at six months** — the exposure
that works is short and the benefit outlives it, which is the least-bad exposure profile available.
