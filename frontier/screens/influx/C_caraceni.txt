Introduction 
Hyperprolaetinemia is an endocrinologic syn- 
drome, which is either an idiopathic condition 
or due to a prolactin-seereting pituitary tumor, 
and is characterized by high plasma levels of 
prolactin ( PRL ) and usually associated with 
menstrual disorders ( amenorrhea, oligomenorrhea) 
headache, infertility, galactorrhea ( I ). A 
lower bone mineral content.( BMC ) in hyperpro- 
lactinemic women than in healthy controls was 
reported only recently ( 2, 3, 4 ). 
Functional hyperprolactinemia may be not 
treated if well tolerated. Pharmacological 
treatment is usually performed with dopamine ag- 
onists or serotonin antagonists. On the contrary 
prolactin-secreting tumors are treated since they 
may enlarge and so cause major damage to adjacent 
anatomic structures~ Treatment in this case may 
be either medical or surgical, but usually the 
first approach is pharmacological. It was re- 
cently shown that patients with prolactin-se- 
creting tumors after tranphenoidal surgery did 
not show modifications of bone mineral content 
whether they are cured or have persistent 
amenorrhea and hyperprolactinemia (3). No 
report exists in literature on the effects of 
pharmacological treatment on bone mass. 
The aim of this study was to investigate the 
effect of pharmacological hypoprolactinemic 
treatment on bone mineral content in hyperpro-

Discussion 
The possible effect of hypoprolactinemie 
treatment on BMC had not previously been investi- 
gated except after transphenoidal surgery for 
prolactinomas wben BMC was found to be unchanged 
( 3 ). Our study indicates that the decrease of 
prolactin plasma levels is associated with an 
increase of bone mineral content. 
The cause of osteopenla in hyperprolactlnemia has 
still to be clarified. It may be assumed that bone 
disorders are connected with the reduction of 
estrogen activity: estrogens are Enown to have a 
positive role in bone metabolism, but hyperpro- 
lactinemic women do not always have low plasma 
levels of estrogens ( 5 ). Some authors found a 
correlation between estrogens levels and BMC in 
hyperprolactinemia ( 2 ), whereas others did not 
(3,4). 
With regard to the relation between sex hormones 
and calcium regulating hormones, the available 
data are conflicting. Some authors reported an 
increase of prolactin after parathyroid hormone 
infusion ( 6 ) ; otherwise hyperprolactlnemic 
women seem to have plasma parathyroid hormone in 
the normal range ( 7 ). PRL was reported induce 
formation of 1,25(OH)2D 3 in vitro (8), although 
in another study it had no effect ( 9 ). In a 
previous study performed in hyperprolactinemic 
women we found a slight increase of 1,25(OH)2D 3 
( iO ), as reported by other authors ( Ii ). 
As regards the relation between calcitonin and 
prolactin there are various reports. In most 
clinical studies there was a mild to moderate 
depression of serum prolactin levels after 
injection of calcitonin ( 12, 13 ). In studies 
on the rats, systemic administration of ealcltonin 
moderatly inhibited the basal secretion of pro- 
lactin: also PRL secretion stimulated in lacta- 
ting rats By the suklng reflex was inhibited ( 14, 
15 ). The latest investigation suggests that cen- 
tral dopaminergic system is involved in this 
effect ( 16 ). On tbe other hand, some relation 
may exist between central dopamfnerglc system and 
mineral homeostasis: it is interesting that BMC 
was found to be increased in patients with Par- 
kinson's disease after treatment with L-dopa (17). 
The normalization of menstrual status suggests a 
role for estrogens in increasing BMC, however no 
correlation was found between PRL and BMC modifi- 
cations. Our preliminary data suggest that dopa- 
minergic agents may play a direct role in calcium 
metabolism, although further investigations are 
required.
