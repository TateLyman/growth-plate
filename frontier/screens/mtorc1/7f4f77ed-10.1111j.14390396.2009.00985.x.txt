ORIGINAL ARTICLE
2-Oxoglutaric acid administration diminishes
fundectomy-induced osteopenia in pigs
E. S´ liwa
Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Department of Biochemistry and Animal Physiology, University of Life Sciences in Lublin, Akademicka 12,
Lublin, Poland
Introduction
Gastrectomy is the surgical removal of the whole
stomach, and fundectomy is a partial gastrectomy
when the fundus of stomach is removed. This
surgery is performed as a treatment of cancer and
may also be performed to treat perforations resulting
from the ulcer of the stomach (Melton et al., 1999;
Svedlund et al., 1999; Heiskanen et al., 2001;
Scho¨lmerich, 2004; Crookes, 2006). Partial gastrec-
tomy also alleviates problems with obesity but
mainly in relation to human beings. However, surgical
removal of the stomach may have different
consequences for patients with short- and long-term
post-operative complications causing weight loss,
anaemia, disturbed nutrition intake, abnormal diges-
tion and absorption. Interventions like partial or
total removal of the stomach induce complex hor-
monal, metabolic and nutritional effects that may
interfere with longitudinal bone growth and bone
mass achievement not only during critical growth
phases of childhood but also during adolescence.
Metabolic bone disease can be a late complication
for approximately 25% of gastric surgery patients

Summary
Partial surgical removal of the stomach (fundectomy, FX) leads to osteo-
penia in animals and humans. FX adversely affects the bone. 2-oxo-
glutaric acid is a precursor of glutamine and hydroxyproline – the most
abundant amino acid of collagen. The aim of the study was to investi-
gate the effects of 2-oxoglutaric acid on FX-evoked osteopenia in pigs.
Eighteen castrated male pigs of the Puławska breed were used. Twelve
pigs were subjected to FX and divided into two groups: FX + AKG (the
AKG group; AKG at the daily dosage of 0.4 g/kg of body weight) and
FX + Placebo (the FXC group; received CaCO 3 as placebo). Remaining
six pigs were sham-operated (the SHO group). The pigs were euthanized
at the age of 8 months and long bones were collected. Area bone min-
eral density (aBMD) and bone mineral content (BMC) were measured;
morphology, geometry and biomechanical properties were determined.
Moreover, the serum concentrations of selected hormones and one
marker of bone metabolism were determined. FX caused osteopenia in
the pigs and treatment with AKG greatly reduced these effects of FX
in pigs. Negative effect of fundectomy on the skeletal system leading to
decreased bone mass in pigs is associated with lowered body gain and
activity of the gastric–hypothalamic–pituitary axis. Better deﬁnitions of
each of the local and systemic hormonal and structural components
associated with fundectomy-induced decreased bone mass that sepa-
rately and together determine the whole bone properties may lead to
identify opportunities for prevention.

(Melton et al., 1999). Gastrectomy or fundectomy
leads to problems with metabolic bone disease with-
out symptoms, until enhanced risk of fracture
mainly vertebra occurs. Both, gastrectomy and fun-
dectomy lead to loss of height, thoracic kyphosis and
chronic back pain, which are difﬁcult to treat (Inoue
et al., 1992; Mellstro ¨m et al., 1993; Persson et al.,
1993; Klinge et al., 1995; Maier et al., 1997; Zittel
et al., 1997; Lehto-Axtelius et al., 1998, 2002;
Svedlund et al., 1999; Stenstro ¨m et al., 2000; Puzio
et al., 2005). Aaron et al., 1974 observed in the
study of patients with fracture of the proximal femur
that the most common predisposing factor among
surgical removals of stomach was partial gastrec-
tomy–fundectomy. Usually, the bone disease devel-
ops insidiously, and symptoms are generally not
seen until 4 or 5 years after surgery in individuals,
in whom malnutrition may develop (Pryor et al.,
1971). Moreover, there is no clear evidence that cal-
cium and vitamin D supplementation is beneﬁcial in
preventing bone loss and malabsorption after partial
gastrectomy (Tirpitz and Reinshagen, 2003).
The best mean of predicting bone fractures is
widely used bone mineral density and content mea-
surement by dual X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA)
which is used mainly for diagnosis and monitoring
response to treatment (Inoue et al., 1992). More-
over, the most reliable markers of bone formation
are serum bone-speciﬁc alkaline phosphatase (BAP)
and osteocalcin. Quantitative measurement of BAP
activity in serum serves as an indicator for the rate
of bone formation. Osteocalcin is the most abundant
non-collagenous protein in the bone, and its concen-
tration in serum is closely linked to bone metabolism
and serves as a biological marker for the clinical
assessment of bone disease (Hoang et al., 2003).
2-Oxoglutaric acid is the metabolite of the Krebs
cycle – the energy producing process that occurs in
cells. Several studies have suggested that 2-oxoglu-
taric acid known as alpha-ketoglutaric acid – the
precursor of glutamine and hydroxyproline (main
amino acid of collagen) – is used by cells during
their growth and may modulate protein turnover
(Hammarqvist et al., 1991; LeBricon et al., 1997;
Boucher and Cynober, 1998; Danbolt, 2001;
Kristensen et al., 2002). In clinical studies on septic,
traumatic or surgical patients, 2-oxoglutaric acid
showed beneﬁcial effects improving the body weight
gain and nitrogen balance. In vitro and in vivo studies
demonstrate that 2-oxoglutaric acid decreases pro-
tein catabolism in the skeletal muscles and increases
protein synthesis in the liver and intestines (Werner-
man et al., 1990; LeBricon et al., 1997). It shows
protective effect against lowering action of dexa-
methasone on bone mineral density, both adminis-
tered simultaneously during prenatal or neonatal
time (S´liwa et al., 2004, 2005a,b; Kowalik et al.,
2005). Considering the results obtained, it was
hypothesized that oral administration of 2-oxoglutaric
acid may induce long-term positive effect on bone
metabolism in pigs after fundectomy.
For these reasons, it was speculated that oral sup-
plementation of 2-oxoglutaric acid might also
improve lower body and bone gain, which follows
malabsorption after surgical removal of a part of
stomach, but no research has been carried out to test
this hypothesis.
To evaluate the long-term effect of oral administra-
tion of 2-oxoglutaric acid on bone tissue in pigs sub-
jected to fundectomy, morphological and mechanical
properties of long bones were determined. Bone
metabolism was assessed by determination of serum
BAP and osteocalcin, and DEXA at the end of study.
Moreover, the various hormones essential for normal
gastrointestinal function and bone health, were used
in the study to assess post-surgical state of pigs,
and to monitor the effects of oral administration of
2-oxoglutaric acid. The study was conducted as a
placebo-controlled study.
Materials and methods
The experimental procedures used throughout this
study were approved by the Local Ethics Committee
on Animal Experimentation of The Agricultural Uni-
versity of Lublin, Poland.
Animals and surgical procedure
The study was performed on 18 castrated male pigs
of the Puławska breed without reported bone disease
approximately 12 days after their weaning from
sows. The animals were placed under standard rear-
ing conditions with constant access to fresh water.
Castrated male pigs were randomly assigned to two
weight-matched groups at the age of 40 days of life
and then subjected to sham operation as a control
(control = SHO group; n = 6) or experimental fun-
dectomy ( n = 12). The mean body weights of piglets
for the periods starting at the age of 40 days were
10.9 ± 0.6 and 11.8 ± 1.1 kg in the SHO and experi-
mental fundectomy groups respectively.
Surgical procedures were performed on 40-day-old
piglets that were fasted for 24 h before surgery; how-
ever, they were allowed to drink fresh water until 3 h
before the operation. Piglets were sedated with an

resection of the fundus and glandular part of the
stomach, followed by anastomosing of the remaining
tissues, which allowed undisturbed passage of the
food to the duodenum. As a result of the fundectomy,
approximately 50% of the stomach was removed.
Throughout the period of the study, the pigs from
all groups were housed indoors in three boxes (one
box for each group, temperature 22 /C176 C, humidity
60–70%) equipped with feeders enabling food con-
sumption for all animals within the group at the same
time. All animals were weighed and sacriﬁced at the age
of 8 months. Femur and tibia were weighed and their
length was measured and frozen for further analysis.
Animal diet
Piglets throughout the study were fed with commer-
cial diet twice a day (0800 and 1600 h), supplied in
accordance with the stage of production cycle and age
of pigs as previously recommended (National
Research Council, 1998; The Scientiﬁc Committee on
Animal Nutrition, 2000). The composition of feedstuff
is presented in Table 1. After recovery, both the fun-
dectomized and sham-operated piglets were food
deprived for next 24 h and an i.v. infusion of the 5%
glucose solution (500 ml/piglet/day) was performed.
During next 3 days, all piglets were fed with one-third
of the daily dosage of the diet, which was increased
up to 50% until the end of the ﬁrst week after the
fundectomy. Starting on eighth day after fundectomy,
the fundectomized as well as control animals received
100% of the daily dosage of the diet.
Fundectomized animals were divided into two
groups dependent on additional diet supplementa-
tion. The fundectomized control group (FXC) com-
prised six males that received CaCO 3 as placebo.
CaCO3 was administered as a solution diluted in
water. Fundectomized animals (FX + AKG) from the
alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) group ( n = 6) received
solution of 2-oxoglutaric acid at the daily dosage of
0.4 g/kg of body weight. The solution was adminis-
tered orally starting on day 41 of life up to the end
of the experiment. This supplementation was
performed regardless of the food procedure after
recovery. Powdered oxoketoglutaric acid (AKG;
Protista AB, Sweden) of 99% purity was mixed with
distilled water to obtain a solution. The pH of these
stock solutions was buffered by the addition of
NaOH to a ﬁnal pH of 7.3.

To provide identical nutritional conditions during
the entire period of the study for all the investigated
groups, the pigs from the FXC group were fed ad
libitum and the remaining part of the feed was taken
from the feeder and weighed. The amount of feed
that was eaten by the FXC group of animals was reg-
istered and after that, the AKG and SHO groups
received exactly the same amount of feed. Thus,
identical nutritional conditions were provided during
the whole period of the study for the sham-operated
and fundectomized pigs.
Analysis of mechanical properties and geometry
Mechanical properties of femur and tibia were deter-
mined identically for all groups after 3 h of thawing at
room temperature using three-point bending test in
INSTRON 4302 apparatus (Instron, Canton, MA,
USA) linked with a computer, registering relationship
between forces perpendicular to the longitudinal axis
of the bone and the resulting displacement (Ferretti
et al., 1993a,b). The distance between supports was
set at 40% of total bone length. The measuring head
loaded bone samples with a constant speed of 10 mm/
min. The maximum elastic strength (Wy) and the ulti-
mate strength (Wf) of the bone were determined.
On the basis of measurements of horizontal and
vertical diameters of the mid-diaphyseal cross-section
of the bone, the cross-sectional area (A), the second
moment of inertia (Ix) and the mean relative wall
thickness (MRWT) were derived (Ferretti et al.,
1993a,b). Moreover, the cortical index (CI) was cal-
culated (Ferretti et al., 1993a,b).
Area bone mineral density and bone mineral content
Area bone mineral density (aBMD) and bone min-
eral content (BMC) for the femur were determined
using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA)
method and Norland XR-46 apparatus and Research
Scan software (Norland, Fort Atkinson, WI, USA).
The DXA scans were obtained by standard proce-
dures supplied by the manufacturer for scanning and
analysis. A daily Quality Control scan was taken to
ensure precision. This scan was required to meet a
standard coefﬁcient of variation.
Serum concentrations of growth hormone, insulin-
like growth factor-1, leptin, insulin, bone alkaline
phosphatase and osteocalcin
Blood samples were carefully collected for serum
using standard venipuncture of the subclavian vein
to avoid haemolysis. After blood clotting, the
serum was immediately separated by centrifuga-
tion. The serum concentration of growth hormone
(GH) was determined with the use of Porcine GH
enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay with intra-
and inter-assay coefﬁcients of variance 3.8% and
8.1% respectively (Diagnostic System Laboratories,
Webster, TX, USA). The serum level of insulin-like
growth factor-1 (IGF-1) was determined with the
use of the commercial immunoenzymometric assay
(IEMA) for the quantitative determination of IGF-1
in serum or plasma with intra- and inter-assay
coefﬁcients of variance 5.1% and 9.3% respectively
(OCTEIA IGF-1, Immunodiagnostic Systems, Bol-
don, UK). The serum concentration of leptin was
determined with the use of Porcine Leptin assay
with intra- and inter-assay coefﬁcients of variance
2.9% and 6.6% respectively (Diagnostic System
Laboratories). The serum concentration of insulin
was determined with the use of Porcine Insulin
ELISA kit with intra- and inter-assay coefﬁcients of
variance 3.5% and 7.1% respectively (Mercodia,
Uppsala, Sweden). The markers of bone metabo-
lism were determined with the use of the commer-
cial kits for BAP and osteocalcin. Bone-speciﬁc
alkaline phosphatase activity in serum of piglets
was performed with the use of BAP enzyme
immunoassay recommended for use in pigs (ME-
TRA BAP EIA Kit, Quidel Corp., San Diego, CA,
USA) with intra- and inter-assay coefﬁcients of
variance 6.2% and 8.3% respectively. Osteocalcin
was determined with the use of GenWay hOST-
EASIA Kit from GenWay Biotech, with intra- and
inter-assay coefﬁcients of variance 4.7% and 3.5%
respectively (GenWay Biotech, 6777 Nancy Ridge
Drive, San Diego, CA 92121, USA).
These results were obtained with the use of
Benchmark Plus microplate spectrophotometer
supplied with the Microplate Manager Software
Version 5.2.1 (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA,
USA).
Statistical analysis
All data are presented as mean ± SEM. Differences
between all the investigated groups were tested for
statistical signiﬁcance with the use of one-way
analysis of variance and post hoc Turkey’s and
Fischer’s tests. All data were found to be normally
distributed according to the Kolmogorov–Smirnov
test and to have equal variance. Differences
showing p £ 0.05 were considered statistically
signiﬁcant.

Results
Body weights
The lowest body weight of the pigs at 8 months of
age was reached in the FXC animals (37 ± 7 kg),
and that value was signiﬁcantly lower than the val-
ues of 63 ± 3 and 111 ± 5 kg obtained in the AKG
and SHO groups respectively (both p < 0.01). More-
over, statistically signiﬁcant differences were noted
between 2-oxoglutaric acid-treated and SHO animals
(p < 0.01).
Morphology, bone mineral density, bone mineral con-
tent, biomechanical properties and geometry of bones
The weight of the femur in the FXC pigs reached the
values of 169.4 ± 17.5 g, which was lower by
40.6%, when compared with the values obtained in
the SHO and 2-oxoglutaric acid-treated animals
(285.4 ± 12.2 and 239.5 ± 3.1 g, respectively,
p < 0.001). The weight of tibia in 2-oxoglutaric acid-
treated pigs reached 143.3 ± 2.3 g and this value
was signiﬁcantly higher than in the FXC group
reaching 102.8 ± 10.7 g (p < 0.01), whereas no sig-
niﬁcant differences were noted between the AKG
and SHO groups (172.7 ± 1.6 g). Fundectomy low-
ered the length of bones (Tables 2 and 3). Fundecto-
my lowered signiﬁcantly BMD, BMC, biomechanical
parameters and geometry of bones in pigs not
supplemented with 2-oxoglutaric acid. Moreover,
supplementation with 2-oxoglutaric acid signiﬁcantly
increased aBMD, BMC and geometry in fundectom-
ized pigs (Tables 2 and 3). Analysis of biomechanical
parameters such as Wy and Wf showed that fundec-
tomy strongly decreased mechanical properties of
bones when compared with the SHO animals. Fur-
thermore, treatment of the fundectomized pigs with
2-oxoglutaric acid signiﬁcantly increased all biome-
chanical parameters of the investigated bones when
compared with values obtained in the FXC group of
animals (p < 0.05; Tables 2 and 3). Parameters char-
acterizing aBMD, BMC, mechanical properties and
geometry of femur and tibia are presented in
Tables 2 and 3.

Fundectomy decreased signiﬁcantly the serum con-
centrations of GH, IGF-1, leptin, bone alkaline phos-
phatise (BAP) in animals not supplemented with 2-
oxoglutaric acid compared with animals supple-

in relation to fundectomy-induced osteopenia. The
results of this investigation showed that oral admin-
istration of 2-oxoglutaric acid during several months
increased the gain of bone mass in fundectomized
pigs compared with control fundectomized pigs,
where decreased gain of long bones followed malnu-
trition and lower body gain was observed. This study
also showed that oral 2-oxoglutaric acid administra-
tion signiﬁcantly improved bone mineralization, the
mechanical endurance of femur and tibia. This effect
was directly expressed by signiﬁcantly higher values
of the maximum elastic strength and the ultimate
strength. Moreover, improved mechanics of the
femur is the result of higher mineralization of the
bone after 2-oxoglutaric acid administration to fun-
dectomized animals compared with those without
supplementation. It is worth to stress that these posi-
tive effects of treatment with 2-oxoglutaric acid were
not inﬂuenced by differentiated body weight values
or sex of pigs because of the fact that animals were
weight- and sex-matched at the beginning of this
experiment in all the groups. Moreover, our ﬁndings
showed that surgical removal of the stomach fundus
in pigs induced signiﬁcant decrease in the body
weight gain, which was similar to the body weight
loss observed after gastrectomy in humans (Tovey
et al., 1992; Meyer, 1994; Zittel et al., 1997; Svedl-
und et al., 1999; Scho ¨lmerich, 2004). Furthermore,
the ﬁnal body weight between the groups of fundec-
tomized not-treated and 2-oxoglutaric acid-treated
pigs was signiﬁcantly different. It indicated higher
body weight gain of animals with partial resection of
stomach after AKG treatment.
Present study indicated that negative effect of sur-
gical fundectomy on the skeletal system leading to
lack of bone gain in pigs was associated with
decreased activity of the gastric–hypothalamic–pitui-
tary axis (Filipponi et al., 1990; Bernstein and Leslie,
2003). This result is supported by the analysis of
hormonal state of animals, which revealed that
fundectomy signiﬁcantly decreased serum concentra-
tions of leptin, GH and IGF-1. It should be stressed
that IGF-1, somatomedin mainly synthesized in the
liver and regulated by GH and diet, acts in an endo-
crine manner and exerts anabolic effects on the bone
tissue (Sugimoto et al., 1997; Cases et al., 2001;
Yamaguchi et al., 2006). Insulin-like growth factor-1
stimulates proliferation and differentiation of osteo-
blasts, enhances synthesis of collagen type I, and
increases the activity of BAP and osteocalcin produc-
tion.
It was not surprising that in this study lower
by approximately 50% concentration of IGF-1 con-
nected with lower bone mass of fundectomized ani-
mals compared with shame operated pigs was
observed. The treatment of fundectomized pigs with
2-oxoglutaric acid increased IGF-1 concentration by
approximately 30% compared with the FXC group,
although this concentration was still lower (by
approximately 30%) compared with the SHO group.
Moreover, observed positive effects of 2-oxoglutaric
acid administration on bone metabolism in fundec-
tomized pigs connected with increased serum
concentration of IGF-1 indicated one of the impor-
tant mechanisms responsible for bone metabolism.
The maintenance of bone mass is associated with
hormonal mechanism. Among the many hormones
that might account for the link between whole-body
energy and bone metabolism is leptin, the product of
the ob gene, secreted from white adipocytes.
Although adipose tissue is the main source of leptin,
it is also produced by the stomach and plays a role
in the short-term regulation of feeding. In humans,
and probably in animals, gastric leptin is released in
response to food. Moreover, leptin plays the main
role in gastroprotection against acute lesions (Bado
et al., 1998; Cinti et al., 2000, 2001; Ptak-Belowska
et al., 2008). Leptin as a gut-brain peptide power-
fully stimulates appetite and GH secretion, directly
regulates proliferation, differentiation and mineral-
ization, and induces prolonged lifespan of human
primary osteoblasts by inhibiting apoptosis (Cornish
et al., 2002; Gordeladze et al., 2002; Ju ¨ rima¨e and
Ju¨ rima¨e, 2006). Little is known about the effects of
fundectomy in pigs on leptin and bone turnover
after supplementation with 2-oxoglutaric acid, as

of leptin synthesis and gastrectomy patients are deﬁ-

(Clowes et al., 2005). In rodents and humans, leptin
gene expression is under complex endocrine and
metabolic control. However, leptin production can
also be stimulated by several hormones such as insu-
lin and GH (Cases et al., 2001; Ju¨ rima¨e and Ju¨ rima¨e,
2006). In fact, leptin demonstrated both central and
peripheral effects thereby inﬂuencing peripheral
metabolism. Leptin may demonstrate acute inhibi-
tory effect on the secretion of insulin as well (Cases
et al., 2001).
This study indicated that conducted fundectomy
resulted in decreased concentration of leptin and
probably it was the work involved in direct stimu-
latory bone-controlling mechanisms leading to
lower gain and lower bone mass in pigs. This
study also showed that lowered by approximately
80% leptin concentration was positively associated
with fundectomy-induced lower concentrations of
GH, lower ﬁnal body weight and lower bone mass
in pigs at the age of 8 months, and showed that
fundectomy-induced decreased leptin concentration
is connected with enhanced by approximately 55%
insulin secretion. It is possible that in some condi-
tions, insulin itself exerts a direct effect on the
general growth. On the other hand, insulin can
also regulate the IGF axis. In this study, signiﬁcant
differences in investigated hormones concentrations
such as insulin, GH, IGF-1 and leptin between the
two pattern of the treatment of animals (fundec-
tomized 2-oxoglutaric acid-treated and not treated)
were observed. Fundectomized pigs treated with 2-
oxoglutaric acid had higher bone mineral density
indicating that the administration with 2-oxoglutar-
ic acid showed a protective effect in this aspect
and probably there was a link between bones and
stomach. In response to oral administration of 2-
oxoglutaric acid, there was observed increase in
the activity of BAP, although there was only a
tendency to increase the level of osteocalcin com-
pared with sham-operated and not treated with 2-
oxoglutaric acid groups of animals. However, 2-
oxoglutaric acid may improve metabolism of the
whole organism including protein metabolism, and
for this reason, it showed protective effects before
lower body gain and decreased bone mass, that
were observed after fundectomy in pigs not treated
with 2-oxoglutaric acid.
It should be stressed that 2-oxoglutaric acid is a
factor, which positively inﬂuences the growth of
the whole organism. It is a precursor of not only
glutamine, but also of other amino acids, such as
arginine and asparagine involved in the synthesis of
protein and proline and hydroxyproline, the main
amino acids of collagen (Harrison and Pierzynowski,
2008). Moreover, the earlier study showed that AKG
given together with dexamethasone improved
weight gain of piglets, which had higher mean body
weight compared with piglets under the inﬂuence of
dexamethasone alone (S´liwa, 2006).
This study showed that fundectomy in pigs
induced osteopenic changes in long bones similar to
those observed both, in humans and animals after
gastrectomy (Lane, 2001). These results showed that
the fundectomy-evoked osteopenia of bones in pigs
is mediated by decreased IGF-1 synthesis, which
may be the effect of undernutrition, which follows
the resection of the part of stomach and the distur-
bance of the gastric–hypothalamic–pituitary axis
function. However, this study raised the question,
whether metabolic factors regulating the synthesis of
hormone involving bone metabolism may be
effective in the prevention of fundectomy-induced
osteopenia. Moreover, these observations may help
to improve the quality of life of people and animals
suffering from functional deﬁcits after gastric surgery
as well; however, further studies in this ﬁeld also for
economic and social cost of treatment are needed.
Moreover, it seems that nowadays, the economic
cost of osteoporosis in general will rise, and on the
other hand, public health will never eliminate
human diseases with the desire for improvement of
quality of life.
There are several limitations to this present study.
First, a total of 18 pigs were used for comparison in
the study. Second, there were only male piglets
enrolled. Sex steroid levels have been reported to
change signiﬁcantly in both males and females after
the surgery. It was not possible to explore the rela-
tionship between sex steroid levels, changes in bone
mineral and markers of bone turnover because of
only one sex used in this study. Third, the shame
operated group had opened only abdomen without
cutting of the stomach.
This investigation has also its advantages. This
was the ﬁrst study that examines long-term effects
of supplementation of 2-oxoglutaric acid in pigs
undergoing fundectomy. About a few months after
surgery, there was found signiﬁcant increase in
biochemical markers of bone metabolism in pigs sup-
plemented with 2-oxoglutaric acid. In addition, there
was smaller potential for lowered bone mass to occur
in pigs not treated with 2-oxoglutaric acid, because
they orally received calcium as a placebo after the

In conclusion, the factors regulating the integra-
tion of energy and bone metabolism are multifactor
and are likely to exhibit signiﬁcant redundancy.
Therefore, it is possible that pig model of fundecto-
my as well as complex human studies may be neces-
sary to elucidate the full impact of supplementation
of 2-oxoglutaric acid and the enteric hormones on
bone physiology. It is clear that there is a signiﬁcant
amount to learn about the impact of nutrients on
bone health and the factors regulating these
responses. Progress in this ﬁeld will require a better
deﬁnition of each of the local and systemic hor-
monal and structural components that separately
and together determine all bone properties.
