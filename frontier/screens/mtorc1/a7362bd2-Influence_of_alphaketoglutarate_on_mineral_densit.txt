INFLUENCE OF ALPHA-KETOGLUTARATE  
ON MINERAL DENSITY AND GEOMETRICAL  
AND MECHANICAL PARAMETERS OF FEMORA  
DURING POSTNATAL LIFE IN PIGLETS

Abstract 
 
The purpose of this stud y was to determine the 
influence of daily administration of alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) 
on mineral density and geometrical, mechanical and 
morphometrical parameters of the femur during postnatal life in 
piglets. The experiment was conducted from the 1st d of neonatal 
life to the 70 th d of postnatal life. Experimental piglets were 
given AKG in the dosage of 0.4 g/kg b.w./d per os, while control 
piglets were treated per os  with physiological saline in the 
dosage of 2 ml/kg b.w./ d. The piglets from both groups were 
divided into 6 age subgroups, namely 3, 14, 21, 35, 56 and 70 d. 
At the end of the experiment the animals were euthanised and 
right femora were isolated and frozen at –25 oC until further 
analyses. Using quantitative computed tomography (QCT) 
method, volumetric bone density of the cortical bone 
compartment was estimated. Moreover, geometrical, mechanical 
and morphometrical parameters of the bones were determined. 
The obtained results indicate that daily administration of AKG 
increased the cortical bone density, geometrical and mechanical 
properties of the femur and whole bone morphometry. 
 
Key words: piglets, alpha-ketoglutarate, 
femur, tomography.  
 
 
Bone growth and development are the effect of 
complex interactions between genetic and environmental 
factors, including diet, hormonal influences, and 
mechanical stimuli (7, 8). A better understanding of 
these relationships is very important and necessary for 
several reasons, including the identification of bone 
diseases and, in consequence, fracture risk not only in 
animals but also in children. Bone is a bifunctional 
organ with primarily a structural role, locomotion and 
gravitation resistance, and simultaneously serves as a 
mineral reservoir (13). Approximately 60% of adult 
bone mass is gained during pubertal growth. The 
mechanisms that regulate bone formation are complex 
and involve multiple cellular interactions that coordinate 
resorption of an existing bone and formation of a new 
bone. The gastro-intestinal tract (GIT) is the largest 
endocrine organ in the body due to its different kinds of 
cells producing peptide-like hormones of dissimilar 
physiological functions and importance. Some of 
biological functions of the majority of GIT cells still 
remain unrecognized not only in the field of peptide-like 
GIT hormones but some nutrients and metabolites as 
well. To these metabolites belongs alpha-ketoglutaric 
acid, a molecule with a centr al role via the Krebs cycle 
in systemic, intestinal, and gut bacteria metabolism (6). 
It was recently revealed that enteral supplementation of 
alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) increased not only the portal 
net appearance of proline but the arterial plasma proline 
concentration in growing pigs as well (5). Moreover, our 
earlier studies indicated that there is a positive influence 
of enteral AKG administration on skeletal system 
quality in piglets, whereas no effect was observed when 
AKG was administered parenterally (3, 4, 9). This 
indicates that the gastro-intes tinal tract is the source of 
proline, which is an indispensable amino-acid for 
connective tissues and bone co llagen synthesis, mainly 
type I, in the development of the skeletal system.  
To define the effect of alpha-ketoglutarate 
enteral administration on sk eletal system properties 
during the first 10 weeks of life of piglets, the 
mineralization, geometrical, morphological, and 
mechanical properties of the femur were evaluated.

Material and Methods 
 
Experimental design and sampling 
procedure. The experiment was carried out on 120 
piglets of Large Polish White breed. The animals were 
housed in piggery under standard rearing conditions 
(temperature and humidity) with constant access to fresh 
water. After weaning, the piglets were fed standard 
commercial diets, twice a day at 7.00 a.m. and 3.00 p.m. 
The piglets were divided into two main groups: the 
experimental and control on e with 60 animals in each 
group. Both groups were divided additionally into 6 age 
subgroups, namely 3, 14, 21, 35, 56 and 70 d of life. The 
experimental procedure was conducted from the 1 st d of 
neonatal life to the 70 th d of postnatal life. The 
experimental piglets were treated per os  with buffered 
alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) solution in the dosage of 0.4 
g/kg b.w./d. At the same time, the piglets from the 
control group were given per os  physiological saline 
(PhS) in dosage of 2.0 ml/kg b.w./d. The body weight of 
the individual piglets was measured every day. After the 
set up periods the animals from both groups were 
euthanised with intravenous injection of lethal doses of 
pentobarbitalum natrium  (Morbital; Biowet Pu ławy, 
Poland) and their right femora were isolated. The bones 
w e r e  s t o r e d  a t  – 2 5oC until analyses were performed. 
During the tests, they were kept at room temperature.  
Analysis of geometrical and mechanical 
parameters. The geometrical parameters such as cross 
sectional area (A), second moment of inertia (Ix) and 
mean relative wall thickness (MRWT) were examined 
through the measurem ent of the horizontal and vertical 
diameter of the femur according to Ferretti’s and co-
workers method (1, 2). These measurements were 
carried out exactly in the middle of the length of the 
bone. Mechanical properties of the investigated bones 
were estimated using three-point bending test, according 
to Ferretti`s and co-workers method in INSTRON 4302 
apparatus linked with a computer, registering the 
relationship between force acting perpendicularly to 
length of bone and resulting in displacement (1, 2). In 
this apparatus the bones were placed on two supports 
distanced at 40% of the total length of the investigated 
bone. The results of this mechanical analysis were 
presented graphically and the maximum elastic strength 
was estimated. 
Bone mineral density. After measuring the 
length of the femur, volumetric bone mineral density 
was measured with quantitative computed tomography 
(QCT) method and SOMATOM AR. T – SIEMENS 
apparatus supplied with VR 3 software, as prescribed 
previously (12, 13). Bone density was measured for 
cortical bone using 2 mm thick, cross sectional QCT 
scans in the middle of diaphysis of the bone. Cortical 
bone mineral density was calculated by automatic 
computation. 
Analysis of bone morphometry. The bone 
morphometrical parameters, such as Weight/Length 
Index (WLI) and Bone Robusticity Index (BRI) were 
determined by measurement of the total weight and 
length of the femur and calculated by following 
equations WLI = weight/length and BRI = 
length/3√weight.  
Statistical analysis. All data are presented as a 
mean ± standard error (±S.E.).  Statistical analyses were 
performed using CORE program. The Student’s t-test 
was used to determine statistical significance of 
differences in variables between the investigated 
parameters. The level of statistic significance was set at 
P≤0.05 for all comparisons. 
 
Results  
 
The mean length of the femur presented similar 
values in both experimental and control groups during the 
whole experimental period with the exception of 35 d of 
life. On that period the length of the femur in AKG group 
was significantly higher (91.6 ± 2.16 mm) than in animals 
of the control group (84.1 ± 2.26 mm) (Tables 1 and 2). 
The mean values of the cross-sectional area the femur were 
significantly increased in the experimental group at the age 
of 35 and 56 d of life and reached 74.23 ± 3.66 mm 2 and 
121.27 ± 8.15 mm 2, respectively. Moreover, the mean 
values of the second moment of inertia were significantly 
higher in these subgroups after AKG administration 
(885.83 ±84.4 mm 4 and 2375.22 ± 209.49 mm 4). No 
significant differences between AKG-treated and control 
animals were found during the whole experimental period 
in mean relative wall thickness value (MRWT). However, 
MRWT tended to lower values at the age of 3, 21, 35 and 
56 d in AKG-treated piglets (Tables 1 and 2). Except for 21 
d of life, the mean values of the maximum elastic strength 
of the femur were increased after the treatment with AKG 
and statistically significant differences were noted in piglets 
at the age of 56 and 70 d of postnatal life (Tables 1 and 2). 
Bone mineral density (BMD revealed significant 
differences between the experimental and control group at 
the 56th and 70th d of life and the cortical mineral density at 
the femoral midshaft increased significantly after AKG 
administration to 2.04 ± 0.02 g/cm3 and 2.09 ± 0.04 g/cm3, 
respectively (Table 2). The mean values of bone 
morphometrical parameters are shown in Tables 1 and 2. 
The obtained results showed that the values of WLI in 
experimental piglets were significantly higher at the 35 th 
and 56th d of postnatal life (P ≤ 0.01). On the other hand, 
the BRI showed the tendency to lower values in piglets that 
received AKG, when compared to the controls with the 
significant difference at the age of 56 d of life (P ≤ 0.01). 
 
 
Discussion  
 
The main goal of the undertaken study was to 
determine the influence of  alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) 
administered orally on bone metabolism of piglets 
(neonates and post-weaned) by measuring selected bone 
parameters. Regarding their intensive growth, piglets 
serve as an attractive model for the investigation on 
nutritional, hormonal and even toxicological factors 
influencing the growth, maturation and mineralization of 
the bones during the neonatal and postnatal life.

However, the pattern of developmental features 
of the skeletal system in pi glets was not exactly assayed 
up to now. Our previous and current studies and 
obtained results provide some basic information on the 
developmental changes in the skeletal system that were 
investigated on different bone models (3, 4, 9). 
Considering the physiological aspects, it is necessary to 
underline that piglets are treated from the experimental 
point of view as the closest model to babies in various 
(or some) systems and organs, and, among them, in 
skeletal development and maturation (10). The genetic 
selection of pig towards maximum intensive production 
and increased body weight has created a predisposition 
to many disorders in their skeletal system (11). Leg 
deformities and bone weakness and fractures disturb 
animal welfare and cause serious economic losses due to 
following infections, decreased final body weight and 
lower carcass quality. The obtained results not only 
document different phases of  the neonatal and postnatal 
development of the skeletal system but precisely show 
how oral administration of AKG accelerates and 
improves essential mechanical and geometrical 
parameters that are conn ected with higher bone 
mineralization. The data of the geometrical and 
mechanical parameters of the femur present proofs that 
earlier maturation of the bones of piglets may be 
determined by AKG. The obtained results show that WLI is 
closely associated with the cross sectional area and the 
second moment of inertia and the maximum elastic strength 
of the femur. The investigation performed in humans (14) 
and birds (12, 13) showed positive influence of AKG on 
skeletal system quality as well. Perhaps, AKG as a 
glutamine derivative may be applied in the future as a 
factor improving the growth, development and 
maturation of the skeletal system, not only in piglets but 
in other animal species.  
The results of this study provide a new 
approach to studying the influence of enterally 
administered AKG on skelet al system development and 
mineralization in piglets during postnatal growth. This 
study does not provide any explanation of the 
metabolism and mechanism of AKG action in piglets but 
it shows marked influence on femur mechanical and 
geometrical parameters during the period of 70 days of 
postnatal life. These effects of enteral AKG 
administration may be mediated by metabolism of AKG 
in the stomach or in other parts of GIT, leading to higher 
synthesis and supply of proline, which is so important 
together with glicyne for collagen formation in bones 
(5). However, AKG may increase absorption of other 
metabolites, which influence the processes of growth 
and mineralization of the skeletal system. Further studies 
are needed for better understanding the metabolism of 
exogenous AKG, administered enterally at the time of 
postnatal development. The results of this study indicate 
that there is a possibility of existence of a gut-bone axis, 
mediated by enteral AKG as a nutrient or precursor of 
proline, influencing the growth and mineralization of the 
bones. Moreover, the femur may be used as a suitable 
model of the bone to follow the developmental changes 
in the mechanical and geometrical parameters during the 
postnatal life in the pig.
