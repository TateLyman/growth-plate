Prenatal programming of skeletal development in the offspring: Effects of
maternal treatment with β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate (HMB) on femur
properties in pigs at slaughter age
Marcin R. Tatara a,⁎, Ewa Śliwa a, Witold Krupski b
a Department of Animal Physiology, Faculty of V eterinary Medicine, The Agricultural University of Lublin, ul. Akademicka 12, 20-950 Lublin, Poland
b II Department of Radiology, Medical University of Lublin, 20-081 Lublin, Poland
Received 14 October 2006; revised 14 February 2007; accepted 15 February 2007
Available online 1 March 2007
Abstract
Alteration in fetal growth and development in response to prenatal environmental conditions such as nutrition has long-term or permanent
effects during postnatal life. The aim of this study was to investigate effects of β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate (HMB) treatment of sows during the
last 2 weeks of pregnancy on programming of skeletal development in the offspring. The study was performed on 141 pigs born by 12 sows of
Polish Landrace breed. Two weeks before delivery, pregnant sows were divided into two groups. The first group consisted of control sows ( N =6 )
that were treated with placebo. Sows that were orally treated with β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate (N = 6) at the dosage of 0.05 g/kg of body weight
per day belonged to the second group. Newborn piglets were weighed and subjected to blood collection for determination of serum levels of
growth hormone (GH), insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), insulin, leptin, glucose and bone alkaline phosphatase (BAP) activity and lipid
profile. At the age of 6 months, the piglets were slaughtered, their femur was isolated for analysis and assessment of lean meat content of carcasses
was performed. The effects of maternal administration with HMB on skeletal properties in the offspring were evaluated in relation to bone mineral
density and geometrical and mechanical properties. Maternal treatment with HMB increased serum levels of GH, IGF-1 and BAP activity in the
newborns by 38.0%, 20.0% and 26.0%, respectively ( P < 0.01). HMB administration significantly increased volumetric bone mineral density of
the trabecular and cortical bone of femur in the offspring at the age of 6 months ( P < 0.001). The weight of femur and geometrical parameters such
as cross-sectional area, second moment of inertia, mean relative wall thickness and cortical index were significantly increased after HMB
treatment (P < 0.05). HMB induced higher values of maximum elastic strength and ultimate strength of femur ( P < 0.01). Furthermore, lean meat
content of carcass was significantly increased in the females born by HMB-treated sows ( P < 0.05). The obtained results showed that maternal
administration with HMB has positive long-term effects on bone tissue and improves volumetric bone mineral density, geometrical and
mechanical properties of femur in the offspring. These effects were connected with increased level of GH and IGF-1 in the newborns indicating
involvement of improved somatotrophic axis function in prenatal programming of skeletal development in pigs.

Introduction
Epidemiological studies in man have shown that impaired
intrauterine growth is associated with increased incidence of
metabolic, cardiovascular and endocrine diseases in later life.
Low birth weight has been linked to hypertension, glucose
intolerance, insulin resistance, type II diabetes, dyslipidemia,
obesity, reproductive disorders, decreased bone mass and an
increased risk of fractures in adult life. These associations have
been reported in populations of various age, sex and ethnic
origin and occur independently of current weight and exercise
level [1–5]. Nutrition is the major intrauterine environmental
factor that may alter cell numbers and expression of the fetal
genome leading to lifelong consequences. This phenomenon,
known as “fetal programming, ” has led to the recent theory of
fetal origins of adult diseases. Alterations in fetal nutrition and
endocrine status may result in developmental adaptations that

of the offspring, thereby predisposing individuals to metabolic,
endocrine and cardiovascular diseases in adult life [6]. Main
nutritional factors that may contribute to bone accretion in
infants and toddlers include maternal nutritional status during
pregnancy, type of infant feeding and calcium and phosphorus
content of infant formula. Maternal vitamin D deficiency during
pregnancy is associated with disturbances in neonatal calcium
homeostasis, and maternal calcium deficiency leads to reduced
neonatal bone mineral content (BMC). Furthermore, preterm
infants are at an increased risk of osteopenia and the reason for
their long-term bone deficiency is not clear [7]. Thus, the
metabolic bone diseases occurring in adult life may also result
from impaired nutrition during prenatal life [3]. Even though
reports showing long-term effects of prenatal or early neonatal
nutritional influences on bone health later in life are strongly
limited, it could be hypothesized that improved maternal
nutrition during intrauterine growth of the fetus may have
beneficial effects for bone mass accretion and skeletal system
development and its proper function throughout postnatal life.
β-Hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate (HMB) is a metabolite of
essential amino acid leucine. HMB is produced through
metabolism of alpha-ketoisocaproate (KIC), which is oxidized
by alpha-ketoisocaproate dioxygenase to produce free HMB
within the cell cytosol. Approximately 5% of leucine metabolism
leads to endogenous synthesis of HMB that is converted to
β-hydroxy-β-methylglutaryl coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) in some
tissues and serves as key carbon source for de novo cholesterol
synthesis in tissues, which is necessary to maintain maximal cell
function [8,9]. Studies with pregnant rats and their offspring
exposed to experimental diet during prenatal life in utero have
shown nutritional ability to induce permanent changes (program)
of the activity of HMG-CoA reductase—the enzyme responsible
for conversion of HMG-CoA into mevalonate and following
cholesterol synthesis. The effects of nutritional alteration on fetal
programming of the enzyme activity were observed in the
offspring during postnatal life [8,10]. Physiological plasma
concentration of HMB in pigs ranges from 1 to 4 μmol and
increases 5- to 10-fold after leucine administration [11]. HMB
treatment in humans subjected to exercise resulted in increased
muscle mass accretion that was associated with inhibition of
muscle proteolysis [12]. Investigations on rats have shown that
dietary supplementation with HMB results in enhanced wound
collagen deposition. Similar results were obtained in other
studies on humans, where HMB administration with arginine and
glutamine significantly enhanced collagen deposition in poly-
tetrafluoroethylene tubes —which was expressed by elevated
hydroxyproline content increasing wounds repair processes[13].
These data suggest that beneficial effects of HMB administration
may also occur in bones, especially considering essential
contribution of hydroxyproline to proper formation of collagen
fibers in bone tissue [14,15].
The present study was performed to test the hypothesis that
HMB treatment of pregnant sows during the two last weeks of
gestation may positively influence development of the skeletal
system of the offspring with beneficial consequences for later
life. The effects of 2 weeks long prenatal treatment with HMB
on serum levels of glucose, insulin, insulin-like growth factor-1
(IGF-1), growth hormone (GH), leptin and serum lipid profile in
the newborn piglets were determined. Measurement of bone-
specific alkaline phosphatase (BAP) activity in the serum was
performed as an indicator of osteoblastic activity. Skeletal
system properties in pigs were investigated at the age of 6
months by determination of the volumetric bone mineral
density, geometrical parameters and mechanical endurance of
femur. Furthermore, assessment of lean meat content of carcass
was performed.
Materials and methods
The experimental procedures used throughout this study were approved by
The Local Ethics Committee on Animal Experimentation of The Agricultural
University of Lublin, Poland.
Animals and experimental design
The study was performed on 141 pigs born by 12 sows of Polish Landrace
(PL) breed. The experimental pigs were sired by the same boar with genetic
potential for increased cross-sectional area of muscles and reduced back fat
content. Two weeks before delivery, the pregnant sows were divided into two
equal groups that were weight- and age-matched. In both groups the differences
of mating between matched sows were not longer than 7 days. The first group
consisted of sows ( N = 6) that were treated per os with placebo (CaCO 3
dissolved in saline at the daily dosage of 0.05 g/kg of body weight) until the
partum. Sows (N = 6) that were treated per os with calcium salt of β-hydroxy-β-
methylbutyrate (CaHMB dissolved in saline; Lonza, Switzerland) at the dosage
of 0.05 g/kg of body weight per day belonged to the second group. Both the
placebo and HMB were administered to sows during the morning meal with the
first portion of the food. During the whole period of pregnancy and lactation, all
sows were fed twice a day at 08:00 a.m. and 16:00 p.m. with well balanced diet
that was supplied in equal doses for all animals, all the sows were kept under
identical environmental conditions. The piglets obtained from sows as the result
of physiological partum were assigned to the control group ( N = 70) or HMB
group (N = 71), according to the treatment of their mothers. The body weights of
the newborn piglets were determined after the partum and blood samples were
immediately taken from subclavian vein of unsuckled piglets for further
analysis. After blood collection, the piglets were allowed to suckle. The piglets
from the control and HMB groups were kept with sows until the weaning at
4 weeks of life. At the age of 3 days of life male piglets were castrated.
Furthermore, during the whole period of the study, the sows and piglets from
both groups received identical diet, according to the stage of the production
cycle ( Table 1 ) and were reared together to provide identical environmental
conditions. Seven piglets from each group died before the study was finished,
mainly due to squeezing by mothers or stress related to blood collection. At the
age of approximately 6 months, the offspring obtained from placebo- and HMB-
treated sows were slaughtered under license at slaughterhouse (Constar S.A.,
Starachowice, Poland).
Biochemical analyses of serum in newborn piglets
Blood samples from unsuckled piglets were centrifuged after clotting and
serum samples were stored at − 25 °C for further analysis. Growth hormone level
in serum of newborns was assessed with the use of Porcine Growth Hormone
Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbant Assay (ELISA; Diagnostic Systems Labora-
tories, Inc., Webster, TX, USA). Intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variance
were 3.8 and 8.1%, respectively. Quantitative measurement of insulin in serum
was performed by ELISA method (Porcine Insulin ELISA, Mercodia, Uppsala,
Sweden). Intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variance were 3.5 and 7.1%,
respectively. Serum level of insulin-like growth factor-1 was determined using
the commercial immunoenzymometric assay (IEMA) for the quantitative
determination of IGF-1 in serum or plasma (OCTEIA IGF-1, Immunodiagnostic
Systems Ltd., Boldon, Tyne and Wear, UK). Intra- and inter-assay coefficients of

Bone analysis
Right femur was isolated 24 h after slaughter from males ( N = 63) and
females (N = 64) and cleaned from remaining soft tissues. Bone length and bone
weight were measured and bone samples were subsequently frozen at −25 °C
until further analyses. Using quantitative computed tomography (QCT) method
and SOMA TOM EMOTION SIEMENS ap paratus (Siemens, Erlangen,
Germany) equipped with Somaris/5 VB10B software, the volumetric bone
mineral density (vBMD) was determined. The volumetric bone mineral density
was measured for both the trabecular and cortical bone with the use of 2-mm
thick, cross-sectional, metaphyseal and diaphyseal QCT scans, respectively. The
trabecular bone mineral density (Td) was assessed on measuring scan placed at
18.5% of total femur length, proximal to the distal growth plate. The cortical
bone mineral density (Cd) was determined from the scan of diaphysis at 52% of
femur length, measuring from the distal bone extremity. All measurements were
carried out by one radiologist blinded to group affiliation.
Mechanical properties of the femur were determined identically for both
groups after 4 h thawing at room temperature using three-point bending test in
INSTRON 4302 apparatus (Instron, Canton, MA, USA) linked with a computer,
registering relationship between forces perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of
the bone and the resulting displacement [17,18]. Results were presented
graphically, and the values of maximum elastic strength (Wy) and ultimate
strength (Wf) of the femur were determined. The distance between bone
supports was set at 40% of total femur length and the measuring head loaded
bone samples with a constant speed of 20 mm/min.
Geometrical properties of the femur were estimated on the basis of
measurements of horizontal and vertical diameters of the mid-diaphyseal cross-
section of this bone. The values of the cross-sectional area (A), the second
moment of inertia (Ix), the mean relative wall thickness (MRWT) and the
cortical index (CI) were estimated as described previously [17–19].
Analysis of lean meat content of carcass
The assessment of lean meat content of carcass was performed accordingly
with the SEUROP grading method [20] with the use of Capteur Gras/Maigre-
Sydel (CGM) apparatus equipped with high-definition probe 8 mm in diameter,
a light-emitting infrared diode and two light sensors (Sydel, Lorient, France).
The results of the measurement were converted automatically into estimated lean
meat content by the CGM apparatus. The lean meat content of carcass was
calculated using the following formula:
Y ¼ 50:11930 /C0 0:62421X1 þ 0:26979X2
where:
Y — the estimated percentage of lean meat in the carcass,
X1 — the thickness of the fat (including rind), between the third and fourth
last ribs (11th or 12th intercostal space) at 6 cm of the dorsal midline, at a
trajectory perpendicular to the rind (in mm),
X2 — the thickness of the Longisimus dorsi muscle (in mm), measured at the
X1 position.
Statistical analysis
All data are presented as means ± SEM. All data were found to be normally
distributed in accordance with Kolomogorov –Smirnov test and have equal
variance. Differences between means were tested for statistical significance with
the use of two-way ANOV A. Post hoc comparisons of the differences were
performed using Tukey's test. The results of hormonal and biochemical analysis
of serum in the newborns were tested with the use of non-paired Student's t test.
Differences showing P-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.
Results
Body weights
At birth, male and female piglets born by sows treated with
HMB had body weights of 1.60 ± 0.04 kg and 1.64 ± 0.04 kg
which were significantly different from the values of 1.32 ±
0.04 kg and 1.30 ± 0.03 g obtained in the controls, respectively
(P < 0.001). The final body weights obtained at mean age of
185 days in the males and females from HMB group reached
mean values of 105.1 ± 1.73 kg and 104.3 ± 2.24 kg which were
not statistically different from the values of 104.1 ± 1.53 kg and
103.6 ± 1.83 kg obtained in the control pigs slaughtered at a
mean age of 196 days of life. The difference of age in pigs from
HMB and control groups was statistically significant
(P = 0.006). The growth rate from birth to slaughter was 559 ±
9.8 g and 555 ± 9.1 g per day in males and females from the
HMB group which was significantly different from the values of
524 ± 8.3 g and 521 ± 10.1 g per day attained by the controls,
respectively ( P < 0.05).
Hormone concentrations and bone-specific alkaline phospha-
tase activity in serum of newborn piglets
The mean serum GH concentration in the piglets from con-
trol and HMB groups reached the values of 23.4 ± 2.3 ng/mL
and 32.3 ± 2.1 ng/mL, respectively ( P = 0.01). HMB treatment
of pregnant sows for the last 14 days before the partum
significantly increased serum concentrations of IGF-1 in the
offspring at birth (72.0 ± 2.4 μg/L) when compared to the
control piglets, where this value reached 60.2 ± 1.4 μg/L
(P < 0.001). The mean serum insulin and leptin concentra-
tions for control piglets were measured to be 49 ± 0.6 μg/mL
and 97 ± 28 ng/L —that was not different from the values of
51 ± 1.0 μg/mL ( P = 0.10) and 86 ± 13 ng/L ( P =0 . 7 3 )
obtained in HMB group, respectively. Furthermore, treatment
of pregnant sows with β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate had a
significant effect on serum BAP activity in the newborn piglets
from HMB group when compared to the controls and the values
of 26.9 ± 1.2 U/L and 21.4 ± 1.1 U/L were measured, respectively
(P = 0.006).
Lipid profile and glucose concentration in serum of the
newborns
Results of triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL-cholesterol,
HDL-cholesterol and glucose concentrations in serum of
newborn piglets are presented in Table 2 . It was shown that
the piglets from the HMB group had significantly decreased
HDL-cholesterol level when compared to the piglets born by the
control sows ( P = 0.003).

V olumetric bone mineral density and mechanical and
geometrical properties of femur in pigs
Results of morphological properties, volumetric bone
mineral density in the trabecular and cortical bones, geometrical
parameters and mechanical endurance of femur in piglets from
the control and HMB groups are presented in Table 3 .
Morphological analysis of femur in pigs has shown significantly
lower values of bone length in piglets that were born by sows
administered with HMB during the last 2 weeks of pregnancy.
The differences of bone length in males and females between
the control and HMB groups reached 2.0% and 1.5%,
respectively ( P < 0.05). However, the weights of femur in
males and females were significantly increased in HMB group
when compared to the controls by 7.1% and 9.5%, respectively
(P < 0.05). Trabecular bone mineral density of femur in male
and female pigs was increased by 6.1% and 8.6%, respectively,
when compared to the controls (both P < 0.001). Similar results
were observed in the cortical bone density values; however, the
changes of this parameter reached 6.9% and 8.7%, respectively
(P < 0.001). The determination of geometrical parameters like
cross-sectional area, second moment of inertia, mean relative
wall thickness and cortical index of femur have shown higher
values in males and females from HMB group when compared
with the values obtained in the control pigs ( P < 0.05; Table 3).
Mechanical testing of femur revealed significant increase of
maximum elastic strength and ultimate strength values in the
females from HMB group when compared to the control
females by 31% and 17%, respectively ( P < 0.01). Furthermore,
the values of Wy and Wf of femur were significantly increased
in male pigs born by sows treated with HMB by 46% and 25%,
respectively ( P < 0.001; Table 3).
Lean meat content in carcass
Mean lean meat content in males and females from the
control group was 51.5 ± 0.4% and 52.0 ± 0.5%, while this
parameter reached the values of 51.0 ± 0.5% and 54.0 ± 0.5% in
the HMB group, respectively. Mean lean meat content in the
females from the HMB group was significantly increased when
compared to the males from this group ( P < 0.001). Further-
more, mean lean meat content was significantly increased in the
female pigs born by sows treated with HMB when compared to
the males and females from the control group ( P < 0.05).
Discussion
Bone strength itself is a function of bone mass, bone
architecture, bone geometry and ongoing repair of use-related
fatigue damage in the bone material [21]. Higher peak bone
mass (PBM) reached at skeletal maturity may lead to lower risk
of osteoporosis development in later life [22]. The bone mass of
an individual in adult life depends on PBM obtained during
skeletal growth and the subsequent rate of bone loss. Poor
growth during fetal life, infancy and childhood is associated
with decreased bone mass in adulthood and following increased
risk of osteoporotic fractures. Epidemiological studies suggest
that environmental factors such as maternal nutrition program
skeletal growth and PBM acquisition during intrauterine and
early postnatal life [4]. However, majority of the available
studies describing fetal programming of systemic development
and physiological functions of organisms have been related to
maternal undernutrition and its negative effects diagnosed in the
offspring during later life [4–6,23,24]. Commonly available
food stuffs are generally poor in β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate.
The highest amount of this substance was found in catfish,
grapefruit and alfalfa and reached 150, 123 and 25 μg/kg of the
product, respectively. Other food stuffs like cheese, orange,
salmon, red wine, corn and red meat consist of HMB in amounts
between 21 and 5 μg/kg of the product [25–27]. As a metabolite
of amino acid leucine, HMB is synthesized endogenously both
in humans and animals. Furthermore, common metabolic
pathway of endogenous HMB production has been shown to
exist in both humans and pigs [11,12,28,29]. Considering this
matter, dosage of HMB similar to those applied to humans was
used in the current study in sows.
This study has examined volumetric bone mineral density,
geometrical properties and mechanical endurance of femur
obtained from the offspring of sows maintained on normal or β-
hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate enriched diets throughout the last
2 weeks of pregnancy. The results of this study are new and, to
our knowledge so far, effects of β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate
on bone tissue metabolism and prenatal programming of
skeletal development have not been investigated in any other

The results of our investigation showed that oral adminis-
tration of HMB to pregnant sows during the last 2 weeks of
pregnancy induced improved development of skeletal system
and bone mass acquisition in the offspring. The beneficial
effects of the maternal administration with HMB were observed
in 6-month-old pigs as increased volumetric bone mineral
density of the trabecular and cortical bone compartments, as
well as improved geometrical and mechanical properties. In
spite of significantly different metabolic activity of the
trabecular and cortical bones, the increase of the volumetric
bone mineral density in both these bone compartments was
similar. In comparison to the controls, significantly increased
vBMD of the femur in HMB-treated pigs resulted in increased
bone weight in males and females, respectively. On the other
hand, improved bone geometrical properties have also con-
tributed to increased bone weight in the animals from the HMB
group. Cross-sectional area and second moment of inertia were
15.5% and 27.0% higher in males from the HMB group, while
the increase of these parameters in females reached 8.6% and
11.8%, respectively. The amount of bone tissue accumulated in
the diaphyseal walls, expressed by the values of mean relative
wall thickness and cortical index, was increased significantly in
bones isolated from the HMB group of animals when compared
to the controls. MRWT and CI were increased by 14.9% and
8.7% in males born by the sows treated with HMB. In the
females, the differences between the groups of these parameters
reached 9.7% and 6.0%, respectively. All these results are in
contrast to the value of bone length that was significantly
lowered in the offspring obtained from HMB-treated sows.
However, it must be underlined that pigs from HMB group were
slaughtered 11 days earlier than the controls, and, the difference
of age at slaughter between the investigated groups may explain
this matter. The mechanical analysis of femur using three-point
bending test revealed values of the maximum elastic strength
and ultimate strength increased by 31% and 17% in the females
from HMB group when compared to the female controls,
confirming positive effects of maternal administration with
HMB on mechanical endurance of bone tissue. It is really
surprising that, in the group of males prenatally treated with
HMB, the increase of both these parameters was 15% and 9%
higher than in the femur of HMB-treated females. Thus, the
difference of the values of maximum elastic strength and
ultimate strength between the HMB- and placebo-treated males
reached 46% and 25%, respectively. On the basis of the
performed analyses of the femur, this study showed that both
the increased volumetric bone mineral density and enhanced
geometrical parameters like cross-sectional area, second
moment of inertia, mean relative wall thickness and cortical
index were responsible for improved bone mechanical endur-
ance. On the other hand, the obtained results suggest differences
in metabolic response of bone tissue between the males and
females to the prenatal treatment with HMB. While the
treatment with HMB induced greater bone mineral density in
females than in males, the geometrical and mechanical
parameters were much more elevated in the males treated
with HMB. The fact that these positive effects of prenatal
treatment with HMB on bone tissue were not influenced by
differentiated final body weight of pigs is noteworthy.
Furthermore, environmental conditions were identical for both
of the investigated groups during the whole period of the study.
It may be suggested that increased body weight at birth after
prenatal treatment with HMB may be associated with increased
bone mineral density, and geometrical and mechanical proper-
ties determined in 6-month-old pigs.
Considering previous data related to the fetal programming
during intrauterine development, three main mechanisms
responsible for long-term effects of this phenomenon were
presented. The first is epigenetic modification of genes involved
in growth and metabolism during development [5,31,32]. The
changes of the genes expression responsible for specific growth
factors, enzymes, transporters and receptors may have direct
effects on cell and tissue metabolism [33]. The second
mechanism is based on intrauterine conditions among which
nutrition is the most important one. Intrauterine conditions alter
the growth and differentiation of specific fetal tissues at critical
stages of the development and lead to changes in the number or
proportion of different cell types in adult organs like bones,
muscles, liver and pancreas [5,34,35]. Finally, metabolic
programming may take place as response to changes in nutrient
availability per se caused by prenatally induced alterations in
postnatal gut function, food preference, appetite, physical
activity or in the homeostatic control mechanisms. It is
suggested that, during growth, the changes in nutrient
availability will influence body composition and fuel reserves
with metabolic consequences in later life. Moreover, all these
mechanisms may function under hormonal control [5,36]. Three
endocrine axes may be involved in programming of growth and
metabolism, namely the hypothalamic –pituitary–adrenal axis,
endocrine pancreas axis and somatotrophic axis [5,25]. The
results obtained in the current study indicate that positive effects
of the prenatal treatment with HMB on skeletal system
development are mediated by improved somatotrophic axis
function. This hypothesis is strongly supported by significantly
increased serum concentrations of growth hormone and insulin-
like growth factor-1 in the newborn piglets from the HMB
group. The differences of GH and IGF-1 levels between the
investigated groups reached 38% and 20%, respectively, and
prenatally induced changes in the function of somatotrophic
axis after HMB treatment positively influenced the develop-
ment of skeletal system during postnatal life. Clearing, the
micronutrient manipulation during pregnancy alters endocrine
status of newborns and results in developmental adaptations that
permanently change the structure, physiology and metabolism
of bone tissue of the offspring, potentially diminishing the risk
or range of bone metabolic diseases in the adult life. This
hypothesis is confirmed by other reports showing great
importance of proper function of GH/IGF-1 axis for skeletal

piglets were investigated [41,42]. Moreover, in studies on
human neonates, umbilical cord serum IGF-1 concentration was
strongly related to neonatal whole body bone mineral content

newborn piglets, the positive influence of prenatal treatment
with HMB on bone metabolism and skeletal system properties
was confirmed by the analysis of bone-specific alkaline
phosphatase activity. Serum concentration of this indicator of
osteoblastic activity and bone formation processes was
increased by 26% in piglets from the HMB group when
compared to the controls. On the other hand, this study showed
that neither the levels of glucose, insulin and leptin, nor changes
in the lipid profile were engaged in the mechanism responsible
for improved bone metabolism in the newborns. The obtained
results of analysis of lipid profile and glucose concentration in
serum of the newborns are very comparable to those achieved
during other studies in which these parameters were measured
in unsuckled newborn piglets [44]. The observed decrease of
serum HDL-cholesterol level in the newborns from sows treated
with HMB is in accordance with the results reported by Nissen
and Abumrad, where the decrease of HDL-cholesterol level by
14% was obtained in humans. In other studies on humans
presented in this report, HDL-cholesterol level was either not
changed in response to the HMB treatment or increased by 11%
[8]. Total or LDL-cholesterol lowering effect of HMB on blood
lipid profile was also reported in humans receiving similar
dosage of HMB to those used in the current study [28].
The results of assessment of lean meat content of the carcass
in female pigs have shown that maternal treatment with HMB
during the last 2 weeks of pregnancy positively influenced
skeletal muscles development. Beneficial effects of HMB on
muscles were observed previously both in humans and animals;
however, in contrast to the current study, in the earlier studies
HMB was administered postnatally [45–47]. Moreover, positive
effects of oral administration of HMB mixture with arginine and
glutamine on fat-free mass and body weight were observed in
patients with cancer-related wasting [48]. Results obtained in the
current study of lean meat content of carcass may suggest that
improved function of the somatotrophic axis may also be
responsible for increased muscle mass in pigs, especially when
considering the fact that IGF-1 promotes myoblast proliferation,
differentiation and protein accretion in muscle through multiple
signaling mechanisms, including the PI3-kinase, MAP kinase
and calcineurin pathways [49]. Increased lean meat content in the
females from HMB group may also be responsible for beneficial
effects of improved muscle function on bone tissue properties.
This indirect effect of muscles on bone tissue properties seems to
be confirmed by the fact that HMB-treated females reached
higher values of the analyzed parameters of femur, even in
comparison to the results obtained in the control males.
The obtained results are in accordance with our previous
reports on neonatal programming of skeletal development in
mammals, where effects of short-term administration with
alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG) during the first 2 weeks of life have
induced long-term effects on bone mineral density and
mechanical properties of ribs in ram lambs. The metabolic
response of bone tissue to the neonatal treatment with AKG was
expressed by an 8% increase of bone mechanical endurance and
7% increase of vBMD which were observed in 5-month-old
animals [50]. In the other studies with the use of sheep model,
similar effects of neonatal AKG administration on femur were
described. However, in contrast to the current study, Harrison
and colleagues did not observe changes in bone geometry and
plasma IGF-1 concentration, excluding the hypothesis that
AKG-induced neonatal programming of skeletal development
in sheep may be mediated by increased IGF-1 synthesis [51].I n
the studies on chickens, pre-hatching feeding in ovo with β-
hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate has shown positive effects on the
systemic development in embryonic and post-hatching life.
Next to the improved development of intestine, the significant
increase of body weight in hatched chickens was recorded;
however, the analysis of skeletal system was not performed [9].
All these results indicate that there is a high sensitivity to
nutritional and metabolic factors during prenatal and early
neonatal life with long-term effects on skeletal development and
bone mass acquisition even though the metabolic or nutritional
factor is no longer present.
In conclusion, this study showed the existence of prenatal
nutritional availability to program development of skeletal
system of the offspring during the last 2 weeks of prenatal life in
pigs. The obtained results indicate that maternal administration
with HMB has positive long-term effects on skeletal system in
offspring and improves volumetric bone mineral density, as well
as the geometrical and mechanical properties of femur. The
anabolic effects of prenatal treatment with HMB on bone tissue
were connected with improved somatotrophic axis function and
increased serum concentration of GH and IGF-1. It is promising
that HMB positively influences bone metabolism, especially
when its prenatal administration and long-lasting effects in
offspring are considered. However, further studies are needed to
explain whether HMB may be an important factor to be
considered in the prevention of osteoporosis in humans.
