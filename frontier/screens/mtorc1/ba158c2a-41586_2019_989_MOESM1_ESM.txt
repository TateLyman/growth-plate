Guide to use the Web-Simulator of Cell division in the growth plate 
This simulator was designed to visualize the contribution of individual chondrocytes in the growth plate 
to bone elongation. Since we discovered that the mechanism of growth  during fetal and juvenile life 
differs, the simulator models these two side-by-side for comparison.  
Upon opening the simulator at http://www.chaginlab.com/sim, a screen will show the “Foetal” growth 
model (A) on the left and “Juvenile” growth model (B) on the right.  
 
Quick-start  
1) Click on any cell and use the drop-down menu to label it with a color 
2) Click the “Start” (C) button in the purple margin on the left -hand side of the screen to model 
growth 
Advanced Instructions 
• Upon clicking the “Start” button , both models will animate. Each cell can be tracked by its 
color, and in addition, all of its descendants will have this same color.  


=== PAGE 3 ===
• Cell division is randomized for each cycle and each population, and those cells transiting 
through cell division temporarily appear grey.  
• After beginning t he “Start” button changes to “Pause”, so that the simulation can be stopped 
temporarily at any point. The progress of the simulation is recorded in real time (D). 
• The “Stop” (E) button resets the model to its starting point, and returns the color-coded cells to 
their original position (which can be viewed by pressing the “Start” button again), whereas all 
cells can be cleared by pressing the “Reset” (F) button. 
• The default rate of cell proliferation is based on our analysis of cell proliferation rate in different 
zones employing confetti-labeled clones and various chasing time (3-14 days).  
• The pre-set rate of cell division and number of cells in each zone can be modified by opening 
the “View Configuration” menu for either the  “Foetal” (G) or “Juvenile” (H) model. For 
purposes of visualization, the number of columns (which behave independently of each other) 
and cell size can also be modified in this menu. 
• For ease of use, the “Speed” (I) and “Maximum days” (J) can be adjusted using the drop-down 
menus at the top of the screen. 
• When c ells reach the bottom of the hypertrophic zone , they  appear in the “Bone (resorbed 
chondrocytes)” underneath the hypertrophic zone, allowing the entire clone to be analyzed. The 
program keeps a track of the total number of cells resorbed (K).

The effect of axitinib treatment on the clonal size  at different ages. Despite blocking the resorption of 
the hypertrophic zone in mice before stem cell niche formation, long clonal columns were not observed: 
Col2CreERT:R26R:Confetti mice were traced for 10 days after receiving tamoxifen at P4 or P36, and 
were additionally treated with axitinib for the final four days. Clones within the entire growth plate were 
included.  In a separate experiment, axitinib was used to block the formation of the SOC, which led to

a significant reduction in clonal size (when all clones within 100 um of the SOC were quantified). 
Tracing was performed from P10 to P28, and axitinib was administered from P18-P27 (same experiment 
as in Fig. 2d). All data were analysed using an unpaired two-tailed t-test. 
Mice were treated with tamoxifen on P15 then injected with VEGF receptor inhibitor axitinib each day 
from P21 until tissue collection on P30. The number of flat cells and the height of the hypertrophic zone 
were measured from hematoxylin and eosin  stained images using the ImageJ software. Sections were 
immunostained for Ki67 and counterstained using DAPI to visualize all nuclei . The ImageJ software 
was used to quantify the proportion of Ki67-positive cells in each zone.

least 10 days and used to assess continuous clonal enlargement. Treatment from P24 till P30 was done 
to allow direct comparison with the one initiated at P15. Treatment at P18 was tolerated well and it 
was possible to block maturation of the SOC whereas at P21 it was too late.
