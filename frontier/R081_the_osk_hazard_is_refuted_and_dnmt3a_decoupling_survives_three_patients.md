## 1. Ask #4 — partial reprogramming RAISES DNMT1. The hazard is refuted

**F-R079 named a hazard:** OSK lowers DNMTs; `Dnmt1^ΔPrx1` shortens bone; therefore AAV-OSK in a growing
animal might phenocopy the knockout. **F-R080 downgraded it to "wrong enzyme."** **This paper kills it.**

Su et al. sorted **Integrin-α6^high CD71^high epidermal stem cells with low self-renewal** — senescent adult
stem cells — and applied transient OSKM, then measured the methylation machinery:

> *"We found that partial reprogramming **increased DNMT1 mRNA expression** in senescent ESCs, but had **no
> effect on TET1, TET2, and TET3** mRNA expression… we verified that partial reprogramming **significantly
> increased the DNMT1 protein expression** in senescent ESCs."*

**And the baseline direction is the same:**

> *"young ESCs also had a **higher** mRNA expression of DNMT1 compared to senescent ESCs."*

**DNMT1 falls with senescence and partial reprogramming restores it.** The effect persisted **two weeks after
factor withdrawal**. And the authors state the mechanism the growth plate independently requires:

> *"**DNMT1 is essential for the preservation of the progenitor state**… lack of DNMT1 in ESCs would result
> in **severe defects in cell proliferation and self-renewal capacity**."*

> ### **That is `Dnmt1^ΔPrx1` in a different tissue.** Yanagihara: lose Dnmt1 in limb mesenchyme → proliferative zone shrinks, cells hypertrophy early, bones short. Su: lose DNMT1 in stem cells → proliferation and self-renewal fail. **Same enzyme, same job, two tissues.**
>
> ### **And partial reprogramming pushes it the RIGHT way.** F-R079's hazard predicted OSK would lower maintenance methylation and shorten bone. **The only direct measurement of DNMT1 protein after partial reprogramming shows it goes UP. The hazard is refuted, not merely downgraded.**

**The most important structural consequence:** methylation **age went DOWN while DNMT1 went UP, in the same
cells, in the same experiment.** **Rejuvenation is not global demethylation.** The branch — and I, in
F-R069 and again in F-R079 — has repeatedly treated "reprogramming" and "demethylation" as the same
direction. **They are not.** Partial reprogramming **raises the maintenance writer and lowers the de novo
writer at Polycomb targets** (F-R080 §1: OSK lowers DNMT3a), and **both of those are the height-positive
direction.**

**Limits, stated plainly:** epidermal stem cells, **not chondrocytes and not growth plate**; **OSKM including
c-Myc**, whereas the cartilage study used OSK with c-Myc deliberately excluded; qPCR and
immunofluorescence at **n = 3**; and *Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci* is a low-tier journal. **This is the only
direct DNMT1-after-reprogramming measurement in existence, and it is not a strong paper. The direction is
clear; the weight behind it is one experiment.**

## 2. Ask #1 — bone age in TBRS, and the decoupling survives

F-R080 §4 claimed DNMT3A loss decouples growth rate from skeletal maturation, and I flagged that **the
crucial cell rested on one patient.** There are now three.

| patient | variant | age | height | **bone age** | advance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Japanese** (Miyoshi, 17-yr follow-up) | p.G587fs | **10 y 7 m** | **166.4 cm, +3.77 SD**, **Tanner 4** | **11.1 y** | **+0.5 y** |
| **Swedish** (Lennartsson) | p.Arg320\* | **12 y 2 m** | 172.5 cm, +2.8 SDS | **12.0 y** | **−0.2 y** |
| **Chilean** (Martin) | p.Arg771\* | 8 y 10 m | 150 cm at 9 y, +2.42 SD | **13 y** | **+4.2 y** |

### The Japanese patient is the strongest single observation in this branch

**+3.77 SD tall, breast Tanner 4 at ten years and seven months — and a bone age of 11.1 years.** In any
ordinary child, that combination of stature and pubertal stage comes with a markedly advanced skeleton.
**Here it is half a year ahead.**

### The Chilean counterexample is disarmed by its own authors

The Chilean boy does have a bone age advanced 4.2 years. **But his 11-year-old sister, who does NOT carry
the DNMT3A variant, also had an advanced bone age (13 years at 10 y 7 m) with no height alteration.** The
authors' own reading:

> *"The advanced bone age in the proband's sister, **who does not carry the variant** identified in the
> DNMT3A gene, **raises the possibility that there are other familial factors** associated with this
> manifestation."*

> ### **The advanced bone age in that family segregates independently of DNMT3A.** It is a family trait, not a syndrome feature — and the authors say so.

**And they state the literature position directly:** advanced bone age *"is a common feature in Sotos
syndrome and Weaver syndrome but **has not been reported frequently in TBRS**."*

### A fourth line of evidence, from the surgeons

Lennartsson's team found the Greulich-Pyle prediction (187.1 cm) matched the achieved height (187.4 cm)
**despite** the epiphysiodesis, and concluded:

> *"A more likely explanation is that **the height prediction underestimated the amount of remaining
> growth**… Height predictions are based on normal populations… and **are not validated for individuals with
> specific growth syndromes**."*

> ### **Bone-age-based prediction UNDER-estimates remaining growth in TBRS.** That is the decoupling stated from the opposite direction: **these children have more growth left than their skeletons say.**

**Verdict: F-R080 §4 stands, and is now supported by two patients with non-advanced bone age, one
counterexample explained by its own family, an explicit literature statement, and an independent
under-prediction finding. It is no longer n = 1.**

## 3. The overgrowth is NOT endocrine — which makes DNMT3A orthogonal to the GH arm

The Japanese work-up, at +3.77 SD:

> **IGF-1 325 ng/mL (+0.22 SD). "Serum GH and IGF-1 levels were not elevated."** Thyroid, gonadotrophins,
> karyotype, urinary homocysteine, MRI, echo, lens exam — all normal. The Chilean case likewise: *"IGF-1…
> within normal ranges."*

> ### **DNMT3A overgrowth runs at +3 SD with a completely normal somatotropic axis.** It is not a GH or IGF-1 phenotype. **That makes it genuinely orthogonal to the stack's GH arm rather than redundant with it** — and it means the effect is intrinsic to the tissue, which is where F-R079's `Dnmt1^ΔPrx1` and F-R078's `Acan`/`Ccn2` levers also live.

## 5. Ask #3 — Heyn 2019, and the mechanism is the branch's own pool axis

**Human:** two unrelated children with de novo **p.W330R** in the PWWP domain, *"significant, proportionate
reduction in head circumference and height"*; a third with an adjacent-residue mutation.

**Mouse:** `Dnmt3a^W326R/+` — *"viable, healthy and morphologically unremarkable, but were **proportionately
small with significantly reduced body and brain weight**."* In vivo bisulfite confirmation of
hypermethylation at **Hoxc13 and Sox1**, and genome-wide RRBS confirmation in cortex.

**And the mechanistic sentence that connects this to everything the branch has built:**

> *"hypermethylation of DMV/DMRs could lead to a **skewing of stem/progenitor cells towards differentiation
> away from self-renewal**."*

> ### That is `a > b`. **F-R022 ("the pool-expanding levers are tumour suppressors"), F-R032 ("output is conjugate to the pool"), F-R066 (mTORC1 switches resting-zone cells to symmetric division) — the branch has been hunting a self-renewal lever since F-R022, and Heyn arrives at the same axis from the opposite end of the literature.** **DNMT3A activity at Polycomb valleys pushes progenitors out of self-renewal. Losing it keeps them in.**

**Two more things from the discussion:**

- *"**NSD1, DNMT3A and EZH2 are both height QTLs** and somatically mutated in cancer"* — **all three of the overgrowth genes are common-variant height loci in the general population, not just syndrome genes.**
- *"Polycomb complexes also cause overgrowth and **PHC1 mutation results in microcephalic dwarfism**"* — the Polycomb axis is bidirectional for body size independently of DNMT3A.

**The authors' own conclusion is the branch's thesis in one line:**

> *"the interplay between DNA methylation and polycomb at key developmental regulators as a **determinant of
> organism size in mammals**."*

**Honest limit: the mouse dwarfism is documented as body and brain WEIGHT. Bone length was not measured.**

## 6. The model that now holds together

| | **DNMT1** | **DNMT3A** |
|---|---|---|
| role | maintenance | de novo, at Polycomb DMVs |
| compartment | **95.9% outside promoters/islands** (my measurement, F-R079) | **Polycomb valleys, bivalent H3K27me3/H3K4me3** (Heyn) |
| what it holds | **the progenitor / proliferative state** (Su; Yanagihara) | pushes progenitors **out of self-renewal** (Heyn) |
| **loss →** | **SHORT** — `Dnmt1^ΔPrx1`; self-renewal failure | **TALL, +3.0 SD, all 13/13** |
| **gain →** | untested | **microcephalic dwarfism**, small mice |
| **partial reprogramming →** | **UP** (Su, protein and mRNA) | **DOWN** (F-R080) |

> ### **Both arms of partial reprogramming point the same way: raise the writer that holds the progenitor state, lower the writer that expels cells from it. Both are height-positive.** The apparent contradiction that has run through F-R069, F-R072, F-R079 and F-R080 was an artefact of treating "DNMT" as one thing.

**And the therapeutic reading is unchanged but now better grounded: lower DNMT3A, preserve or raise DNMT1.**
Nucleoside inhibitors (azacitidine, decitabine) hit both and are the wrong tool; **selective non-nucleoside
DNMT3A inhibitors exist** (F-R080), and **partial reprogramming does both halves at once.**
