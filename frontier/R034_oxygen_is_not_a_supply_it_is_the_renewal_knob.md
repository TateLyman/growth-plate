## 3. The falsification, in Brighton's own words

F-R033 §2 argued the plate has a diffusion-limited thickness budget, that RZ + PZ + HZ compete inside it,
and that the one lever outside every constraint is **supply**. **Every part of that is wrong, and the
paper I said I needed is what refutes it.** Brighton and Heppenstall, same paper, discussion:

> *"**High oxygen consumption in the epiphyseal plate has not been reported by any author under any
> condition or circumstance.** It seems reasonable to believe that **the low oxygen tension present in
> the plate is not the result of cellular utilization of oxygen.**"*

> *"…the oxygen tension at day 0 was the same as that at day 3 and day 7… **Despite increased epiphyseal
> plate growth, the oxygen tension at the zone level did not change. Little oxygen was consumed in the
> face of active bone growth.** One can only conclude that **the low oxygen tension in the plate at the
> zone level is not the result of oxygen consumption.**"*

> *"…it remains that low oxygen tension is due to **less delivery** of oxygen to the plate. One is saying,
> then, that **the delivery of less oxygen to the plate results in more growth of the plate.**"*

Three independent supports, all in that paragraph:

1. *"in an in vitro study, when **less oxygen was supplied** to epiphyseal plate explants, there was
   **greater bone formation**, and when **more oxygen was supplied**, there was **less bone formation**."*
   (Brighton, Ray, Soble & Kuettner, *JBJS* 51A:1383, 1969)
2. *"the **in vivo plate grows at a rate five times greater** than the in vitro plate, yet the oxygen
   tension in the former is some **four to five times lower**."*
3. This paper's own result: the A-V fistula lowered pO₂ **in every zone** (proliferative 47–77 → 22–28
   mmHg, P < 0.001) and tibial lengthening occurred in **100% of puppies** with a femoral fistula.

And the metabolic basis, citing four independent groups: the plate is *"**predominantly glycolytic in
character**"*, the zone of cell columns has *"the **highest activity of the glycolytic enzymes**"*, the
hypertrophic zone *"**too, followed predominantly an anaerobic pathway**"*, and Picard & Cartier
*"demonstrated a **low oxygen consumption in epiphyseal cartilage**."*

Their summary, verbatim:

> **Low O₂ Tension → Anaerobic Metabolism → Increased Plate Growth**
> **High O₂ Tension → Aerobic Metabolism → Decreased Plate Growth**

**So: the growth plate is a glycolytic tissue that barely consumes oxygen, and it grows faster when given
less of it.** There is no oxygen budget to spend, the steep gradient is a delivery/geometry artefact
rather than a consumption ceiling, and **"raise the supply" — the one lever I said sat outside every
constraint — is not merely unsupported but points the wrong way.**

I want this on the record plainly: **had Tate not held me to "mechanism first, agents only after," F-R033
would have produced a hyperoxia recommendation, and it would have been actively counterproductive.**

## 4. Marchini closes flaw 3 — and independently kills the budget

**"Per column", explicitly.** Methods: *"…counted the number of proliferative and hypertrophic
chondrocytes **per column**"*, with *"an average of 24 separate columns for each sample (range 10–40)"*.
So Longshanks selection raised **A**, the amplification term, exactly as the geometry requires.

**Table 1 (least-squares means, body-mass-corrected):**

| | Control | LS1 | LS2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| tibia length (mm) | 11.39 | 12.11 | 12.16 |
| **μCT growth plate (mm)** | **0.408** | **0.506 (+24%)** | **0.545 (+34%)** |
| histology total plate (mm) | 0.516 | 0.566 (+10%) | 0.601 (+16%) |
| **resting zone (mm)** | 0.050 | 0.061 | **0.078 (+56%)** |
| **proliferative zone (mm)** | **0.200** | **0.253 (+27%)** | **0.273 (+37%)** |
| hypertrophic zone (mm) | 0.249 | 0.228 | 0.231 — **P = 0.219, n.s.** |
| **N proliferative cells/column** | **25** | **29** | **33 (+32%)** |
| N hypertrophic cells/column | 18 | 18 | 18 — **P = 0.722** |
| last hypertrophic cell height (mm) | 0.030 | 0.029 | 0.030 — **P = 0.775** |
| labelling index (division rate) | — | — | **no difference** |

**The plate got thicker — up to +34% — and nothing broke.** Growth rate rose from 0.35 to 0.41/0.43
mm/day. Growth *duration* was untouched: 99% complete at P69, P70, P71 — *"all three lines complete
growth within 24–48 hours of each other."*

**And selection moved exactly one term.** Not the division rate, not terminal cell height, not
hypertrophic cell number, not duration. Only the number of amplifying cells per column.

### The workbook test that settles it

The per-animal data (n = 49) let me run the **direct** test of an inter-zone budget — which the paper
never does:

| test | pooled r | p |
|---|---|---|
| **PZ vs HZ (the compensation test)** | **−0.118** | **0.42** |
| PZ vs RZ | +0.629 | <0.001 |
| RZ vs HZ | −0.072 | 0.62 |
| #prolif vs PZ depth | **+0.754** | <0.001 |
| #prolif vs total plate | +0.712 | <0.001 |
| #prolif vs #hypertrophic | −0.115 | 0.43 |
| total plate vs tibia length | +0.371 | 0.006 |
| PZ vs tibia length | +0.286 | 0.040 |
| **HZ vs tibia length** | +0.056 | 0.70 |

Within-line PZ vs HZ: Ctrl **+0.021**, LS1 **−0.013**, LS2 **−0.159** — all null.

**There is no trade-off between zones.** A fixed thickness budget requires PZ and HZ to anticorrelate;
they do not, in any line. The zones vary independently and the whole plate simply gets bigger. And
**total plate height ranges 435 → 753 µm within one cohort at one age — a 1.73× spread**, against the
~1.3× ceiling I computed. Two independent falsifications, arriving the same afternoon as Brighton's.

**What survives:** `#prolif vs PZ depth r = +0.754` is Little's Law confirmed — more cells per column
means a taller proliferative zone. And **PZ predicts bone length while HZ does not**, which is the axial
result, intact.

### A dissociation the paper does not report

The two replicate lines did not do the same thing. Testing tibia length at birth from the raw data:

| | P0 (birth) | P14 | P150 (adult) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LS1 / Control** | **1.145, p ≈ 0.001** | 1.088 | 1.171 |
| **LS2 / Control** | **1.052, p ≈ 0.41 (n.s.)** | 1.087 | 1.178 |

**LS1 is already 14.5% longer at birth and gains ~2.6 points thereafter. LS2 starts equal and gains all
of it postnatally.** Same endpoint, opposite routes. And the internal check is strong: **LS2, the
postnatal line, has the larger growth-plate phenotype** — RZ +56% vs +22%, PZ +37% vs +27%, cells/column
+32% vs +16%. The mechanism tracks the route.

**So the postnatally achievable share of the Longshanks result is best read off LS2: ~18% longer tibia,
from +32% proliferative cells per column and a +16% thicker plate, with no change in division rate,
terminal cell size, or growth duration.** That is the honest number for what maximal selection on a
postnatal growth-plate mechanism buys.

## 6. Breur confirms the column model formally — and challenges h_term

Breur states the field's model in the form I derived independently:

> *"the estimated rate of bone length growth is the product of the **number of newly produced cells per
> column per day**, and the **mean cell profile height**… based on the underlying assumption that the
> complete chondrocytic column is the **functional unit** of bone length growth… (a) the rate of cell
> production at the epiphyseal end of the column is equal to the rate of cell loss at the metaphyseal
> side, and (b) the number of cells per column is constant over the period of a couple days."*

That is F-R033 §0's correction, in the literature since 1997. His 3-D alternative divides by
cross-sectional area explicitly — so **area cancels for length**, from the other direction.

But he also lands a hit on a load-bearing atlas concept. His best model is
**LG = ƒ(v_cell × N_per_mm²_per_day), R² = 0.992**, and:

> *"…it is unlikely that the **mean height** of juxtametaphyseal hypertrophic chondrocytes is an
> indicator of either the mean chondrocytic domain volume or the mean chondrocytic volume itself."*
> *"The results of this study **do not support the application of the traditionally used kinetic model**,
> because models using the 1-dimensional parameter mean height… were unsuitable or not as robust."*

**The atlas's `h_term` axis is a 1-D parameter that Breur says is the wrong parameter** (R² 0.929 for
height vs 0.950 for volume vs 0.992 for the product model). Worth flagging to the atlas, not resolved
here. He also notes the 1-D model *"completely ignores"* matrix formation via transverse septa — the
F-R012 term — while finding **matrix volume per cell constant across ages within a plate.**

Farnum & Wilsman 1993 supply the column kinetics: *"chondrocytes… spend an average of **4 days in the
proliferative zone, representing approximately four cellular divisions**"*, and each column is *"a
**clonal expansion of a stem cell**, which may proceed independently from adjacent columns."* Wilsman
1996: across four plates spanning 396 → ~50 µm/24h, cycle times 30.9 / 34.0 / 48.8 / 76.3 h, *"almost all
differences… attributable to the length of the **G1 phase**"* (S 3.4–6.1 h, G2 3.0 h, M 0.5–0.6 h).

## 7. What replaces the dead lever — and it is better

If oxygen is not a supply, what is it? **It is a control input on the renewal/commitment balance — the
`a − b` term of F-R032 that I said nothing was known to control.**

`leijten2012` (PLOS ONE, PMC3503827; E17.5 mouse tibiae, 21 days, 21% vs 2.5% O₂, N = 18) is the modern
counterpart, and at first reading it contradicts Brighton — **normoxia increased tibia length.** Read
against the conjugacy identity, the two are the same result:

| | normoxia | hypoxia |
|---|---|---|
| tibia length (21 d explant) | **↑** | ↓ |
| **hypertrophic zone length** | **↑** | ↓ |
| **resting zone length** | ↓ | **↑** |
| cartilaginous epiphysis size | ↓ | ↑ |
| RUNX2, COL10A1, ALPL, MMP9, MMP13 | ↑ | ↓ |
| ACAN, COL2A1, SOX9 | ↓ | ↑ |
| **GREM1, FRZB, DKK1** (mRNA **and** ELISA protein) | ↓ | **↑** |

Leijten's own summary: *"**hypoxia retains chondrocytes in the resting zone while normoxia stimulates
them to progress towards the hypertrophic zone.**"*

**That is `a − b` in words.** Normoxia drives commitment — faster now, pool spent. Hypoxia holds cells in
the reserve — slower now, pool preserved. In a **fetal explant with a fixed pool and no influx**, over 21
days, spending wins on length; that is Leijten. In an **intact animal with a renewing pool**, over weeks,
preserving wins; that is Brighton's fistula, 100% of puppies. **The contradiction dissolves into the
velocity-versus-duration trade this branch identified at F-R016, now with a mechanism.**

**And the mechanism is the convergence.** Hypoxia induces **GREM1, FRZB and DKK1** — secreted WNT and BMP
antagonists, confirmed at protein level by ELISA. Set that beside:

- **`chu2026`**: the human root-cell (GP1) niche is *"low in WNT and TGF-β"*, markers **SFRP5** (a secreted
  WNT antagonist) and APOE, with **DKK1, GREM1**, FGF2 and KLF4 in its regulatory set.
- **`trompet2024`**: Hedgehog activation expands the stem pool by *"creating a **Wnt-inhibitory
  environment**"*, and Hh governs *"the balance between generation of daughter stem cells and committed
  progeny."*
- **The atlas's R241**: the root niche is defined by *"self-secreted WNT and TGF-β antagonists."*
- **`leijten2012`**: hypoxia induces exactly those antagonists, and they are *"potent inhibitors of
  hypertrophic differentiation."*

**Four independent lines — a human single-cell atlas, a pharmacological stem-expansion experiment, the
atlas's own synthesis, and an oxygen-tension explant study — converge on one molecular state: the reserve
niche is a WNT-antagonist-high compartment, and low oxygen is one of the physiological inputs that
maintains it.**

That gives the programme something it has never had:

> **A candidate `a − b` lever that is local, physiological, deliverable, has a named effector set
> (GREM1 / FRZB / DKK1 / SFRP5), has human niche validation, and is *not* a tumour suppressor.**

Every pool-expanding lever in the atlas was a tumour suppressor (PTCH1, TSC1/2, PTEN, DEPDC5). This one
is not. It is also **local by nature** — a tissue oxygen gradient is not a systemic signal — which is the
escape from F-R019's cancellation theorem that I have been looking for since round 19.

**Held as a hypothesis, with its failure mode stated:** hypoxia raises `a − b` at the cost of `λ`. That
is a *pool-preserving, rate-slowing* intervention — the arm3 trap in a new location. It buys duration and
unboundedness, and it does not by itself buy speed. **On its own it delivers two of the three terms.**
The three-term solution would require it *co-dosed with* a drive lever, which is exactly the
titration architecture F-R032 §2 derived and nobody has run.

## 8. Where this leaves the three terms

| term | status after this round |
|---|---|
| **never close** | solved and controllable in humans (ESR1 receptor-level durable; ligand-level a knife edge) |
| **unlimited** | `a − b ≥ 0` is now a lever, not a wish — hypoxia / WNT-antagonist tone, with a named effector set and four-way convergence on the human niche |
| **fast** | **still the hard one, and the honest ceiling is modest.** Maximal artificial selection bought **+18% postnatally** (LS2), via +32% cells/column. Wilsman's fastest natural plate runs 396 µm/day against a human distal femoral physis at ~27–55. Rodent tempo may not transfer |

**What died:** the thickness budget; inter-zone competition; supply as the master lever; hyperoxia as
anything but a mistake.
**What is now measured rather than argued:** amplification per column is the term selection moves;
the plate can thicken 24–34% with no compensation; area and width are not in the length equation.
**What is new:** oxygen as the `a − b` knob, and a non-oncogenic, local, human-validated effector set.

## 10. Corrections carried

- **F-R033 §2 — withdrawn in full.** The diffusion-limited thickness budget, the inter-zone competition,
  and "supply is the one lever outside both constraints" are all falsified: by Brighton's own discussion
  (no measurable O₂ consumption; less O₂ → more growth) and independently by the Marchini workbook
  (PZ–HZ r ≈ 0 in all three lines; 1.73× plate-height range).
- **F-R033's seven-line argument** — lines (a)–(g) were individually true and collectively misread.
  Cross-species thickness conservation, haemoglobin bodies, HIF-1α interior death and the SOC-proximity
  clone-size gradient are all real; none of them requires an oxygen *supply* limit, and Brighton shows
  they cannot mean one. `newton2019`'s SOC-proximity effect reverts to **the authors' own reading — niche
  signalling, not feeding** — which I had "corrected" toward supply and should not have.
- **F-R033 §5's flaw 4** — closed. My reconstruction of Wilson's signs was correct.
- **F-R033 §5's flaw 3** — closed. Per column.
- **F-R032 §1's identity** — untouched. It is arithmetic, and every result this round is consistent
  with it; §7 is the first candidate mechanism for moving its one free parameter.
- **A standing caution, promoted:** LS1 was 14.5% longer **at birth**. Any claim that Longshanks
  demonstrates a *postnatal* mechanism must be made on LS2 alone. The atlas's CORR-299 concern was
  justified and is now quantified.
