## 1. Nilsson 2014 — two effects of one hormone, and only one of them comes back

Ovariectomised rabbits, estradiol cypionate from 11 to 16 weeks, then five weeks off.

| parameter | during E2 | **5 weeks after stopping** |
|---|---|---|
| tibial growth rate | decreased (P<.05) | **normalised (P=.76)** |
| chondrocyte proliferation | decreased (P<.01) | **normalised (P=.52)** |
| terminal hypertrophic cell size | decreased (P<.01) | **normalised** |
| growth plate height | advanced | **remained advanced** |
| proliferative + hypertrophic cell number | advanced | **remained advanced** |
| **resting zone cells per mm plate width** | **accelerated loss** (PT P<.01; DR P<.001) | **remained advanced** (DR P<.01) |
| **TUNEL⁺ resting zone cells** | **4.6 ± 0.6% vs 4.4 ± 1.0%, P = .87** | **not apoptosis** |
| epiphyseal fusion | **hastened** | — |

**The mechanism, verbatim:**

> *"estrogen accelerates the loss of progenitor cells in the resting zone by **decreasing proliferation of
> resting zone chondrocytes without delaying — or possibly accelerating — their differentiation to
> proliferative zone chondrocytes**."*

**In this branch's notation: oestrogen lowers `a` and leaves or raises `b`.** A net drain with no output
to show for it.

**And the detail that makes it worse than anything else:**

> *"after estrogen was stopped, the growth rate, proliferation rate, and hypertrophic size returned to
> normal levels **but did not rebound above normal. Thus, no catch-up growth occurred**… This pattern
> **differs from that of other growth-inhibiting conditions, including hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid
> excess, and malnutrition, all of which are followed by increased chondrocyte proliferation and catch-up
> growth**."*

> ### Every other growth-inhibiting condition slows senescence and is repaid with catch-up growth. Oestrogen is the only one that takes the growth and slows nothing. It is a pure, unrecoverable loss.

**Human corroboration, in the same paper:** in precocious puberty treated with a GnRH analogue, once
oestrogen falls back to prepubertal levels *"the growth rates are subsequently **below normal for age** and
correlate with the advancement in bone age."*

**Consequence for the theory:** the write-off already incurred is not retrievable by any arm in the stack,
and the marginal value of continued oestrogen exposure is strictly negative. **Ablation is not one arm
among several; it is the only one whose cost is measured in permanently lost cells.**

## 2. Nilsson / Schrier 2006 — the only agent ever shown to raise resting-zone cell number

**Age effect, distal femur, resting-zone BrdU labelling index:**

| age | index |
|---|---|
| 0 wk (fetal) | **95.6 ± 0.8%** |
| 5 wk | **9.2 ± 1.2%** |
| 9 wk | **9.2 ± 1.1%** |
| 17 wk | **7.6 ± 1.5%** |

> **The resting zone collapses tenfold before 5 weeks and is then essentially flat — 9.2 → 9.2 → 7.6 —
> across the whole postnatal growth period, while the growth rate falls sevenfold.** The resting zone is
> not winding down. It runs at a low constant rate and is drawn down.

**Dexamethasone, 0.5 mg/kg/day SC, 4-week-old rabbits, two weeks:**

| endpoint | result |
|---|---|
| RZ BrdU labelling index | **decreased, P < 0.001** |
| **RZ chondrocyte number** | **INCREASED, P = 0.016** — driven by the reserve resting zone (P < 0.001) |
| serum IGF-1 | **112 ± 8 vs 108 ± 6 ng/mL — unchanged** |

The authors' reading: *"dexamethasone may additionally **inhibit the differentiation of resting to
proliferative chondrocytes**."*

> ### Dexamethasone lowers `a` and lowers `b` harder. It is the only agent in this literature demonstrated to increase the number of resting zone chondrocytes, and it did so with IGF-1 untouched.

**Rabbit → human by Km scaling: 0.5 × (12/37) ≈ 0.16 mg/kg/day ≈ 11 mg dexamethasone for 70 kg** —
roughly 70 mg prednisone equivalent. **Not a sustainable exposure.** What survives is the mechanism: **`b`
has a knob and glucocorticoid turns it**, and glucocorticoid-induced arrest is the one growth inhibition
that is repaid with catch-up growth. That makes "bank cells during a deliberate non-growth period" a real
strategy rather than a fantasy — with the standing cost that glucocorticoid takes bone, which collides
directly with §3.

**And the ligand-sensitivity number that governs everything in §4:** in the same experiment, oestradiol
lowered the RZ labelling index (P = 0.011) at a serum E2 of **11 ± 2 pg/mL versus <5 pg/mL in vehicle**.

> **Eleven picograms per millilitre measurably suppressed resting-zone self-renewal.** That is a low-normal
> male concentration. Partial suppression is not suppression, and a standard immunoassay cannot see the
> difference that matters.

## 3. The mechanical envelope — and a correction I owe

**Farouk Sait 2023, seven patients under 18 on FGFR TKIs (Debio1347 and erdafitinib), MSKCC:**

| finding | |
|---|---|
| **increased linear growth velocity** | **7 of 7 — 100%** |
| **slipped capital femoral epiphysis** | **3 of 7 — 43%** |
| ages at SCFE | **9, 13, 17 years** |
| time to SCFE | **5 months and 40 months** |
| minimum exposure for any bony toxicity | ***">4 months… appears necessary"*** |
| **hyperphosphataemia** | **all patients** |
| obesity | **2 of 3 — *"the third was a tall thin adolescent"*** |
| physeal widening with rapid growth | ***"observed in all our patients"*** |

**CORRECTION to F-R046 §5 and F-R047.** I wrote, from the five-case FDA series, that both SCFE cases were
obese and no growth-only case was, and I drew the conclusion that the mechanical failure is load-dependent
and therefore controllable by weight. **The dedicated seven-patient series says one of three was tall and
thin.** Obesity is an amplifier, not a requirement, and my inference was too comfortable.

**Brizini 2024 closes it from the other side:** an obese patient (BMI >99th centile, 82.1 kg) on
**erdafitinib 4.7 mg/m²/day — 8 mg per 1.7 m², capped at 8 mg/day, the APEC1621B paediatric dose** —
slipped a hip **with no growth acceleration at all** (height stayed 25th–50th centile), with
hyperphosphataemia requiring chelation.

> ### Across both series hyperphosphataemia is present in 100% of SCFE cases — including the one with no growth acceleration. It is the only universal correlate, and it is the one thing on the list that is fully controllable.

Mechanism: FGFR inhibition blocks FGF23 signalling → phosphate retention → *"hyperphosphatemia… causes
bone resorption and may have contributed to SCFE."* **The class toxicity everyone logs as a nuisance lab
value is load-bearing in the failure mode.** Phosphate is a primary variable in this design, not
side-effect management — that is a change from F-R046 and F-R047, both of which treated it as incidental.

**What the envelope says about rate.** Seven of seven grew faster; three of seven slipped. The failure
needs **>4 months** and is preceded in every case by **physeal widening from rapid growth**. A slower plate
widens less. **The published human evidence brackets 19 cm/yr as the rate that breaks hips and gives no
information at all about 6–9 cm/yr, because nobody has run it.**

## 4. The IGF-1 question, and a reversal of F-R047

### What IGF-1 is for here

Not growth. **Erdafitinib alone is apoptotic** — in the index patient's own primary cells, 100–300 nM
produced **PARP cleavage and cleaved caspase-3** — and **IGF-1, through sustained AKT and not ERK,
completely suppressed it**: *"Only with IGF-I treatment was activation of AKT sustained for 48 hours."*
IGF-2 was weaker and inconsistent; FGF2 did nothing.

**The human dose-response, such as it is:**

| patient | IGF-1 | velocity on erdafitinib |
|---|---|---|
| Raimann pt 2 | **SDS −3.8** (~20 ng/mL) | **10 cm/yr** |
| Majlessipour | **normal for Tanner II–III** | **19.06 cm/yr** |

**Growth occurs at −3.8 SDS and is roughly twice as fast at normal. Nothing anywhere suggests
above-normal is better, and the pool argument runs hard against it.**

> **The target is IGF-1 SDS 0 to +1 — mid-to-upper normal for age and sex. Not above +2.**

### GH or rhIGF-1 — I am reversing F-R047

F-R047 chose mecasermin on Chu's receptor gradient: **GHR highest in GP1 (root stem cells), IGF1R highest
in GP5 (hypertrophic)**, so IGF-1 drives output without touching the stem pool. **That logic is correct and
it stands.** The pharmacology nonetheless goes the other way:

| | **somatropin** | mecasermin |
|---|---|---|
| IGF-1 profile | **stable over 24 h** | **free IGF-1, ~10–20 min half-life** — spike and trough |
| IGFBP-3 / ALS | **raised**, forming the 150 kDa ternary complex (IGF-1 t½ ~12–15 h) | **not raised** — the complex is never reconstituted |
| hypoglycaemia | no | **yes; must be taken with food, and it is dose-limiting** |
| schedule | once daily | **twice daily with meals** |
| stem-pool risk | acts on GHR, highest on GP1 | acts on IGF1R, lowest on GP1 |

**The resolution is the word "excess."** `chu2025`'s depletion result and Chu 2026's
phospho-STAT5-in-the-resting-zone finding are **pharmacological-dose** GH experiments. A dose titrated to
a *normal* IGF-1 reproduces normal GH action, not excess. And because total oestrogen ablation **lowers**
endogenous GH secretion — Rochira's aromatase-deficient men had **GH peaks of 1.0–2.8 µg/L against a ≥11
µg/L cutoff** — replacement restores toward normal rather than exceeding it. A spiking free-IGF-1 profile
with no binding proteins is not physiological in either direction.

**Dose provenance:** adult GH replacement is **0.2–0.5 mg/day starting**, titrated on IGF-1 measured
8–12 h post-dose and reported as **SDS**, with maintenance typically **0.3–0.6 mg/day** in a young adult
male; guidelines put the target inside the age-adjusted reference range.

> **The dose to avoid, with the arithmetic:** paediatric ISS dosing is **0.3–0.37 mg/kg/week** — for 70 kg
> that is **3.0–3.7 mg/day, five to eighteen times replacement.** That is precisely the "excess GH" that
> `chu2025` showed depletes the stem pool, in a design whose binding constraint is cell number.

**Two interactions worth recording:** oestrogen ablation removes oestrogen's suppression of hepatic IGF-1
generation, so **GH sensitivity should be expected to rise and the maintenance requirement to fall** once
ablation is complete; and **GH and glucocorticoid both worsen insulin resistance**, which compounds if
abiraterone's mandatory prednisone is present.

## 5. The agent set, with dose provenance

Doses below are **the doses used in the cited human literature**, not recommendations. Where a dose is my
extrapolation rather than a published one, it says so.

### Oestrogen ablation — eight routes, and an AI closes one (F-R047 Part I)

| target | agent | dose in the literature | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ERα protein** | **fulvestrant** | 500 mg IM, d1/15/29 then q28d | *"pure antiestrogen with **no agonistic effects**"*; **also a GPER agonist**, which is the favourable sign |
| — | **NOT elacestrant** | — | nonclinical *"agonist effects on bone"*. A bone-agonist SERD is the one thing that must not be used |
| **aromatase** | exemestane 25 mg/day; letrozole 2.5 mg/day; anastrozole 1 mg/day | standard adult oncology | exemestane is **irreversible**; all are 285–296 Da, so they cross into cartilage and block the chondrocyte's **own CYP19A1** as well as the systemic enzyme |
| **steroid sulfatase** | **irosustat (STX64)** | **40 mg/day oral** in phase 1/2 | **the bypass every AI protocol leaves open** — E1S is the most abundant circulating oestrogen and **STS is upregulated in response to AI treatment**. Also blocks DHEA-S → DHEA |
| **CYP17A1** | abiraterone acetate | 1000 mg/day fasting **+ prednisone 5 mg** (mandatory) | removes **adrenal and gonadal** precursor supply, so arms 2 and 3 have no substrate |
| **androgen add-back** | oxandrolone | **0.06 mg/kg/day** (Turner trial dose; **+2.7 cm adult height over GH alone**, Cochrane) | non-aromatizable. Cannot be converted to oestrogen by any enzyme |
| **27-hydroxycholesterol** | high-intensity statin ± ezetimibe | standard lipid dosing | 27-HC is *"an endogenous SERM"* made by CYP27A1 **inside bone itself**; no AI touches it |
| **exogenous ligands** | eliminate soy, flax, hops; calcium-D-glucarate for gut β-glucuronidase | — | 8-prenylnaringenin (hops) is the most potent phytoestrogen known; **genistein is a potent ERβ agonist — the exact escape receptor** |

**Why ligand ablation is needed even with a SERD.** Chagin 2004: **ERα⁻/⁻ mice all had fused femoral and
tibial plates at 18 months, via ERβ, under high oestradiol; only ERα⁻/⁻β⁻/⁻ stayed unfused.** Fulvestrant's
**ERα** degradation is documented; its **ERβ** degradation is not. The ligand arms exist so ERβ has nothing
to bind.

**And GPER1 must be left alone.** Chondrocyte-specific GPER1 knockout **decreases** proliferative-zone
thickness and cell number and **shortens the tibia**, acting *"via PTHrP/Ihh regulation."* It is
growth-promoting.

**Verification, per §2:** LC-MS/MS, and **E1S measured, not just E2** — 11 pg/mL is biologically active and
the reservoir is the thing being drained. The master readout is **bone age**, which in Rochira's four men
sat at **14.8–15.5 for years** while they kept growing.

## 7. Holes, named

1. **The STS arm is imported from oncology.** STS-mediated AI resistance is documented in tumours. I could
   confirm aromatase and *"enzymes involved in estrogen metabolism"* in chondrocytes but **not steroid
   sulfatase specifically in the growth plate.** If STS is not expressed there, the arm protects only the
   circulating reservoir.
2. **Fulvestrant's effect on ERβ is undocumented**, and Chagin makes ERβ a proven fusion route. I cannot
   say the receptor arm alone is sufficient.
3. **Irosustat is investigational** — phase 1/2 only.
4. **The erdafitinib dose that produces 6–9 cm/yr has never been measured.** Every published exposure is an
   oncology patient dosed for a tumour. §3 brackets 19 cm/yr as the rate that breaks hips and says nothing
   about 8.
5. **Everything in the pool section is untried**, and its strongest mechanism — Hedgehog — has no molecule.
6. **Abaloparatide has no paediatric open-plate record**; teriparatide does.
7. **No one has taken this combination.** The interaction table in §6 is reasoned, not observed.
8. **The mechanical ceiling is not a safety opinion.** Three of seven slipped a hip. Strength scales with
   area and load with volume. At some height this stops being a growth-plate problem.
