## => ⛔⛔⛔ PART 1 — **CORRECTION 1: IT IS THE WHOLE DRUG, NOT A FRAGMENT**

R169 said: *"`Triethylphosphine gold` is NOT a class analogue — it is the exact **fragment** auranofin delivers."*
**Wrong, and in the direction that matters.** The Methods name the compound in full:

> ⭐⭐⭐ ***"2,3,4,6-tetra-O-acetyl-1-thio-β-D-glucopyranosato-S (triethylphosphine) gold (0.3, 1.5 and 3 µg/ml)… supplied by **Smith, Kline & Dauelsberg GmbH**"***

**That is the complete IUPAC name of AURANOFIN, supplied by its own manufacturer.** Not a fragment, not an
analogue — **the drug.**

### ⛔ AND THAT BREAKS MY ARITHMETIC, BECAUSE THE MOLECULAR WEIGHT IS WRONG
I computed µM using **Et₃PAuCl (350.58)**. The correct MW is **auranofin, 678.48** — and the paper confirms
it: *"approximately **one-third** of the triethylphosphine gold compound is gold"* (29.0% by my calculation).

| | R169 (wrong) | ⭐ **R171 (correct)** |
|---|---|---|
| NOAEL 0.3 µg/mL | 0.86 µM | ⭐ **0.442 µM** |
| **LOAEL 1.5 µg/mL** | 4.28 µM | ⛔ **2.211 µM** |

| ⭐ **window vs TET1 IC₅₀ (0.076 µM)** | **11.3×** | ⛔ **5.8×** |

> ### ⛔⛔ **EVERY MARGIN IN R169 WAS 1.9× TOO GENEROUS, IN MY OWN FAVOUR. The therapeutic window is 5.8×, not 11.3×. Projected cartilage total gold is 45× over the cytotoxic threshold, not 23×. Measured nuclear gold (4.6–12.5 µM) is 2.1–5.7× over it, not 1.1–2.9×.**

## => ⛔⛔ PART 2 — **THE AUTHORS DID THE THERAPEUTIC COMPARISON THEMSELVES, AND IT IS THE MOST UNCOMFORTABLE NUMBER IN THE ARM**

> ⭐ ***"The concentration range of triethylphosphine gold investigated in the present studies was 0.3 to
> 3 µg/ml, corresponding to a gold concentration of 0.1 to 1 µg/ml, **comparable to the blood levels
> achieved during chrysotherapy** with triethylphosphine gold."***

And they give the human numbers, from auranofin's own development programme:
- **`Finkelstein 1976`** — auranofin **6–9 mg/day orally for 12 weeks → peak blood gold 0.9–1.2 µg/mL**
- **`Walz 1979`** — auranofin **6 mg/day for 4 weeks → mean serum gold ≈ 0.6 µg/mL**

| | as auranofin | **as gold** |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ chondrocyte **NOAEL** | 0.3 µg/mL | **0.1 µg/mL** |
| ⛔ chondrocyte **LOAEL (cytotoxic)** | 1.5 µg/mL | ⛔ **0.5 µg/mL** |
| ⛔⛔ **human serum, 6 mg/day, 4 wk** | — | ⛔ **0.6 µg/mL** |
| ⛔⛔ **human peak, 6–9 mg/day, 12 wk** | — | ⛔ **0.9–1.2 µg/mL** |

> ### ⛔⛔⛔ **HUMAN THERAPEUTIC BLOOD GOLD (0.6–1.2 µg/mL) EXCEEDS THE CONCENTRATION THAT KILLED CHONDROCYTES IN THIS ASSAY (0.5 µg/mL gold) BY 1.2–2.4×, AND EXCEEDS THE NO-EFFECT LEVEL BY 6–12×. The standard adult dose puts more gold in blood than the dose that was cytotoxic in a dish.**

### ⛔ AND THE ASSAY'S SPECIFICITY CONTROLS ARE STRONG, WHICH MAKES THIS HARDER TO DISMISS
The same system was **not** toxic to:
- ⭐ **aurothioglucose at 10 µg/mL** — which is **5 µg/mL gold, well ABOVE any therapeutic level**
- ⭐ **piroxicam, diclofenac sodium, sulindac sulphide and indomethacin** at synovial/plasma concentrations
  (their earlier paper, ref 13)

> ### ⛔ **SO THE ASSAY IS NOT INDISCRIMINATELY TOXIC. Five other antirheumatic drugs — including another gold compound at a supratherapeutic concentration — were clean, and auranofin alone was cytotoxic. I cannot wave this away as a fragile-culture artefact.**

## => ⭐⭐ PART 3 — **BUT THE RECONCILIATION IS REAL, IT IS ALBUMIN, AND IT IS QUANTIFIABLE**

The medium was **DMEM + 10% foetal bovine serum**. FBS albumin ≈ 23 g/L, so 10% gives ≈ **2.3 g/L ≈ 35 µM**.

| system | albumin | gold | ⭐ **albumin : gold** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⛔ **Kirkpatrick culture at the LOAEL** | ~35 µM | 2.21 µM | ⛔ **≈ 16 : 1** |
| ⭐ **human plasma at 6 mg/day** | ~600 µM | 1.6–3.1 µM | ⭐ **≈ 195–380 : 1** |

> ### ⭐⭐⭐ **HUMAN PLASMA HAS 12–24× MORE ALBUMIN BUFFERING CAPACITY PER UNIT GOLD THAN THE CULTURE DID. Au(I) binds albumin Cys34 rapidly and near-quantitatively (Nguyen: 50% of unbound drug consumed in 2–3 min), so at the same TOTAL concentration the free, cell-available fraction in vivo is roughly an order of magnitude lower. That is why children tolerate serum gold levels sitting above this assay's cytotoxic threshold.**

### ⭐ AND THE AUTHORS NAME THE SECOND REASON THEMSELVES
> ⭐ ***"In our system **matrix components were present in quantities much smaller than in the in vivo
> situation**."***
> ⭐ ***"The chondrocyte cytotoxicity of triethylphosphine gold in our experimental system **does not
> necessarily mean that this gold compound is damaging to human articular cartilage in vivo**."***

⭐ **A monolayer chondrocyte has almost no pericellular matrix. In situ it sits inside a sulphated
proteoglycan cage that — per Lawrence's own hypothesis — BINDS metal cations.** ⚠ **Both mitigations are
mechanistically sound and neither is measured.**

⭐ **Exposure was also acute:** cytotoxicity was read at **2 and 6 hours** (and 7/24/72 h in subculture) —
a bolus onto passage-II dedifferentiated cells, not a chronic steady state.

## => ⛔⛔⛔ PART 4 — **CORRECTION 2: THIS PAPER CONTRADICTS R165's CENTREPIECE**

R165's headline was Lawrence 1961: **articular cartilage 0.64 mg/100 g — the HIGHEST tissue assayed, 64×
plasma and 32× synovium.** I built the ~100 µM cartilage projection on it.

> ⛔⛔ **Kirkpatrick, citing `Gottlieb, Smith & Smith, Arthritis Rheum 1972;15:16–22` — a human autopsy
> tissue-gold survey in a chrysotherapy patient — states the opposite: *"the levels achieved in cartilage
> are **considerably LOWER** than, for example, in reticuloendothelial tissues, **SYNOVIUM**, renal cortex
> or adrenal glands."***

> ### ⛔⛔ **THESE CANNOT BOTH BE TRUE. And I already flagged the weakness in R165 myself: Lawrence's synovium value (0.02 mg/100 g) was anomalously LOW — below skin — from a single 0.03 g sample in one patient. If that synovium number is simply wrong, the 32× cartilage:synovium ratio collapses with it.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ AND NOTICE WHICH WAY THIS CUTS — IT IS NOT ALL BAD
| if cartilage gold is… | consequence |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **HIGH (Lawrence, ~100 µM)** | ⛔ 45× over the cytotoxic threshold — a **safety** problem |
| ⭐ **LOW (Gottlieb)** | ⭐ **the safety margin is fine** ⛔ but the question becomes whether **enough gold reaches the plate to engage TET1 at all** — an **efficacy** problem |

> ### ⭐⭐ **THE PROBLEM FLIPS RATHER THAN VANISHING. R169's "free fraction must be <4%" derivation was built on the high estimate; if Gottlieb is right, that derivation is unnecessary and the real question moves to the other end. Either way the dose conclusion is unchanged — start low, titrate on a measurement — but WHICH failure mode we are titrating away from is now genuinely unresolved.**
