A familial case of overgrowth syndrome caused by a 9q22.3 microdeletion in

ABSTRACT
Microdeletions in the 9q22.3 chromosomal region can cause macrosomia with characteristic features, including
prenatal-onset overgrowth, metopic craniosynostosis, hydrocephalus, developmental delay, and intellectual
disability, in addition to manifestations of nevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (NBCCS). Haploinsu ﬃciency of
PTCH1 may be responsible for accelerated overgrowth, but the mechanism of macrosomia remains to be elu-
cidated. We report a familial case with a 9q22.3 microdeletion, manifesting with prenatal-onset overgrowth in a
mother and post-natal overgrowth in her daughter. Although both were clinically diagnosed with NBCCS, they
had characteristic features of 9q22.3 microdeletion, especially the daughter. Microarray comparative genomic
hybridization analysis revealed a 4.0 Mb deletion of chromosome 9q22.3 in both individuals. Among the 11
reported patients of overgrowth and/or macrosomia, a 550 Kb region encompassing PTCH1, C9orf3, FANCC, and
5 miRNAs is the most commonly deleted region. The let-7 family miRNAs, which are involved in diverse cellular
processes including growth and tumor processes, were identi ﬁed in the deleted regions in 10 of 11 patients.
Characteristic features of 9q22.3 microdeletion might be associated with decreased expression of let-7.
1. Introduction
The 9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome is a disorder caused by the
deletion of a small piece of chromosome 9, including the protein pat-
ched homolog 1 ( PTCH1) gene, which is mutated in nevoid basal cell
carcinoma syndrome (NBCCS; MIM 109400). NBCCS, also known as
Gorlin syndrome, is an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by
the presence of basal cell carcinoma (BCC), jaw cysts, palmoplantar
pits, lamellar calci ﬁcation of the falx cerebri, ovarian ﬁbroma, and rib
anomalies ( Kimonis et al., 1997 ). As these manifestations appear in an
age-dependent manner, they are di ﬃcult to clinically diagnose and
distinguish in early childhood. 9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome man-
ifests with phenotypic features that are distinct from NBCCS, such as
prenatal-onset overgrowth, developmental delay, intellectual disability,
metopic craniosynostosis, hydrocephalus, and Wilms tumor ( Cayrol
et al., 2018; Kosaki et al., 2011 ; Muller et al., 2012).
Although overgrowth is not a common feature of NBCCS, it has been
reported in some patients diagnosed with 9q22.3 microdeletion syn-
drome. It is conceivable that prenatal-onset overgrowth in 9q22.3
microdeletion syndrome is not only associated with deletion of PTCH1,
but may also be related to other genetic factors, such as deletion of
growth suppressor genes on 9q22.3 or loss of the paternal allele ( Kosaki
et al., 2011 ; Shimojima et al., 2009 ). However, not much has been done
to clarify the pathophysiology of overgrowth associated with 9q22.3
microdeletion because fewer than 50 patients have been reported as of
2018 ( Cayrol et al., 2018 ). Here we report a familial case that was in-
correctly diagnosed as NBCCS, though it was ultimately proven to be
9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome, that presented with prenatal-onset
overgrowth in a mother and postnatal-onset overgrowth in her
daughter.

pregnancy and delivery. She exhibited macrosomia at birth; the weight,
length, and head circumference were 4100 g (+2.7 SD), 56 cm (+3.9
SD), and 37 cm (+2.9 SD), respectively. Overgrowth was observed
throughout childhood (height 173.3 cm, +2.9 SD; weight 75.2 kg,

Patient 2, the daughter of patient 1, was delivered at 37 weeks of
gestational age by cesarean section. The placental weight was 630 g
(50th to 90th percentile). She exhibited macrocephaly at birth; the
weight, length, and head circumference were 3070 g (+1.3 SD),
48.9 cm (+0.8 SD), and 35.5 cm (+2.1 SD), respectively ( Fig. 1 ).
Dysmorphic features were noted, including brachycephaly, frontal
bossing, broad nasal bridge, bilateral polydactyly, and hypertelorism.
Taken together, it was assumed that she had NBCCS shortly after birth.
Multiple compound nevi appeared at 4 years of age, and calci ﬁcation of
the falx cerebri and tentorium cerebelli was identi ﬁed at the age of 6
years. However, she had several atypical features of NBCCS, namely
ventricular septal defect and atrial septal defects, which closed spon-
taneously during infancy, hydrocephalus requiring surgical ventricle-
peritoneal shunting, and overgrowth (height 143.8 cm, +2.3 SD;
weight 39.9 kg, +1.9 SD, at 9 years of age) ( Fig. 1). Furthermore, she
had mild intellectual disability and speech delay. Cardiac ultrasound at
the age of 9 years showed mild mitral regurgitation and trivial tricuspid
regurgitation. G-band chromosomal analysis showed a normal female
karyotype.
Although both patients exhibited symptoms meeting the diagnostic
criteria of NBCCS, they had atypical manifestations of NBCCS.
Therefore, we conducted genetic analysis including microarray com-
parative genomic hybridization (CGH).
2. Methods and results
All experiments were approved by the ethics committee of Kitasato
University. Written informed consent for the present study was ob-
tained from the parent. Pathogenic mutations in the coding region of
PTCH1 were not detected by PCR-based direct sequencing. Multiplex
ligation-dependent probe ampli ﬁcation analysis revealed the deletion
of the entire PTCH1 gene in patients 1 and 2. Using a custom-designed
oligonucleotide microarray targeting a 20.8-Mb genome region sur-
rounding PTCH1 (Nagao et al., 2011 ), the size of the deletion was de-
termined to be 4.0 Mb. To characterize the junction sequence, PCR was
performed using LA Taq (Takara Bio Inc., Otsu, Japan) following the
manufacturer's instructions. Primers were designed based on the dele-
tion boundaries estimated using the array data. Primer sequences by
which the junction breakpoints were successfully ampli ﬁed were as
follows: G159F1, 5′ - GGTCTTCACTGTCATTAGGTGC-3 ′, G159R1, 5′ -
AAGGCAGCCATGGCCCCATCAC-3′. The PCR product was then isolated
from the gel using the QIAEX II gel extraction kit (Qiagen) and se-
quenced. The deletion ranged from 95,946,863 bp to 99,986,314 bp, a
region of 4.0 Mb, which includes PTCH1 (NCBI 36/hg18 build) leading
to a diagnosis of 9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome ( Figs. 2 and 3 ). The
deletions were identical at the nucleotide levels in both patients. The
novel microdeletion has been submitted to ClinVar ( https://www.ncbi.
nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/) with accession number of SCV000992361.
3. Discussion
Prenatal-onset overgrowth was observed in 20% of patients with
9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome ( Muller et al., 2012). Previous studies
have reported that overgrowth was associated with loss of the paternal
9q22
(Shimojima et al., 2009 ), but no imprinted genes have been
identiﬁed in this region. The 9q22.3 microdeletion patients with
overgrowth and/or macrosomia whose breakpoint is available are listed
in Fig. 2 (Garavelli et al., 2013 ; Isidor et al., 2013 ; Kosaki et al., 2011;
Muller et al., 2012 ; Redon et al., 2006 ; Reichert et al., 2015; Shimojima
et al., 2009; Yamamoto et al., 2009 ). Isidor et al. reported a familial
case exhibiting prenatal-onset overgrowth or macrocephaly with a
1.7 Mb deletion of the maternal allele. In the familial case, a mother
with a mosaic state showed only macrocephaly, whereas the daughter
with a non-mosaic state showed generalized prenatal-onset overgrowth
(Isidor et al., 2013 ). The deleted region common to the patients in Fig. 2
is 550 Kb located between 96,771,456 –97,322,234, encompassing
C9orf3, FANCC, PTCH1, and 5 miRNAs, including mir-6081, mir-23b,
mir-27b, mir-3074, mir-24-1. However, no documented associations

between these genes or miRNAs and pathological conditions, such as

Of note, let-7a-1, let-7f-1, and let-7d, which were distinct from the
550 Kb common region, were deleted in 10 of 11 patients ( Fig. 2 ). The
let-7 family plays an important role in the Lin28/let-7 pathway, which
is involved in growth, metabolism, puberty phenotypes, and cancer
(Zhu et al., 2011). Mammals have two Lin28 family members, and
enhanced Lin28b expression is involved in Wilms tumor via repression
of let-7 (Balzeau et al., 2017). Overexpression of Lin28a or repression of
let-7 delayed onset of puberty and enhanced glucose metabolism and
growth, whereas loss of Lin28a or overexpression of let-7 led to insulin
resistance and impaired glucose tolerance ( Zhu et al., 2011 ). Interest-
ingly, a patient with 9q22.3 microduplication spanning let-7 and PTCH1
developed type 2 diabetes mellitus, precocious puberty, and short sta-
ture ( Izumi et al., 2011). Therefore, 9q22.3 microdeletions, including
let-7, might contribute to decreased expression of let-7 and lead to
prenatal-onset overgrowth and Wilms tumor. However, there are
several patients with chromosomal deletions spanning let-7 and PTCH1
that do not show prenatal-onset overgrowth and further studies are
required to elucidate the mechanism of overgrowth ( Boonen et al.,
2005; Olivieri et al., 2003; Shimkets et al., 1996).
Here we presented a familial case with a 9q22.3 microdeletion that
includes let-7 and PTCH1. The daughter, who had loss of the maternal
allele, showed postnatal-onset overgrowth while her mother showed
prenatal-onset overgrowth. The let-7 family miRNAs, which are in-
volved in diverse cellular processes, are identi ﬁed in deleted regions in
10 of 11 patients. Characteristic features of 9q22.3 microdeletion, such
as prenatal-onset overgrowth and Wilms tumor, could be explained by
the decreased levels of let-7. Further study is required to elucidate the
relation of miRNAs and 9q22.3 microdeletion syndrome.
