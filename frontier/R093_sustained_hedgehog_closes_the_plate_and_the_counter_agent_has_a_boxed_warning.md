# F-R093 — Sustained Hedgehog closes the plate. That is our plan's failure mode, run as a genetic experiment. And the counter-agent is identified by an FDA boxed warning.

Five papers, two database hits, and the round turns on one of them. **The news is bad first and then
better than it has been.**

---

## 1. The structural objection: our plan already has a genetic experiment, and it failed

`Xiu Y, Gong Y, Luo Q, Ma X, Zhang L, Chen J. Suppressor of fused-restrained Hedgehog signaling in
chondrocytes is critical for epiphyseal growth plate maintenance and limb elongation in juvenile mice.
Front Cell Dev Biol 2022;10:1005499. PMC9479194.` Open access, found by search, not supplied.

They conditionally deleted **Sufu** — the principal intracellular brake on GLI — in Agc1-CreER
chondrocytes from two weeks of age. **Sufu deletion is constitutive, cell-autonomous, permanent Hedgehog
activation in exactly the cells we are targeting.** It is our proposal in its most potent possible form.

Section title, verbatim:

> **"Chondrocyte-specific deletion of Sufu in juvenile mice caused a *transient expansion* of growth
> plates followed by their *premature fusion*."**

| timepoint | Sufu-cKO growth plate |
|---|---|
| **P30** | **"obviously expanded"**, "enlarged growth plates of both tibiae and femurs" |
| P30, structure | *"this orderly structure was **lost**… instead occupied by a **poorly-organized mass of chondrocytes with heterogeneous morphology**"* |
| **P120** | **"prematurely closed"**, *"significant loss of Safranin O-stained cartilage"* |
| limbs | **shorter** |

**Read that table as our own experiment.** At day 30 it looks like success — a thicker plate, more
cartilage, exactly the readout Trompet and Li/Yang report. **At day 120 the plate is gone and the animal
is short.** The early expansion was not growth; it was the plate spending itself.

And Smo deletion — the opposite manipulation — **also** caused premature closure and shorter limbs.
Both directions fail:

> *"chondrocyte-specific ablation of Smo or Sufu accelerated or suppressed chondrocyte hypertrophy,
> respectively, whereas **both decreased chondrocyte proliferation and survival**."*
>
> *"the reason why inactivation and over-activation of Hh signaling exerted **the same detrimental
> effects** on chondrocytes is still unclear… **Hh signaling needs to be tightly regulated**."*

They also, in passing, sharpen the qualifier on Trompet that I under-weighted in F-R089: SAG at P31–P34
**"did not alter the number of CD73⁺ epiphyseal stem cells within the resting zone, but instead affected
their cycling"**, and **"both Smo agonist (SAG) and inhibitor (LDE225) treatments significantly decreased
number of columns in the proliferating zone."**

**This is the most serious objection in the file, and it is not a delivery problem or a dosing problem.
The Hedgehog axis is a homeostatic set-point with failure on both sides, and permanent elevation — the
thing "infinite growth" requires — is one of the two failure modes.**

## 2. The correction it forces on me: I conflated route with schedule

F-R022 concluded the exposure profile had to be **"transient, local, self-limiting."** In F-R089 I found
Trompet's systemic arm and Trompet's own line — *"3 intra-articular injections had a similar effect as 7
systemic injections"* — and wrote that the local requirement was dissolved. **That part was right. What I
then did wrong was read it as licensing a chronic systemic regimen.**

Look at what actually produced gains:

| study | schedule | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Trompet, systemic | **7 doses**, P30–36 | pool up, no length change in 8 days |
| Trompet, bead | **Gli1 signal gone by 3 weeks** | length diverging at 1, 2 and **6 months**; no OA |
| Li/Yang | **every other day**, ~3 weeks | +18.9% body, +32.1% vertebral (rescue) |
| **Xiu, Sufu-cKO** | **permanent** | **transient expansion → premature fusion → shorter limbs** |

**Route was never the constraint. Schedule is.** F-R022 had two words right — *transient* and
*self-limiting* — and one word wrong — *local*. I corrected the wrong word and discarded the right ones
with it. **Systemic is fine. Chronic is the failure mode.**

The Trompet bead is the existence proof that matters: **three weeks of exposure produced a length
divergence still widening five months later.** If a pulse buys durable length and the system returns to
baseline between pulses, repeated pulsing may accumulate without ever entering the Sufu regime. **Nobody
has tested repeated pulsing. That single experiment decides this programme.**

## 3. A new mechanism for senescence, from their reporter mice

Gli1-LacZ and Ptch1-LacZ knock-in reporters, tibial growth plates:

- Hh-responsive cells are the **resting and columnar** chondrocytes — *"almost no LacZ expression was
  observed in hypertrophic zones."* **This independently confirms my GSE288028 result in mouse: the
  responsive cell is the stem/proliferative compartment, not the hypertrophic one.**
- And: *"As the epiphyseal growth plates matured from **2 weeks to 4 months** of age, the numbers of
  LacZ-positive chondrocytes in **both resting and columnar zones tended to decrease gradually.**"*

**The Hedgehog-responsive population shrinks with age.** That is a candidate mechanism for growth-plate
senescence in its own right — and it means the agonist's own target population is contracting over
exactly the window we would want to treat. Start later, hit fewer cells.

Their mechanistic proposal also closes a loop we already have: *"Hh signaling suppresses hypertrophic
differentiation of chondrocytes by promoting **PTHrP** expression in the resting chondrocytes."* That is
the Ihh–PTHrP loop operating postnatally, and it puts **abaloparatide** (F-R089 Tier 5) directly
downstream of the effect we are trying to buy.

## 4. The counter-agent — and its direction is confirmed by an FDA boxed warning

Xiu's reference list pointed at the paper that changes the picture.

`Koyama E, Mundy C, Saunders C, Chung J, Catheline SE, Rux D et al. Premature growth plate closure caused
by a hedgehog cancer drug is preventable by co-administration of a retinoid antagonist in mice. J Bone
Miner Res 2021. PMID 33724538 · PMC9661967.`

LDE225 (sonidegib, an Hh **inhibitor**), 100 mg/kg gavage × 2 days in juvenile mice → shortened growth
plates, decreased proliferation, decreased *Ihh* and *Sox9*, **decreased number of reserve progenitors**,
then growth-plate involution and impaired lengthening.

**The mechanism is not Hedgehog. It is retinoid:**

> *"LDE225 treatment markedly **decreased the expression of retinoid catabolic enzyme Cyp26b1** within
> growth plate, whereas it **increased and broadened the expression of retinoid synthesizing enzyme
> Raldh3**, thus subverting normal homeostatic retinoid circuitries and **in turn accelerating maturation
> and closure**."*

> *"**All** such severe skeletal and molecular changes were **prevented** when LDE-treated mice were
> co-administered the selective retinoid antagonist **CD2665 (1.5 mg/kg/d)**, a drug targeting **retinoic
> acid receptor γ**, which is most abundantly expressed in growth plate."*

And the line that makes this an arm rather than a footnote:

> *"**When given alone, CD2665 elicited the expected maturation delay and growth plate expansion.**"*

Their general principle: *"drug-induced premature growth plate closures can be prevented or delayed by
targeting a **separate phenotypic regulatory mechanism** in chondrocytes."*

### The human direction check, and it is as strong as this evidence ever gets

**Palovarotene (Sohonos, Ipsen) is an approved selective RARγ *agonist*. Its label carries a BOXED
WARNING for premature physeal closure in growing children. Premature physeal closure occurred in
21 of 57 patients under 14 years of age — 36.8%.**

**So RARγ agonism closes the human growth plate, at FDA-label evidentiary standard. The antagonist
direction is therefore the one that holds it open — and in mice, alone, it expands the plate and delays
maturation.**

This is the first agent in this entire programme with a **human-validated closure lever**. Every previous
never-closing candidate (anastrozole, the ESR1 arm) works through oestrogen. **RARγ is a second,
independent axis, its direction is confirmed by an approved drug's boxed warning, and it was shown to
prevent exactly the lesion that Hedgehog manipulation produces.**

**And that is the counter-move for §1.** If sustained Hh activation causes premature fusion (Xiu), and
Hh-driven premature fusion runs through the retinoid circuit (Koyama), then **a RARγ antagonist is the
candidate agent that lets Hh activation be sustained without paying the closure penalty.** The honest
caveat, stated plainly: **Koyama's closure came from an Hh *inhibitor*, not an agonist.** Whether
over-activation closes the plate through the same Cyp26b1/Raldh3 circuit is untested. But both converge
on accelerated maturation, and CD2665 delays maturation regardless of what drove it.

## 5. Oral systemic Hedgehog agonism works in aged animals and is tolerated

`McKenzie JA, Maschhoff C, Liu X, Migotsky N, Silva MJ, Gardner MJ. Activation of hedgehog signaling by
systemic agonist improves fracture healing in aged mice. J Orthop Res 2019;37:51–59.` (supplied)

**18-month-old C57BL/6 females** — well past skeletal maturity. **Hh-Ag1.5 (XcessBio), 20 mg/kg,
20 mg/mL, daily ORAL GAVAGE.**

| readout | effect |
|---|---|
| Hh target genes in callus | significantly increased |
| callus healing score, POD14 | significantly higher |
| callus volume, POD21 | **+40%** |
| bone volume, POD21 | **+25%** |
| callus vascularity (3D µCT angiography), POD14 | **+85%** |
| mechanical strength, POD21 | significantly greater |
| **tolerability** | **<8% mean body-weight loss over the study** |

**Oral, systemic, daily, in an aged animal, tolerated.** That answers F-R092's ask #3 in the direction of
feasibility: the pathway remains pharmacologically activatable long after skeletal maturity, and an oral
route works. It does *not* show growth-plate effects — 18-month mouse plates are largely inactive — so it
speaks to route and tolerability, not to length.

*(Also supplied and consistent: `lee2016` — SAG produces dose-dependent calvarial defect healing, maximal
at 1.0 mM local; `kashiwagi2016` — local SAG accelerates fracture healing; `JPATH177003100` — Hh
activation in periosteum-derived MSCs induces bone formation in vivo, which is the same PDGFRα⁺
perichondrial population F-R020/F-R022 identified as the influx source.)*

## 7. Where the architecture now stands

| arm | agent | status |
|---|---|---|
| **pool** | SAG / SAG21k, **pulsed, systemic** | mechanism confirmed in human root cells (F-R092); **must not be chronic** (§1) |
| **never-closing — new** | **RARγ antagonist** (CD2665 in mice) | **human direction confirmed by palovarotene's boxed warning**; alone expands the plate and delays maturation |
| **never-closing — existing** | anastrozole | unchanged, oestrogen axis, independent of RARγ |
| **hypertrophy brake** | abaloparatide | now placed downstream of Hh via the Ihh→PTHrP loop (§3) |
| rate | erdafitinib, mecasermin | unchanged |
| **do no harm** | somatropin down + intermittent | unchanged (F-R089) |
| **dropped** | dexamethasone; FGSAs; KY19382 | F-R090, F-R092 |

**The single most important open experiment, stated as precisely as I can:** *does repeated pulsed
Smoothened agonism accumulate length, or does it converge on the Sufu-cKO phenotype?* Trompet's bead says
one pulse compounds for six months. Xiu says permanent activation fuses the plate by P120. **Nobody has
run anything in between, and everything depends on which way that goes.**
