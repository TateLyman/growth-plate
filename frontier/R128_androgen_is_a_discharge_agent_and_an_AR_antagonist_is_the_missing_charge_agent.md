## 1. THE PAPER, LOCATED — AND WHY FIVE DATABASE SEARCHES MISSED IT

**Kang H-Y, Chen Y-J, Huang K-E, Chang C.** *"Loss of androgen receptor suppresses chondrogenic
proliferation during endochondral ossification in mice."* **43rd Annual European Calcified Tissue Society
Congress, Rome, 14–17 May 2016, poster P58.** Chang Gung University Kaohsiung + University of Rochester.

**It is a conference abstract in Bone Abstracts, which is not PubMed-indexed.** That is precisely why
`TITLE:"androgen receptor" AND TITLE:chondro*` returned zero — the search was correct, the venue was
outside the index.

**Content:**
- **Col2-ARKO male mice have SHORTER bone length than wild-type**
- delayed endochondral bone formation, impaired chondrocyte proliferation
- mechanism: **AR promotes chondrogenic IGF-1 expression by demethylating H3-K27**, thereby promoting
  proliferation; **AR silencing reduced proliferation and was rescued by IGF-1 activation**
- ⚠ **no zone-specific quantification** — no resting/proliferative/hypertrophic breakdown

**⚠ EVIDENTIARY WEIGHT: it never became a full paper.** Kang H-Y has ten PubMed-indexed papers on androgen
and bone; none is this study. A 2016 abstract unpublished ten years later is weak evidence on its own, and
**it does not contain the zone data that was the reason to want it.**

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ BUT THE SAME GROUP PUBLISHED SOMETHING STRONGER, AND IT POINTS THE OTHER WAY

**Huang C-K, Tsai M-Y, Luo J, Kang H-Y, Lee S-O, Chang C.** *Biochim Biophys Acta* 2013;1833:1222–34
(PMID 23333872) — **"Suppression of androgen receptor enhances the self-renewal of mesenchymal stem cells
through elevated expression of EGFR."**

- **BM-MSCs and adipose-derived MSCs from AR-knockout mice have HIGHER self-renewal than wild-type**
- AR knockdown in MSC lines: same result
- mechanism: AR depletion → **EGFR** activation → Erk and Akt
- ⭐ **ASC-J9 (AR degradation enhancer), hydroxyflutamide (AR antagonist), and AR-siRNA ALL enhanced
  self-renewal** — three independent routes, one direction

And the companion paper (PMID 23859805, *Stem Cell Res* 2013): **AR loss suppresses osteogenesis and
promotes adipogenesis** in BMSCs via IGFBP3-mediated IGF signalling. **AR holds progenitors on the
osteo/chondro lineage and away from fat.**

## 3. ⭐⭐ THE RECLASSIFICATION — ANDROGEN IS A **DISCHARGE** AGENT

Four independent lines now say the same thing:

| source | finding |
|---|---|
| Raz 2005 | testosterone **decreases** resting-zone DNA synthesis; DHT does nothing there |
| Peralta 1994 | testosterone accelerates growth **"then depleting the source of stem cells"** |
| Kang 2016 (abstract) | **AR loss → shorter bone**, impaired chondrocyte proliferation |
| **Huang/Kang/Chang 2013** | **AR suppression → HIGHER stem-cell self-renewal**, by three independent routes |

> **AR converts stem/progenitor cells into differentiated output. Remove it and the pool is preserved but
> the bone is shorter. Activate it and the pool is spent, the bone is longer, and then it runs out.**
> **Androgen is not a charge agent. It is a DISCHARGE agent — and it is the only discharge agent in this
> file with a length endpoint attached.**

This is exactly R119's framework. R119 established that **every** pool expander produces *pool without
flux* — it expands by blocking the exit — and that the decisive unrun experiment is *"charge with mTORC1,
discharge with vismodegib… **no bone length was measured after the discharge step, in that paper or any
other.**"*

**Androgen is a discharge agent that HAS a length endpoint** (Peralta: +length; Kang: AR loss → shorter).

---

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ AND THEREFORE — AN AR ANTAGONIST IS A **CHARGE** AGENT

If AR suppression raises self-renewal by three separate routes, then **anti-androgens expand the progenitor
pool.** That completes the pair R119 said had never been assembled:

| phase | agent | status |
|---|---|---|
| **CHARGE** | **AR antagonist** — bicalutamide, hydroxyflutamide, ASC-J9, enzalutamide | **obtainable; bicalutamide and enzalutamide are approved** |
| **DISCHARGE** | **androgen** — already running at ~2× endogenously via the AI (R127) | **already in the stack** |

**A charge-then-discharge cycle built from AR antagonism followed by AR agonism.** Both halves obtainable,
the discharge half already present, and the switch between them is a single drug being started and stopped.

### ⚠ THE CAVEATS, AND THEY ARE SERIOUS
1. **The self-renewal result is in bone-marrow and adipose MSCs — NOT growth-plate resting-zone
   chondrocytes.** Different cells, different niche. This is a hypothesis by analogy, not a demonstration.
   The one experiment that would test it in cartilage is the Col2-ARKO zone data, **which does not exist.**
2. **During the charge phase an AR antagonist removes the androgen signal the plate depends on** — Kang's
   own finding is that AR loss shortens bone. Charge would cost growth while it ran.
3. **AR loss promotes adipogenic drift** (PMID 23859805). A charge phase risks the fate problem R117 and
   R118 identified, in a new form.
4. In a male at bone age 16, AR blockade also removes the anabolic and pubertal androgen signal
   systemically. This is not a small intervention.

## 5. THE NAAS VERDICT IS UNCHANGED

Nothing here reopens it. R127's argument stands: **DHT is the active species, the plate makes it locally
via an unsaturated SRD5A1, and the AI already doubles the substrate feeding that enzyme.** A NAAS remains a
dose increment on a pathway already running at double strength.

**What Kang changes is not whether to add androgen, but what androgen IS in the ledger:** it moves from
"velocity agent, redundant" to **"discharge agent, already present at 2×, and the only one with a length
endpoint."**

**And it sharpens R127's contraindication.** If androgen is the discharge arm, then **finasteride does not
merely blunt a redundant velocity agent — it disables the stack's discharge step**, at the required
enzymatic conversion, silently.

## 6. WHAT I STILL NEED

**The Col2-ARKO growth-plate zone measurements do not exist in the public record.** The 2016 abstract has
no zone data and never became a paper. If you can reach the authors, the single question that decides §4 is:

> **In Col2-ARKO mice, is the RESTING ZONE larger or smaller than wild-type, and is resting-zone cell
> number increased?**

If AR loss *expands* the resting zone while shortening the bone, the charge/discharge pair in §4 is real and
this becomes the most important arm in the programme. If the resting zone is unchanged or smaller, §4
collapses and androgen is discharge-only.
