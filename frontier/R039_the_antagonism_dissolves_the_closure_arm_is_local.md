# F-R039 — The central antagonism dissolves: oestrogen's growth arm is systemic, its closure arm is local, and they are separable

**Branch:** `claude/height-enhancement-research-v34b4r`
**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Status:** The Term A / Term C conflict I flagged as unresolved and never closed is **resolved, in a
mammal, with the separation demonstrated genetically.** The acetyl-CoA lever takes a hard hit.

---

## 1. The problem this round solves

Since F-R028 this branch has carried an unresolved tension, logged as U12 and never answered:

> *Every documented human with ER or aromatase loss grows **slowly** — 0.3–1.3 cm/yr — and continuously.
> The pubertal growth spurt is oestrogen-driven. So "never close" and "fast" may be antagonistic through
> oestrogen itself.*

If true, that antagonism is fatal to the whole goal: you cannot have a plate that never closes **and**
runs fast, because the thing that makes it run fast is the thing that closes it.

**It is not true. The two arms run through different tissues.**

---

## 2. The separation, demonstrated

The **Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻** mouse — ERα deleted **only in cartilage** (Col2α1-Cre × ERα^flox/flox) — against the
whole-body **ERα⁻/⁻**. From Börjesson et al. and reviewed in *Cell Mol Life Sci* (PMC11114058):

| | whole-body ERα⁻/⁻ | **cartilage-only Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻** |
|---|---|---|
| growth during sexual maturation (male) | **reduced** → shorter bones | **normal** |
| serum IGF-I / GH secretion | **significantly reduced**, disturbed (reduced hepatic MUP) | intact |
| growth in old age | continued; **tibia +8.3%, plate height +18%** at 16–19 mo | **continued** — *"local ERα in the growth plate is important for the normal reduction in longitudinal bone growth seen in elderly mice"* |
| **supraphysiological E2 challenge** (gonadectomised) | plate height reduced | **NO reduction in plate height, in either sex** |
| E2 effect on bone mass, uterus, thymus | — | **normal in both genotypes** |

The authors' own conclusion on the first row:

> *"This demonstrates that in male mice, **indirect, probably GH/IGF-I-mediated effects NOT requiring ERα
> in growth plate cartilage** are responsible for the role of ERα to modulate skeletal growth during
> early sexual maturation… ERα via altered activity in the GH/IGF-I axis is of importance for the
> **pubertal growth spurt**."*

And on the challenge:

> *"both female and male WT mice displayed reduced growth plate height after E2 treatment… **this effect
> was not seen in either female or male Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ mice**, demonstrating an **essential role of
> cartilage-located ERα** for the effect of high-dose E2 to reduce growth plate height."*

> ### Deleting ERα in cartilage alone gives: a normal pubertal growth spurt, normal systemic oestrogen action on bone mass / uterus / thymus, complete resistance to supraphysiological E2 closing the plate, and continued growth into old age.

**That is Terms A and B together at normal velocity, in one animal, without the systemic costs.**

**And it explains why the human cases are slow.** Aromatase deficiency and whole-body ERα mutation remove
oestrogen action *everywhere* — including the GH/IGF-I amplification that drives the spurt. **The slow
growth of those men is not a property of an open plate; it is a property of a broken systemic axis.**
A cartilage-restricted block would not carry it.

**Mechanism, and it lands on the right compartment.** In WT mice the E2-induced plate reduction was
*"due to a reduction of the **proliferative zone**, while the height of the hypertrophic zone was
unchanged"*; Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ showed no change in either. That is `A` — amplification — the same term
Longshanks selection moved and the same term F-R033 identified as the one selection can reach.
Consistent with *"E2 accelerates the proliferative exhaustion, and thereby senescence, of growth plate
chondrocytes."*

**The dose-direction is confirmed by its mirror.** ERαAF-1⁰ mice carry a **hyperactive** ERα: growth
ceased, tibia **−4.9%**, and *"the proximal tibial growth plates were **closed in all old ERαAF-1⁰ mice**
while they were **open in all WT mice**."* So the axis is bidirectional and dose-graded — hyperactive
ERα closes, absent cartilage ERα cannot be closed. And *"the process of growth plate senescence and
closure is dependent on growth plate-located ERα via a signaling pathway that **does not require ERα
AF-1**"* — which names the branch to target.

## 3. What this does to the goal

**Before this round:** never-close was solved but appeared to cost velocity — an open plate producing
0.3–1.3 cm/yr forever. "Fast" had to be won *against* the closure requirement.

**After:** never-close and normal velocity are compatible, because the two arms are in different tissues.
**"Fast" no longer has to beat "never close" — it has to be added on top of a normal-velocity,
non-closing plate.**

That is a materially easier problem, and it reframes the target. **Sustained normal pubertal velocity —
roughly 8–10 cm/yr — held indefinitely does not require exceeding any human rate that has ever been
observed.** It requires holding one. Ten years of it is +80–100 cm without a single superhuman step.

**The remaining open term is therefore narrower than I have been treating it.** Not *"how do we make a
plate exceed human maximum velocity"* but *"how do we hold an adult plate in the pubertal state."*

## 4. What this does NOT show — stated before anyone builds on it

1. **Mouse growth plates do not normally fuse.** "Never close" is weaker evidence in mouse than human by
   construction. The **strong** part is the E2 challenge: supraphysiological E2 could not thin the plate.
   The age-related part is real but is a slowing, not a fusion.
2. **"Normal growth" is normal, not enhanced.** Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ mice grew like WT during maturation. This
   removes a brake; it does not add a driver.
3. **No human has a cartilage-restricted ER defect.** Every human datapoint is ligand-level (aromatase)
   or whole-body (ESR1), i.e. the *slow* configuration. The separation is mouse-only.
4. **The magnitude of continued growth in Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ at one year is not given in the review.** The
   +8.3% / +18% figures are whole-body ERα⁻/⁻ at 16–19 months.
5. **Delivery**: a cartilage-restricted block in a human would need either a cartilage-targeted agent or a
   receptor-level intervention. F-R036's transport work says small molecules reach the plate freely, and
   `horike2026` supplies the targeting chemistry — *"cystine-dense peptides and **octaarginine**
   preferentially accumulate in cartilage"* — with the cationic charge that Williams's charge hypothesis
   predicts should partition into a polyanionic matrix.

## 5. The acetyl-CoA lever takes a hard hit

F-R038 nominated raising glycolytic flux / acetyl-CoA → histone acetylation → FGFR3 down. **The one
available way to push acetylation up in humans points the wrong way.**

**Valproic acid**, an HDAC inhibitor at therapeutic concentrations, is **associated with short stature in
children**. In fetal rat metatarsals it *"markedly suppressed longitudinal growth"* and *"suppressed
chondrocyte proliferation."* Mechanism reported: histone acetylation → **COX-2 up → caspase-3 →
apoptosis** in growth plate chondrocytes. It also *"repressed chondrogenesis… and reduced expression of
Sox5, Sox6, Sox9 and type 2 collagen."*

There is also a mechanistic reason to expect this: **HDAC4 represses chondrocyte hypertrophy** (its loss
causes ectopic hypertrophy and premature ossification), so global HDAC inhibition **de-represses
hypertrophy** — accelerating the spend rather than sparing the pool.

**And the gain arm remains untested, which I confirmed at source rather than by inference.** I pulled the
GEO metadata for `Kobayashi 2023`. **GSE192971**, verbatim: *"To test the consequence of **reduced**
glycolysis and **reduced** cytoplasmic acetyl-CoA in mouse skeletal development."* Both series are
loss-of-function by design. **Nobody has raised acetyl-CoA in a normal plate and measured length**, and
the nearest available proxy — global HDAC inhibition — shortens bones in children.

> **Verdict: the acetyl-CoA lever is downgraded from "best-supported open candidate" to "mechanistically
> coherent but with a human negative on its only accessible proxy."** The *chain* explaining flaw one
> (hypoxia → glucose → acetyl-CoA → FGFR3 → jammed reserve) stands as an explanation of the failure mode.
> Running it backwards as a therapy does not.

| term | status |
|---|---|
| **never close** | **solved, and now solved without a velocity cost** — the closure arm is cartilage-local ERα, separable from the systemic growth arm. Mouse-demonstrated, human-untested in the restricted form |
| **unlimited** | **follows from A** in the Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ configuration: continued growth into old age with no plate thinning under E2 challenge. The pool question is unresolved but no longer obviously binding, because these plates kept growing for a year |
| **fast** | **the only genuinely open term — and its target has changed.** Not "exceed human maximum" but "hold the pubertal state." No demonstrated lever adds velocity without spending the pool; but velocity no longer has to be won against closure |

## 8. Corrections carried

- **U12 (fast versus never-close antagonism) — resolved, negatively.** The antagonism was an artefact of
  every available human and whole-body model blocking oestrogen systemically. It is not intrinsic.
- **F-R038's acetyl-CoA nomination — downgraded**, on the valproate human negative and the HDAC4
  mechanism, with GEO confirming no gain-arm experiment exists.
- **The reading of the human aromatase/ESR1 cases is revised.** Their slow growth is not evidence that
  open plates grow slowly; it is evidence that a broken systemic GH/IGF-I axis grows slowly. Those two
  were confounded in every round of this branch up to now.
