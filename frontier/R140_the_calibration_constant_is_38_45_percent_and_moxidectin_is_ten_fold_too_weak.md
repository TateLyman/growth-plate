## => ⭐⭐⭐ THE ROUTE INSIGHT, WHICH FALLS OUT OF THE COLLIE DATA AND IS THE MOST USEFUL THING HERE

**In P-gp-deficient dogs: oral 0.4× label = coma and death. Topical 5× label = nothing at all.**

The difference is the **shape of the exposure curve.** Oral gives a sharp 3.7-hour peak; topical gives
a slow, flat, sustained release — the FDA data show topical tmax ranging from **1 day to 21 days**.

> ### **AND A FLAT, LOW, SUSTAINED CURVE IS EXACTLY WHAT THE MAGNITUDE LADDER WANTS.** The growth plate integrates over months; the CNS is injured by peaks. **Oral dosing maximises the toxicity we fear and wastes exposure on a spike the growth plate cannot use. Topical delivery inverts both.**

**So the ideal object is now fully specified, and it is not hypothetical chemistry:**

| requirement | why | already exists as |
|---|---|---|
| **~10× moxidectin's potency** | to reach 38–45% engagement at achievable plasma levels | ⭐ **selamectin (12.3×)** |
| **topical / sustained-release route** | flat curve; 5× label safe even in P-gp-null animals | ⭐ **selamectin is formulated topically (Revolution/Stronghold)** |
| **avermectin/milbemycin chemotype** | mechanistically validated: dnTCF mimicry, TCF-VP16 rescue, **bryostatin inactive as an internal control** | ⭐ **the whole family** |

> ⛔ **AND THE HOLE IS EXACTLY ONE THING: there is no HUMAN-APPROVED member of this family with
> selamectin-class potency. Moxidectin is human-approved and too weak; selamectin has the right
> potency and the right route and is veterinary-only. That is the entire remaining gap, and it is a
> regulatory gap, not a scientific one.**
