## 1. `jin2021` (J Endocrinol 250:13–24) read in full — the lever

**Protocol:** 3-week-old female C57BL/6, n = 6/group. **PHTPP** (selective ERβ antagonist) or **MPP**
(selective ERα antagonist), **0.3 mg/kg/day i.p., 5 days/week, from 4 to 12 weeks** — pre-puberty to end
of puberty. Systemic administration; no targeting.

**PHTPP (ERβ blockade) results:**

| endpoint | direction |
|---|---|
| **femur length** | **increased, P < 0.01** |
| **femur growth plate height** | **increased, P < 0.01** |
| **proliferative zone height** | **increased** |
| hypertrophic zone height | **unchanged** |
| **PZ / HZ ratio** | **highest of any group** |
| **PCNA proliferation** | **increased** |
| **TUNEL apoptosis** | **no difference** |
| Col2a1, Acan, Sox9, Col10a1, MMP13, Runx2 | **all increased** |
| **PTHrP** | **increased** |
| **Ihh** | **decreased** |
| local IGF-1 (IHC and qPCR) | **unchanged** |
| **serum GH and IGF-1** | **higher than control and MPP** |
| lumbar spine length | no difference |
| vertebral GP height | no difference (Suppl. Fig 1) |

The authors' mechanism: *"ERβ blocking can promote appendicular bone growth via the **Ihh/PTHrP
signaling pathway rather than the GH/IGF-1 pathway in situ**."*

**Why this is the best-shaped lever this programme has found:**

1. **It moves `A`, the amplification term, specifically.** PZ up, **HZ unchanged**, PZ/HZ ratio highest.
   That is the exact signature of the term Longshanks selection moved (+32% cells/column), the term
   Börjesson showed E2 suppresses (*"a reduction of the proliferative zone, while the hypertrophic zone
   was unchanged"*), and the one term that multiplies output while appearing nowhere in `dn/dt`.
2. **It does not work by killing fewer cells.** TUNEL unchanged — so it is not an apoptosis artefact.
3. **PTHrP up, Ihh down** is the loop shifted toward *delaying hypertrophic commitment* — `b` down,
   which is the favourable direction on `a − b`, arrived at from a completely different pathway than
   anything previously in this branch.
4. **It raises the systemic drive as a bonus**, not a cost: serum GH and IGF-1 higher.
5. **It needs no cartilage targeting.** Systemic i.p. dosing worked. That sidesteps FLAW 8 entirely for
   this arm.

**And MPP is the control that validates the whole ERα story.** Systemic ERα blockade *decreased* femur
length, GP height, PZ and PCNA — reproducing whole-body ERα⁻/⁻ (Börjesson: IGF-1 −20%, MUP −24%) and the
human aromatase/ESR1 phenotype. **Systemic ERα blockade loses the spurt; that is why the ERα arm has to
be cartilage-restricted.** Two independent experimental systems now say so.

## 2. FLAW 1 (axial) — substantially repaired, by the other receptor

F-R040 recorded as structural that this is a limb theory: ERα work moves femur and not spine
(Börjesson crown-rump 2.1% ± 1.7% ns; *"eunuchoid habitus"*), and `jin2021` found no lumbar effect.

**But `jin2021`'s discussion points at the answer, and the primary confirms it:**

> **Chagin et al. 2004** (*JBMR*, PMID 14753739): *"Young adult (4-month-old) **ERβ⁻/⁻ mice demonstrated
> an increased AXIAL- and APPENDICULAR-skeletal growth**, supporting the notion that ERβ inhibits
> skeletal growth in young adult female mice."* Conclusion: *"**ERβ is a physiological inhibitor of
> appendicular- and axial-skeletal growth**."*

**The two receptors have different anatomical reach.** ERα is appendicular-selective (Vidal 1999: ERα⁻/⁻
appendicular decreased, *"axial skeleton was normal, crown–rump length 98% of control"*). **ERβ inhibits
both.** So the ERβ arm reaches the spine and the ERα arm does not.

**The residual, and it is a real one:** `jin2021`'s PHTPP, given *during puberty only* (4–12 weeks), did
**not** move lumbar length or vertebral GP height, while genetic lifelong ERβ⁻/⁻ *did* increase axial
growth by 4 months. **Either the axial effect needs longer or more complete blockade than 8 weeks of
0.3 mg/kg/day, or it is a late/adult effect.** Unresolved, but the ceiling is no longer obviously capped
at half of stature.

## 3. NEW FLAW — and it is in Term A, which I had called solved

From the same Chagin 2004 abstract:

> *"the growth plates were **consistently FUSED in the appendicular skeleton of 18-month-old female
> ERα⁻/⁻ mice**. This fusion of growth plates, caused by a prolonged exposure to supraphysiological
> levels of estradiol in female ERα⁻/⁻ mice, **must be mediated through ERβ** because old ERα⁻/⁻β⁻/⁻ mice
> displayed **unchanged, unfused growth plates**."*

> ### Blocking ERα alone does not give permanent non-closure. Those plates fused — and only the double knockout stayed open.

This matters because every "never close" result this branch has relied on is an **ERα** result, and
Börjesson followed his mice to **12 months**. Chagin followed to **18** and found fusion.

**Three caveats, stated honestly, because they bound how bad this is:**

1. **The driver is an artefact of the model.** Chagin states it explicitly: *"the ERα⁻/− and the
   ERα⁻/−β⁻/−, **but not the ERβ⁻/−**, mouse models have clearly increased serum levels of estradiol."*
   Whole-body ERα loss breaks HPG feedback and produces compensatory hyperoestrogenaemia. A
   **cartilage-restricted** ERα block should not — Börjesson's Col2α1-ERα⁻/⁻ had entirely normal systemic
   oestrogen responses.
2. **It still proves ERβ *can* mediate fusion**, given enough oestrogen for long enough. Any protocol
   that leaves oestrogen elevated leaves that route open.
3. **The human case cuts the other way.** The ERα-mutant man resisted a 10× oestradiol challenge with
   ERβ intact and did not close. Either human ERβ does not do this, or the exposure was far shorter than
   Chagin's 18 months (most of a mouse lifespan).

**Net effect on the theory: Term A is downgraded from "solved" to "solved for the ERα route, with a
demonstrated ERβ-mediated escape under sustained high oestrogen."** And the fix is the same molecule as
the velocity lever — **blocking ERβ closes the escape route and raises growth at the same time.**

## 4. The theory, updated: it converges on two receptors

> **Block ERβ systemically** — raises `A` (PZ up, HZ unchanged, PZ/HZ ratio up), raises PCNA without
> raising apoptosis, shifts PTHrP up / Ihh down, raises serum GH and IGF-1, **reaches the axial skeleton**,
> and **removes the ERβ-mediated fusion escape**.
>
> **Block ERα in cartilage only** — removes the local closure and age-related-slowing signal while
> preserving the systemic GH/IGF-1 spurt. Must be cartilage-restricted: systemic ERα blockade loses the
> spurt (MPP, whole-body ERα⁻/⁻, every human case).

Both halves now have direct experimental support, they act on different compartments, and — unusually
for this programme — **they do not cancel.** The ERβ arm supplies velocity and axial reach; the ERα arm
supplies non-closure without the systemic penalty.

## 5. `henry2012` — Sox9 postnatally

Read for completeness because PHTPP raises Sox9. **Postnatal Sox9 inactivation causes stunted growth with
decreased proliferation, increased cell death and de-differentiation**; in the IVD it causes *"disk
compression and subsequent degeneration."* **Sox9 is required postnatally** — but this is loss-of-function
only, so it establishes Sox9 as a necessary maintenance factor and a downstream marker, **not a lever
with a demonstrated gain arm.** Same restoration/elevation trap as CREB and acetyl-CoA. Recorded, not
promoted.
