# F-R052 — The dose–response is a threshold, HSD17B1 is dead, and every node has a counter-move

**Branch:** `claude/height-enhancement-research-v34b4r`
**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Status:** Nine documents read in full. **The FGFR dose–response exists and it is not the gradient the
paper's summary claims — it is a threshold, and the safe window is real and measurable.** The HSD17B1
arm is dead three ways over. **And the deep finding: the oestrogen system answers every single blockade
with a compensatory move, including one measured inside chondrocytes that I had no idea about.**

---

## 1. PROPEL 2 — the dose–response, and what the abstract does not say

**Savarirayan R et al., N Engl J Med 2024 (NEJMoa2411790), PROPEL 2.** Change from baseline in annualised
height velocity at 6 months, by cohort:

| cohort | dose (mg/kg/day) | N | **Δ AHV (cm/yr)** | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.016 | 8 | **−1.82** | −4.96 to 1.32 |
| 2 | 0.032 | 19 | **+1.13** | 0.47 to 1.79 |
| 3 | 0.064 | 16 | **−0.06** | −0.93 to 0.81 |
| 4 | 0.128 | 16 | **+0.94** | 0.15 to 1.74 |
| **5** | **0.25** | **13** | **+3.38** | **1.67 to 5.10** |

The paper says *"a dose-dependent increase in the annualized height velocity was observed."* **Look at the
column. Cohort 1 is negative, cohort 3 is null, and cohorts 2 and 4 are indistinguishable from each other
across a 4-fold dose difference.**

> ### This is not a dose–response gradient. It is a threshold: nothing below 0.25 mg/kg, and a clear effect at 0.25. There is no "low dose that gives modest growth" — below the top dose there is essentially nothing.

**And cohort 5 over time shows the front-loading signature again:** **+3.38 cm/yr at 6 months → +2.51 at
12 → +2.50 at 18.** Partial decay to a plateau. Height z-score +0.54; upper-to-lower segment ratio
2.02 → 1.88 (−0.12). Increases in 10 of 11 patients at 18 months; ≥25% over baseline in 8 of 11.

### The safety contrast is the answer I have been chasing for four rounds

**72 children, mean exposure 485 ± 83 days, doses 0.016–0.25 mg/kg:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **slipped capital femoral epiphysis** | **zero** |
| bone-related adverse events | ***"No accelerated progression of bone age, changes in bone mineral density, or other bone-related adverse events were reported"*** |
| **hyperphosphataemia** | **one patient**, cohort 3, grade 1 (6.2 mg/dl vs ULN 6.0), resolved on interruption, did not recur at half the dose |
| corneal or retinal disorders | **none** |
| serious adverse events, grade 4–5, discontinuations | **none** |
| drug-related events | 7 patients (10%), **all mild** |

**Set that against Farouk Sait: 7 patients on erdafitinib or Debio1347 at oncology doses — 7 of 7 grew
faster and 3 of 7 slipped a hip, with hyperphosphataemia in all.**

The paper states the dose gap explicitly: *"More than 1100 adult patients have been exposed to infigratinib
monotherapy at doses up to 200 mg per day in oncology studies. To date, doses of infigratinib **lower by a
factor of 10 to 100** than those used in oncology studies have been used in children with achondroplasia."*

> ### The safe window exists, it is measured, and it is the achondroplasia dose range. Every published SCFE occurred at oncology-range dosing. Seventy-two children at 0.016–0.25 mg/kg over sixteen months produced no bone toxicity of any kind — and no bone-age advance.
>
> **It buys +2.5 cm/yr, not 19. That is the trade, and it is now quantified rather than guessed.**

**One thing the investigators say outright, which matters for anyone reading the 0.25 mg/kg ceiling as
arbitrary:** the dose was selected *"on the basis of maximum efficacy while balancing safety
considerations, **including the potential for excessive growth**."* **They stopped there deliberately.
Nobody has tested higher in a growing child outside oncology.**

**Caveat that bounds all of it:** achondroplasia is **mutant** FGFR3. What 0.25 mg/kg does to a wild-type
plate is unmeasured.

## 2. Irosustat — the enzyme is completely blocked and the hormones barely move

Three trials read in full.

**Stanway 2006 (phase I, 5 days):** median STS inhibition **98% in peripheral blood lymphocytes, 99% in
breast tumour tissue.** Serum E1, E2, **androstenediol** and DHEA all fell significantly; *"unexpectedly,
androstenedione and testosterone concentrations also decreased."*

**Coombes 2013 (dose escalation, 1 / 5 / 20 / 40 / 80 mg, n = 50):**

| endpoint | result |
|---|---|
| **STS inhibition at 28 days** | **≥95% in all evaluated patients at 5, 20, 40 and 80 mg** (and 1 of 2 at 1 mg) |
| **E2 reduction** | **28–75%; significant only at 40 mg — ratio 0.72 (95% CI 0.54–0.96)** |
| **androstenediol reduction** | **59–81%**, significant except at 80 mg |
| **E1S** | **increased, geometric mean ratio 1.21 to 3.52** |
| DHEAS | increased in all five groups; DHEAS:DHEA ratio 2.00 to 6.59 |
| dose–response | ***"No clear dose-response relationship was evident"*** for E2, androstenediol, E1, E1S, androstenedione, DHEA, DHEAS or testosterone |
| doses | OBD 5–40 mg; **recommended dose 40 mg**; MTD 80 mg; t½ ≈ 24–30 h; blood:plasma 166–586× |

> **Complete enzyme blockade produces a 28% fall in circulating oestradiol. The enzyme is not the
> bottleneck the plasma readout reflects.**

**Palmieri 2017 (IPET, 2 weeks pre-surgery in early breast cancer) rescues it, in tissue:**
**Ki67 fell in 6 of 7 patients, median −52.3% (P = 0.028)**, with FLT-PET responses; STS fell in tumours
with high basal STS, and *"significant decreases were also noted in aromatase, and 17β-hydroxysteroid
dehydrogenase type 1 and 2."* **In the one patient with low baseline STS expression, Ki67 rose 19.7%.**

> ### A 28% fall in plasma oestradiol corresponded to a 52% fall in tissue proliferation. The plasma number understates the tissue effect by a wide margin — which is the resolution of the IRIS null in F-R051, and it means serum oestradiol is the wrong endpoint for judging this arm.

**And two mechanistic facts from Stanway that I did not have:**

- **E1S has a plasma half-life of 10–12 hours against 20–30 minutes for unconjugated oestrogens.** That is
  why it is the reservoir — it is not just more abundant, it persists 20–30× longer.
- **E1S enters tissue through a specific transporter, organic anion transporter polypeptide B (OATP-B).**
  **That is a fourth intervention node nobody in this programme has considered: block the uptake of the
  sulfate rather than its hydrolysis.**

## 3. The human growth plate is an intracrine organ — confirmed, with one important boundary

**Öz OK et al., J Mol Endocrinol 2001;27:249 — the human primary.** Growth plates from an adolescent male
and female undergoing **epiphysiodesis**: aromatase transcripts detected by RT-PCR in both, and
**aromatase protein localised mainly to the zone of maturation and the hypertrophic zone, greatest in the
hypertrophic zone.**

**Blanchard 1991:** human cartilage from newborns and prepubertal children converts testosterone to DHT
and, less, to oestradiol — *"human cartilage tissue in vivo contains both 5α-reductase and aromatase
activities during post-natal skeletal growth."* And the responsiveness is itself developmentally gated:
**cells from children up to one year old did not respond to DHT or E2; cells from boys and girls in early
puberty responded best.**

**The boundary — Villalvilla 2016, and it is a real caution:** in **articular** cartilage, aromatase is
*"almost undetectable"* in fresh tissue and in chondrocytes cultured for 2 hours, but **expression arises
after 48 hours of culture and stays up.** Their conclusion: *"human cartilage might act as a mere target
for estrogens rather than a producer."*

> **Some of the intracrine literature is measuring a culture artefact.** Villalvilla's finding does not
> overturn Öz or van der Eerden — those used **fresh growth-plate tissue** by in-situ hybridisation and
> immunohistochemistry, not cultured cells, and the growth plate is a different tissue from articular
> cartilage. **But it means any intracrine claim resting on cultured chondrocytes is suspect, and
> Schicht's is one of those.**

## 4. The finding I did not expect — the target tissue fights back

**Schicht M et al., Arthritis Res Ther 2014:** human C-28/I2 chondrocytes incubated with **letrozole at
10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁷ M**:

| result | |
|---|---|
| oestrone synthesis | **significantly downregulated** — the drug worked |
| **ERα mRNA** | **significantly INCREASED** |
| **ERβ mRNA** | **significantly INCREASED** |
| CYP1A1 (catabolic oestrogen metabolism) | increased |

Their own summary: ***"Blocking of estrone synthesis by the aromatase inhibitor letrozole is counteracted
by an increase in ER-α and ER-β."***

> ### Aromatase inhibition raises oestrogen receptor expression in chondrocytes. The cell responds to ligand withdrawal by building more receptor — so the residual ligand acts on a more sensitive tissue, and the signal does not fall in proportion to the hormone.

**Put that beside everything else measured this round and a pattern appears that I think is the real
structural finding:**

| block this | the system does this | source |
|---|---|---|
| **aromatase** | **ERα and ERβ mRNA rise in chondrocytes** | Schicht 2014 |
| **aromatase** | **intratumoral STS rises** — *"a possible compensatory and adaptive response"* | Palmieri 2017 |
| **aromatase** | **serum DHEAS rises significantly** in women progressing on third-generation AI | Palmieri 2017 |
| **steroid sulfatase** | **E1S rises 1.21–3.52×; DHEAS rises in every dose group** | Coombes 2013 |
| **the receptor (fulvestrant)** | **ERβ mRNA and protein rise** | F-R050 |

> ### Every node has a counter-move. Block synthesis and the tissue makes more receptor and more of the bypass enzyme. Block the bypass and the substrate accumulates behind it. Block the receptor and the other receptor rises.

**This does not say the goal is unreachable. It says three specific things:**

1. **No single-node strategy can work** — each one is actively compensated, and the compensations were
   measured, not inferred.
2. **The blockade has to be simultaneous across nodes and continuous.** Coombes' E1S rise and IRIS's DHEAS
   rise mean that **stopping any arm releases a reservoir larger than the one you started with.** Pulsing
   is worse than not starting.
3. **Serum hormone levels are the wrong endpoint.** Schicht's receptor upregulation and Palmieri's 52%
   Ki67 fall on a 28% plasma E2 fall both say the tissue response and the plasma number are decoupled in
   opposite directions.

## 5. HSD17B1 is dead, and the human data is the reason

F-R050 §1 promoted 17β-HSD1 to *"the pubertally-regulated node"* — absent at 1 week, present at 7, and the
potency step. **The clinical answer is unambiguous and negative.**

**OG-6219 (Organon, from the Forendo acquisition), ELENA phase 2, NCT05560646 — 354 women, 93 centres,
11 countries, 50 / 100 / 150 mg BID for 3 cycles.**

**Mean change from screening in oestradiol (pmol/L), end of treatment cycle 3:**

| | placebo | 50 mg | 100 mg | 150 mg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E2** | **−104.0** | **+21.8** | **+59.7** | **+52.3** |
| E1 (pg/mL) | −25.5 | +47.1 | −3.9 | −3.8 |
| DHEA, DHEAS, testosterone, progesterone | — | essentially unchanged across all arms | | |

> **Oestradiol rose on the HSD17B1 inhibitor, in a dose-related way, while it fell on placebo.**

And the trial **missed its primary endpoint** (pelvic pain change: placebo −1.8; 50 mg −1.6; 100 mg −1.5;
150 mg −2.0), after which **Organon discontinued the OG-6219 programme entirely in July 2025.**
**FOR-6219's phase I (NCT03709420) completed with no results posted.**

> ### There is no HSD17B1 inhibitor in development, the only phase 2 failed, and the only human pharmacodynamic data shows oestradiol going up. The node is closed.

**Hole 3 from F-R050 is resolved, negatively. That is one fewer arm and one fewer thing to chase.**

## 6. Where this leaves the oestrogen question — and why I am not writing the oestrogen stack yet

You asked me not to produce the oestrogen side until the growth side is certain. **I agree, and this round
sharpens why.**

**What is now settled about oestrogen:**

- The endpoint is human-proven: fusion **never** occurs in congenital oestrogen deficiency or resistance
- The human growth plate makes its own oestrogen (Öz, fresh tissue) and the enzyme set is complete
- The sulfatase route dominates the aromatase route by 265–660× in the plate and ~100× in bone
- **Complete STS blockade is achievable (≥95–99%) at 5–40 mg/day and is well characterised**
- **Every node is compensated, so the blockade must be combinatorial and uninterrupted** (§4)
- **HSD17B1 is not available and does not work** (§5)
- **Serum oestradiol is the wrong endpoint** — tissue effects are larger and receptor density moves the
  other way

**What is not settled, and none of it is a missing paper:**

1. **What oestradiol concentration is actually achieved inside a human growth plate under any regimen.**
   Never measured, at any age, under any condition. Serum is a proxy that §4 shows is systematically
   misleading in both directions.
2. **Whether pharmacological ablation can reach the genetic state.** AI in men reaches ~20 pg/mL; the
   damage threshold is ~11; genetic deficiency is undetectable at 0.6. **That gap is unmeasured under
   combined blockade.**
3. **The pool.** This is the one that actually blocks "grow forever", and it has not moved: the oestrogen
   write-off is irreversible and produces no catch-up growth, dexamethasone is the only agent ever shown
   to raise resting-zone number and at an unusable dose, and everything else — ex vivo expansion,
   FoxA2⁺ injury recruitment, partial reprogramming — is untried. **Hedgehog remains the strongest
   mechanism with no molecule.**

> **Oestrogen ablation buys a plate that does not close. It does not by itself buy a plate that keeps
> producing. Until (3) has an answer, the oestrogen protocol would be a well-engineered lid on an
> unknown quantity of remaining cells — and I would rather tell you that than write it.**

1. **Muruganandan S et al., *"A FoxA2⁺ long-term stem cell population is necessary for growth plate
   cartilage regeneration after injury"*, Nat Commun 2022;13:2515** — full text. I have a summary only.
   **This is the only demonstrated `a > b` in a mammalian growth plate.** I need the ablation and
   serial-transplantation figures, the expansion kinetics, and any signal identified as the trigger.

2. **Weise M, De-Levi S, Barnes KM, Gafni RI, Abad V, Baron J, *"Effects of estrogen on growth plate
   senescence and epiphyseal fusion"*, PNAS 2001;98:6871–6876** — the parent paper for the entire
   senescence model, cited by every Nilsson paper I have read. I have never seen it in full.

3. **Gafni RI, Weise M, Robrecht DT, Meyers JL, Barnes KM, De Levi S, Baron J, *"Catch-up growth is
   associated with delayed senescence of the growth plate in rabbits"*, Pediatr Res 2001;50:618–623.**
   **This is the only experiment showing that growth-plate capacity can be *conserved* and then spent
   later.** If banking works, it is the mechanism, and I have only the citation.

   Periosteal Ihh maintains the resting-zone stem cells and vismodegib causes premature fusion, so the

6. **OATP-B / SLCO2B1 expression in growth-plate cartilage**, and whether any OATP inhibitor blocks E1S
