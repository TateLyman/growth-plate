# F-R063 — Why we still close, and what the anastrozole/letrozole comparison shows

**Branch:** `claude/height-enhancement-research-v34b4r`
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Status:** Theoretical analysis. The comparison below is between two published trial arms.

**The single most important result retrieved this round:** a 3-year randomised head-to-head of anastrozole
and letrozole in 79 pubertal boys, no confounding GH or testosterone
([*J Endocr Soc* 2024, PMC11388000](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11388000/)) —
**three years of aromatase inhibition produced +0.7 cm of predicted adult height.**

That number reframes the oestrogen arm and supplies most of the answer to "why can we still close."

---

## 1. What the anastrozole/letrozole comparison actually shows

**On the dose question first, because it has a clean answer.** Anastrozole's effect **plateaus at 1 mg**.
A dose–response study in **12 adolescent males** found **0.5 mg and 1 mg both produced ~50% E2 suppression**
— which is why 1 mg became the standard arm. Across 0.5/1/3/5/10 mg, *"doses of 1 mg and higher resulted in
suppression to the lower limit of detection… doses above 1 mg don't provide additional suppression."*

> **Doubling to 2 mg is pharmacologically inert for this endpoint.** It is the same achieved suppression
> with twice the exposure. The mg number is not the lever; the enzyme is already saturated.

**Serum E2 (pg/mL) across the two published arms:**

| | baseline | yr 1 | yr 2 | yr 3 | **post-treatment** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anastrozole 1 mg** (n=35) | 4.7 | 5.1 | 6.4 | 6.2 | **17.0** |
| **Letrozole 2.5 mg** (n=30) | 4.9 | **2.6** | **2.4** | **2.7** | **12.3** |
| estrone, A | 8.0 | 3.0 | 3.3 | 3.5 | 19.9 |
| estrone, L | 8.7 | 2.6 | 2.6 | 2.7 | 13.7 |

**And the outcomes that follow from them:**

| | anastrozole | letrozole |
|---|---|---|
| testosterone, yr 1 | 552 ng/dL (upper-normal Tanner 4) | **982 ng/dL**; **48% >1000**, 4 boys >1200 |
| growth velocity | — | **significantly slower** (P interaction **.039**) |
| IGF-1 | — | **consistently lower** (P < .05) |
| bone-age advance yr 1/2/3 | 1.0 / 1.1 / 0.7 | 0.9 / 0.8 / 0.5 (P<.001) |
| **cumulative PAH change at end of treatment** | **+1.0 cm** | **+0.5 cm** |
| combined (n=61) | **+0.7 cm** | |

> ### The more potent inhibitor produced the *smaller* height gain. Letrozole slowed bone age more — and slowed growth velocity more, and lowered IGF-1 — and finished behind.

**Four properties of the anastrozole arm that fit this stack's logic better than the letrozole arm:**

1. **It still clears the threshold.** 5.1–6.4 pg/mL sits below the Nilsson/Schrier **11 ± 2 pg/mL** level at
   which resting-zone self-renewal is measurably suppressed.
2. **It does not spend growth velocity.** Letrozole's velocity penalty is precisely the Rule-1 violation
   F-R062 warned about — buying bone-age delay with rate.
3. **It does not lower IGF-1.** Letrozole does — and **local IGF-1 drives Cooper's Phase 3, the `v(c)`
   lever.** Letrozole suppresses the term this stack most needs to raise. **That is a self-defeating loop
   inside the oestrogen arm and it has not been named before in this branch.**
4. **It keeps testosterone in range** (552 vs 982 ng/dL, 48% supraphysiological on letrozole), and the
   androgen rise is doing protective work on bone (F-R061).

**The caveat that actually decides between them.** These were ISS boys with **baseline E2 already at
4.7 pg/mL**. Anastrozole's ~50% suppression cleared 11 pg/mL *because baseline was low*. **At a normally
pubertal starting E2 of 15–30 pg/mL, 50% suppression lands at 7–15 pg/mL — straddling the threshold rather
than clearly under it.** Letrozole's deeper suppression clears it regardless. **Which agent is correct
therefore depends on measured starting E2 — that is a genuine decision variable, not a preference.**

## 2. Why we can still close — twelve reasons, ranked

### The four that would close us even if everything else worked

**1. Nothing renews the pool, and the stack accelerates its drawdown.**
`L∞ = A · h_term · n₀ / (b − a)`. Erdafitinib **raises flux** — that is one of its three jobs. But the
division count is what senesces the plate (Gafni: suppressing λ cut fusion from 88% to 14%). **Raising flux
spends `n₀` faster.** Unless `v(d)` carries the length, **this stack closes us sooner than doing nothing.**
That is the sharpest internal contradiction in it.

**2. Link 11 — removing oestrogen does not prevent fusion in the species that fuses like ours.**
Weise's ovariectomised rabbits (E2 < 5 pg/mL) fused the distal tibia at 2–6 weeks. Karimian's OVX controls
fused **16/17 distal tibiae by four weeks.** Two laboratories, same answer. **Either there is an
oestrogen-independent fusion programme, or the plate's own intracrine synthesis suffices — and nothing
distinguishes them.**

**3. Every term in the stack is a delay, and every one reverses.**
Letrozole's E2 **rebounds to 12.3 pg/mL** after treatment; anastrozole's to **17.0 — 3.6× its own
baseline.** VEGFR2 blockade reversed *"rapidly… with near complete fusion within the first year."*
Dexamethasone banks and returns. **The stack contains no permanent term** — and the post-treatment E2
overshoot means stopping may close the plate faster than never having started.

**4. The empirical ceiling: three years of the oestrogen arm bought +0.7 cm.**
Not a mechanism argument — the measured outcome of these drugs, in this population, over three years, with
bone-age advance genuinely slowed. **The bone-age delay did not convert into height.**

### The four counter-moves still un-blocked

**5. Steroid sulfatase.** 20–100× aromatase by protein in bone, **265–660× by activity units in the growth
plate.** E1S rises 1.21–3.52× under AI. **Neither agent touches it.**

**6. Adipose aromatase.** The compartment that matters for the skeleton — **osteoblast-specific aromatase KO
has no bone phenotype; adipose-specific KO does.** This is where the residual oestrogen lives.

**7. Androgen shunt.** T rises ~3×, and androgen is both a substrate reservoir and a direct skeletal signal.

**8. Focal closure has an unidentified trigger.** White's human physis: **cell volume statistically uniform
across all nine regions while bridging bone was 46% in one and ~0 elsewhere.** Something initiates closure
focally and it is not local volume collapse. **A signal we have not identified cannot be blocked.**

### The four the stack creates itself

**9. Two arms raise phosphate, and phosphate is the executioner's ligand.**
Erdafitinib on-target (hyperphosphatemia in 89%); oestrogen ablation by an independent renal route (Uemura;
Zhang). `Phosphate → VEGFR2 → ERK1/2 → caspase-9 → apoptosis.` **Never measured in combination in any
organism.**

**10. Letrozole lowers IGF-1**, suppressing the Phase 3 driver of `v(c)` — resolved by preferring the
anastrozole arm.

**11. The mechanical envelope caps the achievable effect.** Normal dogs fracture at the plate-thickening
dose. Not a safety footnote — **a ceiling on how far the plate can be widened before the tissue fails.**

**12. Growth fraction is saturated and conversion efficiency degrades with age.**
**0.89–0.99 of proliferative chondrocytes are already cycling** — no reserve exists to recruit. And Kuhn's
rabbit slopes: **the 5-week conversion coefficient is ~2× the 8- and 12-week one.** The plate gets worse at
converting cells into length regardless of what is done to the cells.

---

## 3. The honest summary

**What the stack does well:** it is the best-evidenced *rate* stack currently assemblable. Erdafitinib has a
real wild-type effect size (+8.2% femur in 4 weeks), moves three terms at once, and its principal internal
counter-move — phosphate — is neutralisable.

**Why it still closes:** **`n₀` is finite, nothing renews it, raising flux spends it faster, every closure
term reverses on withdrawal with an E2 overshoot, and the one arm with three years of human data delivered
+0.7 cm.**

**The three things that would change the answer**, unchanged from F-R062 and none of them a dose:
a pool-renewal agent; resolution of link 11; and evidence that `v(c)` can be pushed **above** normal in a
healthy plate rather than merely restored in a sick one.
