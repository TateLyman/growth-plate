## 1. The number that settles term C

`smith2008`, the ESR1-null man, at chronological age 28.5:

> "serum hormone profile revealed total testosterone 445 ng/dl (nr 265–800), **estradiol 119 pg/ml
> (nr 10–50)**, FSH 33, LH 37, and **IGF-I 528 ng/ml (nr 123–465)**."

**His IGF-1 was above the adult reference range. His oestradiol was 2.4× above it.** And the paper says
so explicitly: *"the IGF-I concentrations in the propositus were **slightly elevated for an adult
male**, and testosterone levels were persistently within normal limits."*

Now the growth he achieved on that:

| measure | interval | change | velocity |
|---|---|---|---|
| stature | 204 cm @ 28.5 y → 206.2 cm @ 33.5 y | +2.2 cm / 5 y | 0.44 cm/yr *(confounded — bilateral distal femoral surgery for genu valgum)* |
| **arm span** | 213 cm @ 28.5 y → 216 cm @ 38.5 y | **+3 cm / 10 y** | **0.30 cm/yr** *(unconfounded)* |
| bone age | 15 @ 28.5 → 15.5 @ 31.5 → 17.5 @ 35.5 | **2.5 BA-years over 7 chronological years** | — |
| **hands and feet** | 28.5 → 38.5 y | **"length of the hands and feet was unchanged"** | **zero** |

> **A growth plate that cannot be closed, containing its cells, at bone age 15, under an IGF-1 ABOVE
> the adult reference range and an oestradiol 2.4× above it, produced 0.3 cm per year.**

`maffei2004` gives the cleanest untreated series in the literature — his Table: **height 177 → 179 →
180 → 182.5 → 183.5 cm** across five adult years, *"during 5 yr of observation, starting from a height
of 177 cm, the patient reached a height of 183.5 cm before estrogen"* — **+6.5 cm / 5 y = 1.3 cm/yr,
and approximately linear.**

**Range across the two best-documented cases: 0.3 – 1.3 cm/yr, against a pubertal 8–10.**

### What this kills

The obvious plan — *hold the plate open, then drive it with GH/IGF-1* — **is refuted before anyone runs
it.** The drive was already supranormal in `smith2008` and it bought 0.3 cm/yr. `maffei2004` adds the
androgen arm: **27 months of supraphysiological testosterone with bone age frozen at 15 throughout.**

**The systemic endocrine axis is not the limiting factor in an open, uncloseable, cell-containing
plate.** That is a hard result from human data, and it forecloses the entire hormonal branch of term C.

**Therefore term C is local.** Which is exactly what F-R023 says it must be:
`dH/dt = P_swell × f_axial × Φ`. The cells are there, the endocrine drive is there, and the tissue is
producing almost nothing — so the failure is in pressure generation, vectoring, or throughput.

## 2. A second finding I did not expect: the two ways of never closing are not equivalent

Put the three cases side by side on the *closure* axis:

| case | lesion | closure attempt | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `maffei2004` | **aromatase deficiency** (no ligand) | oestradiol given | **CLOSED.** 183.5 → 184.5 cm after 6 months, then **stopped at 184.5** |
| `herrmann2002` | **aromatase deficiency** (no ligand) | oestradiol given | bone density rose steeply (radius QCT 52 → 83 mg/cm³ in 6 months); growth already ceased |
| **`smith2008`** | **ESR1 disruption** (no receptor) | **transdermal oestrogen, 6 months, free oestradiol raised TENFOLD** | **"could not be closed by any means… no detectable effect."** Bone age 15 → 17.5 over 3.5 years |

**Removing the ligand gives a plate that closes the moment you give the ligand back. Removing the
receptor gives a plate that cannot be closed by any amount of ligand — because there is nothing to
receive it.**

And `smith2008` proves the mechanism is receptor-absence rather than dose: **his own endogenous
oestradiol was already 119 pg/ml, 2.4× the upper limit of normal**, precisely because ER-α disruption
removes the negative feedback. He was swimming in oestrogen and his plates did not care.

**So the durable form of term A is receptor-level, not ligand-level.** That is a distinction this branch
has not made and it changes what the closure-blocking arm should target: the atlas holds the SERD/ER
antagonist class well (`fulvestrant` 33 files, `tamoxifen` 135, `SERD` 25, `elacestrant` 17), but
`ESR1 disruption` returns **2** — the phenotype that is actually uncloseable is the one barely
represented.

**I am flagging this as a mechanistic reading of three case reports, not a recommendation.** The
relevant biology is that ligand-blockade and receptor-blockade produce different closure phenotypes in
humans, and only one of them survived a deliberate 10× challenge.

## 3. The third finding: residual growth is not uniform across plates

> **"Arm span increased 3 cm… but length of the hands and feet was unchanged."** — `smith2008`

Same man, same hormones, same decade. **Long bones grew; the small bones of the hand and foot did
not.** And the Marmara case (`luac014.013`) lists what was still open at 31 in *its* patient:

> "bilateral **humerus, radius, ulna, femur, tibia and fibula** epiphyses were still unfused" —
> long bones only, with genu varum, scoliosis and scaphocephaly alongside.

That is a **spatial gradient of residual output**, and under F-R023 it is the kind of thing the
architecture would produce rather than the endocrinology: a metacarpal's physis spans nearly the whole
width of a narrow bone with little surrounding cortex, while a femoral physis sits inside a wide
metaphysis behind a substantial perichondrial ring. **Different cylinders.** I cannot prove that from
three case reports — but it is a testable prediction of the pressure-vessel model and an anomaly the
endocrine model does not address at all.

## 4. Where this leaves the goal

**Never close · constant · fast.**

| term | status | best evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **never close** | **SOLVED, and now resolved into two grades.** Ligand-level = reversible. **Receptor-level = survived a deliberate 10× closure attempt.** | `smith2008` vs `maffei2004` |
| **constant** | **SOLVED.** Linear, sustained, multi-year adult growth: 177→183.5 cm over five years; bone age frozen at 15 for four of them; open epiphyses at BA 16 in a 27-year-old | `maffei2004`, `herrmann2002` |
| **fast** | **UNSOLVED — and now much better characterised. It is NOT endocrine.** Supranormal IGF-1 and 27 months of supraphysiological testosterone both failed. The failure is local. | `smith2008`, `maffei2004` |

**The whole problem is now one question: why does an open, uncloseable, cell-containing, well-driven
human growth plate run at a tenth to a thirtieth of pubertal speed?**

F-R023 gives three candidate answers and F-R015 gives the leading one:

1. **P_swell — the proteoglycan program is off.** Gated at **pO₂ < 8%** (F-R015). An adult epiphysis is
   far better perfused than a child's. **Above the switch the chondrocyte makes collagen instead of
   proteoglycan, generates no swelling pressure, and does nothing — with every cell intact.** This
   would explain the entire 1 cm/yr and it is testable with **one Safranin-O stain**.
2. **f_axial — the cylinder.** Adult cortical geometry and a matured perichondrial ring differ from a
   child's. Consistent with §3's hand/foot-versus-long-bone gradient.
3. **Φ — throughput and convection.** These are adults with reduced loading, and `serrat2010` showed
   delivery is exercise-dependent and local.

**None of the three requires a drug, and none touches F-R022's tumour-suppressor ceiling.**
