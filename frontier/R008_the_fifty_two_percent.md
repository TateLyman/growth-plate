## 0. ⭐⭐⭐ THE ONE I FOUND WHILE LOOKING FOR SOMETHING ELSE — and it is pharmacological

`newton2018` cites, in passing, *"our recent report that activation of mTORC1 signaling stimulated

**`newton2015` — *Autophagy* 11(9), PMID 26259639, PMC4590675 — Newton PT, Vuppalapati KK,
Bouderlique T, Chagin AS.** Abstract, verbatim:

> *"in contrast to this well-accepted concept, in **epiphyseal chondrocytes** inhibition of lysosomal
> activity by **v-ATPase inhibitors bafilomycin A1 or concanamycin A** potently **ACTIVATED MTORC1**
> signaling … Maximal RPS6 phosphorylation was observed at 48-h treatment and reached as high as a
> **12-fold increase (p < 0.018)**. This activation of MTORC1 was further confirmed in **BONE ORGAN

> effect was observed in **ATG5-deficient** bones suggesting a **macroautophagy-INDEPENDENT**
> mechanism."*

> ### **A pharmacological agent, applied to bone, activated mTORC1 twelve-fold and produced potent stimulation of LONGITUDINAL GROWTH at p < 0.001 — and it is autophagy-independent, so the growth is not a confound of blocking autophagy.**

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ THE SECOND MEASUREMENT — genetic, and it is +52%

## 2. ⛔ WHY IT WAS WRITTEN OFF — and why the dismissal does not survive its own paper

The authors' conclusion, two sentences later:

> *"We concluded that additional factors are required for stimulation of mTOR-dependent growth in
> vivo and that the general growth phenotype displayed by Tsc1cKO mice may not be related to the role
> of Tsc1 in chondrocytes."*

They discounted their own +52% because the **whole animals** grew badly. Now read what was wrong with
the whole animals, in the same paper:

> **"Our Tsc1cKO mice began dying at approximately 4 weeks of age … For ethical reasons, we had to

> CHRONIC WASTAGE."**
> **"we detect Col2-Cre activity in NON-CARTILAGINOUS TISSUES (INCLUDING THE BRAIN) … mouse phenotypes
> following genetic ablation using Col2-Cre should be interpreted with care."**

**Seizures and wasting are TSC1 loss in the brain. They are tuberous sclerosis, not a growth plate
phenotype.** And `newton2018` says explicitly that this is what contaminates the competing paper:

> *"side-effects associated with Col2-Cre leakage in combination with the general suffering/dying of
> Tsc1cKO mice after 4 weeks of age, the time-point at which **Yan and colleagues performed most of
> their phenotypic analyses**."*

`yan2016`'s dwarfism — *"20–25% shorter spinal column"*, body length *"stopped increasing at 5 weeks"*
— was measured in animals that were dying of brain lesions and chronic wasting at exactly that age.

> ### **An isolated metatarsal in a dish has no brain, no seizures and no wasting. It is the cartilage alone. And the cartilage alone grows 52% faster.**

**The confound runs in one direction only.** A systemically sick animal makes a bone look shorter, not

## 7. ⛔⛔ HAZARDS — the honest list

1. **TSC1 loss is tuberous sclerosis.** Hamartomas, seizures, tumours. The genetic experiments are not
   a model of a drug and the mice died at four weeks of exactly that.
2. **`yan2016` is a real negative and I am not dismissing it** — I am relocating it. Constitutive,
   embryonic, whole-pathway saturation, in a dying animal, gives chondrodysplasia. That is a true
   result about that manipulation.
3. **The +52% is ex vivo.** No vasculature, no load, no endocrine input. It measures intrinsic
   capacity, which is the right readout for a confounded systemic phenotype and is not a whole-animal
   result.
4. **The expanded resting zone could still be a jam.** Column architecture is normal, which argues
   against it — but only a length endpoint settles charge-versus-discharge, and that is the one thing
   missing.
5. **NV-5138 has no cartilage data of any kind.** Peripheral tissue activation is reported generically;
   whether it reaches an avascular growth plate is unmeasured.
6. **mTORC1 is the central growth and cancer pathway.** Chronic systemic activation is not a benign
   proposition and nothing here is a recommendation to take anything.
