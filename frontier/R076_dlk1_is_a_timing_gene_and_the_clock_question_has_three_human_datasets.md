# F-R076 — DLK1 is a timing gene, not a capacity gene, and the clock question now has three human datasets

**Branch:** `claude/height-enhancement-research-v34b4r`
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Status:** F-R065 named **DLK1** *"the most tractable entry point"* of Lui's imprinted senescence network.
**It has a human loss-of-function phenotype, the phenotype is now quantified, and it forces a retraction —
followed by the most favourable structural result the branch has recorded.**

---

## 1. DLK1 loss is a human disease, and the height it costs is entirely recoverable

Lui's growth-plate network (F-R065) lists **Mest, Dlk1, H19, Gtl2/Meg3** falling with age. **DLK1 and
GTL2/MEG3 are the same imprinted locus — human 14q32.2** — and losing the paternal copy of that locus is a
named human syndrome. There are **two** genotypes, and the difference between them is the whole finding.

| genotype | phenotype |
|---|---|
| **Paternal deletion of DLK1 alone** (exon-1 or whole-gene, ~14–69 kb) | **central precocious puberty**, increased fat mass, *"did not demonstrate additional features of the imprinted disorder Temple syndrome"* |
| **Paternal loss of the whole 14q32.2 domain** (mat UPD14, 14q32 deletion, IG-DMR hypomethylation) — **Temple syndrome** | IUGR, hypotonia, feeding difficulty, small hands and feet, **precocious puberty**, **short stature**, truncal obesity |

**Isolated DLK1 loss gives premature puberty and nothing skeletal beyond what premature puberty explains.**
That is the dissociation the branch needed, and it was already in the literature.

### The adult heights, and the sentence that settles it

The Chinese case-and-review series tabulates **17 reported DLK1-defect CPP individuals**. Adult heights:

| | untreated / irregular | on regular GnRH agonist |
|---|---|---|
| **females** | **137.8, 145.2, 146.2 cm** (plus 155.7 and 156.7 on irregular/oral) | **153.5–163.5 cm** |
| **males** | **145, 147.3, 160.5 cm** | — |

> *"Female patients, previously reported in the literatures, who received **regular GnRHa treatment all had
> reached normal-range adult heights**."*

> ### **Correction to F-R065.** DLK1 is **not** a growth-plate capacity gene. **Its entire effect on human adult height runs through the timing of puberty, and blocking the pubertal cascade recovers all of it.** A human born with no functional DLK1 at all — one of Lui's eleven genes absent from conception — reaches **normal adult height provided the plate is given time.** As a capacity lever, DLK1 is withdrawn.

**But read the second half of that sentence again, because it is the most favourable structural fact in the
branch.** The branch's central architecture is that **capacity and duration are separable and duration is
the binding constraint.** Here is a human experiment, already run, in which one of the network's genes is
deleted outright and **the plate's capacity is demonstrably intact** — the only thing lost was time, and
returning the time returned the height. **F-R065's inference that the imprinted network gates plate
capacity does not survive; the inference that closure timing is what actually costs height is
strengthened.**

### The reciprocal direction, in mouse, and its ceiling

Dosage does move growth upward, and it has a hard ceiling:

- **Dlk1 at ~2× normal → overgrowth during embryogenesis**, with postnatal lethality attributed to feeding defects.
- **Dlk1 at ~3× → death in late gestation** with **severe oedema, skeletal defects and underdeveloped lungs.**

> **A two-fold DLK1 excess grows a mouse; a three-fold excess kills it before birth.** The effect is real,
> the direction is right, **and it is prenatal with a window under one doubling wide.** That is not a
> postnatal lever.

## 2. Temple syndrome prices the whole domain — and the plate still runs at 11.8 cm/year

**Brightman DS, Lokulo-Sodipe O, Searle BA, Mackay DJG, Davies JH, Temple IK, Dauber A. "Growth Hormone
Improves Short-Term Growth in Patients with Temple Syndrome." *Horm Res Paediatr* 2018;90:407–413.**

**14 patients (3 male, 11 female); 13 mat UPD14, 1 IG-DMR epimutation. 12/14 small for gestational age.**

**The three untreated adults in the cohort:**

| participant | age | height SDS |
|---|---|---|
| 9, male | 24.0 | **−3.67** |
| 12, female | 28.0 | **−3.41** |
| 14, female | 21.0 | **−2.73** |

**Losing the paternal 14q32.2 domain costs roughly three standard deviations of adult height — on the order
of 20 cm.** Median birth length in the cohort was **47 cm** (≈ −1.5 SDS), so **roughly half of that deficit
is acquired after birth**, and puberty in the affected females began at a median **thelarche of 6.8 years
(range 4.3–9.0).**

**And the growth-hormone arm, which is the part that matters:**

| | 6 treated patients |
|---|---|
| age at start | median **3.3 y** (2.3–4.1) |
| dose | median **0.042 mg/kg/day** (0.030–0.057) |
| height SDS | **−2.52 → −1.41**, median change **+1.31 SDS** (0.58–1.62) |
| **height velocity** | **7.13 → 11.81 cm/year** (range 9.95–13.20) |

> ### A growth plate missing **two** of Lui's eleven imprinted genes — DLK1 and GTL2/MEG3, at the locus itself — **runs at 11.8 cm/year on a standard paediatric growth-hormone dose.** Whatever the imprinted senescence network does, it is not holding down the achievable rate of a plate that is still open.

**The paper's own limitation, verbatim, and it is the one that matters here:**

> *"Unfortunately, **longitudinal bone age data were not available** in our cohort and thus **we cannot
> comment on the effect of growth hormone on predicted adult height**."*

**So this measures velocity, not final height, and I am not going to over-read it.** The honest reading:
**velocity is recoverable in these patients; whether the recovered velocity survives to adult height is
unmeasured.** That is exactly the ambiguity the whole programme is about.

## 3. The clock question — three human datasets, and they do not agree

F-R066 proposed the pacing law; F-R074 found the Horvath clock's logarithmic childhood transform consistent
with it; F-R075 added ALSPAC. **Two more datasets exist, and one of them is an intervention.**

### 3a. EPOCH — an independent replication of the split I argued in F-R075

**"Epigenetic age acceleration is associated with speed of pubertal growth but not age of pubertal onset,"
*Scientific Reports* 2024 — EPOCH cohort, n = 135** (68 girls, 67 boys), methylation at mean age **10.4 y**.

| association | extrinsic EAA (Hannum) | intrinsic EAA (Horvath) |
|---|---|---|
| **peak height velocity** | **β = 0.018 (0.008–0.028), p = 0.0008** | β = 0.011 (−0.006–0.027), **p = 0.22** |
| **age at peak height velocity** | β = −0.0022, **p = 0.067** | β = −0.0029, **p = 0.12** |

> ### **The rate variable is associated; the timing variable is null.** F-R075 §2 argued that ALSPAC's null on *age at* PHV was expected rather than refuting, because growth-pacing predicts on **accumulated growth**, not on **when** the peak falls. **EPOCH reproduces that split independently and adds the positive on the rate itself** — which F-R075 did not have.

**And the objection that cuts against it, which I am stating rather than burying:** the significant result is
on **extrinsic** EAA — the Hannum-based measure, which is sensitive to blood **cell-composition** shifts.
**Intrinsic (Horvath) EAA, which is designed to be independent of cell composition, was null (p = 0.22).**
If the effect were a clean growth-pacing signal it ought to appear in the intrinsic measure too.
**Subcutaneous adipose tissue explained 8.4% of the association**; BMI and visceral fat explained
essentially none. The authors' own verdict is deflationary: *"the magnitude of the association between EAA
and growth speed was small."*

### 3b. The only interventional dataset — and it points both ways at once

**"Evaluation of Epigenetic Age Acceleration in Growth Hormone (GH)-Deficient Children After 6 Months of
Recombinant Human GH Replacement Therapy: Anti-Ageing GH vs. Pro-Ageing Insulin-like Growth Factor 1?"**
— **n = 10** (5F/5M), mean age **11.0 ± 2.7 y**, rhGH **0.025–0.035 mg/kg/day**, methylation age from a
**five-CpG forensic predictor (ELOVL2, C1orf132/MIR29B2C, FHL2, KLF14, TRIM59)**.

| | baseline | 6 months | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **height velocity** | **3.9 ± 1.4 cm/y** | **8.7 ± 2.6 cm/y** | **< 0.0001** |
| height SDS | −2.5 ± 0.3 | −2.2 ± 0.4 | 0.0712 |
| IGF-1 | 120.5 [102–182] µg/L | **341 [203–510] µg/L** | 0.0076 |
| **epigenetic age acceleration** | **+0.92 ± 2.8 y** | **−0.92 ± 3.1 y** | **0.179 — not significant** |
| age acceleration, **adjusted for IGF-1** | | **−4.137 y (−7.862 to −0.412)** | **0.0295** |
| **IGF-1 → age acceleration** | | **β = 0.011** | **0.0260** |

> *"GH exerts anti-ageing effects, while IGF-1 has pro-ageing effects"* — and *"the anti-ageing effect of GH
> can be evidenced only after removal of the pro-ageing effect by IGF-1."*

**This is the closest thing to a causal test of growth-pacing in humans that exists, and it splits.**

- **Growth velocity more than doubled** and **raw epigenetic age acceleration fell** — the *opposite* of what
  growth-pacing predicts. **But that comparison is non-significant (p = 0.179) at n = 10.**
- **IGF-1 — the actual mediator of the growth — is positively associated with age acceleration
  (β = 0.011, p = 0.026).** That is growth-pacing's prediction, on the variable that carries the growth.

**What I will and will not claim.** n = 10, single arm, no untreated control, six months, and a **five-CpG
forensic predictor rather than a validated multi-CpG clock**. The two significant coefficients are both from
adjusted models on ten children. **This does not settle anything in either direction, and anyone reading it
as settling it — including me — would be over-reading it.** What it does establish is that **the experiment
is cheap, the population is standing, and nobody has run it with a real clock.**

## 4. Where the pacing law stands after this round

| evidence | direction | strength |
|---|---|---|
| Lui: tryptophan restriction **delayed the programme** across organs | **for** | direct experimental, rat |
| Horvath clock logarithmic <20, linear >20 | for | shape correspondence only |
| ALSPAC n=1,018: older at 7 → taller now, **slower after** | **for** | opposite-sign, p≈0.02 both |
| **EPOCH n=135: EAA → higher peak height velocity, p=0.0008; age at PHV null** | **for** | **independent replication of the rate/timing split** |
| **EPOCH: significant only on extrinsic (cell-composition-sensitive) EAA; intrinsic null p=0.22** | **against** | **a real weakness, not a quibble** |
| **GH intervention n=10: velocity 3.9→8.7 cm/y, raw EAA FELL** | **against** | non-significant, p=0.179 |
| **Same study: IGF-1 → age acceleration, β=0.011, p=0.026** | **for** | adjusted model, n=10 |

> **Four for, two against, and the only interventional dataset supplies one of each.** The law is still
> **supported and unproven**, and it is now unproven in a more specific way than before: **the observational
> associations replicate; the one experiment that manipulated growth directly did not reproduce them.**

## 5. What this does to the stack, and one question it raises that the branch had not asked

**Nothing here changes an agent or a dose.** But §3b raises something the branch has never confronted.

Tate's argument for the high growth-hormone arm was explicit: *"if supply is the issue and it's finite we
should spend it before oestrogen closes us: blast."* **That argument accepted, deliberately, that spending
faster spends the budget faster.** F-R071 then showed the budget is denominated in **cost per division**,
not divisions — which is what makes the blast survivable in principle.

> ### §3b is the first measurement that bears on it. **IGF-1 was the term positively associated with epigenetic age acceleration**, and IGF-1 is precisely what a growth-hormone arm raises — from 120 to 341 µg/L in six months at **0.025–0.035 mg/kg/day**, which is **roughly half** the 0.07 mg/kg/day the stack carries. **If IGF-1 is the pacer, the blast strategy is not neutral on the clock; it is the accelerant.**
>
> **This does not overturn the blast argument** — F-R065 settled that oestrogen ablation prevents fusion in
> humans outright, so the closure deadline the blast was racing is removable, and a removed deadline changes
> the optimisation. **But it converts "spend it fast" from a free choice into a measured trade**, and the
> measurement that would price it is the same one §3b failed to power: **methylation age before and after
> growth-hormone treatment, with a validated clock and a control arm.**
