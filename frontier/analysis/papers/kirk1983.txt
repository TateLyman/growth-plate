A Comparison of the Effects of Two Gold-Containing Therapeutic Agents 
on Articular Chondrocyte Growth in vitro 
C. J. Kirkpatrick and W. Mohr 
Department of Pathology, University of Ulm, Oberer Eselsberg, M23, D-7900 Ulm, Federal Republic of Germany 
Received November 8, 1982/Accepted January 26, 1983 
Summary. Chondrocyte structure and function under the 
influence of two gold-containing therapeutic agents, 
aurothioglucose and triethylphosphine gold, were studied 
in a monolayer culture system for cultivating lapine 
articular chondrocytes. The functional parameters in- 
vestigated were chondrocyte proliferation and the incor- 
poration of 3~SO4 as indicator of glycosaminoglycan syn- 
thesis. Aurothioglucose (0.2, 1 and 10 ~tg/ml) failed to 
affect either parameter and caused no cytotoxic effect 
detectable with the light microscope. Triethylphosphine 
gold (1.5 and 3 ~tg/ml) prevented subcultured chondro- 
cytes from forming monolayers and was cytotoxic to 
chondrocytes present in established monolayers, studied 
with the light and scanning electron microscope. A 
0.3 ~tg/ml concentration did not alter chondrocyte pro- 
liferation or sulphate incorporation and was not cytotoxic. 
Key words: Chondrocyte - Monolayer culture - Gold 
Cell growth 
Introduction 
In 1934, Forestier published his studies on the use of gold 
in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis [1]. As Jessop 
points out, gold was subsequently adopted by many 
physicians and treatment regimes were initiated without 
sufficient double-blind controlled studies having estab- 
lished its efficacy [2]. However, the results of clinical in- 
vestigations have been published, claiming that gold, for 
example in the form of gold sodium thiomalate, causes not 
only symptomatic improvement in rheumatoid arthritis, 
but also slows progression of the disease as measured 
radiologically [3]. Whilst the mechanisms of the action of 
gold-containing therapeutic agents in arthritic processes 
are incompletely understood, a variety of in vitro studies 
indicate that gold salts can inhibit macrophage lysosomal 
enzyme activity [4], inhibit antigen- and mitogen-induced 
proliferation of human lymphocytes [5], inhibit human 
neutrophil chemotaxis [6], inhibit monocyte antibody- 
dependent cellular cytotoxicity [7], inhibit chemotaxis and 
phagocytosis of human peripheral blood monocytes [8] 
and inhibit humoral immunity, measured by complement- 
dependent antibody responses to L929 cells [9]. 
Unwanted side-effects ofchrysotherapy include derma- 
titis, haematological problems, such as eosinophilia, agran- 
ulocytosis, thrornbocytopenia and aplastic anaemia, and 
renal complications, such as membranous glomerulone- 
phritis [10]. Our interest has been centred on direct 
mechanisms of cartilage damage arising not only within 
the scope of a variety of arthropathies, such as haemo- 
philia and gouty arthropathy [11, 12], but also as a possible 
complication of therapeutic intervention [13]. This paper 
describes experiments using a monolayer culture system 
for lapine articular chondrocytes to study the effects of two 
gold-containing therapeutic agents, aurothioglucose and 
triethylphosphine gold, on chondrocyte morphology and 
behaviour in subculture, cell proliferative capacity and 
synthesis ofsulphated macromolecules. 
Materials and Methods 
Culture Technique. Articular chondrocytes were isolated from the 
shoulder, hip and knee joints of 3-month-old rabbits using a 
modification [11, 12] of the method of Green and Sokoloff et al. 
[14, 15]. Briefly, after treatment with 0.05% (w/v) bovine testicular 
hyaluronidase (Type I-S, 300 U/rag, Sigma, Munich, FRG) for 
3 rain at room temperature, and 0.25% (w/v) trypsin (Gibco. 
Glasgow, Scotland) for 30rain at 37°C, chondrocytes were 
released from the articular cartilage shavings using two 60-rain 
digestions with 0.2% (w/v) clostridial collagenase (CLSII, 
131 U/rag; Worthington, supplied by Seromed, Munich, FRG) at 
37°C. Cells were inoculated at a density of 3 x l0 S cells/flask into 
75 ml plastic culture flasks (Falcon, supplied by Becton-Dickin- 
son, Heidelberg, FRG) containing 12 ml of Ham's F-12 medium, 
supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS; Boehringer, 
Mannheim, FRG, lots 671466 and 673773 and Rehatuin, lot FS 
053812, supplied by Medac GmbH, Hamburg, FRG) and 
streptomycin-penicillin (Gibco; 10 ~tg/ml and 10 units/ml respec- 
tively). Cell viability was tested using the trypan blue exclusion 
test. Cultures were fed three times weekly and cultivated in an 
atmosphere in 10% CO2 in air. On reaching confluence (7-14 
days of primary culture), chondrocytes were trypsinized using 
0.25% trypsin (w/v) (1:250 in solution A, Gibco) for 30 rain at 
37 °C. 
Study of DNA Synthesis and Proteoglycan Metabolism. Sub- 
cultures were established by seeding the cells into 25 ml plastic

Amersham-Buchler, Braunschweig, FRG). The DMEM was

Cultures were harvested after a 20 h incubation with the 
radioactive isotope. Sulphate incorporation into proteoglycans 
was measured using a modification of the method of Fratantoni 
et al. [16, 17]. The culture medium and trypsin solution used to 
remove the cells from the flasks were treated separately, each 
being dialysed for two periods of 2 h against 0.1 M (NH4)2SO4 at 
4 °C, followed by an overnight dialysis against running tap water. 
0.4 ml samples were added to 5 ml of scintillation fluid (Lumagel, 
J. T. Baker Ltd., Deventer, The Netherlands) and counted in a 
liquid scintillation counter. Quench corrections were made. The 
total bound sulphate was calculated by addition of the values for 
medium and trypsinate. The DNA content of the cell pellet from 
each flask was determined by a slight modification of Burton's 
diphenylamine spectrophotometric method [17, 18] and used as a 
parameter of cell proliferation. The synthesis of sulphated macro- 
molecules was expressed as dpm/~tg DNA. 
Morphological Studies. In one series of experiments, 2.5 x l0 s cells 
were randomly seeded into 60 mm plastic Petri dishes containing 
a sterile 24x40 mm glass coverslip and 3.3 ml control medium. 
Cultures were fed after 48 h and allowed to grow to subcon- 
fluence (96 h). Test substances in fresh medium were then added 
to the monolayers - aurothioglucose (0.2, 1 and 10 ~g/ml) and 
triethylphosphine gold (0.3, 1.5 and 3 ~tg/ml). Control and test 
cultures were removed for cytological investigation after a further 
2, 6 and 24 h for aurothioglucose and 2 and 6 h for triethylphos- 
phine gold. Coverslips were washed briefly in isotonic saline 
solution, fixed in absolute methanol, stained with 2% Giemsa 
solution (Merck, Darmstadt, FRG), air dried and mounted on 
histological slides. 
In another series of experiments, test substances (as above) 
were added to cultures at the beginning of subculture. For 
aurothioglucose, 105 chondrocytes were added per 60 mm Petri 
dish, and for triethylphosphine gold 4× 104 chondrocytes/dish. 
Control and test cultures were harvested for cytological investiga- 
tion at 7, 24 and 72 h. 
Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM). SEM studies were per- 
formed on cultures treated with triethylphosphine gold at concen- 
trations of 0.3 and 1.5 ~g/ml. 3x 104 chondrocytes (passage II) 
were seeded in 35 mm Petri dishes containing 1.5 ml of control 
medium (DMEM+ 10% FBS +penicillin/streptomycin as above) 
and sterile 5 mm (diameter) glass covershps. After 48 h growth, 
the medium was changed and either control or test (triethylphos- 
phine gold, 0.3 and 1.5 ~xg/ml) medium added. 6 h later the cells 
were washed once with Gey's balanced salt solution and fixed in 
3% glutaraldehyde (Serva, Heidelberg, FRG) in cacodylate buffer, 
pH 7.4, washed in 0.1 M phosphate buffer, and dehydrated in 
ethanol. The cells were then transferred to a CO2 critical point 
drying apparatus (Balzers) containing 2,2-dimethoxypropane 
(Baker, Deventer, The Netherlands). After drying, the specimens 
were coated with gold by vacuum evaporation in a Hummer V 
chamber (Technics, Kirchheim, Munich, FRG) and examined in 
a Philips 500 scanning electron microscope. 
Statistical Analysis. Mean values for DNA content and synthesis 
of radioactive sulphated macromolecules were analysed using 
conventional analysis of variance. In the case of culture DNA 
content under the influence of triethylphosphine gold, a statisti- 
cally significant inhomogeneity of values within the groups pre- 
cluded the application of conventional analysis of variance. The 
data were thus analysed using the Kruskal-Wallis one-way analy- 
sis of variance test [19] and statistical significance tested using 
Nemenyi's multiple comparisons [20].

Morphological Studies. The behaviour of lapine articular 
chondrocytes in primary culture and in subculture has 
been described in dedail elsewhere [21]. Briefly, subcultured 
chondrocytes began to adhere to the glass or plastic sub- 
strate after 4 to 6 h. Cells that had just adhered were seen 
as small, rounded, darkly stained cells (Giemsa). With 
time, these 'adhering' cells extended cytoplasmic processes

to become 'spreading' chondrocytes with a clearly defined 
nucleus. The addition of triethylphosphine gold to the 
culture medium at the beginning of subculture prevented 
the cells from adhering to the substrate. This was the case 
each time, i.e. 7, 24 and 72 h after commencing subculture, 
and for all three drug concentrations (0.3, 1.5 and 3 ~tg/ml). 
To test whether this effect was due to a reversible in- 
hibition of chondrocyte adherence, chondrocytes were 
grown in triethylphosphine gold-treated medium (3 ~ug/ml) 
at cell densities of 105 cells/T-25 flask. After 48 h incuba- 
tion at 37 °C, cultures were pooled, centrifuged for 1 rain 
at 1 400rpm, washed once with Gey's balanced salt 
solution and recentrifuged for 1 rain at 2 000 rpm. The cell 
pellet was then resuspended in DMEM+ 10% FBS and 
plated into a T-25 flask. The cells were incubated at 37 °C 
in an atmosphere of 10% CO2 in air and observed for a 
period of 72 h. No ceils adhered and spread, showing that 
the inhibition of cell adherence under the action of 
triethylphosphine gold was irreversible. 
The principal action of triethylphosphine gold on 
chondrocytes in vitro was clearly seen in cultures that had 
been allowed to grow to subconfluence for 96 h in control 
medium and then treated with 0.3, 1.5 or 3 ~tg/ml of the 
test substance for 2 or 6 h. Control cultures revealed 
healthy chondrocytes with no evidence of cytotoxicity 
(Fig. 2a). Similarly, cultures treated with 0.3 ~tg/ml tri- 
ethylphosphine gold showed no alteration in chondrocyte 
structure compared with controls. However, on increasing 
the drug concentration to 1.5 ug/ml, 3% of chondrocytes in 
test cultures showed disruption of cell structure at light 
microscopical level after 2 h. Following 6 h of treatment, 
the proportion of non-viable chondrocytes had risen to 
10%. This finding was much more dramatic on doubling 
the drug concentration. Thus, in the presence of 3 ~tg/ml 
triethylphosphine gold, 20% of chondrocytes showed 
disruption of cell structure (Fig. 2b). The proportion of 
disrupted cells (Fig. 2 c) rose to 75% after 6 h. 
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of chondrocyte 
cultures grown in the presence or absence of triethylphos- 
phine gold underlined the extent of the cytotoxic effect of 
this substance (Fig. 3 a-c). This took the form of a marked 
contraction of the cytoplasm with subsequent cellular 
fragmentation.

Discussion 
This paper shows that the effects of gold-containing 
therapeutic agents on lapine articular chondrocyte growth 
in vitro is dependent on the type of chemical moiety 
associated with the gold. Thus, aurothioglucose in con- 
centrations of 0.2, 1 and 10 ~tg/ml failed to alter chondro- 
cyte proliferation or net synthesis of proteoglycans. Fur- 
thermore, none of the three concentrations was cytotoxic. 
On the other hand, triethylphosphine gold, at concentra- 
tions of 1.5 and 3 ~tg/ml prevented the attachment and 
growth of subcultured chondrocytes and was cytotoxic to 
chondrocytes in established monolayers. The 0.3 ptg/ml 
concentration exerted no effect on cellular morphology, 
proliferation or synthesis of sulphated macromolecules. 
It must be stressed that the method employed to 
measure the incorporation of radioactive sulphate into 
macromolecules involved no separation of proteoglycans 
from other macromolecules synthesized by the chondro- 
cyte. Thus, some radioactivity may be present in non- 
proteoglycan macromolecules. Nevertheless, the bulk of 
the sulphate incorporated by the chondrocyte is in the 
form of glycosaminoglycans, which are then bound to the 
core protein of proteoglycans and secreted by the cell [22, 
23]. It should also be noted that the method represents a 
net incorporation of 35SO4 into macromolecules and thus 
does not differentiate between anabolic and catabolic 
components. The method provides, however, a useful and 
convenient measure of net glycosaminoglycan synthesis. 
It is important in the discussion of these findings to 
examine the validity of the experimental system in the 
light of gold concentrations achieved during chrysotherapy. 
Rubinstein and Dietz [24] studied serum gold levels in 
patients with rheumatoid arthritis treated with sodium 
aurothiomalate or aurothioglucose. In five subjects receiv- 
ing weekly intramuscular injections of 25 mg sodium 
aurothiomalate, the mean serum value for gold ranged 
from 1.1 to 3.2 gg/ml. Peak serum gold levels following 
intramuscular injection of 25 or 50 mg aurothioglucose 
were in the range 1-5 gg/ml. According to Finkelstein 
et al. [25] triethylphosphine gold administered orally for a 
12-week period in doses of 6 to 9 mg/day achieved peak 
blood levels of 0.9 to 1.2 gg/ml without any tendency to 
stabilise. Walz et al. [26] reported mean serum gold levels 
of approximately 0.6 ~tg/ml following 4 weeks' oral treat- 
ment with triethylphosphine gold (6 mg/day). The con- 
centration range of triethylphosphine gold investigated in 
the present studies was 0.3 to 3 b~g/ml, corresponding to a 
gold concentration of 0.1 to 1 gg/ml, comparable to the 
blood levels achieved during chrysotherapy with triethyl- 
phosphine gold. This calculation is based on the fact that 
approximately one-third of the triethylphosphine gold 
compound is gold [27]. For aurothioglucose, the concen- 
tration range 0.2 to 10 ,ug/ml corresponds to a gold con- 
centration range of 0.1-5 ptg/ml, also in accordance with 
the blood levels recorded in humans, as described above. 
The calculation is based on the fact that aurothioglucose 
has a gold content of 50% [24]. 
Thus, we see that gold in the form of aurothioglucose 
with a gold content of up to 5 ~g/ml exerted no effect on 
the parameters studied in this investigation, in particular 
there was no cytotoxic action, whereas triethylphosphine 
gold showed a cytotoxic effect on chondrocytes at con- 
centrations equivalent to a gold content of 0.5 and 
1 ~tg/ml. The cytotoxicity of triethylphosphine gold has 
also been documented by Simon et al. [28] for other cell 
types in vitro. Gold concentrations of 0.5 and 1 ~tg/ml (i.e. 
approximately 1.5 and 3 btg/ml triethylphosphine gold) 
reduced the viability of HeLa and RAJI cells to between 
30 and 60% after a 24 h treatment. Although no patho- 
genetic mechanism was apparent, it was suggested that the 
hydrophobic nature of the triethylphosphine gold com- 
plex, as well as its possession of no net charge, might 
facilitate its membrane transport and penetration into cells 
with resultant alteration of cellular biological processes. 
The present experimental studies provide no indication of 
the mechanism involved in the cytotoxic action of triethyl- 
phosphine gold on articular chondrocytes in vitro. 
Concerning possible therapeutic consequences of this 
finding, it must be stressed that the lapine articular 
chondrocyte monolayer was adopted as a sensitive screen- 
ing method to investigate the susceptibility of cartilage 
cells to a spectrum of therapeutic agents. Thus, we have 
described elsewhere the failure of piroxicam, diclofenac- 
sodium, sulindac sulphide or indomethacin to exert any 
deleterious effect on chondrocytes in this system at con- 
centrations reached in human synovial fluid or plasma 
[13]. The same result has been described in the present 
paper for aurothioglucose. The chondrocyte cytotoxicity of 
triethylphosphine gold in our experimental system does 
not necessarily mean that this gold compound is damaging 
to human articular cartilage in vivo. However, we feel that 
this finding is worthy of more intensive investigation, 
particularly when set against the background of the non- 
damaging effects of another gold compound, as well as of 
four other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory agents. The 
reasons for adopting the lapine chondrocyte monolayer 
model have been given in detail in other publications 
[13, 29]. 
It is clear from a survey of the literature that there is 
insufficient knowledge of the ability of therapeutic agents 
to diffuse through the matrix of hyaline articular cartilage.

In our system matrix components were present in quanti- 
ties much smaller than in the in vivo situation. It is pos- 
sible that only low concentrations of triethylphosphine 
gold are achieved in cartilage in vivo, although it has been 
well documented that gold is concentrated in a variety of 
human tissues during chrysotherapy with other gold-con- 
taining agents [30-32]. However, the levels achieved in 
cartilage are considerably lower than, for example, in 
reticuloendothelial 
tissues, 
synovium, renal 
cortex or 
adrenal glands [30]. Nevertheless, with the help of the 
electron microscope and electron probe X-ray analysis, it 
has been shown that gold can accumulate in the chondro- 
cyte [33-35]. Thus, Oryschak and Ghadially demonstrated 
the presence of single membrane-bound lysosomal bodies 
with electron-dense contents (aurosomes) in the articular 
cartilage of rabbits injected intra-articularly with sodium 
aurothiomalate [33]. Parenteral administration of this gold 
compound resulted in the production of aurosomes in the 
articular 
chondrocytes 
of immature, 
but not mature, 
rabbits [34]. Moreover, in long-term studies of the effects 
of intra-articular sodium aurothiomalate on rabbit articu- 
lar cartilage, Ghadially and Lalonde described the deposi- 
tion of large amounts of intracytoplasmic filaments in 
chondrocytes and suggested that this phenomenon could 
represent regressive or degenerative change [35]. 
Further information is also required on the metabolism 
of triethylphosphine gold in vivo. It has been claimed that 
approximately 25% of an oral dose is absorbed in the 
upper gastrointestinal tract with subsequent splitting off of 
sulphur and phosphine ligands and binding of the released 
aurous atoms to serum globulins and albumin [36]. Shaw 
has shown that this protein binding represents a complex 
group of gold-binding molecular species in experiments 
using the cytosol of renal cortical cells from rats treated 
with gold [37]. It would be of interest to know what 
metabolites of triethylphosphine gold are formed in vivo 
and what effects they might have on the chondrocyte 
culture system employed in the present experiments. It is 
possible that the molecular complex is altered in vivo in 
such a way as to produce metabolites that do not exert a 
damaging effect on chondrocytes.
