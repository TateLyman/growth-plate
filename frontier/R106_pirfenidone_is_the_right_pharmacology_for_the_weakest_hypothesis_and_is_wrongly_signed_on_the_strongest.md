# F-R106 — Pirfenidone hits exactly the machinery row 3 needs. It is also wrongly signed on the best-evidenced arm we have. Holes, in order.

Tate proposed pirfenidone as the methylation-increasing drug. **He is right about the pharmacology, and
it is better-targeted than I would have guessed.** The holes are below, in the order that matters.

`Miranda-Roblero HO et al. **Pirfenidone Reverts Global DNA Hypomethylation, Promoting DNMT1/UHRF/PCNA
Coupling Complex in Experimental Hepatocarcinoma.** Cells 2024;13:1013. PMC11201610.` Open access, read
in full.

---

## 1. What it actually does — and it is the right complex

Rat F344 hepatocarcinogenesis (DEN + 2-AAF), **PFD 300 mg/kg/day p.o. × 12 weeks**:

| protein | effect of PFD | p |
|---|---|---|
| **DNMT1** | **increased** | <0.001 |
| **UHRF1** | **increased** | <0.0001 |
| **DNMT3a** | **increased** | <0.0001 |
| DNMT1-acetyl | increased | <0.00001 |
| DNMT3b | not decreased in HCC; modest in vitro | <0.001 |
| **global 5mC** (dot blot + ELISA) | **restored** | <0.001 |

In vitro, HepG2 after the DNMT inhibitor 5-Aza: PFD **elevated PCNA, UHRF1, DNMT1 and DNMT3a
(p<0.00001)** and *"DNA methylation regains its basal levels."* **Rosiglitazone did not** — so this is not
simply a PPARγ effect.

**DNMT1 + UHRF1 + PCNA is the replication-coupled maintenance-methylation complex.** That is precisely
the machinery F-R105 §3 identified as the ceiling: Nilsson says the resting zone loses maintenance
methylation; Yanagihara says DNMT1 enzyme activity is the limit, not SAM. **Pirfenidone raises DNMT1 and
both of its targeting cofactors, and it is an approved oral drug.** Tate's instinct was correct and I had
not made this connection.

## 2. The holes, in order of how much they matter

### Hole 1 — it raises DNMT3a as hard as DNMT1, and that is the wrong direction on our best-evidenced arm

**DNMT3A loss of function is the single largest human height effect in this entire file: +3.0 SD, 13/13
penetrant, four truncating alleles at +2.4 to +3.8 SD (TBRS).** Row 1 needs DNMT3A **down**.

**Pirfenidone raises DNMT3a at p<0.0001 — more significantly than it raises DNMT1.**

So it is **correctly signed for row 3 and wrongly signed for row 1**, and it is the exact mirror of
partial reprogramming, which raises the maintenance writer *and lowers* the de novo writer (F-R081).

**Net effect on height: unknown, and plausibly negative** — because the row-1 cost is measured in humans
at +3.0 SD, while the row-3 benefit is entirely hypothetical (see Hole 5). **This is the hole that
decides it, and I do not think it can be argued away.**

### Hole 2 — both arms are rescue, again

The HCC animals had **reduced** DNMT1/DNMT3a/UHRF1 and PFD restored them; the in vitro arm rescued after
5-Aza knocked them down. The paper's own phrasing is that PFD *"maintains global DNA methylation in a
similar way to the NT group"* — **restoration toward normal, not above it.**

**Nobody has shown pirfenidone raises methylation above baseline in an untreated animal.** That is
F-R094's rescue law, for the fourth time, and it has held every previous time.

### Hole 3 — liver and hepatoma cells; no skeletal data at all

No bone, no cartilage, no growth plate. Whether chondrocytes respond the same way is untested.

### Hole 4 — global 5mC is the wrong resolution

Measured by dot blot and 5mC ELISA. **Our own analysis (F-R079/F-R083) found the Dnmt1-dependent
compartment is 95.9% outside promoters and CpG islands.** Global 5mC going up does not tell you the
growth-gene set was remethylated, and that is the set that matters.

### Hole 5 — and this is the one I have to be straight about: "methylation is positive" is not established

Nilsson 2005 is an **association**. His own words are that the finding is *"consistent with the
hypothesis"* that methylation loss limits replication. **Nobody has shown that raising methylation slows
growth-plate senescence or extends growth in any animal.**

Meanwhile the strongest *human* evidence linking methylation to height runs the other way: **less DNMT3A,
taller.**

**So row 3's direction rests on a mechanistic chain — Nilsson → Yanagihara → Su — that is coherent and
entirely untested as an intervention.** I built that chain last round and I should not now treat it as
established enough to justify an agent that costs us row 1.

### Hole 6 — dose

300 mg/kg/day in rat. FDA body-surface-area conversion (rat Km 6 / human 37) gives a human-equivalent of
roughly 48 mg/kg — **above the approved 2403 mg/day (~34 mg/kg)**. The effect was produced at a dose
above the human-equivalent of the label. Not disqualifying, but the label dose may not reproduce it.

### The one hole that runs in its favour

**Pirfenidone is also a TGF-β inhibitor**, and F-R098 flagged that as favourable: Chu showed the human
GP1 root sits in a **low-TGF-β** niche and GH activates TGF-β autocrine in those cells. And my GSE16981
analysis found **Tgfbr2 rises with senescence (r=+0.899)**. **So pirfenidone may be correctly signed on
the GP1-preservation arm as well as on maintenance methylation.** That is a genuine double hit and it is
the strongest argument for it.

Against that: TGF-β is required for chondrocyte matrix function, and blocking it in cartilage has no
safety or efficacy data.

---

## 3. Verdict

**Pirfenidone is the right pharmacology for the weakest-evidenced arm in the file, and it pushes the
best-evidenced arm backwards.**

I am not adding it to the stack. What would change that, in order:

1. **Evidence that pirfenidone raises DNMT1 without raising DNMT3A** — a dose, a schedule, or a
   tissue where the two separate. As reported they move together and DNMT3a moves harder.
2. **Any demonstration that raising maintenance methylation slows growth-plate senescence** in any
   animal. Row 3's whole direction is one untested inference.
3. **Any bone or cartilage data for pirfenidone at all.**

**Where it should sit meanwhile:** recorded as the **only approved drug that hits the DNMT1/UHRF1/PCNA
maintenance complex**, held for the day row 3 has a direction test rather than a mechanistic chain. That
is a real asset and it is worth having found — it just cannot be spent yet.

**And the honest general point:** I have now proposed three row-3 substrate or agent arms — sulfate,
methyl donors, and this — and the first two were closed at the enzyme step by papers already on disk.
This one is not closed; it is *unsupported at the level of the hypothesis it serves*. **The bottleneck in
row 3 has stopped being "find an agent" and become "test the direction."**

---

*Tate found a drug that hits the exact complex, which I had not done. The reason it does not go in is not
the drug — it is that the arm it serves has never been tested in the direction we need, and the drug
carries a measured cost on the arm that has.*
