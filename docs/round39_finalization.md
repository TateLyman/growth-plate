# Round 39 — the finalization: two checks, one of which refutes me

You asked whether anything exists to finalize this. Two of the three missing measurements were findable.
**One of them refutes the strongest objection I raised.**

## Check 1 — does growth deform the spine? **No. It improves it.**

`canopyspine2026` — **randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled**, 75 children, spinal radiographs at
baseline and 1 year:

| measure | vosoritide vs placebo |
|---|---|
| L4 sagittal spinal canal width | **+1.433 mm (0.547–2.320), P = .002** |
| L4 interpedicular distance | +0.509 mm (−0.034 to 1.052), P = .066 |
| **pathological kyphosis (TLK ≥20°)** | **33.3 % vs 59.3 %, P = .037** |

And TYRA-300 increased lumbar vertebral length and improved intervertebral disc shape.

**I argued last round that the growth and the spinal deformity might be the same physical event, so no
dose could separate them. A trial that measured the spine directly finds the opposite.** Vosoritide
*halved* pathological kyphosis.

**The reconciliation with the erdafitinib case is dose, not mechanism.** Erdafitinib at an *oncology*
dose caused kyphoscoliosis with cord compression. The genetic extremes on **both** axes — CATSHL, NPR2
activating, NPR3 loss — all carry scoliosis. **Spinal deformity is a function of how hard the plate is
driven, not of which axis drives it.** My axis-based safety argument against FGFR inhibition is
withdrawn.

(Caveat kept: achondroplasia has a pathologically narrow canal and high baseline kyphosis, so vosoritide
is correcting a deficit. This says nothing about a *normal* spine driven above normal.)

## Check 2 — final adult height: **confirmed absent, for every agent on both axes**

The 2026 systematic review (`cnpmeta2026`, 11 studies, N=542, 4 RCTs at low risk of bias) states it
explicitly: long-term studies are still needed to define the effect on adult height, and children in
vosoritide studies **"will be followed until they achieve final adult height."**

**This is now confirmed from a systematic review rather than inferred from my failing to find it** —
a materially stronger statement. Every claim in this atlas rests on velocity, and velocity is the thing
this project has repeatedly shown does not predict final height.

**And the velocity numbers do not favour the CNP arm:**

| | pooled effect |
|---|---|
| CNP analogs (meta-analysis, 4 RCTs, n=326) | **+1.36 cm/yr** (1.05–1.68) |
| infigratinib (PROPEL3, n=74 vs 39) | **+1.74 cm/yr** (1.31–2.17) |

Separate trials, overlapping CIs, and a pooled estimate isn't comparable to a single trial — but **I
should stop implying the CNP arm is the larger effect, because on the available numbers it is the
smaller one.**

## The finalized verdict

**You were right about two of the three things I pushed back on.**

1. **The FGFR axis is not the wrong axis** — on velocity it is numerically the *better* one (+1.74 vs
   +1.36 cm/yr).
2. **The spine fear was overstated** — refuted by an RCT, and the deformity is dose-dependent rather
   than mechanism-intrinsic.
3. **But erdafitinib specifically still fails**, and for one narrow reason that survives everything
   above: **its FGFR1-first selectivity ordering (1.20 < 3.00 < 5.70 nM) means it cannot be dosed into
   the therapeutic window without leading with the receptor that carries the liability.** Infigratinib at
   0.25 mg/kg achieves +1.74 cm/yr *with no FGFR1 or FGFR2 signal at all*. That is the window erdafitinib
   has no way to reach.

**So the answer is: right axis, wrong molecule** — and the right molecule is a selective or low-dose
FGFR3-preferential agent (infigratinib, TYRA-300 class), which gets the same or better velocity, an
improved spine, no accelerated bone age, and no FGFR1 engagement.

## What remains genuinely unknown, for either choice

- **Final adult height on anything.** Not "I couldn't find it" — it does not exist yet, and the trials
  are still running.
- **The sign of local FGFR1 and FGFR4 inhibition on bone length.** No chondrocyte-restricted deletion
  with a length endpoint, in any species.
- **Whether the CNP and FGFR arms are additive.** They act on different zones and nobody has
  co-administered them.
