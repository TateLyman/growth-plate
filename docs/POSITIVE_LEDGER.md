# The positive ledger — what we actually know, and what it implies

**Date:** 2026-08-06 · Built from the atlas, not from memory.

Everything below is grade **A or B** with **direct human evidence** unless marked otherwise.
The atlas holds **151 grade-A human-direct nodes** and **359 human quantitative rows carrying a
length or stature unit** at grade A/B. This document assembles them into one account.

The organising question is not "what don't we know." It is: **given only what is established
in humans, what does the answer look like?**

---

## 1. The identity everything reduces to

```
adult height  =  birth length  +  Σ over plates Σ over years ( velocity × duration )
```

Both terms are measured in humans, at grade A.

**Velocity, per plate** — distal femur **1.4 cm/yr** at ages 5–8; proximal humerus 1.2 (girls)
/ 1.3 (boys); distal radius 0.9 / 1.0; ulna 1.0 / 1.1. Peak height velocity **8–9 cm/yr**.
Distal femur supplies ~⅔–71% of femoral growth.

**Duration** — fusion completes **16–18 y in females, 18–20 y in males** (MRI, 958 subjects).
The distal radius is consistently last: at 17 y only 75% of female distal radial plates are
fused, against 97–98% of ankle plates. Post-menarcheal height gain **8.0 cm**.

That is the whole system. Everything else is a claim about which term something moves.

---

## 2. The velocity levers: all real, all measured, all small

| Intervention | Effect | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| rhGH | +3.0 to +5.0 cm/yr short-term; **+0.3 to +0.9 SDS** final height | A |
| Lonapegsomatropin (weekly GH) | +0.91 cm/yr vs daily; +1.01 SDS at 52 wk | A |
| Vosoritide (CNP analogue) | **+1.57 cm/yr** annualised velocity | B |
| Navepegritide + lonapegsomatropin | 8.69 cm/yr annualised | A |
| Letrozole (aromatase inhibitor) | +1.3 cm predicted adult height — **not sustained** | A |
| Meclizine | +0.11 cm/yr | B |
| Ultra-low-dose childhood estrogen + GH (Turner) | **+0.32 SDS (2.1 cm)** adult height | A |
| Early very-low-dose depot estradiol + GH | **+3.5 cm** vs GH alone | A |

**Every velocity lever lands between 2% and 4% of final height.** That convergence across
completely unrelated mechanisms is the atlas's oldest finding and it has never broken.

The negative side is the same size in the other direction: inhaled budesonide costs **−1.2 cm**
of adult height, dose-dependently at −0.1 cm per µg/kg/day.

---

## 3. The duration levers: also real, also measured — and an order of magnitude larger

Nature has run the experiment. Two men, two genes, one answer.

| | ESR1 nonsense, homozygous | CYP19A1 aromatase deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| adult height | **204 cm** | **204 cm** (second case 188 cm) |
| bone age | 15 → 17.5 over **3.5 years** | **14** at chronological age **24.25** |
| epiphyses | incomplete closure at 28 | open |
| still growing? | **yes, into adulthood** | yes |
| lumbar spine BMD | Z **−3.85** | distal radius **−4.7 SD** |
| estrogen replacement | no effect (ERα broken) | *does* close the plates |

Untreated aromatase-deficient men: **188–204 cm**. Fifteen published male cases.

**204 cm is roughly +25 to +28 cm above the male population mean — about +4 SD.** No velocity
lever in existence approaches a tenth of that.

---

## 4. The fact that reorganises everything

> **The ESR1-null man was still growing at 28 years old, with a bone age of 17.5.
> The aromatase-deficient man had a bone age of 14 at chronological age 24.**

In a normal human, growth stops because the plate is **switched off**, not because it has run
out. At 204 cm and age 28, the plate still had capacity.

This is the single most consequential positive fact in the ledger, and it cuts against the
framing the rest of this atlas has been built around. The division-budget model — senescence is
division-dependent, the stem pool is finite, GH spends it — is well supported in mouse and it
may well be true. **But in a normal human it is not the binding constraint. Fusion arrives
first, and it arrives with capacity to spare.**

Which means: *the ceiling on human height is not a resource limit. It is a timer.*

---

## 5. The timer is estrogen, and it is biphasic with a narrow window

The only direct human dose–response experiment: five prepubertal/early pubertal boys, 4-day iv
estradiol at 4, 20 and 90 µg/day, double-blind randomised order, ulnar growth velocity measured
over three weeks before and after each, washout between.

| dose | serum E₂ | 3-week ulnar velocity |
|---|---|---|
| **4 µg/day** | 10 pg/mL | **0.45 → 1.38 mm** (P<0.05) — *tripled* |
| 20 µg/day | 16 pg/mL | 0.49 → 1.0 mm — ns |
| 90 µg/day | 96 pg/mL | 0.46 → 0.84 mm — ns |

**Low estrogen grows. High estrogen fuses. The two limbs are separated by less than an order of
magnitude.** Half-maximal pubertal growth acceleration in girls occurs at a morning estradiol
of **20 pmol/L**.

And the therapeutic proof of concept already exists, in trials, with adult height as the
endpoint: ultra-low-dose childhood estrogen added to GH in Turner syndrome gave **+2.1 cm**;
early very-low-dose depot estradiol plus GH gave **+3.5 cm** over GH alone.

Those are small. They are also *positive adult-height results obtained by playing the biphasic
curve deliberately* — which is the only lever class in this entire atlas that has a
demonstrated +25 cm version.

---

## 6. So the question is not "can height be raised." It is this

> **Why is the pharmacological duration lever ten times weaker than the genetic one?**

Genetic: complete, lifelong, from conception → +25 cm.
Pharmacological: partial, 2–3 years, started mid-puberty → +1.3 to +3.5 cm.

Three differences, all in principle addressable, none tested:

1. **Timing.** Letrozole and anastrozole trials start in already-pubertal boys. The genetic nulls
   never had the signal at all. Nobody has run an aromatase inhibitor across the *whole* growing
   period.
2. **Depth of blockade.** Aromatase inhibitors are partial. The window in §5 is narrow enough
   that partial blockade may land on the *wrong side* of the biphasic curve.
3. **Duration.** 2–3 years against 20.

And one that is not addressable, and must be stated because it bounds the whole class:

4. **The bone cost is real and it is severe.** BMD Z of −3.85 and −4.7 SD. Any duration
   extension is paid for in mineralization. The genetic nulls are the proof of concept *and*
   the warning.

---

## 7. What we know about combining them

Nothing. And the prior is good, because the two lever classes act on **different terms of the
same product** — velocity and duration — so they should multiply rather than compete.

Two facts support the prior:
- Estrogen and GH already show amplification (`estrogen_gh_axis_amplification`, grade A).
- Every positive adult-height result in §5 is *already* a combination — low-dose estrogen **plus
  GH** — not either alone.

One fact constrains it: sacubitril and CNP are **non-additive** in mouse (they saturate the same
step). Levers on the *same* term don't stack. Levers on *different* terms have not been tried.

**No trial has ever combined a duration-extending agent with a velocity agent and measured adult
height.** That is the largest untried experiment in the field, and it needs no new molecule.

---

## 8. The three newest facts, and where they fit

Rounds 19–21 added three things that change the picture at the margins:

- **Human terminal cell height does not vary with age** (29–38 µm, birth→13 y). So velocity is
  carried by cell *production*, not cell size. Any velocity agent working through terminal cell
  enlargement is acting on a term human physiology holds constant — which may mean no homeostat
  to fight (that is how vosoritide works) or a tightly defended parameter.
- **In rat, 89–99% of proliferative-zone chondrocytes are cycling** (Wilsman, continuous BrdU).
  In human, the labelling index is 3–5×  lower and **nobody has decomposed it.** If most of the
  human proliferative zone is idle, recruitment is a multiplicative lever nobody has touched.
- **GH depletes the stem pool** in mouse by shifting divisions toward the committed side. If
  that transfers, the standard therapy is spending the very resource that would otherwise let
  the plate outlast a delayed timer.

The third one matters most *given §4*: if fusion is the binding constraint rather than
exhaustion, then extending the timer only helps if the pool survives long enough to use the
extra time — and the drug we would give alongside it is the one that spends the pool.

---

## 9. The account, in one paragraph

Human adult height is the integral of a measured velocity over a measured duration. Velocity is
carried by cell production, not cell size, and every agent that raises it — GH, CNP analogues,
IGF-1, aromatase inhibitors — converges on 2–4% of final height, which after thirty years of
independent attempts looks like a property of the system rather than a coincidence. Duration is
carried by an estrogen timer that is biphasic within one order of magnitude, and removing it
entirely produces **204 cm men who are still growing in their late twenties with bone ages of
14–17** — proof that the normal plate is switched off with capacity to spare, at a cost of
roughly 4 SD of bone mineral density. The largest validated lever on human height is therefore
**not velocity but duration**, its demonstrated ceiling is about **+25 cm**, its price is bone,
and the only two positive adult-height results ever obtained from deliberately exploiting it
(+2.1 and +3.5 cm) were both partial, both late, and both already combined with GH. Nobody has
run the full-period, correctly-dosed version, nobody has combined a duration agent with a
velocity agent to an adult-height endpoint, and nobody has measured whether the human
proliferative pool has idle capacity to recruit while the timer runs.

## 10. What follows — ranked by (size of the known effect) × (nearness of the evidence)

1. **Reconstruct the aromatase-inhibitor literature to an adult-height endpoint, by age at
   start.** The prediction from §6 is that effect size scales with how much of the growing
   period is covered. If the published trials show that gradient, the timing hypothesis is
   supported without a new trial. This is a re-analysis of existing data, and the **506-trial
   corpus already downloaded** in Round 18 is where it starts.
2. **Test whether any existing cohort combined a duration agent with a velocity agent.** Same
   corpus. If none did, that absence is itself the finding.
3. **Settle the human growth fraction** (the Karolinska ask, already drafted).
4. **Test whether GH's pool depletion is visible in humans** (the KIGS request, already drafted).
5. **Sacubitril in the human explant** — the only novel-mechanism velocity candidate with an
   approved paediatric drug attached.

Items 1 and 2 need no new tissue, no new trial and no new permission. They run on data that is
already on this disk.
