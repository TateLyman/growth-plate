# Round 31 — the test came back positive, and it brought its own failure mode

## The prediction, and the result

Round 29 predicted: a **velocity** lever spends capacity and advances the skeleton; a **yield** lever
should raise height **without proportionate skeletal maturation**. The discriminator was Δheight ÷
Δbone-age under a CNP analogue in a **non-FGFR3** population.

`dauber2026` — phase 2 basket trial of vosoritide in RASopathies, ACAN deficiency and NPR2 deficiency,
all non-FGFR3, each child their own control against a pretreatment observation period:

| | baseline | on treatment | |
|---|---|---|---|
| annualized growth velocity | 4.53 ± 1.61 cm/yr | **8.09 ± 1.58 cm/yr** | P < .0001 |
| AGV Z-score | −1.34 ± 1.64 | **+2.66 ± 1.89** | **+4.0 SD** |
| **bone age ÷ chronological age** | 0.94 ± 0.20 | **0.92 ± 0.17** | **P = .22** |
| CXM (type X collagen turnover) | — | **nearly doubled** | |

**Velocity rose 4.0 SD. Bone age did not move — not in the whole cohort, not in any subgroup.**

### The authors make the comparison themselves, and it is the sharpest part

Growth hormone in these same conditions: **0.49 SD** in Noonan, **0.62 SD** in ACAN deficiency,
median **0.7 SD** in NPR2 deficiency — first-year height gain almost identical to vosoritide's **0.69
SD** here. But in their words, *in all of those growth hormone studies growth rates slowed in
subsequent years*, whereas vosoritide has produced sustained multi-year velocity gains.

**Same first-year SDS. Different trajectory. Flat bone age.** That is the signature this atlas predicted
a yield lever would have and no velocity lever does.

## And the ceiling comparison

| | genotype | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `herrmann2002` | aromatase deficiency — **unlimited duration**, epiphyses still open at 27 | **197 cm** |
| `hannema2013` | NPR2 activating Arg655Cys | **221 cm** (+5.2 SDS) — *after* epiphysiodesis |
| `moffatt2025` | NPR3 homozygous LoF | **211.1 cm** at 19, parental target 184.5 cm — *after* epiphysiodesis |

**Duration without yield gave 197 cm. Yield had to be surgically stopped at 211 and 221.** Both CNP-axis
figures are **truncated** — epiphysiodesis was performed — so they understate the genotype. `moffatt2025`'s
patient exceeded the maximum size of the DXA equipment.

`hannema2013`'s limb proportions are the CNP signature: sitting-height ratio 0.49 at **−1.3 SDS**, arm
span 232 cm — the gain is in limbs.

## What is NOT established, and one of these is serious

- **Neither big-height case has a bone age.** `hannema2013` states explicitly that endocrine status and
  bone age were not studied; `moffatt2025` reports none. The discriminating measurement is **absent in
  exactly the two cases with the largest heights** and is supplied only by the drug trial.
- **`hannema2013` mixes duration in**: puberty started late, ~15 years. Not a clean yield observation.
- **The bone-age null is weak.** 12 months, and bone age carries ~0.5 y of reading error. P = .22 with no
  equivalence margin is *failure to detect*, not *demonstration of absence*.
- **It does not identify which term of the yield moved.** CXM doubling localises the activity to the
  **hypertrophic** compartment — type X collagen is made only by hypertrophic chondrocytes — but a CXM
  rise is equally consistent with *more* hypertrophic cells, *larger* ones, or faster turnover.
- **Neither individual's height was costless**: scoliosis needing spinal fusion at 12, macrodactyly
  needing epiphysiodesis, BMI 34, kyphosis.

## The failure mode was predicted by a paper read the same week

**Five children discontinued for musculoskeletal complications. Three developed slipped capital femoral
epiphyses.**

`williams2001`, read four rounds ago for an unrelated reason, measured in bovine proximal tibia:

> **ultimate tensile stress (MPa) = 3.2 − 2.8 × growth plate thickness (mm)**, R² 0.55, P < 0.0001

**A thicker growth plate is a mechanically weaker one.** The entire premise of this route is that CNP
expands the hypertrophic zone — which *thickens the plate*. The same paper measured the **human capital
femoral physis** at 1.35 ± 0.33 mm with ultimate stress 0.98 ± 0.29 MPa, about half the bovine value —
at the exact anatomical site where slipped epiphysis occurs.

So the route carries a specific, mechanically predictable, load-bearing-joint failure mode, and it
appeared in the first non-FGFR3 trial. **The inference is this atlas's, not either paper's** — `dauber2026`
does not attribute the slips to plate thickening, `williams2001`'s regression is bovine, and these
syndromes may carry independent SCFE risk. It is testable: measure physeal thickness on imaging in
treated children.

## Erdafitinib — fact-checked, and it does not hold up as stated

The claim: erdafitinib is trapped in lysosomes and leaks back out, giving prolonged FGFR inhibition,
per Janssen.

**I could not verify it.** Searches of Europe PMC for erdafitinib with lysosome/sequestration/trapping,
for JNJ-42756493 with lysosomal accumulation, and for erdafitinib with "prolonged inhibition" returned
**no primary paper establishing this for erdafitinib**. The hits are general FGFR reviews. Lysosomal
trapping of kinase inhibitors is a **real and well-documented class phenomenon** — there is current work
on masitinib doing exactly this — and erdafitinib is a weak base, so it is chemically plausible. But
plausible is not established, and a company statement in a label or EPAR is not a document I retrieved.
Recorded as unverified, per CORR-029/CORR-030.

**And it is the wrong axis of comparison anyway.** Three reasons:

1. **Prolonged target engagement is a PK property.** It changes dosing convenience, not which term of the
   yield the drug moves.
2. **The height-relevant selectivity is FGFR3 vs FGFR1.** FGFR1 blockade disrupts FGF23/phosphate
   handling — hyperphosphatemia is the class's dose-limiting toxicity — which is directly counterproductive
   in a mineralising skeleton. Erdafitinib is pan-FGFR1–4; infigratinib is FGFR1–3 and was actually
   developed for achondroplasia.
3. **FGFR inhibition is less h_term-selective than CNP.** FGFR3 restrains growth partly by
   dephosphorylating NPR2 and lowering cGMP — so blocking it does converge on the CNP node — but FGFR3
   *also* directly restrains proliferation. Blocking FGFR3 therefore raises **both** terms of the yield,
   including the one that spends the pool. **CNP is the more term-selective entry point.**

The reasoning style was right — drug properties do matter, and the atlas already holds `ursachi2026_2`
showing infigratinib is a *weak* inhibitor of the FGFR3-N540K hypochondroplasia mutant, which is exactly
the kind of difference that could make one inhibitor genuinely better. But that is a **potency-against-target**
argument, not a lysosomal-residence one.

Erdafitinib is an oncology drug for metastatic urothelial carcinoma carrying central serous retinopathy
and retinal detachment. It is not a height intervention.
