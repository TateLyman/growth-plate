# PMID 31787777 — Mechanical stimulation of growth plate chondrocytes: previous approaches and future directions (2019, Exp Mech)

Read in full (PMC, ~26k chars; review).

**Content.**
- Tools: external fixators (static, or pneumatic dynamic), compression rigs, Flexcell tension, 4-point-bending membranes, microfluidics.
- Results summary:
  - Static compression: lower proliferation, shorter proliferative and hypertrophic zones, column disruption.
  - Static tension (Alberty, rabbit): zones taller initially, then **effect fades with time to control levels**. That is adaptation again.
  - Stokes, rat vertebra: compression −48% growth; tension +13% (hypertrophic size unchanged under tension).
  - Valteau: static 0.2 MPa is more damaging than dynamic 0.2±0.06 MPa at 0.1 Hz. Peak compression magnitude dominates suppression.
  - **Dynamic tension promotes chondral growth** (Wang & Mao): more height and more proliferating cells than static tension. Higher-frequency tension gives more collagen and proteoglycan synthesis. Extreme tension kills cells.
- Mechanotransduction: RhoA/ROCK promotes proliferation and reduces hypertrophy. N-cadherin drives MSC → chondrocyte condensation. Cadherin/β-catenin are needed for column formation.
- Counter-evidence: paralysed fetuses still achieve near-normal length, so mechanics matters more for bone formation than for growth-plate function.

**Relevance.**
- **Adaptation recurs**: static tension's growth-promoting effect fades. This matches the condyle advancement data (response decays after ~3 weeks), so a sustained effect would need *changing/dynamic* stimuli.
- **Dynamic (cyclic) tension** is the most anabolic mechanical regime for growth cartilage. Repurposing candidates: cyclic distraction-type loading (traction) of a joint, which, unlike surgical distraction, is non-invasive.
- Column formation (cadherin/β-catenin, oriented cell rotation) is needed for *directed* longitudinal growth. An engineered front without columns would grow isotropically (like condylar hyperplasia or osteophytes), not longitudinally.
