# PMID 38144567 — Effects of physiological and injurious hydrostatic pressure on murine ex vivo articular and growth plate cartilage explants: an RNAseq study (2023, Front Endocrinol)

Read in full (PMC, ~41k chars).

**Model.** 4-week mouse hip caps (articular) and E15 metatarsals (growth-plate model). A single 1 h of hydrostatic pressure (HP) at 0, 5 or 50 MPa, then RNAseq at 24 h. n = 4 (two hip outliers excluded).

**Results.**
- Hip cap vs metatarsal at baseline: 2775 genes up and 3368 down.
  - Up in hip caps: Col10a1 (×7.7 log2FC), Frzb, Cytl1, Gpx3, Clec3a.
  - Down in hip caps: Mfap4, Igfbp2, Fgf10, Hoxd13, Ptn.
- Hip caps responded strongly: 5 MPa gave 375 up / 322 down; 50 MPa gave 1022 up / 724 down. **Metatarsal (growth-plate) cartilage barely responded:** 5 up / 23 down at 5 MPa.
- 5 MPa: oxidative phosphorylation/ATP up; nuclear transport down.
- 50 MPa (injurious): ossification, cartilage development and chondrocyte differentiation down; developmental growth and cell size up. OA-risk genes changed (Ctsk up; Chadl and Apoe down). Piezo2 up and Trpm4 down at both pressures.

**Relevance.**
- Growth-plate-type (embryonic metatarsal) cartilage is far less HP-responsive than articular cartilage, at least transcriptionally after one 1 h dose. Articular chondrocytes are the more mechano-plastic population.
- Injurious HP pushes articular cartilage toward a "transient/developmental-like" program (OA-like). OA is the pathological route by which articular cartilage re-acquires growth-plate-like behaviour.
- Dose matters: physiological HP (5 MPa) is mostly metabolic; supraphysiological HP (50 MPa) is damaging. Any HP-based lever has to stay in the 0.5–10 MPa window.
