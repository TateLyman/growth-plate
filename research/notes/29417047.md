# PMID 29417047 — Staines et al. A Computed Microtomography Method for Understanding Epiphyseal Growth Plate Fusion (2018, Front Mater)

Read in full (PMC, ~20k chars).

**Model.** Mouse tibiae: CBA males at 8 and 40 weeks, imaged with synchrotron microCT (1.1 µm pixels); one C57BL/6 female at 20 months, imaged on a benchtop scanner (5 µm). Mouse growth plates bridge progressively but do not vanish.

**Methods.** Bony bridges crossing the whole plate width were identified (bridges under ~125 voxels excluded) and projected onto the joint surface as density per 256×256 µm window. A linear-elastic finite-element model of the tibia applied a static 3× body-weight load (~1 N).

**Results.**
- Bridges are enriched at the periphery of the plate; thicker bridges sit peripherally.
- At 8 weeks, bridges co-locate with high local von Mises stress.
- Bridge number and density rise with age.
- With few bridges, the plate is loaded as a "sandwich" under mostly compressive hydrostatic stress, with shear only at the edges.
- At 40 weeks, thick bridges act as stress concentrators and raise stress beneath the plate.

**Authors' mechanobiology argument.** Tension and shear accelerate ossification; hydrostatic compression maintains cartilage (Carter & Wong; Stokes). This explains why bridges start at the periphery, where shear is high. Bridges then concentrate stress, which may in turn accelerate arrest in their vicinity.

**Background they restate.**
- Growth stops before histological fusion in humans and rats (Becks 1948; Moss & Noback 1958; Weise 2001; Parfitt 2002).
- Estrogen-deficient humans (ERα mutation, CYP19 mutation) never fuse and keep growing slowly into adulthood.
- Injury bridges may form intramembranously (Xian 2004).

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Indirect. Fusion is spatially heterogeneous and mechanically patterned.
- Cartilage persistence is favoured where loading is hydrostatic compression; ossification is favoured where shear or tension dominate.
- This is the Carter rule, applied directly to closure.

**Levers.**
- Mechanical stress state (hydrostatic vs deviatoric) is a candidate control of whether cartilage persists or ossifies.
- A mechanism that converts the local stress state in a cartilage layer to predominantly cyclic hydrostatic compression with low shear should favour persistence and resist bridging.
- The reverse should accelerate ossification, which you would want at the trailing (metaphyseal) side of any new growth zone.

**Chase.** Staines 2016 (SR microCT bridging in OA mouse model); Carter & Wong 2003; Villemure & Stokes 2009; Pichler 2013 (histology of closure); Xian 2004.
