# PMID 15574348 — Xiong H, Rabie ABM, Hägg U. Mechanical strain leads to condylar growth in adult rats (2005, Front Biosci)

Read in full (PDF, 9 pp).

**Model.** 78 female Sprague-Dawley rats, **120 days old** (called "adult"; caveat: rats never fully fuse and still grow slowly at this age). Bite-jumping appliance for continuous mandibular advancement: 9 experimental + 4 controls per time point (d3, 7, 14, 21, 30, 60). Posterior condyle measured (1104×811 µm frame); PCNA, SOX9, Col2a1 ISH.

**Results** (exact numbers only as p-values in text; magnitudes in figures).
- Controls: adult condylar cartilage is only 2–3 cell layers, with no obvious hypertrophic layer. Proliferation stays low and unchanged over 60 days.
- Advanced animals:
  - Fibrous layer thicker from d14 (p<0.01) and through d30–60 (p<0.001).
  - Proliferative layer thicker at d21 (p<0.001), then back to control.
  - Col II-positive layer thicker from d14, peak d21, still elevated d30 (p<0.001), back to control d60.
  - PCNA+ proliferating cells up at d3 and d21. PCNA+ cells at the erosive (ossification) front up from d21, peak d30, still elevated d60 (p<0.01).
  - SOX9 peaks d3 and d21 in the proliferative layer; Col2a1 strongest d21.
- Mechanism proposed: strain alignment → **Ihh as mechanotransduction mediator** (Tang 2004) → PTHrP → SOX9. VEGF-driven neovascularisation brings perivascular mesenchymal cells, and the size of that pool correlates with new bone.
- Clinical context: Herbst appliance in young adults gives condylar growth on MRI (Ruf & Pancherz 1999), with "reactivation of cells of the prechondroblast zone".

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- A sustained mechanical strain (continuous tensile/compressive repositioning of the condyle relative to the fossa) **reactivates endochondral growth in a quiescent adult secondary cartilage**.
- Cascade: proliferation → SOX9 → Col II cartilage → ossification front.
- Response is *transient* (~3–4 weeks), returning to baseline by 60 days despite continued appliance. **Adaptation/accommodation** limits sustained growth.

**Levers.**
1. Change of strain state (not merely load magnitude) reactivates progenitors under the fibrous/articular layer.
2. The response decays as tissue adapts, so a growth-sustaining mechanism probably needs *progressive* or *varying* stimulus (cf. step-wise advancement; Rabie 2003 stepwise advancement correlates replicating cells with osteogenesis).
3. Ihh is the mechanotransducer; neovascularisation (VEGF) recruits perivascular progenitors.
