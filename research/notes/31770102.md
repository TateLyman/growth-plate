# PMID 31770102 — Maione et al. Congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism/Kallmann syndrome is associated with statural gain in both men and women (2020, Eur J Endocrinol)

Read in full (accepted manuscript PDF, 29 pages incl. figures).

**Cohort.** 219 patients (165 male, 54 female), single centre 1975–2018. Strict CHH/KS diagnosis with normal GH/IGF-1.

**Results.**
- Men: adult height 179.0±8.2 cm vs mid-parental target 175.0±5.6 (p<0.0001). Taller than brothers (181.3±7.9 vs 179.0±6.9, p=0.045, n=77).
- Women: 166.0±6.7 vs target 162 (p<0.0001); no difference vs sisters (n=16).
- ΔH (adult minus target height): patients +6.2±7.2 cm vs siblings +3.4±5.2 (p<0.0001). ΔH rises with age at diagnosis (r=0.28, p=0.0015).
- Growth during sex-steroid replacement falls with age at start (r=−0.57):
  - start <16 y: +16.6±5.9 cm; ≥16 y: +4.1±5.2
  - start <18 y: +13.1±7.3; ≥18 y: +2.5±3.9
  - Patients starting around 30 y: **no observable height change**, suggesting the plates had fused anyway despite severe sex-steroid deficiency.
- Bone age before treatment ~3–3.5 y behind chronological age.

**What it establishes.**
- Sex-steroid deficiency delays but does **not** prevent fusion: by ~30 y the plates fuse even without puberty, probably via residual adrenal-derived estrogen and cumulative senescence.
- The net extra height from very late closure is only ~3 cm over siblings.
- Even a decade of extra open-plate time adds little, because the growth rate of an old, unstimulated plate is low.
- So keeping cartilage "open" is insufficient. Growth needs active proliferation and hypertrophy drive.

**Levers.** None mechanical. It sets a quantitative ceiling: in humans, a senescent but unfused plate contributes only a few cm over a decade. Any adult mechanism needs a *new, young* progenitor population, not prolongation of an aging one.
