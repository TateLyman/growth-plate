# PMID 25474590 — Zhou X, von der Mark K, ... de Crombrugghe B. Chondrocytes transdifferentiate into osteoblasts in endochondral bone during development, postnatal growth and fracture healing in mice (2014, PLoS Genet)

Read in full (PMC, ~73k chars).

**Model.** Mice: Col10a1-Cre and Agc1-CreERT2 with LacZ/Tomato/Osx-flox-EGFP reporters; 2.3Col1-GFP osteoblast reporter; semi-stabilised tibial fracture in 2–3-month-old (adult) mice.

**Findings.**
- Hypertrophic chondrocyte-derived cells become functional osteoblasts: ~63% of Ocn+ trabecular and ~62% of endosteal osteoblasts at 1 month; ~60% (trabecular) and ~68% (endosteal) at 3 weeks.
- Contribution falls with age. Narrower area at 11–13 weeks; by 8 months almost no labelled cells in primary spongiosa. Growth-plate activity correlates with chondrocyte→osteoblast output.
- **Adult fracture.** Callus chondrocytes (labelled d6–7) give rise to callus osteoblasts (Tomato+Col1-GFP+ at d14, more at d29). Adult callus recapitulates growth-plate-type endochondral ossification including transdifferentiation.
- Possibly via dedifferentiation to marrow progenitors (preliminary).

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- In adults the full endochondral program (cartilage → hypertrophy → bone, via transdifferentiation) can be re-run de novo in a fracture callus, from adult progenitors.
- The adult skeleton keeps the machinery; it lacks only the trigger and the polarised architecture.

**Levers.** Callus-type endochondral ossification is the template for adult cartilage-to-bone conversion. If a callus-like cartilage could be made to persist as a polarised layer with continuous replacement at one face, it would act as a neo-growth plate. Mechanical stimulus governs callus cartilage formation.
