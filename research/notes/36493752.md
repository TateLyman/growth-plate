# PMID 36493752 — Fibroblast inflammatory priming determines regenerative versus fibrotic skin repair in reindeer

Sinha S, Sparks HD, Labit E, et al. *Cell* 2022;185(25):4717-4736. PMC9888357. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2022.11.004

**What I read:** the complete PMC full text — abstract, Introduction, all seven Results sections, Discussion, Limitations of the Study, STAR Methods (animals, wounding, ectopic grafting, in vivo drug delivery, single-cell processing, co-culture, CSF1R/CXCR4 inhibition, proteomics, all bioinformatics subsections), and the supplementary figure/data legends. Not an abstract read.

## Model
Adult reindeer (*Rangifer tarandus*), both sexes, during intense early antler growth. Identical 12 mm full-thickness excisional wounds (and 12 mm full-depth contact burns, 145 °C rod, 30 s) in dorsal back skin vs antler velvet. **Important for our purposes: "In all cases, full thickness injuries were made on velvet that ensheaths the pedicle bone; a site that is distant from the growth zone located at the antler tip."** So this is velvet-as-skin, not the antler growth zone. Plus autologous velvet→back-skin ectopic grafts followed to 6 months, scRNA-seq/scATAC-seq/bulk RNA-seq/proteomics, and a mouse WIHN (wound-induced hair neogenesis) pharmacology arm.

## Exact numbers
- HF density restored to **73% of pre-injury in velvet vs 12% in back skin** at 30 dpw. Back skin contracted significantly more; velvet healed by new tissue, not contraction.
- Velvet burns regenerated appendages and pigment by 30 dpw; back-skin burns hypertrophically scarred.
- Fibroblasts and Schwann cells the most discordant cell types (Pearson r = 0.87 and 0.73); **10/12 fibroblast subclusters tissue-specific**. Back-enriched states 7-fold higher in back (p = 0.0006); velvet-enriched 22-fold higher in velvet (p = 0.0003).
- Velvet fibroblast markers: **CRABP1, MDK, PTN**; back skin: CXCL1, CXCL3, CCL2. 98–99% of DEGs have human orthologs.
- **CRABP1+ fibroblasts: 82% of fetal human skin vs 10% of adult human skin.** CRABP1 protein absent from adult human interfollicular dermis; throughout velvet interfollicular dermis.
- Machine classifier: **93% of adult human fibroblasts "pro-fibrotic"; 68% of fetal human "pro-regenerative."**
- Fibrosis-primed fibroblasts shared **602 genes (20%)** across reindeer/mouse/human; regenerative fibroblasts shared only **32 (1%)**.
- Immune infiltrate: 71% vs 31% of cells at 3 dpw (p = 0.0272, back vs velvet); converged at 7 dpw (76.3 vs 69.5%, p = 1); re-diverged at 14 dpw (47.1 vs 15.8%, p = 0.0236).
- Ectopic graft: regeneration preserved at 30 days post-graft, **progressively lost by 6 months**; fibroblasts drift unidirectionally from velvet-enriched root clusters 0/8 into back-enriched pro-inflammatory clusters 6/7/9.
- Transitionary fibroblast signature: **YAP1 (padj 5e-4), TEAD1 (1e-5), EN1 (5e-5), FAK/PTK2 (3e-5)**. Re-training reclassified **86% of previously "Unclassified" fetal human fibroblasts as "transitionary."**
- Pharmacology, mouse WIHN, **local**: CSF1R inhibitor (PLX5622) **2.8-fold** more neogenic HFs (6.4 ± 2.0 → 17.8 ± 5.0); CXCR4 antagonist (plerixafor, topical gel) **5.1-fold** (8.1 ± 3.2 → 41.6 ± 9.7). **Systemic i.p. plerixafor: no effect (7.3 ± 3.0 → 10.3 ± 2.6).**
- Reverse direction, in velvet: midkine inhibitor (iMDK) suppressed appendage regeneration **2.2-fold (padj 0.001), persisting at 42 dpw**; CRABP1+ cells 29% vs 44% control. PLAU **delayed but did not prevent** regeneration (2.4-fold at 30 dpw, padj 0.028; recovered by 42 dpw), CRABP1+ cells 60% vs 44%.
- Pro-regenerative fibroblast secretome: **MDK, PTN, BMP3, BMP4, RSPO3, NDP, INHBA, FGF10, LAMC3, SLIT2/3**. Pro-inflammatory: CSF1, CXCL12, GAS6, PLAU, PLAT, C3, C4A, CFH, CCL2, PTGS2.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **Regenerative competence is a fibroblast *ground state*, not an injury response.** It is cell-autonomous (travels with the graft), conserved to humans, and marked by CRABP1 — a retinoic-acid binding protein. This is the same gene that rises in the de-repressed perichondrial state (see [24572990](24572990.md)) and that Monaghan 2012 found nerve-dependently elevated in regenerating axolotl limb wounds (cited in [31026439](31026439.md)). Three unrelated literatures converge on CRABP1 as the licence for regenerative competence.
2. **The ground state decays, and the decay mechanism is named.** Not age *per se* — mechanotransduction: YAP1/TEAD1/EN1/FAK. CSF1 is a fibroblast-specific YAP target. So the loss of competence is a mechanically driven, actively maintained repression — which is in principle a drug target, not a fact of adulthood.
3. **Back-skin fibroblasts transiently open the regenerative epigenome and still fail.** At 7 dpw, PROM1, INHBA and GREB1L accessibility rose in *back skin* to velvet levels; back-skin fibroblasts activated regenerative programs in greater magnitude and frequency than velvet — and still scarred. The authors' conclusion is the important one: competence "depends on either exceeding, rather than simply initiating, a critical threshold of network activity, or timing of this initiation." **Opening the locus is not enough; the threshold and the window are the variables.** This is the single most transferable lesson to the cartilage problem.
4. **Local, not systemic.** The same CXCR4 antagonist worked topically and did nothing i.p. Any de-repression strategy built on this axis has to be local.

## Levers (correctly signed, from this paper)
- **CSF1/CSF1R blockade** — local, 2.8-fold. PLX5622 is a tool compound; pexidartinib is an approved CSF1R inhibitor.
- **CXCL12/CXCR4 blockade** — local, 5.1-fold. **Plerixafor is an approved drug.** Strongest effect in the paper, and the local/systemic dissociation is explicit.
- **YAP/TEAD, FAK, EN1** — the erosion machinery. Converges with THEORY.md and COMPOUNDS.md Node D, but note the sign is tissue-dependent (see limits).
- **MDK/PTN** — required, not merely correlated (iMDK ablates regeneration durably). Both are heparin-binding; they signal through HS proteoglycans/PTPRZ1 — which links to the heparan-sulfate perichondrial border (de-repression instance #1) and to [27499874](27499874.md), where positional information in axolotl *and mouse* limb ECM is HS-mediated.

## Anti-levers
- PLAU, CSF1, CXCL12 as agonists — all suppress regeneration.
- Systemic CXCR4 antagonism — inert.

## Honest limits
1. **This paper does not identify the periosteum–velvet interactive substance.** The wounds were made on pedicle-level velvet, explicitly distant from the antler growth zone, and nothing here concerns the periosteal partner tissue. The Tier 000 item stays open.
2. The output is **hair follicles, pigment and dermal architecture — not length, not cartilage, not bone.** Everything about magnitude has to come from elsewhere.
3. **Sign conflict on YAP/TAZ.** Here YAP1-TEAD1 is the pro-fibrotic, anti-regenerative driver in fibroblasts. In COMPOUNDS.md Node D, YAP/TAZ was read as a pro-growth switch in cartilage. Both can be true — YAP's output is lineage-dependent — but I cannot keep treating "YAP activation" as a single-signed lever. Flagged in COMPOUNDS.md.
4. Reads mapped to *Bos taurus*; ~33% did not map contiguously and ~39% of mapped reads lacked annotated orthologs. Authors estimate ~20% additional mapping with a true reindeer reference.
5. Mouse pharmacology is WIHN — hair neogenesis in a large excisional wound — not a reindeer or human endpoint.
6. The 6-month graft decay and the single-cell graft time course were **different cohorts**, so the authors could not correlate molecular drift with functional loss in the same animals. They say so.
