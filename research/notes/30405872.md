# PMID 30405872 — Development of an artificial finger-like knee loading device to promote bone health (2017, J Biomed Sci Eng)

Read in full (PMC, ~21k chars). An engineering paper.

**Content.**
- Under-actuated 4-finger "hand" (2 phalanges each, torsion springs, slider–crank plus worm gear) for **position-specific** lateral knee loading. Up to 20 N per finger (40 N total), 1–5 Hz. Force/displacement feedback with safety retraction.
- Mannequin testing only; no biological data.
- Rationale: preclinical data suggest efficacy depends on the *stress distribution* at the knee, so targeted contact points may work better.
- Mentions the commercial non-contact stimulators already in use: **Exogen LIPUS** and **Physio-Stim** (pulsed electromagnetic field).

**Relevance.**
- Hardware concept for *spatially targeted* cyclic loading on a joint's surface, e.g. aiming at the epiphyseal rim or perichondrial ring rather than the whole joint.
- If the theory needs mechanical stimulation localised to the peripheral (perichondrial/Ranvier) zone of a fused bone end, a multi-contact, independently driven array is the delivery form.
- Exogen (LIPUS) and PEMF are existing approved devices that could be **repurposed** as components.
