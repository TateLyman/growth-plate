# 34362001 — Ossification and fusion of the vertebral ring apophysis as an important part of spinal maturation

**Citation.** J Clin Med 2021;10(15):3217. PMC8347734. Read in full.

## Model / method
Retrospective CT study. 456 of 4,775 available high-resolution thoracic/abdominal CT scans from a tertiary paediatric hospital, ages 6–21, 50% female, no spinal or bone pathology, no GH treatment. Every superior and inferior ring apophysis from T1 to sacrum scored at 4 points (anterior, posterior, two lateral) on exact mid-sagittal and mid-coronal reconstructions, by two blinded observers. Four-phase ossification/fusion scale → five-stage ring apophysis maturation (RAM) score. Compared against Risser grade and the Proximal Humerus Ossification System (PHOS).

## Exact numbers
- RAM correlated with age at **R = 0.892 (p < 0.001)**; with Risser 0.900, with PHOS 0.908.
- **Ossification ages 9–15 (males), 7–15 (females); fusion 14–19 (males), 13–19 (females)**, earlier in females (p = 0.002).
- **At age 21, 98% of rings were completely fused.**
- Inferior ring ossifies earlier; **superior ring fuses earlier**. Anterior and posterior parts ossify and fuse ~half a year before the lateral parts (p = 0.031).
- High thoracic and low lumbar levels ossify later but fuse earlier than thoracolumbar levels.
- Peak height velocity: ~13 in females, ~15 in males. At 13, female median RAM was stage 0 at T1–T6 and stage 1 at T7–T12; at 15, male median was stage 3 at T1–T2 and stage 1 at T3–T12.

## The two statements that matter here
1. **"The ring apophyses are not inside the epiphyseal plate and are not involved in the longitudinal growth of the spine."** They are the anchor into which Sharpey's fibres of the annulus fibrosus insert — the disc's attachment to the vertebral body. **So this is not the spinal growth structure**, and anyone (including me, provisionally) who reaches for the ring apophysis as "the spine's growth plate" has the wrong structure. The growth structure is the **vertebral endplate epiphyseal plate**, which this paper explicitly distinguishes and which Risser grade "does not reflect."
2. **"The spine continues to grow longer than the lower extremities… the growth of the spine continues after the lower limb's growth has ceased."** (citing Sanders 2020 and Di Meglio, with differing yearly height-gain velocities for trunk versus lower limb.) The spine also "continues to mature after Risser 4 and 5," and Risser 5 is not synchronous with the end of ring maturation.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The spine is the last axis to stop growing, and therefore the most recently closed.** If the exhaustion/closure clock is estrogen-paced rather than division-counted ([24485503](24485503.md)) and the limiting variable is how recently a growth apparatus was active, the vertebral endplates are the youngest-closed growth cartilages in an adult skeleton. That makes them the most plausible candidate for reactivation, not the lower limb — which is where [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) currently points.
2. **The spine is also where the surface count is largest.** 24 vertebral bodies, each with a superior and an inferior endplate, is **48 growth surfaces on the stature axis** against 4 per leg (femoral head, distal femur, proximal tibia, distal tibia). Sitting height is a real and substantial component of stature. Per-surface rates that would be negligible in a leg become material across 48 surfaces. **This is the clearest route in the corpus to raising the magnitude ceiling of §7 of ACTIVATION.md, and it is a route that document does not consider.**
3. **It also corrects a structural error I would otherwise have made.** The target is the endplate epiphyseal plate, not the apophyseal ring. The ring is mechanical hardware for the disc; manipulating it would change disc anchorage, not height.
4. **It is a usable timing dataset.** 98% fusion at 21, and level-dependent heterogeneity of several years, means the spine presents a gradient of recently-closed structures rather than a single state — useful if one wanted to test the "most recently closed is most reactivatable" prediction within one patient.

## Levers
- **The vertebral endplate epiphyseal plate** — 48 surfaces, latest-closing, explicitly not the apophysis. The structure to get papers on.
- **Level-dependent maturation heterogeneity** — a within-subject gradient for testing reactivation.
- The **disc** as a separate, mechanically distinct, and independently cm-scale axis (not addressed in this paper; see §Chase).

## Honest limits
- Entirely about the apophyseal ring, which the authors say is **not** a growth structure. Its value to this project is the two statements above plus the surface-count realisation, not the data.
- Ages 6–21 only. Nothing about adults.
- CT-based ossification/fusion scoring, no histology, no growth measurement, no cartilage assessment.
- Retrospective, scans acquired for unrelated indications, 90% excluded for image quality.
- Framed entirely around idiopathic scoliosis aetiology.

## Chase
- **Ref 13 (Woo et al.) and ref 19 (Uys et al.)** — the ring-apophysis vs endplate-plate distinction, in full.
- **Ref 30 (Sanders 2020) — "the spine continues to grow longer than the lower extremities."** Priority: this is the claim that reorders the whole target list.
- **Refs 22, 31 (Di Meglio) — yearly height-gain velocity, trunk vs lower limb.** Needed for the arithmetic: how much of adult stature is spine, and at what rate each axis grew.
- **2922637 and 7089697** (already Tier 0 of [NEEDED_FULLTEXTS.md](../NEEDED_FULLTEXTS.md)) — vertebral end-plate growth cartilage.
- Anything on **vertebral endplate cartilage in adults**: thickness, cell populations, turnover, calcified-zone advance. The spinal equivalent of the tidemark question.
- **Intervertebral disc height and stature**: adults lose 1–3 cm of standing height to disc degeneration, and disc height is restorable in principle. That is a mechanistically independent, cm-scale axis that stacks with rather than competes against the endplate route. 15296209 (stadiometry + MRI of disc height under hyperextension) came back abstract-only; on the needed list.
