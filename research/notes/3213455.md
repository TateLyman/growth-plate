# PMID 3213455 — Peltonen J, Kahri A, Karaharju E, Alitalo I. Regeneration after physeal distraction of the radius in sheep (1988, Acta Orthop Scand)

Read in full (PDF, 6 pp).

**Model.** 18 growing sheep (2–5 months). External-fixator distraction of the distal radial physis, 0.5–1.0 mm/day for 2–4 weeks. Followed 1 week to 5 months.

**Findings.**
- Separation always occurred through the hypertrophic layer at 5–7 days (epiphysiolysis). The gap filled with haematoma, then distraction-aligned collagen bundles. **Lamellar bone formed directly along linearly organised collagen**; woven bone formed where collagen was disorganised and from the inner periosteum.
- Length gained 4–16 mm (~7% of radius).
- **From the perichondrium, transverse inclusions with chondrocytes penetrated the metaphyseal gap.** Capillary proliferation occurred at the ends and periphery.
- **In 2 sheep a "double physis" formed:** a zone of chondrocytes across the metaphysis, still active at 18–20 weeks.
  - Proliferative-like cells in the centre, hypertrophic cells at both ends, palisaded columns, and tetracycline uptake on both sides.
  - It was **a bipolar ectopic growth plate making bone in both directions**, 9–11 weeks after fixator removal.
  - Source: proliferative chondrocytes displaced at separation, or the **perichondrial ring**.
- Epiphyseal diameter increased by 2 (0–4) mm, attributed to increased perichondrial vascularity. The physis remained open in most animals.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth (important).**
- **A displaced island of growth-plate chondrocytes (or perichondrial cells), placed under tension, can self-organise into a functional bipolar physis inside bone** and keep elongating it for months.
  - This "seeded growth plate" phenomenon shows that a growth-plate architecture can arise de novo within metaphyseal bone when proliferative chondrocytes are present and the mechanical field is longitudinal tension.
- The **perichondrial ring** is a source of chondrocytes that invade a tension gap.
- Tension organises collagen, and collagen orientation sets bone architecture.

**Levers.**
- (1) Seed: chondroprogenitors (perichondrial/periosteal origin).
- (2) Field: longitudinal tension.
- (3) Result: a self-assembling bipolar growth zone.
- After fusion, the non-surgical analogue would need to recruit periosteal/perichondrial progenitors into a compliant zone under tension. In the sheep, the zone was a surgically created gap; without surgery, such a zone would need to be generated biologically (e.g., a cartilage-rich callus-like region at the old physeal scar or articular margin).
- Bipolar growth cartilage (like apophyses) is a viable growth architecture. It does not need an SOC on one side.

**Chase.** De Bastiani 1986 (chondrodiatasis, slow distraction without fracture); Connolly 1986 (epiphyseal traction, clinical); Monticelli 1981 (morphology); Kenwright 1990 (distraction close to maturity).
