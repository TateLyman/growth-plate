# PMID 39090666 — Intermittent mechanical loading on mouse tibia accelerates longitudinal bone growth by inducing PTHrP expression in the female tibial growth plate (2024, Physiol Rep)

Read in full (PMC, ~30k chars).

**Model.**
- 75 TOPGAL mice, 4 weeks old (juvenile, fast growth).
- Right tibia axial cyclic compression: 5 N, 2 Hz, 100 cycles/session, 3×/week for 4 weeks. Left tibia as control.
- 25 mice followed 4 more weeks without loading.

**Results.**
- Loaded tibiae grew significantly more over the 4 weeks (exact mm only in figures/Data S1). The difference **persisted** 4 weeks after loading stopped. Both sexes.
- Whole growth-plate height, epiphyseal (resting) zone height and growth-plate area were greater in loaded tibiae.
- **Pthrp mRNA rose in loaded growth plates in females only.** IGF-1 and β-catenin were unchanged.
- No microCT bone-mass change.
- Cited:
  - Xu 2013: cyclic tensile strain raises PTHrP in growth-plate chondrocytes.
  - Zhang 2010: 0.5 N knee loading lengthened femur and tibia in 8-week mice with thicker plates.
  - High loads (8.5–17 N in rats) suppress growth.

**What it establishes.**
- **Intermittent, moderate, physiological-range cyclic loading *increases* longitudinal growth** (in juveniles), with resting-zone expansion and PTHrP induction. This contradicts the simplistic Hueter–Volkmann view: the *mode* (intermittent vs sustained) and *magnitude* determine the sign of the effect.
- **Mechanical loading can induce PTHrP**, the niche-maintaining factor for resting-zone stem cells and for the condylar superficial layer.

**Levers.**
- Low-to-moderate intermittent loading is a PTHrP-inducing stimulus. For the theory: cyclic loading could maintain the PTHrP source that keeps a progenitor layer from exhausting (cf. the Tsutsumi-Arai condyle PTHrP gradient).
- Caveat: juvenile animals with active plates. There is no evidence that this applies to fused bone.

**Chase.** Xu 2013 (cyclic tensile strain → PTHrP in growth-plate chondrocytes); Zhang 2010 (knee loading lengthening, 0.5 N); Robling 2001; Hammond 2010; Umemura 1995.
