# PMID 17344109 — Zhang P, Yokota H. Effects of surgical holes in mouse tibiae on bone formation induced by knee loading (2007, Bone)

Read in full (NIH manuscript PDF, 16 pp).

**Model.** Female C57BL/6 mice, ~14–16 weeks old.
- Knee loading: 5 Hz, 0.5 N, 3 min/day for 3 days. Sham contralateral.
- One group had a 0.5 mm hole drilled through the tibia at the 25% site (opening the medullary cavity) on both sides. Bone formation was measured at the 50% and 75% sites.

**Results.**
- **Without holes**, knee loading:
  - 50% site: periosteal BFR 12.9 → 26.7 (×2.1); cortical area 0.52 → 0.57 mm²; cortical thickness 0.183 → 0.203 mm.
  - 75% site: BFR 8.1 → 21.6 (×2.7).
- **With holes:** the hole itself caused a large regional acceleratory phenomenon (RAP; periosteal BFR ×3.5 at the 50% site in sham). On top of that, **knee loading added no significant effect.**
- Interpretation: venting the medullary cavity abolishes the pressure-gradient mechanism, so **intramedullary pressure is necessary** for joint-loading anabolism. This is indirect: RAP confounds it.
- The authors note in passing: *"it might be easier to transmit a pressure signal over a cartilaginous growth zone than over an ossified growth zone due to its lower mechanical impedance."*

**What it establishes.**
- Joint-loading effects depend on a sealed hydraulic compartment.
- Separately, injury (a drill hole) is a far stronger local anabolic trigger (RAP) than loading. That says something about the scale of injury-induced responses, which are excluded here (no surgery).

**Levers.**
- The pressure-pumping mechanism needs a closed compartment.
- At a fused physis the physeal scar is ossified bone, which is stiffer and transmits pressure differently from cartilage. Mechanical delivery to the old physis region of a fused bone differs from a growing one.
