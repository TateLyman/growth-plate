# 38672135 — Cellular and molecular mechanisms of heterotopic ossification in FOP

**Citation.** Biomedicines 2024;12(4):779. PMC11048698. Review. Read in full (§1–5).

## What it is
A current review of ACVR1 R206H signalling and the cell-of-origin question in FOP. Used here as the map of **how an adult mammal builds a complete endochondral bone from scratch in soft tissue, and how fast** — because that is the process any post-fusion lengthening scheme would have to borrow and control.

## Exact numbers / mechanism
**The mutation.** R206H in the glycine–serine domain; ~97% of FOP patients. Prevalence 0.6–1.39 per million, ~900 confirmed cases. Median life expectancy 56 y; leading cause of death thoracic insufficiency from rib-cage ossification. Congenital great-toe malformation; flare-ups from ~age 5; most HO forms in adolescence and young adulthood, and **new HO volume decreases with age, attributed to reduced initiation and to "tissue availability"**.
- ACVR1 R206H **does not require a type I receptor heterodimer partner** to signal — it escapes the normal receptor-pairing constraints.
- **Activin A neofunction**: activin A is normally a *competitive antagonist* at ACVR1 (blocking SMAD1/5/8); the mutant converts it into an agonist. Activin A injection induces HO; anti-activin-A antibody abolishes it.
- Reduced binding of **FKBP12**, the negative regulator of type I receptors → ligand-independent basal activity.
- Non-canonical: raised p38 MAPK, PI3K/AKT/mTOR (mTOR and PI3K inhibitors reduce HO in FOP mice).
- **Mechanotransduction crossover: *Acvr1*^R206H cells misread soft microenvironments as stiff**, with raised RhoA, pCofilin, pMLC2 and YAP/TAZ, and consequent osteogenic differentiation. In *Acvr1*^Q207D mice, **genetic deletion of Yap ablated extraskeletal bone formation.** BMPs activate Rho-family GTPases → YAP/TAZ, and RhoA-mediated mechanotransduction feeds back to activate SMAD1/5/8.

**Cell of origin — settled by exclusion.** Ruled out: bone marrow (marrow transplant failed in mice *and* in an FOP patient), pericytes, smooth muscle, muscle stem cells (Pax7-Cre does not recapitulate), and true endothelium (VE-Cadherin-Cre lineage contributes nothing; Tie2-GFP+**CD31+** cells transplanted give no HO, Tie2-GFP+**CD31−** cells do). Established contributors:
- **PDGFRα+ Sca1+ CD34+ fibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs)** of the muscle interstitium — the predominant source. Pdgfrα-Cre gives the earliest and most severe HO.
- **Mx1+** muscle-interstitial cells (probably a FAP subset; Mx1+Sca1+Pdgfrα+ cells had markedly higher osteogenic capacity).
- **Scx+** tendon/ligament progenitors — spontaneous HO in Achilles, patellar ligament, tibialis anterior, knee joints at 8–18 wk; Scx+ lineage gives most of the hypertrophic chondrocytes in the lesion.
- **Tppp3+** tendon-sheath progenitors in non-genetic burn/tendon-injury HO.
- FAP heterogeneity: mouse FAP1 (Cxcl14+ Lum+, ECM-rich) and FAP2 (Dpp4+, migratory/signalling); human LUM+, FBN1+, MME+ subsets.

**The timeline — the number that matters most here.** After cardiotoxin muscle injury in an *Acvr1*^R206H mouse: FAPs activate and fibroproliferate by **4 dpi**, accumulate by **5 dpi** (and this accumulation is itself what stiffens the tissue), undergo **chondrogenic differentiation by 7 dpi**, and **mature heterotopic bone is present by 14 dpi.** Normal FAPs peak at 3–4 dpi and are cleared by macrophage TNF-α-mediated apoptosis at 5 dpi; the mutant FAPs' defect is **failure of clearance**, not excess proliferation (proliferation was equal at 3, 5 and 7 dpi).

**Therapeutics are all brakes.** Palovarotene — FDA/Canada/Australia approved, restricted to ≥8 y (female) and ≥10 y (male) because of skeletal growth toxicity (see [30226468](30226468.md)). Garetosmab (anti-activin A) — reduced HO and flare-ups in adult FOP in phase 2.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **An adult mammal can build a complete, vascularised, marrow-containing endochondral bone, de novo, in soft tissue, in fourteen days.** That is the single hardest fact to reconcile with "longitudinal growth is impossible after closure". The machinery is intact, resident, and fast. What is absent in the normal adult is not capability — it is *initiation*, *location* and *polarity*.
2. **The competent cell is everywhere and is identifiable.** PDGFRα+ Sca1+ CD34+ FAPs sit in the interstitium of every muscle; Scx+/Tppp3+ cells sit in every tendon and ligament, including those crossing the knee. A post-fusion lengthening scheme does not need to import progenitors; it needs to recruit and orient the ones already adjacent to the metaphysis.
3. **Two things are required, not one: a signal and an injury.** Spontaneous HO happens, but injury is the dominant trigger, and failure of FAP *clearance* at day 5 is what lets the lesion proceed. This matches [28629737](28629737.md) (BMP signalling alone insufficient; injury also required). It also matches the clinical fact that physeal bars form after trauma. The implication is uncomfortable but clear: **controlled injury is part of the mechanism, which is why this cannot be made into a benign drug.**
4. **The mechanical and pharmacological theories are the same theory.** R206H cells *misread stiffness* — soft read as stiff — via RhoA→YAP/TAZ, and Yap deletion abolishes the bone. So the osteogenic decision in these progenitors is a mechanosensing decision that a receptor mutation can spoof. That means a *mechanical* input (stiffness, strain, cyclic load) and a *pharmacological* input (BMP/ACVR1 tone, RhoA/YAP state) converge on the same node, and either could in principle set the chondro- vs osteo- fate of a periarticular progenitor. This is the strongest mechanistic bridge in the whole corpus between [THEORY.md](../THEORY.md) and the compound question, and it argues the two should be used together rather than chosen between.
5. **The reason this pathway is a dead end as a *systemic* lengthening strategy is written in the disease.** Uncontrolled activation of exactly this axis gives jaw ankylosis, joint fusion, a median life expectancy of 56, and death from a fused rib cage. Every ligament and tendon near the target site contains a competent progenitor, so a diffuse signal ossifies the joint rather than lengthening the bone. The limiting factor is spatial control.
6. **"Tissue availability" declines with age.** HO accrual slows in older FOP patients. Worth noting against any assumption that an adult has an unlimited competent progenitor pool.

## Levers
- **PDGFRα+ FAPs and Scx+/Tppp3+ tendon progenitors** as the resident competent cell population adjacent to any metaphysis. Named, sortable, and demonstrably able to complete endochondral ossification postnatally.
- **Activin A ↔ ACVR1** as an agonist/antagonist switch that is already drugged in both directions conceptually (garetosmab blocks it; the mutation converts it). Note that in *wild-type* tissue activin A is an ACVR1 *antagonist* — so activin A blockade in a normal adult would, if anything, raise BMP-SMAD tone, which is the opposite of its effect in FOP. Needs checking before anyone reasons from the FOP direction.
- **RhoA / YAP-TAZ** as the mechanochemical node where load and BMP signalling meet. YAP is required for ectopic endochondral bone. Already linked in my corpus to the hypertrophic-chondrocyte death threshold ([33063669](33063669.md)) — the same effector, opposite sign, in two tissues.
- **FKBP12** as the endogenous brake on type I BMP receptors.
- **FAP clearance at day 5 (macrophage TNF-α-driven apoptosis)** as the natural off-switch; the window 4–7 dpi is when fate is decided.
- **The 4→7→14 dpi schedule** as the timetable any induced-cartilage scheme would run on.

## Honest limits
- A review. Every number is secondary; the primaries are listed below and several are not yet read in full.
- Almost all mouse. The human data are the clinical natural history and two trials of *inhibitors*.
- FOP is a gain-of-function receptor disease. It shows the machinery exists; it does not show how to switch it on at one place and in one direction in a normal adult. Nothing here produced *length*; everything here produced *lumps*.
- Activin A's role is cell-type-dependent and contested: no difference in serum activin A/BMP4/BMP6 in FOP patients; FOP iPSC-derived endothelial cells show no SMAD1/5/8 activation on activin A.
- No mechanical loading experiment anywhere in it, despite the RhoA/YAP mechanosensing finding. That is a conspicuous gap.
