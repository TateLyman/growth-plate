# PMID 9246990 — Pereira BP, Cavanagh SP, Pho RWH. Longitudinal growth rate following slow physeal distraction: proximal tibial growth plate in rabbits (1997, Acta Orthop Scand)

Read in full (PDF, 7 pp).

**Model.** 6-week-old rabbits (open plates). External distractor across the proximal tibial physis (K-wires in epiphysis and diaphysis), 0.5 mm/day for 2, 3 or 4 weeks, then 3 days neutralisation, then removal.

**Results.**
- Normal growth rate G = 0.44 − 0.0024·age (mm/day): 0.33 mm/day at 6 weeks, 0.15 at 16 weeks.
- Net extra length vs the control limb at removal: 3.0 mm (2 weeks), 3.6 mm (3 weeks), 4.2 mm (4 weeks). Distraction totalled 8.5–14.6 mm, so ~55%, 47% and 40% *beyond* control growth.
- **After removal, the growth rate was identical to controls** (no acceleration, no retardation) up to 13 weeks. The gain was retained.
- Complications in 9 of 32: epiphysiolysis, fracture, collapse, sepsis. Distraction faster than ~2× normal growth fractures the plate.
  - Jones 1989: keep stress below 0.15 MPa to avoid separation (all epiphyses separated within 32 days at a fixed rate).
- Historical background: distracted growth plates thicken (proliferative and hypertrophic), with hyperplasia; Kenwright 1990 discusses distraction close to maturity.

**What it establishes.**
- A living growth plate under slow tension can be *mechanically pulled* to yield extra length (partly as cartilage expansion/interstitial growth, partly separation), and it then resumes normal growth.
- There is a **safe tension window (<~0.15 MPa; rate < ~2× native growth rate)**.
- It needs a cartilaginous plate. After fusion there is nothing to distract except bone (that is osteotomy distraction osteogenesis, which is surgery and excluded).

**Lever.** For any engineered adult cartilage front, there is quantitative guidance for tension dosing: stay below ~0.15 MPa and match the rate to the front's intrinsic production rate.
