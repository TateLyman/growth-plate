# 28629737 — The obligatory role of Activin A in the formation of heterotopic bone in FOP

**Citation.** Bone 2018;109:255–258. PMC6706059. Review/synthesis by the Regeneron group. Read in full.

## What it argues
A synthesis of Hatsell 2015, Hino 2015 and Dey 2016. Three claims, in order of importance for this project:

1. **Activin A is an obligate factor for the initiation of heterotopic ossification in FOP.** It signals aberrantly through mutant ACVR1 to transduce osteogenic signals. Blocking it prevents HO; supplying it causes HO.
2. **The responding cells are distinct tissue-resident mesenchymal progenitors in muscles and tendons** that recognise activin A as pro-osteogenic *only* in the context of FOP-causing mutant ACVR1, and that **give rise to the cartilaginous anlagen** which then form heterotopic bone. The lesion is genuinely endochondral: a cartilage template first, bone second.
3. **The crucial negative claim: the activity of the mutant receptor does not by itself explain the triggered, episodic, inflammatory nature of HO.** Something else, introduced by inflammation and injury, is required. The authors say so explicitly and call for research into what it is.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **De novo endochondral bone in an adult needs two inputs, not one.** A sufficient osteogenic signal (here, activin A through a hypersensitised receptor) is *necessary but not sufficient*. Inflammation/injury supplies the missing permission. This is the same two-input structure as [28645539](28645539.md) (BMP4 transgene plus a 5 mm skin cut) and [38672135](38672135.md) (signal plus failure of FAP clearance at day 5). Three independent lines, one conclusion.
   The consequence for a compound-only approach is direct and unwelcome: **no drug alone is expected to initiate a new endochondral unit in intact adult tissue.** The literature's own gain-of-function disease cannot do it reliably without a trigger. Any plausible scheme has to include a local, controlled injury — which is precisely what makes it a procedure rather than a prescription, and what makes the risk profile non-trivial.
2. **It also explains why nothing happens by accident.** A healthy adult carries competent progenitors in every muscle and tendon and is exposed to BMP ligands continuously, yet does not ossify, because the receptor is not hypersensitised and the inflammatory licence is normally withdrawn on schedule. The system has at least two independent locks. A scheme that opens both, at one site, on purpose, is conceivable; a drug that opens one of them everywhere is how you get FOP.
3. **The template comes first.** The progenitors "give rise to the cartilaginous anlagen." Even in the pathological case, bone is made by building cartilage and converting it. There is no shortcut that lays down bone directly in the direction you want. This is the structural reason the whole problem reduces to: make the right cartilage, in the right place, with the right polarity.
4. **Non-genetic HO works the same way.** Recombinant BMP ligands implanted in vivo are sufficient to drive HO even without the FOP mutation — i.e. the pathway is intact and drivable in a normal adult, given enough local ligand and an injury. That is the permissive half of the finding, and the reason this route is worth taking seriously at all.

## Levers
- **Activin A ↔ mutant ACVR1** as the obligate initiating pair in FOP. Note the sign problem: in **wild-type** tissue activin A is an ACVR1 *antagonist* ([37770652](37770652.md)), so this pair does not transfer to a normal adult.
- **Local recombinant BMP ligand + injury** as the generic, mutation-independent initiator of adult endochondral bone. The only route in this corpus that is demonstrably sufficient in a normal animal.
- **The inflammatory licence** — undefined here, and named by the authors as the open question. Whatever it is, it is the control variable that decides whether a signal produces bone or nothing.

## Honest limits
- A short review by the group with a commercial interest in activin A blockade.
- All of it concerns a gain-of-function receptor. The transfer to normal physiology is the paper's own stated gap.
- Mouse and patient-derived iPSC data; no quantitative outcome of its own.
- Nothing about length, polarity, plates, or growth. It establishes capability and its preconditions, nothing more.

## Chase
- Hatsell et al. 2015 (Sci Transl Med) — the humanised FOP mouse and activin A dependence.
- Hino et al. 2015 (PNAS) — patient iPSC confirmation.
- **Dey et al. 2016 — the tissue-resident mesenchymal progenitors in muscle and tendon that make the cartilaginous anlagen (Mx1+, Scx+).** The cell-identity primary.
- Refs on recombinant BMP implantation producing HO without the mutation (cited as 53, 54 in [38672135](38672135.md)).
