# 36719517 — Loss of lumbar disc height with age and its impact on pain and sensitivity associated behaviors in mice

**Citation.** Eur Spine J 2023;32(4):1248–1259. PMC9998347. Read in full (Introduction, Methods, all Results, Discussion).

## Model / method
Observational. **150 mice (77 female), mixed FVB × C57BL/6J, ages 3–23 months.** Sagittal radiographs under isoflurane; **disc height index (DHI)** measured for every lumbar disc L1–S1 as mean disc height / mean adjacent vertebral body height. DEXA (weight, BMD, muscle mass, fat mass) in 94 of them. Behavioural battery within two weeks of imaging: open field (rearing, distance), tail suspension (rearing, reaching, immobile, self-suspended), acetone cold allodynia, capsaicin thermal hyperalgesia. Mediation analysis.

## Exact findings
- **DHI falls significantly with age at every lumbar level (p < 0.0001 each).**
- **No sex difference** in the rate of narrowing.
- **Rate differs by level** (one-way ANOVA F(5,852) = 2.566, p = .0257), with **L6/S1 narrowing fastest** — significantly faster than L5/L6 (p = .0167), L4/L5 (p = .0447), L3/L4 (p = .0138) and L2/L3 (p = .0314).
- **The loss is independent of body composition.** No correlation of lumbosacral (L4–S1) DHI with weight, BMD, muscle mass, fat mass, percent lean or percent fat. (Scattered correlations at L1/2 and L2/3 only.)
- Behaviour: DHI correlated positively with rearing and mobility in open field, immobility in tail suspension, and thermal hyperalgesia; negatively with cold allodynia and tail-suspension rearing. The **lumbosacral disc significantly mediated the effect of age on open-field rearing**, but not cold allodynia.
- From the introduction, and the single most useful line in the paper for this project: **"non-surgical decompression procedures, which increase disc height, result in decreased pain ratings in elderly individuals with cLBP"** (their ref 17). In older adults, shorter disc height is associated with increased presence, recurrence risk and intensity of chronic low back pain.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **Disc height loss with age is intrinsic, not a loading or body-composition artefact.** Every level, both sexes, no correlation with weight or BMD. So the height an adult loses through the discs is a tissue property with its own clock, and therefore a candidate target in its own right.
2. **It is level-graded, which makes it a within-subject gradient.** L6/S1 fastest. The same shape of finding as vertebral ring apophysis maturation varying by level ([34362001](34362001.md)) — the axial skeleton is not one state but a series.
3. **The axial axis is a genuinely separate, mechanistically unrelated route to stature, and it stacks with the endplate route rather than competing with it.** Adults lose standing height to disc narrowing; disc height is at least partially restorable by non-surgical decompression in humans (their ref 17, unread). This is not longitudinal bone growth and it is not what [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) describes — it is fluid and matrix content in a hydrostatic chamber. But it is measured in millimetres per disc across 23 discs, and it is the one axis where an existing non-surgical intervention already produces a measurable height change in adults.
4. **The caution is equally clear.** Nothing here measures stature, and the paper's framing is pain. Disc height restoration is reversible, diurnally variable, and not growth — a taller disc is not a longer bone. Treating it as part of the same programme would conflate two mechanisms with different ceilings and different durability.

## Levers
- **Disc height as an independent axial contribution to stature**, with a demonstrated non-surgical intervention in humans (decompression) and an intrinsic age clock independent of body composition.
- **Level-dependence (L6/S1 fastest)** as a within-subject control.
- **DHI** as a cheap, longitudinal, radiographic endpoint — the method any axial experiment would use.

## Honest limits
- Mice, cross-sectional across ages (not longitudinal in individuals), mixed genetic background.
- **DHI is a ratio**, not an absolute height. No millimetre values anywhere, so no magnitude can be extracted for stature.
- The paper is about pain behaviour. Height, stature and growth are not measured or discussed.
- Mouse lumbar discs are notochordal-cell-rich and differ from human discs; the authors cite but do not resolve this.
- The decompression claim is secondary (their ref 17, unread).
