# PMID 1146509 — Hansson LI, Stenström A, Thorngren KG. Effect of venous stasis on longitudinal bone growth in the rabbit (1975, Acta Orthop Scand)

Read in full (PDF, 8 pp).

**Model.** 131 rabbits aged 35–40 days. Left femoral, circumflex and deep femoral veins ligated (Group I); plus the sciatic vein in Group II. Daily growth measured by oxytetracycline double labels (accurate method) at proximal tibia, distal tibiofibula and distal radius, up to 40 days.

**Results.**
- Venous pressure 8.5 → 24 cmH₂O immediately. It normalised in 2 days (Group I) or was mildly raised (~11) at 10 days (Group II), owing to rapid collaterals.
- **No difference in daily growth between ligated and control limbs** at any time point (e.g., proximal tibia 516 vs 515 µm/day pre-op). There was transient general growth retardation post-op.
- Earlier positive reports are attributed to measurement error, tourniquets compressing other structures, or operative trauma (active hyperaemia from trauma *does* stimulate growth; Hansson 1967).
- Venous ligation produces passive hyperaemia with slightly reduced intraosseous flow and variable intramedullary pressure.

**Relevance.**
- **Venous congestion / intramedullary pressure elevation does not stimulate physeal growth** in a careful study. That rules out a large class of "hydraulic" tricks (tourniquets, compression garments, venous occlusion) as growth levers, even with open plates.
- *Active* hyperaemia from trauma does stimulate growth (the regional acceleratory phenomenon, RAP). The relevant variable is arterial/angiogenic activation, not venous pressure.
- After fusion, none of this applies directly. But the lesson is that hydraulic pressure in marrow is not a growth signal; cartilage-level mechanics are.
