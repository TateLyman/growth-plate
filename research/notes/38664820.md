# 38664820 — Thyroid hormone induces ossification and terminal maturation in a preserved OA cartilage biomimetic

**Citation.** Arthritis Res Ther 2024;26:89. PMC11044551. Read in full.

## Model / method
**Human osteochondral explants** from macroscopically preserved condylar areas of knees removed at total knee replacement for OA. 20 donors; RNA-seq on 16 paired control/T3 samples from 8 donors (**mean age 69.9 y, range 59–76**, 6M/2F), RT-qPCR validation in an independent 22 samples from 8 donors (mean 63.3 y, range 50–81). Explants cultured 3–4 d, then **T3 10 ng/ml for 10 days** vs untreated paired control. Mean Mankin score 4.2 (SD 0.8), unchanged by T3.

## Exact numbers
- **247 FDR-significant differentially expressed genes** (182 up, 65 down) out of 10,060 passing threshold.
- Top up: **CCDC80 (FC 9.7, FDR 1.3e-43)**, **CLEC3A (FC 14.6, FDR 2.9e-29)**, **COL2A1 (FC 6.4, FDR 8.2e-8)**, **PHOSPHO1 (FC 6.4, FDR 2.0e-14)**, SPARC (3.7, 1.1e-15), HR (9.0), KLF9 (3.8), PAPPA (2.7/3.4), CDON (2.7), COL11A1 (2.2), AKT1 (2.1), LOXL3 (2.0), ANKH (1.9, 2.9e-4), SMO (1.9), RARG (1.7), ACAN (1.5), BGN (1.6), EPAS1 (1.59, 9.6e-3), KLF2 (2.6).
- Top down: **ANGPTL7 (FC −8.1, FDR 2.4e-25)**, VCAN (−4.8), **COL10A1 (FC −3.9, P 9.2e-4)**, TNFRSF11B/osteoprotegerin (−3.3), **COL1A1 (FC −3.0, P 2.9e-3)**, COL12A1 (0.35), TNC (0.31), RSPO2 (−1.8), ATOH8 (−1.8).
- Enriched pathways: **ECM organisation and extracellular structure organisation (both P_adj 7.7e-16, same 34 genes)**; **ossification GO:0001503 (22 genes, P_adj 8e-6)**, endochondral bone morphogenesis GO:0060350.
- **37 of the 247 are GWAS-identified OA risk genes** (COL12A1, TNC, SPARC, PAPPA, COMP, CHST3, CILP, COL11A1, SMO…).
- 10 genes change in the same direction in T3-treated cartilage, in early OA (vs healthy) and in ongoing OA (preserved vs lesioned): CCDC80, ANKH, CDON up; ATOH8 down.
- Many DE genes carry THRA/THRB/RXRA response elements; KLF9 does not, implying non-genomic T3 signalling as well.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
**This is the closest thing in the literature to a compound, applied to adult human cartilage, that pushes it back toward a growth-plate program.** The authors' own words: T3 "causes chondrocytes to leave their maturationally arrested state, become metabolically active, and increase production of matrix proteins, possibly implicating a loosening of epigenetic control that sets them on the path towards recuperation of a growth plate-like morphology."

1. **Adult human articular chondrocytes are not locked.** Ten days of one hormone at 10 ng/ml, in explants from 60–76-year-olds, moved 247 genes, with ECM organisation and ossification as the two dominant pathways. The "maturational arrest" of adult articular cartilage is a maintained state, not an irreversible one.
2. **The reactivation is real but it is late-stage, not early-stage.** COL10A1 went **down** (−3.9) and COL1A1 down (−3.0), while EPAS1/HIF-2α, ANKH, PHOSPHO1, CLEC3A went up. T3 did not recreate a proliferative column and a COL X+ hypertrophic zone; it pushed cells straight toward mineralisation. For lengthening you need the *whole* sequence — resting → proliferative → hypertrophic → ossified — and this is the last third only. On its own, T3 on adult articular cartilage would calcify it, not lengthen the bone.
3. **The decisive and uncomfortable finding: the program required for post-fusion longitudinal growth is molecularly the same program as osteoarthritis.** 37 of the 247 T3-responsive genes are OA risk genes. CCDC80, ANKH and CDON move in the same direction in T3 explants, in early OA and in progressive OA. The authors' entire framing is that this reactivation is the *disease*. Independently: DIO2 risk variants (the enzyme that makes local T3) raise OA risk; hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism and thyroid-hormone sensitivity all associate with OA.
   This is the single strongest reason to doubt any compound-only approach to adult height. "Make adult cartilage behave like growth cartilage again" and "give the person osteoarthritis" are, at the level of transcription, the same instruction. Any scheme that claims otherwise has to specify what makes its version of the reactivation ordered and self-limiting, and nothing in this literature does.
4. **The anabolic half is reassuring and the architecture problem remains.** COL2A1 up 6.4-fold and ACAN up — the cells make more real cartilage matrix, not just degrade. So the raw synthetic capacity of aged human chondrocytes is intact. What is missing is polarity: a direction in which to put the new tissue, and a conversion front at one end. Exactly the gap that [40118845](40118845.md) fills architecturally.
5. **Negative controls worth keeping:** no macroscopic change in 10 days (Mankin unchanged), and TNC and COL12A1 — both repair-associated — went *down*, which the authors read as T3 impairing repair.

## Levers
- **T3 (triiodothyronine), 10 ng/ml locally, 10 days** — a cheap, licensed, well-characterised molecule that demonstrably moves adult human articular cartilage toward terminal maturation and mineralisation. Converges with [37126720](37126720.md) (T3 as the terminal-hypertrophy switch in hPSC chondronoids) and the mouse secondary-ossification-centre literature.
- **DIO2** (deiodinase 2) as the local switch that sets intracellular T3 availability in cartilage — a tissue-local lever rather than a systemic one, and an OA risk gene.
- **PHOSPHO1 and ANKH** as the mineralisation effectors (Pi handling); **EPAS1/HIF-2α** as the driver of MMP13 and VEGFA, i.e. of matrix breakdown and vascular invasion — the two things a conversion front needs.
- **CCDC80** as a quantitative read-out of hypertrophic reactivation in human cartilage. Useful as an endpoint for any experiment of this kind.
- **RSPO2** down: R-spondin-2 drives proliferating→hypertrophic transition via Wnt/β-catenin. T3 went around that route rather than through it.

## Honest limits
- Ex vivo explants, 10 days, gene expression only. **No ossification was actually observed** — the title means ossification *pathways*. Mankin score unchanged.
- Cartilage from 60–76-year-old osteoarthritic knees. Not young adult cartilage, and already diseased.
- No histology of mineral, no measurement of thickness, no mechanical testing, no measurement of anything resembling length.
- Thyroid receptor localisation not validated (the authors say so).
- n = 8 donors for RNA-seq; two validations (PAPPA, COMP) failed to reach significance.
- Systemic T3 in a euthyroid adult has never produced height, and the dose/exposure here is a local bath, not a plasma level.
