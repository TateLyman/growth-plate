# PMID 39563452 — Stem cells from the apical papilla (SCAPs) combined with mandibular advancement in "adult" rats (2024)

Read in full (PMC).

**Model.** Male Sprague-Dawley rats, **8 weeks old** (called "adult" by the authors, but still growing: rat long-bone and condylar growth continues well past 8 weeks).
- Mandibular advancement by incisor grinding: 3 mm at first, then 1 mm more every 3 days (a *stepwise, progressive* protocol).
- Groups: control, advancement alone, advancement + SCAP injection (2×10^5 cells/side into the TMJ region) every 1 or 2 weeks, over 4 and 8 weeks.

**Results.**
- 8 weeks of advancement significantly increased mandibular body length (Go–Id) and ramus height (Co–Go) vs control (p<0.001).
- 4 weeks of advancement increased only ramus height (p<0.05).
- Advancement + SCAPs gave the thickest condylar cartilage and the highest SOX9 and COL2A1 expression. Advancement alone was intermediate.
- Exact millimetre values are only in figures.

**Critical interpretation.**
- 8-week rats are adolescents in skeletal terms, so this is augmentation of active growth, not reactivation after cessation.
- The stepwise re-advancement (renewing the strain every 3 days) is consistent with Xiong 2005: a single change in strain gives a transient ~3-week response. Repeatedly renewing the strain sustains output, and the gain appears with duration (4 → 8 weeks).
- The cell supplement adds to the response. This suggests that the *progenitor pool size* limits the mechanically triggered output.

**Levers.**
- **Ratcheting strain**: a progressive, repeatedly renewed displacement beats a fixed one.
- **Progenitor supply** is a second limiting factor beyond the mechanical trigger. In a non-surgical scheme the supply would have to come from local recruitment (periosteum, perivascular cells, articular superficial zone), not from injected cells.
