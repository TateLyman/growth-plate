# PMID 27690057 — A mechatronic loading device to stimulate bone growth via a human knee (2016, Sensors)

Read in full (PMC, ~23k chars). An engineering paper.

**Content.**
- Human lateral knee-loading device: brushless DC motor, worm gear, modified slider–crank driving a spring-loaded pad (k = 3333 N/m).
- Up to 40 N lateral force (10/20/30/40 N settings via crank radius), 1–5 Hz, max pad travel 12 mm, Arduino control.
- Tested on a mannequin knee only. **No human or biological data.**
- Explicitly derived from the Yokota mouse joint-loading work.

**Relevance.**
- Feasibility: a cheap, portable device can deliver controlled cyclic lateral compression to a human knee epiphysis. A human-scale delivery platform for epiphyseal mechanical stimulation already exists to repurpose.
- Force scaling: 0.5 N in a 20 g mouse vs tens of N in humans. The dose equivalence is unknown.
- In the theory, this is the candidate **hardware chassis** (repurposing allowed) for any periodic epiphyseal or articular stimulus.
