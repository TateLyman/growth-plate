# PMID 39463341 — Shim KS. Growth plate closure and therapeutic interventions (2024, Clin Exp Pediatr) — review

Read in full (PMC, ~16k chars).

**Content.**
- Three growth controllers: resting-zone progenitor differentiation potential, proliferative capacity, and timely hypertrophy/calcification/replacement.
- Senescence: progenitors deplete, proliferative cell number falls, and hypertrophic cell number *and size* fall.
- Closure follows: hypertrophic cells replaced, matrix calcified and vascularized.
- Senescence models: cell-counting vs a biological timer. Transplanted growth plates grow at donor-age rates (finite capacity). Telomerase loss has little effect; DNA methylation changes may limit replication.
- All existing interventions (GnRHa, aromatase inhibitors, CNP analogs, FGFR3 inhibitors) only *delay closure* in children. GnRHa adds 2–10 cm in precocious puberty and 1.7–6.7 cm in idiopathic short stature. None act after closure.
- CNP inhibits MAPK/FGF signalling and stimulates late hypertrophy.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Confirms that no pharmacological intervention acts after closure.
- The determinants named are progenitor supply and hypertrophic cell size and number. Any adult mechanism must create both a progenitor supply and a hypertrophy-competent zone.
- Hypertrophic cell size is itself a lengthening lever (see Hunziker / Cooper 2013).

**Levers.** Hypertrophy magnitude via CNP, IGF-1, local signals. This is a mechanical target too, since chondrocyte swelling is osmotic/mechanical (Cooper 2013).
