# PMID 26945615 — Ochiai T(?) / Koyama E et al. Osteophyte formation and matrix mineralization in a TMJ osteoarthritis mouse model are associated with ectopic hedgehog signaling (2016, Matrix Biol)

Read in full (PMC, ~32k chars).

**Model.** Prg4 (lubricin)-null mice; temporomandibular joint (TMJ) followed from P0 to 12 months (n up to 12 per age). Glenoid-fossa primary cells from 2-month-old wild-type mice treated with rhSHH (100–250 ng/mL) or HhAntag (2–5 µM).

**Findings.**
- **Lack of boundary lubrication** (higher friction/shear at the articular surface) causes **excess chondrogenesis in the presumptive articular layer**, then widespread hypertrophy and endochondral ossification.
  - Chondroid bone forms and the glenoid-fossa roof thickens progressively: 3 → 6 → 12 months.
  - Osteophytes grow into the joint cavity. Condyles become *larger* (antero-posterior and medio-lateral). Discs develop ectopic cartilage.
- The articular surface loses its Prg4+ superficial cells and instead carries **Sox9+ chondroprogenitors**.
- Peripheral osteophyte chondrocytes express Ihh, Gli1, Col II, Col X, then Col I/Bsp/Osx. Central and basal cells undergo senescence/apoptosis (TUNEL). **Growth is driven at the free peripheral surface.**
- In vitro, SHH increases ALP, Col II and Col X; HhAntag blocks maturation and mineralisation.
- The authors cite: "**secondary cartilage is induced by mechanical stimuli such as those occurring in articulations, sutures and muscle attachment sites** (Beresford 1993; Solem 2011)". Also: "Ihh and BMP2 are essential components of mechanotransduction mechanisms eliciting ectopic chondrogenesis" (Wu 2001).

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- In *adult* animals (to 12 months), altering the mechanical boundary condition at an articular surface (friction/shear) yields a **sustained, progressive endochondral growth front** that thickens bone *perpendicular to the articular surface*. Geometrically that is the same axis as longitudinal growth for a long-bone end.
- It is pathological (disorganised, OA-like), but it proves the concept: articular-surface progenitors in adults can be mechanically switched into a chondrogenic, endochondrally ossifying state for months.

**Levers.**
- **Surface shear/friction** activates Hh → chondroprogenitor expansion → endochondral ossification at an adult articular surface.
- Controlled, cyclic, *non-destructive* modulation of articular surface shear (rather than permanent lubrication loss) could be a candidate trigger.
- The challenge is keeping it organised (columnar, polarised) and non-degenerative.

**Chase.** Beresford 1993 (secondary cartilage); Solem 2011 (mechanical induction of secondary cartilage); Wu Q 2001 (Ihh mechanotransduction, JBC); Bechtold 2016.
