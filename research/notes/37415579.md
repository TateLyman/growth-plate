# PMID 37415579 — Delayed epiphyseal closure in an adult with panhypopituitarism detected by 99mTc-MDP SPECT/CT (2023, Clin Case Rep)

Read in full (PMC, ~7.7k chars).

**Case.** 23-year-old man, two craniopharyngioma resections, Tanner I.
- Labs: IGF-1 <2.5 ng/mL; LH <0.1; testosterone 1.7 ng/mL; low T3/T4.
- Bone scan: intense symmetric uptake at multiple epiphyseal lines (humeral heads, wrists, knees, ankles).
- CT: knee epiphyses unclosed, physeal lines thickened and denser.

**Caveat.**
- The authors claim "long bones keep growing because GH normal", but IGF-1 was undetectable and **no growth was measured**.
- Uptake shows osteoblastic/mineralizing activity at open physes, not proven elongation.

**What it establishes.**
- Sex-steroid deficiency keeps physes open into the 20s.
- Open, metabolically active physes in adults are detectable by MDP SPECT/CT.
- It is not post-fusion growth.

**Levers.** None mechanical. It does provide a biomarker: MDP SUV at a cartilage–bone junction correlates with growth rate (ref 15). That would be a non-invasive read-out for any experimental adult growth zone.
