# PMID 18048437 — Zhang P, Malacinski GM, Yokota H. Joint loading modality: its application to bone formation and fracture healing (2008, Br J Sports Med)

Read in full (NIH manuscript PDF, 10 pp; review).

**Content.**
- Summarises elbow, knee and ankle loading: 0.5 N, 2–15 Hz, ~1000–2000 cycles/day for 3 days, ~10 µε at the bone formation site. Effects reach along the whole bone length; periosteum > endosteum.
- Knee loading accelerated closure of surgical tibial wounds.
- **Bone lengthening section:**
  - Leg lengthening during spaceflight unloading is documented.
  - Lateral joint loading "stretches long bones longitudinally".
  - **Preliminary data suggest knee and elbow loading lengthen tibia and femur, and ulna and humerus, respectively, in mice.** (Published separately: Zhang 2008 knee-loading lengthening, PMID 19098525 / 19442616 are in the corpus.)
  - Axial ulnar loading in rats suppressed longitudinal growth dose-dependently (Ohashi 2002). Stokes 2002: sustained loading modulates chondrocyte enlargement.
- Mechanism hypothesis: a steep strain gradient along the bone plus oscillatory IMP drives lacunocanalicular flow.

**Relevance.**
- The joint-loading group claims a lengthening effect, but in *growing* mice with open plates; see the primary papers.
- Key principle: a lateral squeeze of the epiphysis produces a Poisson-type longitudinal extension and hydraulic flow in the growth-plate region. That is a non-axial, non-compressive way to stimulate the physis.
- For a fused bone, it is unknown whether such loading can act on the residual physeal scar (bone), the articular cartilage, or the periosteum to produce any length.
