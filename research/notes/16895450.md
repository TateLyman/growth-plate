# PMID 16895450 — Anatomic demarcation by positional variation in fibroblast gene expression programs

Rinn JL, Bondre C, Gladstone HB, Brown PO, Chang HY. *PLoS Genet* 2006;2(7):e119. PMC1523235. doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.0020119

**What I read:** the complete PMC full text — abstract, Introduction, all six Results sections, the full Discussion, with all figure legends read in place. Not an abstract read.

## Model
**47 primary fibroblast populations from 43 anatomic sites across the human body**, from 20 autopsy or surgical donors. Vimentin+ only, negative for cytokeratin, desmin, PECAM, GFAP and CD11b. Every culture propagated **at least five passages under identical conditions to strip away the native environment**, then profiled on cDNA microarrays (41,121 elements, 24,421 genes; >1 million measurements). 7,580 genes passed filtering.

## Exact findings
- **Anatomic origin dominates donor identity.** Where multiple sites came from one donor, cells grouped with the *same site from other people* rather than with their own donor. 35/47 samples clustered with anatomic neighbours on unsupervised clustering of all 7,580 genes.
- **Three binary axes, and only three:**
  - **Anterior–posterior, with the boundary at the umbilicus.** 20/20 samples from above and 15/27 from below segregated (p < 0.001); **1,172 genes** at 1% FDR. Samples nearer the umbilicus had intermediate profiles.
  - **Proximal–distal in both limbs.** Upper limb: 7/7 distal to the wrist in one cluster, 4/6 proximal in the other (p < 0.01). Lower limb: 3/3 distal to the ankle vs 3/3 proximal (p < 0.025). Combined p < 0.001. The boundary **corresponds to the embryonic autopod**.
  - **Dermal vs non-dermal**: 396 genes at 0.1% FDR.
- **The code is general, not local.** **98 "distal" genes** shared between hand and foot — spatially distant tissues with no plausible shared local interactions — and the same signature marks **foreskin** (developmentally a distal structure). An "anterior centroid" built from head/chest/trunk correctly separated all arm from all leg fibroblasts. Held-out prediction of positional origin: **80% accurate (p = 0.0046)**.
- **337 positional-identifier genes clustered 47/47 samples into clusters composed exclusively of anatomic neighbours.** None of 100 random 337-gene sets came close (p = 0.00017; **7 standard deviations** from the random median of 35). Deleting the 337 and re-selecting dropped performance back to 35/47. The 337 also correctly grouped **10/10 samples from an independent panel** of four sites, three of which (toe, gum, buttock) were absent from training.
- **Contents of the 337** — three groups matter here:
  - Positional transcription factors: **HOXA5, HOXA11, HOXA13**, HOXD8, TBX2, TBX15, EMX2, FOXF1, FOXP1, TRPS1.
  - Developmental signalling: WNT1, WNT5b, WISP2, **DKK2, GPC6 (glypican-6), BMP4, GREM2**, SMAD3, PTCH, GULP1.
  - Matrix and matrix-modifying: COL4A2/4A5/8A2, **COL10A1**, COL11A1, FN1, EMILIN2, and **HS3ST3B1** — a heparan sulfate 3-O-sulfotransferase. Plus ADAM9, ADAMTS5, MMP3, PLAT, PLAU.
- **The HOX code is retained, at protein level, in adult cells:**
  - **HOXA13 mRNA and protein** present in **foot fibroblasts and foreskin, absent from thigh fibroblasts** (immunoblot). Immunofluorescence: ~**one third of dermal fibroblasts in human foreskin express nuclear HoxA13 in vivo.** Not keratinocytes (immunoblot control).
  - HOXB2–B9 confined to trunk and non-dermal; HoxB4 protein in fetal lung fibroblasts, absent in foreskin. HOXD4/D8 in trunk and proximal leg only.
  - Homeodomain genes alone grouped 43/47 correctly; removing canonical HOX genes dropped it to the 35/47 baseline.
- Essentially nothing along the **dorsal–ventral** axis: one gene (Protocadherin 18), and TWIST1, ENGRAILED, SNAIL, BMP4, RHBDL2/F1 showed no D–V variation.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **Adult human connective tissue cells carry a proximal–distal positional record, and it is cell-autonomous and stable.** Five-plus passages in identical medium did not erase it; anatomic origin beat donor identity. This is the mammalian substrate that the entire §18 route needs, and it exists in adults. [27499874](notes/27499874.md) showed the positional code in *matrix* dies by mouse PN9 — but the record in *cells* persists in adult humans. §18.6's "lock 2" is therefore better described as a **reading and writing problem than an erasure**: the coordinates are still there, the tissue has stopped acting on them.
2. **HOXA13 — the distal determinant — is present as protein in adult human distal fibroblasts and absent proximally.** In the axolotl, talarozole erased the HOXA13 reporter, and *Shox* expression is mutually exclusive with Hoxa13+ cells ([40494878](notes/40494878.md)). The exact molecular boundary that the CYP26-inhibition experiment moves is demonstrably present and position-specific in adult humans. That is the single most encouraging translational fact in the project.
3. **An HS sulfotransferase is one of the 337 positional-identifier genes.** HS3ST3B1 varies with position in adult human fibroblasts; HS3ST3B1 appears in the axolotl sulfotransferase table of [27499874](notes/27499874.md), where HS sulfation *is* the positional code. GPC6, a heparan sulfate proteoglycan, is also in the 337. Two unrelated studies, one amphibian and one adult human, converge on heparan-sulfate modification as positional machinery.
4. **COL10A1 is a position-varying gene in adult human fibroblasts.** COL10A1 is the hypertrophic-chondrocyte collagen — the marker of the terminal growth programme this project is trying to re-open (§3, §5). Its baseline expression in fibroblasts is set by anatomic position. I do not know what to make of this yet, but it is not noise: it survived a 7-SD gene-selection filter.
5. **Maintenance is chromatin-based.** The authors cite Bernstein et al.: HOX loci are differentially chromatin-modified in adult fibroblasts in a site-specific manner. **This is the same machinery as §3b** — where PRC2/H3K27me3 represses *IGFBP3/5* in the hypertrophic zone ([27897169](notes/27897169.md), [37425751](notes/37425751.md)). One chromatin system appears to hold the positional lock and the IGF-gate lock. **This is a tension, not a win:** PRC2 loss *derepresses* IGFBP3/5 and *shrinks* hypertrophic cells (the sign error I corrected in COMPOUNDS.md), so a PRC2 inhibitor would be correctly signed for releasing positional identity and wrongly signed for the volume gate. Any epigenetic approach has to be told apart at these two loci, not applied globally.

## The hard limit this paper imposes
**The adult human proximodistal code, as measured, is BINARY — autopod vs everything else.** Explicit multiclass search found **no third signature distinguishing stylopod from zeugopod**, in either arm or leg, and **"we also did not find genes that were expressed in fibroblasts according to a gradient along the limbs."**

That matters because the length-generating rule is **intercalation across a positional gap** ([35241664](notes/35241664.md)), and intercalation needs intervening values to generate. A graded axolotl limb has a continuum; an adult human limb, on this evidence, has one boundary. **This is the most serious constraint on §18 and it comes from the best mammalian data in the project.**

The authors offer three readings and do not choose: (i) **underpowered** — "subtle, systematic gradations in expression would require larger number of samples to detect, our results do not rule out the possibility there may be finer segmentation or a gradient"; (ii) **wrong cell type** — the finer code may sit in other cells or require heterotypic interaction; (iii) **genuinely sparse in adults** — "while an elaborate coordinate system is employed during embryogenesis, only a sparse coordinate system is maintained in adulthood." Reading (iii) would be close to fatal for the §18 route in adult humans. Reading (i) is plausible on power grounds alone: the proximodistal comparisons rest on **6–7 samples per limb**.

## Levers
- **The 337-gene positional signature and the HOX code** are a readout: any candidate goal-changer compound can be scored by whether it moves adult human fibroblasts along these axes, in culture, cheaply. This is an assay, not a treatment, and it is the cheapest one in the project.
- **HOXA13 suppression** in a proximally-located target population — the axolotl-validated direction, with a demonstrated adult human substrate.
- Chromatin at HOX loci — the maintenance mechanism, with the sign caveat above.

## Anti-levers
- None directly. The paper is descriptive.

## Honest limits
1. **Dermal (and visceral) fibroblasts in culture.** Not cartilage, not perichondrium, not periosteum, not vertebral endplate. Whether the cells this project actually cares about carry the same code is untested — and is now a named experiment (ACTIVATION.md §14.10).
2. **Binary, not graded** — see above. The single biggest constraint, and acknowledged by the authors as possibly a power artefact.
3. **2006 cDNA microarrays**, 70%-measured filtering, and negative results ("no gradient") carry whatever power that platform had. A modern scRNA-seq or ATAC repeat across a limb would settle it and does not appear to have been done.
4. **Purely correlative.** No functional manipulation of any HOX gene; the authors say lineage-specific genetic studies "will be needed."
5. Positional identity here is about *differentiation state and signalling output*, not about growth. Nothing in this paper connects the positional code to size, length or any skeletal outcome.
6. Culture itself selects; five passages removes environment but may also drift.
