# PMID 31056740 — Pattappa G, Zellner J, Johnstone B, Docheva D, Angele P. Cells under pressure: hydrostatic pressure and MSC chondrogenesis (2019, Eur Cell Mater)

Read in full (PDF, 22 pp; review).

**Key content.**
- In articular cartilage, ~90% of load is initially carried by interstitial fluid pressurisation (Soltz & Ateshian). Peak hydrostatic pressure (HP) is up to ~12 MPa. HP is highest just below the superficial layer.
- **HP ranging from 0.1 to 10 MPa (cyclic ~0.5–1 Hz, 1–4 h/day) enhances MSC chondrogenesis** (Sox9, Col2a1, Acan, GAG, Col II).
  - Miyanishi 2006: 10 MPa, 1 Hz, 4 h/day for 14 days induced Sox9/Col2/Acan *without TGF-β*. Other studies needed TGF-β pre-conditioning or failed without it, so whether HP alone suffices is unresolved.
  - Low magnitudes (0.4 MPa) raise Col2a1; high (5–10 MPa) raise matrix deposition.
- Multiaxial loading (compression + shear + HP) **activates latent TGF-β** (shear of synovial fluid activates latent TGF-β1; Albro 2012) and induces chondrogenesis without exogenous TGF-β (Schätti 2011; Gardner 2016).
- Hypertrophy: conflicting. HP tends to suppress calcification and Col X in several studies (Carroll 2013/2014; Saha 2017; Zhao 2016) but raises it in others. **Physioxia (2–5% O₂)** prevents hypertrophy; physioxia + HP is proposed for stable cartilage.
- Computational (Giorgi 2014; Carter & Wong): **cyclic HP promotes cartilage, static pressure promotes bone** in joint morphogenesis.
- Mechanotransduction:
  - Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels; purinergic ATP release via connexin hemichannels → P2X/P2Y → SERCA Ca²⁺ stores.
  - Rac1 (+N-cadherin condensation) promotes; RhoA inhibits.
  - ERα → JNK (oestradiol synergises; tamoxifen blocks); vimentin; integrins (α10β1 candidate).
  - TRPV4 (osmotic channel; Sox9 via Ca²⁺/calmodulin; needs a primary cilium); PI3K/Akt/FOXO (anti-hypertrophy).
- Stiffer matrices give a larger HP response; soft unloaded gels favour chondrogenesis. A pericellular matrix is needed to transduce load.

**Relevance to the theory.**
- Intermittent hydrostatic pressure is the archetypal *pro-cartilage* mechanical signal (Carter's law). **Cyclic HP keeps tissue cartilaginous; static load or shear-dominant conditions push it to bone.**
- For an engineered adult growth front, there are two phases:
  1. Progenitor zone: cyclic HP plus some shear → chondrogenesis (Sox9).
  2. Deep zone: lower HP, higher octahedral shear or static load, plus vascular contact → hypertrophy and ossification.

  A *depth-graded mechanical field* (high cyclic HP superficially, shear and relief deeper) mimics a growth plate's zonation.
- Joints naturally generate MPa-range HP at the cartilage surface. The bone-end articular cartilage is therefore already a high-HP environment, which is why it stays cartilage and does not ossify. **The tidemark is where HP falls off and shear dominates**: the natural "ossification front" of adult articular cartilage.
- TRPV4 (an osmotic channel) links to osmotic loading: hypo-osmotic stress through TRPV4 raises chondrogenic matrix. Osmotic manipulation is another lever.
