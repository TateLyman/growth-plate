# PMID 40478277 — Mineral content and ECM in mouse growth plates during epiphyseal fusion (2025, Calcif Tissue Int)

Read in full (PMC, ~30k chars).

**Model.** Male C57BL/6 mice at 10 vs 55 weeks (W10, W55). Methods: microCT (n=3), SEM/EDS (n=6), H&E, Alcian blue and IHC (n=3).

**Results.**
- At W55 the plate is partially "fused": microCT shows bridges between epiphysis and metaphysis.
- SEM/EDS shows the bridged/bright region is *calcified cartilage, not bone*. It has high P and Ca, is Alcian-blue positive, has no type I collagen increase, and contains no bone tissue on H&E.
- The W55 plate is thinner and disordered, with no distinguishable zones and low cell density.
- Its chondrocytes are odd cells: not flat, not hypertrophic, small nuclei, bright cytoplasm.
- Aggrecan core protein and Col II are sparse at W55 but the GAGs remain.
- Col X and MMP-13 are present at W10 and absent at W55.

**Background cited.**
- Rabbit growth-plate transplant: growth rate follows **donor** age, not recipient age, so senescence is intrinsic to the plate.
- Senescence is a function of cumulative divisions, not time (Nilsson/Baron).
- Resting-zone stem-like cells have finite capacity.
- ER-mutant man reached 204 cm with unfused plates.
- Emons: in human fusion, hypertrophic chondrocytes show hypoxia/necrosis signs, not apoptosis or autophagy.
- Fukuda & Matsuoka: rodents fuse incompletely, unlike humans, monkeys and dogs.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- In mice, "closed-looking" plates on CT are calcified cartilage with living (if odd) chondrocytes, not bone.
- The authors speculate humans may pass through a calcified-cartilage stage too, but that is unproven.
- For adult humans, CT/X-ray "fusion" cannot distinguish calcified cartilage from bone.

**Levers.**
- Mineralization of the cartilage matrix, plus loss of aggrecan core and Col II, is the terminal state that freezes growth.
- A key question for any mechanism: does a calcified-cartilage remnant (epiphyseal scar) in adult humans contain any viable chondrocytes? Human data are needed (Emons 2009; epiphyseal-scar papers).
- Note: the growth-rate-follows-donor-age finding argues against systemic environment as the limiter. The limiter is intrinsic cell state, so any adult mechanism must bring a *new* cell population (progenitors), not just signals.
