# PMID 38058151 — Tsutsumi-Arai C, ... Ono W, Ono N. A PTHrP gradient drives mandibular condylar chondrogenesis via Runx2 (2024, J Dent Res)

Read in full (PMC, ~34k chars).

**Model.** Pthrp-mCherry knock-in reporter (a null allele). Heterozygotes are reporters; homozygotes are PTHrP knockouts (KO). Examined at E15.5, E18.5 and P3. Embryonic and neonatal only; nothing in adults.

**Findings.**
- Mandibular condylar cartilage (MCC) has 4 layers: superficial → polymorphic → flattened chondrocyte → hypertrophic.
- **PTHrP is made only by the superficial layer** (2–3 cell layers on the surface, contiguous with the periosteum). It diffuses as an organised gradient into the polymorphic and flattened layers, where PTH1R+ RUNX2+ cells sit.
- **The polymorphic layer (RUNX2+ SOX9−, highly proliferative) is the chondroprogenitor compartment.** It develops from, and stays contiguous with, the periosteum of the ramus.
- In the PTHrP-KO:
  - The condylar process is shorter and narrower, with less cartilage. The gonial process (no PTHrP+ surface) is unaffected.
  - Polymorphic-layer proliferation falls.
  - Premature hypertrophy appears at E15.5 (Col10a1 and Ihh domains expand), then **exhaustion** at E18.5 (SOX9, Col10a1 and Ihh domains shrink).
  - Runx2 (not Sp7) falls in the polymorphic layer.
- Unlike the long-bone growth plate, MCC chondrocytes don't form columns. Nearly all SOX9+ flattened chondrocytes co-express RUNX2, and PTHrP and Runx2 are both prochondrogenic here.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- A growth cartilage can be sustained as a **surface-fed system**: a thin PTHrP-secreting surface layer (the source) over a proliferative progenitor layer, then the chondrocyte and hypertrophic layers, then bone.
- This is an *appositional, periosteum-derived growth cartilage architecture*. It differs from the columnar, internally fed growth plate and does not need a resting zone trapped between two bones.
- The architecture is built from **periosteum + a surface PTHrP source**. The PTHrP gradient prevents progenitor exhaustion, so the gradient is what sustains the front.
- Geometric point: in the condyle, the progenitor layer is at the *free articular surface*, and new endochondral bone is added beneath it, pushing the articular surface outward. That is *growth at the bone end without a growth plate*. A limb long bone's end is also covered by articular cartilage, so the topology is analogous.

**Levers.**
- To create an adult "condyle-like" growth front at a limb bone end, the requirements are:
  1. a periosteum-contiguous polymorphic progenitor layer;
  2. a surface PTHrP source to set a gradient;
  3. a mechanical regime that keeps it chondrogenic (intermittent hydrostatic compression with superficial shear, per Prg4-null and advancement data).
- Mechanically, the limb's equivalents are the **perichondrial/periosteal ring at the articular margin** (groove of Ranvier remnant), which is contiguous with articular cartilage.

**Chase.** Bechtold 2019 (MCC as periosteal secondary cartilage); Shibata 2013, Kurio 2018 (polymorphic layer contiguous with periosteum); Robinson 2015; Liao 2019 (Runx2 required postnatally).
