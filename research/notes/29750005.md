# PMID 29750005 — Persistence of vertebral growth plate cartilage in aged cynomolgus monkeys (2018, J Toxicol Pathol)

Read in full (PMC, ~11k chars).

**Model.** 11 normal cynomolgus monkeys (3 male, 8 female), aged ~9–15 years. Lumbar L1–L7 end plates, 66 specimens; H&E and Alcian blue.

**Results.**
- Unclosed vertebral growth-plate cartilage was present in all 66 specimens.
- Proliferative and hypertrophic zones were visible.
- Vessels penetrated hypertrophic cartilage, with osteoid at the chondro-osseous junction, i.e. active endochondral ossification histology.
- The oldest animal (female, 14 y 10 m) had a thin, discontinuous plate, no proliferative zone, chondrocyte *clusters*, and a partial bony end-plate-like structure (early closure).
- Context: monkey tibia closes at ~5 y, human tibia at ~16 y; the human spine closes at 18–25 y.
- In humans, closure means the cartilage border with marrow is "sealed off by bone" to form a bony end plate.

**Key caveat (authors).** Trunk length in cynomolgus monkeys stops increasing at ~9 y. The persistent cartilage, despite looking like active endochondral ossification, "may not contribute to longitudinal growth".

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- It shows morphologically "active-looking" growth cartilage that is functionally non-growing. This dissociation matches the rat data (Roach 2003).
- Hence open cartilage ≠ growth. Growth requires something beyond a cartilage layer: a proliferating progenitor supply and hypertrophic expansion.
- The authors attribute earlier human vertebral closure to habitual erect bipedal loading accelerating maturation.

**Levers / theory relevance.**
1. Mechanics accelerates maturation/closure (bipedal loading hypothesis); a corollary is that unloading regimes delay it.
2. Endochondral ossification at a junction can continue (vascular invasion, osteoid) without measurable net elongation. Likely the ossification front advances at the same rate the cartilage is consumed, with no new cartilage made, so the layer thins. Net length gain = cartilage production rate, not ossification.
3. The human sealing step, a bony end plate cutting cartilage off from marrow, again shows a diffusion/vascular barrier as the terminal event.

**Chase.** Ref 7 (Japanese macaque vertebral union to age 23); ref 15 (cynomolgus trunk morphometry 0–9 y); ref 19 (rat-tail passive motion disorganizing growth cartilage).
