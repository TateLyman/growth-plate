# 23821404 — Heparan sulfate in skeletal development, growth, and pathology: the case of hereditary multiple exostoses

**Citation.** Dev Dyn 2013;242(9):1021–1032. PMC4007065. Review by the Pacifici group. Read in full (all eight sections).

## Why it is here
**This is the complete account of how a new, properly zoned growth plate forms de novo in a living mammal, what sets its growth axis, and the fact that it keeps running in an adult whose own plates have closed.** It is the natural-experiment counterpart to the manufactured two-domain construct in [40118845](40118845.md).

## The sentence that matters most
> **"Although no new exostoses form after puberty when the growth plates close, existing exostoses can continue to grow and cause further pain and complications, leading to a surgical rate of 67% in adults."**

An exostosis is "a growth plate-like cartilaginous cap overlaying a bony base," with — per [24875294](24875294.md) — the normal architecture of a cartilage cap and a narrow COLX zone at its base. **So in humans: an organised ectopic growth plate continues to produce bone in an adult after the native physes have fused. What the adult cannot do is initiate a new one.** That is the same initiation-versus-propagation split that garetosmab showed pharmacologically (new heterotopic lesions 40.9% → 0%, existing ones untouched, [37770652](37770652.md)), and it is the strongest human evidence I have found for the central claim of [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md): **the boundary is the hard part; once it exists it runs.**

## The axis
> **"The exostoses exhibit an intriguing growth plate-like organization in which their main axis of elongation is at about 90° angle compared to that of the adjacent native growth plate."**

They form "next to, but never within, the growth plates and protrude into perichondrium." Confirmed independently in the SHP2 model, where COLX+ columns in lateral outgrowths ran perpendicular to the native axis ([24875294](24875294.md)), and in zebrafish *dak/ext2* transplants, where mutant cells "occasionally grow out from the edge of developing cartilage, oriented perpendicular to the WT cells."

**Why 90°: because HS deficiency lets Ihh and BMP leak sideways into the perichondrium, so the new morphogen gradient runs radially outward, and the new plate aligns to it.** The axis is set by the direction of the signal, not inherited from the limb. This is stated mechanistically below and it is the key to the polarity problem.

## The mechanism, step by step
**HS restricts morphogen range; the perichondrium is an actively maintained anti-chondrogenic border.**
- Growth-plate chondrocytes express syndecan-3, glypican-5 and perlecan. HS chains set the distribution and range of Ihh, BMP, FGF and Wnt.
- *Ext1*-null: embryonic lethal E8.5. *Ext1*^Gt/Gt hypomorph: shortened elements, fused vertebrae, **increased distribution and signalling of Ihh within the growth plate and delayed hypertrophic differentiation**. *Ndst1* loss: severe changes in hedgehog distribution and plate function.
- *Ext1*+/− mice look normal but have **raised collagen II, reduced collagen X and increased BrdU incorporation in the proliferative zone**; adult bone lengths equal to wild type, so compensated.
- **Dose-dependence**: *Ext2*+/− (≈50% HS) → a small percentage develop rib exostoses. ***Ext1*+/−;*Ext2*+/− double hets (≈25% HS) → almost half develop stereotypic exostoses beside long-bone plates**, with a growth-plate-like arrangement of zones and typical Sox9, Col2, Ihh and Col10 patterns, and shorter HS chains. "Exostosis initiation and frequency are inversely related to overall Ext expression… and increase almost linearly."
- Full conditional postnatal *Ext1* inactivation in chondrocytes → **numerous exostoses throughout the appendicular skeleton**.
- *Prx1-Cre* *Ext1* deletion in limb mesenchyme → joint fusion and variable digit number, attributed to **broader non-physiologic BMP distribution**.
- HS depletion **enhances BMP2 bioactivity** while blocking its internalisation; heparinase raises pSmad1/5/8 in human MSCs.

**The small molecule.**
- **Surfen, a small-molecule heparan sulfate antagonist**, applied to mouse limb mesenchymal **micromass cultures**, produced a **dose-dependent increase in Alcian-blue-positive cartilage nodules**, raised **aggrecan, collagen II, Runx2 and Sox9**, and made the nodules **fuse** — they "were unable to maintain their border and circumscribed perimeter normally occupied by flat perichondrium-like cells."
- **pSmad1/5/8 rose greatly with Surfen and the rise preceded nodule formation**; cells became more sensitive to both endogenous and exogenous BMP2 by reporter assay. **All of it abrogated by Noggin.**
- Adenoviral Cre on *Ext1*^fl/fl limb-bud micromass reproduced it.
- In vivo: **conditional *Ext1* deletion in the perichondrium flanking the upper growth plate (*Gdf5Cre*) turned perichondrial cells nuclear-pSmad1/5/8-positive, followed by formation of ectopic cartilage within the perichondrium. Limb explants treated with Surfen did the same.**
- Conclusion as the authors put it: Ext1 and HS are "critical regulators of the perichondrium phenotype — allowing it to act as an **anti-chondrogenic border** around the growth plate — and are also essential to **restrain and contain cartilage growth**."

**The endogenous versions of Surfen.**
- **Heparanase (HPSE)** cleaves HS chains, is pro-proliferative, is **increased in exostosis tissue**, and **is increased at the chondro-osseous junction in developing bones**, implying a role in late chondrocyte differentiation. "Regulated degradation of HS chains could promote factor release and signaling during other key points of the bone formation process."
- **Sulfatases (Sulf1/2)** remove 6-O-sulfates post-synthetically, acting negatively on BMP and FGF and so **shaping morphogen gradients**. *Sulf1*−/− articular cartilage shows spontaneous osteoarthritis with decreased BMP and upregulated FGF.

**Polarity and lifespan.**
- **Primary cilia organise chondrocytes into columns, and that polarity is lost in human osteochondroma cells.** *Kif3a* (ciliary) deletion in chondrocytes drastically reduced syndecan-3 and perlecan, broadened Ihh along the perichondrium, and produced ectopic hedgehog signalling, ectopic chondrogenesis and exostosis-like masses.
- **Postnatal β-catenin ablation in cartilage causes exostosis-like masses off the growth plate and in the periosteum**, and HME osteochondroma cartilage has little or no β-catenin-positive cells, "potentially extending the lifespan of exostosis chondrocytes."

**Cell of origin.** Three hypotheses: border growth-plate chondrocytes; perichondrial progenitors undergoing de novo chondrogenesis; or **the groove of Ranvier**, "a specialized region of perichondrium near the epiphysis which is rich in progenitor cells and may contain a specialized stem cell niche." The authors "believe the contribution of perichondrial/groove cells may be preponderant." Exostoses contain **mixed HS-expressing and HS-null cells**.

**The price.** 1 in 50,000 children, ~100% penetrant. Shortened stature, forearm bowing and shortening, limb-length inequality, angular deformity at knee and fingers, early-onset osteoarthritis, nerve/vessel/tendon compression, spinal cord compression from vertebral exostoses, oesophageal and pulmonary compromise. **70% operated by age 18; 67% of adults operated. Malignant transformation to chondrosarcoma in 2–5%, mean age at diagnosis 35.** Also: HS loss "may be important for directing the ratio between peripheral and longitudinal growth," and recent work found no change in overall bone volume and no correlation between forearm shortening and the presence of an osteochondroma.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The polarity problem has an answer, and it is the gradient's direction.** Exostoses grow at 90° to the native plate because the de-restricted Ihh/BMP gradient runs radially outward into the perichondrium. The new plate aligns to the signal. **That means polarity is not something to be engineered into a construct — it is set by where the morphogen comes from.** For [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) this is a strong argument for its site choice that the document does not currently make: **at an articular surface the geometry is already axial** (subchondral bone below, joint above), so a broadened BMP/Ihh gradient emanating from the subchondral plate would run **along the bone's long axis**, producing an axially-oriented plate rather than a lateral exostosis. The exostosis is a lateral lesion because it forms at a lateral border. Put the same perturbation at an axial border and you should get an axial plate.
2. **De-restricting an endogenous gradient beats adding exogenous factor, and there is a small molecule for it.** Surfen raised pSmad1/5/8, turned perichondrium chondrogenic, and did so **using the BMP already present** — Noggin abolished it. Compare the alternative: implanting recombinant BMP, which is sufficient for adult de novo endochondral bone but makes every subsequent scratch an ossification site ([28645539](28645539.md)). **Lowering the barrier to an existing local signal is inherently more spatially contained than raising the signal systemically.**
3. **The perichondrium is a maintained barrier, which means barriers are the control system.** "An anti-chondrogenic border… essential to restrain and contain cartilage growth." The adult skeleton is not passively quiescent; it is actively fenced. That reframes the whole problem: an adult does not lack the capacity to grow cartilage, it maintains a fence, and the fence is made of heparan sulfate.
4. **Heparanase is the endogenous Surfen and it is already concentrated where the conversion front is.** HPSE is raised at the chondro-osseous junction in developing bone and in exostosis tissue, and implicated in late chondrocyte differentiation. So the body already uses regulated HS degradation to release factors at the ossification front — which is Stage 3 of ACTIVATION.md, with an endogenous enzyme doing it.
5. **The dose-response is the feasibility argument and the safety argument at once.** 100% HS → nothing; 50% → occasional rib lesions; 25% → half the animals get stereotypic exostoses beside long-bone plates. Initiation frequency scales almost linearly with HS loss. A rheostat, not a switch — but a rheostat whose far end is a tumour syndrome with 2–5% malignant transformation.
6. **Primary cilia are the column-forming machinery, and they are lost in osteochondromas.** Columns are what make growth longitudinal rather than isotropic ([31787777](31787777.md)). So an exostosis is a growth plate with a *correct axis but degraded columnar order*, which may be exactly why it makes a lumpy mass rather than a clean elongating shaft. **Preserving ciliary function while de-restricting the gradient is a specific, testable design requirement that nothing in my corpus has tried.** It also connects to (−)-epicatechin's reported rescue of primary cilium defects ([42650460](42650460.md)).
7. **β-catenin loss extends chondrocyte lifespan in these lesions.** A third independent node, and the same direction as the Wnt-inhibition result that switched physeal repair from bone to cartilage.

## Levers
- **Surfen** — existing small-molecule HS antagonist; dose-dependently converts perichondrial/limb mesenchyme to cartilage by de-restricting endogenous BMP; effect Noggin-sensitive.
- **Heparanase (HPSE)** — endogenous HS-cleaving enzyme, naturally enriched at the chondro-osseous junction.
- **Sulf1/Sulf2** — post-synthetic 6-O-desulfation; shapes BMP and FGF gradients. *Sulf1*−/− gives OA with low BMP and high FGF signalling.
- **EXT1/EXT2 dose** as the rheostat (25% HS ≈ half the animals get organised ectopic plates).
- **Primary cilia / Kif3a** — columnar polarity; lost in human osteochondroma. **The order-preserving requirement.**
- **β-catenin low** — extends chondrocyte lifespan in ectopic cartilage.
- **Groove of Ranvier perichondrial progenitors** — the probable cell of origin.
- **Noggin** — the off-switch for the whole thing, which is how you would stop it.
- **Anti-lever: uncontrolled, body-wide HS reduction.** That is HME: 67% adult surgery rate, 2–5% chondrosarcoma, early OA, limb-length inequality, spinal cord compression.

## Honest limits
- **A review.** Almost every datum is secondary; the primaries (Huegel 2013 for Surfen, Zak 2011 for the double hets, Koyama 2007 for Kif3a, Cantley 2012 for β-catenin, Jones 2010/2013) are unread.
- **Surfen's evidence is micromass culture and limb explants**, plus a genetic in-vivo correlate. No animal was given Surfen and shown to grow a plate or a longer bone.
- Everything in vivo is **embryonic or juvenile onset**. The adult-propagation fact is a clinical observation about pre-existing human lesions, not an experiment.
- **No length measurement anywhere.** Exostoses are measured as lesions. Indeed HME patients are *shorter*, with limb-length inequality — the lesions interfere with native plate function and HS loss is said to shift the peripheral-to-longitudinal growth ratio. **The natural experiment produces mass, deformity and shortening, not height.**
- Cell of origin unresolved; the authors list three hypotheses and favour one.
- Exostoses lose columnar polarity, which is precisely the property longitudinal growth requires.
- The malignant transformation rate (2–5%, mean age 35) is the dominant fact about this pathway and it is not a hypothetical risk.

## Chase
- **Huegel et al. 2013 — the Surfen micromass and limb-explant study, and the *Gdf5Cre;Ext1*^fl/fl perichondrial deletion.** The primary for the only small molecule here. Top priority.
- **Zak et al. 2011 — *Ext1*+/−;*Ext2*+/− double hets with stereotypic, properly zoned exostoses beside long-bone plates.** The dose-response primary.
- **Koyama et al. 2007 — *Kif3a* deletion, HSPG loss, broadened Ihh, ectopic chondrogenesis.** And de Andrea 2010 — **loss of columnar polarity in human osteochondroma cells.** The polarity primaries.
- Cantley et al. 2012 — postnatal β-catenin ablation producing exostosis-like masses off the growth plate and in periosteum; β-catenin-negative HME osteochondroma cartilage.
- **Jones et al. 2013 — HS loss directing the ratio between peripheral and longitudinal growth.** Directly about direction of growth; needed.
- Jones et al. 2010 — postnatal conditional *Ext1* inactivation giving numerous exostoses.
- Trebicz-Geffen 2008, Yang 2010, Brown 2008 — **heparanase in exostoses and at the chondro-osseous junction.**
- Otsuki 2010 — *Sulf1*−/− spontaneous OA with altered BMP/FGF balance.
- Hilton 2005 — *Ext1*+/− plates with raised Col II, reduced Col X and increased proliferative-zone BrdU, compensating to normal adult length.
- **41056386 — "An ectopic Hedgehog signaling axis drives *directional* tumor outgrowth in a mouse model of hereditary multiple exostoses" (Sci Signal 2025).** Not open access; on the needed list. The title is the polarity question stated directly.
