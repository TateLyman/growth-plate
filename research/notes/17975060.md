# PMID 17975060 — Molecular basis for the nerve dependence of limb regeneration in an adult vertebrate

Kumar A, Godwin JW, Gates PB, Garza-Garcia AA, Brockes JP. *Science* 2007;318(5851):772-777. PMC2696928. doi:10.1126/science.1147710

**What I read:** the complete PMC full text — abstract, introduction, all four Results sections, the full Discussion. (Figures and supplementary are referenced but not included in the PMC text body.) Not an abstract read.

## Model
Adult newt (*Notophthalmus viridescens*) limb regeneration. Yeast two-hybrid screen with the 69-aa newt Prod 1 as bait against normal-limb and blastema prey libraries. Denervation by cutting spinal nerves at the brachial plexus of the right limb, then bilateral amputation, then plasmid electroporation into the distal stump at day 5 post-amputation.

## Exact findings
- **nAG is a secreted ligand for Prod 1.** Newt Anterior Gradient protein family (the XAG2/AGR family), single thioredoxin fold, secretory signal. Pull-down complex with Prod 1 confirmed; secreted myc-nAG from Cos 7 medium bound the surface of mouse PS cells expressing anchored newt Prod 1, and not untransfected cells.
- **Expression is sequential and strictly nerve-dependent.** Day 5 post-amputation: strong staining in the distal **nerve sheath, in Schwann cells (HNK1+) and not axons**. After day 10: glandular structures in the **wound epidermis**. Denervation abolished **both** (day 8: innervated sheath strongly positive, denervated sheath no reactivity; innervated wound epidermis full of nAG+ glands, contralateral none — and no glandular structures at all).
- **A single protein rescued a denervated limb.** Electroporation of the nAG plasmid into denervated stumps: **half of the animals regenerated to digit stage by day 30–40**, against **0 of the empty-vector controls**. Electroporation reached 30–50% of mesenchymal cells and persisted up to 3 weeks.
- **The rescue was not via re-innervation.** Acetylated-tubulin staining showed few nerve profiles in rescued limbs versus dense innervation in controls.
- **The rescue was partial and selective.** Rescued limbs were atrophic with less muscle (anti-myosin) — "the dependence of skeletal muscle on its innervation was not satisfied by substituting nAG." But "the nerve requirement for completion of the PD axis was met."
- **nAG is a direct mitogen.** On dissociated blastemal cells (epidermis removed, serum-free attachment, 1% serum), ~70% of which were surface-Prod 1+: **S-phase entry stimulation index 8.3 ± 3.3-fold, SD across 8 independent experiments**, all preparations responsive. 18 kD band in conditioned medium.
- nAG also **induced nAG+ glands in the denervated wound epidermis** (5/6 animals vs 0/6 vector), mirroring XAG2's induction of ectopic cement gland in *Xenopus*.

## The architecture this completes
The authors state the division of labour explicitly: **"PD identity is manifested by the quantitative gradation of Prod 1, and nAG has no role in specifying that identity but acts through Prod 1 to promote cell division."**

So the positional determinant is also the **growth receptor**. And they name the condition under which growth happens:

> "Blastemal growth is stimulated in experimental confrontations of cells differing in positional identity, for example in PD intercalation where a wrist level blastema is grafted onto a shoulder stump, and **this is always dependent on the presence of the nerve.**"

**Growth = positional disparity × permission signal.** Both required, neither sufficient. That is the complete rule, and it closes the gap left by [35241664](notes/35241664.md) (disparity is what generates length) and [31026439](notes/31026439.md) (FGF/BMP substitutes for the nerve). The ALM confirms it from the other side: a deflected nerve alone gives a "bump"; it only becomes a limb if disparate-identity dermal fibroblasts are also supplied. And **dermal fibroblasts express Prod 1, upregulated by retinoic acid.**

This is why the §18.4 two-step table is right but under-specified. Step 1 (competence/permission) and step 2 (goal/disparity) are not merely sequential — the permission ligand signals *through* the positional determinant. Supply permission to a positionally uniform field and you get proliferation without pattern; supply disparity without permission and you get nothing.

## The human candidate pair — the most useful thing in this paper
The authors note that **human AG2 (AGR2)** was found by the same yeast two-hybrid approach to complex with **C4.4A (LYPD3)**, a GPI-anchored protein with **two Ly6-type domains** related to uPAR — the same superfamily as Prod 1 (which is a GPI-anchored Ly6 protein). Their conclusion: "these results suggest that functional interactions between AG proteins and this class of small Cys-rich protein domains may be conserved."

**So the mammalian candidate for the positional-value × growth-permission coupling is AGR2 (secreted) acting on LYPD3/C4.4A (GPI-anchored, Ly6/uPAR family).** Both are real human genes, both are well characterised in cancer biology, AGR2 is a secreted protein, and anti-LYPD3 antibodies exist. **This is the first named, mammalian, molecularly specific candidate for "permission to build a structure" that the project has found.** It is the authors' inference from domain relatedness, not a demonstrated mammalian function — flagged accordingly below.

## Levers
- **AGR2 / AGR3** — secreted Anterior Gradient proteins, the human nAG counterparts. Supplyable in principle.
- **LYPD3 / C4.4A** — the candidate human receptor side.
- **Prod 1 is retinoic-acid inducible in dermal fibroblasts**, so the positional-receptor level is itself RA-tunable — which links this directly to the CYP26 route ([40494878](notes/40494878.md)).
- **Local delivery.** The authors' own closing proposal: "The local delivery of permissive regulators like nAG could then evoke formation of the appropriate structures without the need for subsequent intervention." Consistent with the local-only efficacy in [36493752](notes/36493752.md).

## Anti-levers
- Denervation — abolishes nAG in both niche compartments and, after the initial cell accumulation, kills regeneration irreversibly ("denervated adult newt blastemas undergo fibrosis and other tissue changes that stop them from making a delayed regenerative response").
- Permission without disparity — a "bump," not a limb.

## Honest limits
1. **Prod 1 is salamander-specific** and in the axolotl lacks a membrane anchor ([35241664](notes/35241664.md)). The AGR2–LYPD3 analogy rests on Ly6-domain relatedness, and the authors say plainly that "the degree of relatedness between the three dimensional structures of Prod 1 and C4.4 domains is not yet resolved." **No mammalian function has been demonstrated for this pair in patterning or growth.**
2. The authors' own verdict on transfer: **"the blastema is an autonomous unit of organisation for which there is no obvious mammalian counterpart."** Their proposal is to engineer a blastema in mammals first — which is exactly the §17/§18 competence problem, unsolved.
3. **Half the animals, and only to digit stage**, with atrophic, muscle-poor limbs. The authors attribute the failures to variable electroporation levels, which is plausible and untested.
4. Everything is overexpression by electroporation. No loss-of-function for nAG.
5. The mitogen assay is on dissociated blastemal cells in 1% serum — a permissive context, and the readout is BrdU, not structure.
6. Singer's old hypothesis that low mammalian nerve density explains non-regeneration is explicitly rejected by the authors as "now seems unlikely."
7. Nothing here is about length, cartilage, bone or post-closure anything.

## Chase list
- **Does AGR2 act on LYPD3 to drive proliferation in a mammalian mesenchymal cell?** The decisive question. If yes, there is a human permission ligand/receptor pair.
- **AGR2 and LYPD3 expression in adult human perichondrium, periosteum, cartilage and vertebral endplate.** Cheap, and would say whether the permission machinery is present in the target tissue.
- **Oliveira et al. 2017 and da Silva et al. 2002** — the other Prod 1 primaries, for the graded expression and the focal-electroporation proximalization.
- Whether **AGR2 is nerve- or Schwann-cell-derived in mammals**, and whether mammalian periosteal/perichondrial niches have a Schwann-cell component that could supply it. The groove of Ranvier's innervation status is not something I have read on.
- Kumar et al.'s later work on nAG in the axolotl and on whether nAG substitutes in a mammalian wound.
