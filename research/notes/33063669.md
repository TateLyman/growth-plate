# PMID 33063669 — Xie M, ... Chagin AS. Secondary ossification center induces and protects growth plate structure (2020, eLife)

Read in full (PMC, ~80k chars).

**Findings.**
- **Evolution.** The secondary ossification centre (SOC) appears with amniotes, i.e. full terrestrial life.
  - Anamniotes, chelonians and crocodilians have none: they have cartilaginous epiphyses, many with indeterminate growth.
  - Archosaurs (birds, dinosaurs) instead have **ossified marrow protrusions into the epiphyseal cartilage that sequester hypertrophic chondrocytes between them**.
  - Whales secondarily reduced or lost SOCs (orca, sperm whale: absent).
  - Bats and jerboa: SOCs form first in the limbs that are loaded first.
- **FEA** (Carter-type 2D; cartilage E = 6 MPa, ν 0.47; SOC E = 500 MPa): the SOC stiffens the epiphysis and **reduces octahedral shear stress in the hypertrophic zone**. Archosaur protrusions protect the hypertrophic zone even better, but do not stiffen the joint.
- **Ex vivo loading** (mouse P30 with SOC vs rat P10 without; same size):
  - Without SOC, 40% of chondrocytes die at 1 N. Hypertrophic chondrocytes die at loads as low as **0.2 N (~0.023 MPa)**.
  - With SOC they tolerate 3–5 N (≈25× more).
  - Death is caspase-3 apoptosis via **YAP–p73**. Ihh, PTHrP and Col X fall with loading.
  - Death distribution matches **principal compressive stress** (plus octahedral), not hydrostatic stress.
  - Identical pressure kills chondrocytes before SOC formation but not after. Death correlates inversely with SOC size.
- **In vivo.** Delaying the SOC (axitinib; Gnas activation; Sik3 cKO) increases hypertrophic chondrocyte apoptosis.
- **AFM.** Hypertrophic chondrocytes have ~25% of the stiffness of columnar chondrocytes. They are the most mechanically fragile cells, because they swell ~20-fold in 12 h.
- SOC formation is genetically programmed: unloading (sciatic resection, taping) did not change SOC size, and terrestrial newts did not form one.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth (central).**
- **The hypertrophic zone, the length actuator, must be mechanically shielded from compressive and deviatoric stress.** Hydrostatic stress is tolerated.
- In adult articular cartilage there is no bony layer between the loaded surface and the deep/calcified zone. Any attempt to make the deep articular zone hypertrophy under normal weight-bearing would kill those cells, which is a plausible reason articular cartilage evolved to *not* hypertrophy.
- Two natural solutions exist:
  - (a) A stiff cap: the SOC, in mammals.
  - (b) **Subchondral bone protrusions that sequester hypertrophic cells in pockets**: archosaurs, which grow without SOCs.
- In an adult articular-surface growth scheme, (b) is the relevant architecture. The irregular, interdigitating calcified-cartilage/subchondral interface would provide the "pockets".
- Practical consequence: **hypertrophic expansion should be scheduled into unloaded or hydrostatic-only windows**, not during compressive/shear loading.

**Levers.**
- Stress-type gating: HP is safe for hypertrophic cells; principal compression and octahedral shear are lethal.
- Loading schedule: hypertrophy in unloaded or traction periods; progenitor induction in loaded/sheared periods.
- YAP–p73 is the death pathway (mechanosensor).
