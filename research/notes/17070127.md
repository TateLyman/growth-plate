# PMID 17070127 — Zhang P, Su M, Liu Y, Hsu A, Yokota H. Knee loading dynamically alters intramedullary pressure in mouse femora (2007, Bone)

Read in full (NIH author manuscript PDF, 12 pp).

**Model.** Female C57BL/6 mice, ~16 weeks old (n=3). A fibre-optic pressure sensor was cannulated into the femoral medullary cavity in vivo.
- Lateral knee loading at 0.5–5 N, 0.05–50 Hz.
- Ex vivo: 3 µm microparticles tracked in a glass tube inserted into the femoral cavity.

**Results.**
- Baseline intramedullary pressure (IMP): 1290±150 Pa (~9.5 mmHg).
- Knee loading produced IMP oscillation synchronous with the load frequency:
  - Amplitude rose ~20 Pa per N from 1–4 N, then plateaued at ~130 Pa.
  - Flat up to ~10 Hz; fell significantly above 20 Hz (0.5 Hz: 2.8 mV; 5 Hz: 2.4 mV; 50 Hz: 0.76 mV at 40 V).
- Microparticles oscillated at ~10.6 µm amplitude (0.5 Hz, 4 N), superimposed on a linear outflow.
- Pressure swings are 0.2–10% of baseline but dynamic. Oscillatory flow enhances solute dispersion.

**What it establishes.**
- Lateral epiphyseal compression makes the bone a **hydraulic pump**: it changes medullary volume and pressure, with viscoelastic low-pass behaviour (<20 Hz effective).
- Correlation with anabolic effects, not causation.

**Levers.**
- Bone-end compression gives a controllable pressure/flow signal through the marrow and subchondral space. That is relevant if the target cells (periosteal/perichondrial or marrow progenitors near the old physis scar) respond to fluid flow or pressure.
- In cartilage biology, *hydrostatic pressure* (MPa-level in joints, not 100 Pa) is the chondrogenic signal. The marrow pressures here are far too low to be chondrogenic; this pathway is osteogenic.
- So joint loading is an **osteogenic** (width) tool, not a chondrogenic (length) tool, unless the loaded tissue is cartilage itself.
