# 20200963 — A key role for membrane transporter NKCC1 in mediating chondrocyte volume increase in the mammalian growth plate

**Citation.** J Bone Miner Res 2010;25(7):1594–1603. PMC3154001. Read in full (Introduction, Methods, all Results, Discussion).

## Model / method
Rat. Three complementary preparations:
- **Whole metatarsal/metacarpal rudiment culture**, P7, 24 h, ± the specific NKCC inhibitor **bumetanide** (0–200 µM, dose-response); then ± the anion-exchange inhibitor **DIDS** (0–1 mM) on top of 100 µM bumetanide. Length measured by calibrated stereomicroscopy, growth plates then sectioned and zone heights measured.
- **Confocal immunohistochemistry** of P7 proximal tibial growth plates (n = 7) with the T4 monoclonal anti-NKCC antibody, quantifying intracellular vs plasma-membrane fluorescence in eight equidistant growth-plate sections (≥421 cells, ≥4 animals).
- **Laser-capture microdissection + microarray + RT-PCR** of proliferative-zone (PZC) and hypertrophic-zone (HZC) cells from tibiae of rats at P49, P53, P58 (n = 5 each), screening 161 membrane-transport genes.

## Exact numbers
**The actuator, quantified.**
- Hypertrophic volume increase is "predominantly in the longitudinal axis, which accounts for **approximately 80% of bone lengthening**." PZC cell division contributes ~10%.
- Cell volume goes from **~1,000 µm³ (PZC) to ~15,000 µm³ (HZC)** — a 15-fold increase.
- Swelling cannot be driven by falling extracellular osmolarity: that would require plasma osmolarity to drop from ~300 to ~20 mOsmol, "clearly implausible." So it must be **active import of osmolytes**. Catabolism of intracellular macromolecules does not account for it, and organic solutes (sugars, amino acids) account for only **~9%** of the osmolytes required.

**NKCC1 inhibition.**
- Bumetanide reduced bone lengthening **dose-dependently**, significant at 12.5, 100 and 200 µM. Best fit a three-parameter hyperbolic decay (r² = 0.966). Metatarsals alone: control grew ~6% in 24 h; 100 µM bumetanide reduced lengthening by **~35% (p < .01)**.
- **The dissociation that matters.** Total growth plate height unchanged (636 ± 150 → 689 ± 130 µm, p = .655). **Late hypertrophic zone height 204 ± 25.6 → 151 ± 15.0 µm (p = .015); HZ as a fraction of total 33.8% → 22.7% (p = .008).** And **hypertrophic cell number was identical — 193 ± 11.3 vs 192 ± 13.6 (p = .937)** — as were cell densities (1400 ± 125 vs 1377 ± 350 cells/mm², p = .953).
  **So a 35% loss of longitudinal growth was produced purely by shrinking per-cell volume, with zero change in cell number.**

**Localisation.**
- NKCC present throughout the plate but **translocates from an intracellular (perinuclear, probably ER/Golgi) pool in PZCs to the plasma membrane in HZCs** (p < .05 in every section except S5). Fig. 5 overlays cell volume on the localisation curve: **NKCC appears in the plasma membrane immediately *preceding* the volume increase.**
- NKCC1 mRNA rose PZ→HZ: microarray fold change 2.93 (P49), 2.37 (P58); RT-PCR 3.25 and 2.17, r² = 0.986 between methods. **NKCC2 was at background throughout** (kidney-specific), so the T4 signal is NKCC1.

**The second transporter.**
- Of 161 transport genes screened, **AE3 (Slc4a3) was the only other one to change: ~14-fold up PZ→HZ (217 → 3122 AEU)**, against no change in AE1 (−1.18×) or AE2 (1.04×).
- **DIDS added on top of bumetanide inhibited lengthening by up to ~80% of control (p < .001 at 500 µM)**, significant from 175 µM.
- So NKCC1 + anion exchange together account for roughly the whole ~80% share that HZC volume increase contributes to lengthening.

**Negative / safety data the authors themselves raise.**
- **NKCC1-knockout mice are smaller over the first 2 weeks (peak growth) but recover to wild-type size by maturity** — i.e. redundancy exists.
- Koo et al.: furosemide given to 4-day-old rats for 24 days produced a **significant reduction in tibial length** with normal plasma/bone calcium, sodium, magnesium, potassium and PTH — a growth effect, not a mineralisation effect.
- The paper closes by warning of "the possible detrimental effect on bone growth with chronic exposure to loop diuretics," and notes paediatric growth retardation on furosemide may be under-reported because the underlying diseases suppress growth anyway.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
**This is the molecular machinery of *h_max*, and it is the most important addition to [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) since the document was written.**

1. **It experimentally dissociates *h_max* from *n*.** ACTIVATION.md §3 argues that the hypertrophic volume set-point is a per-cell variable and therefore bypasses the proliferation wall of [COMPOUNDS.md](../COMPOUNDS.md) §2. That was an inference from Decker's volume measurements ([28438606](28438606.md)). **Here it is a direct experiment: 35% of longitudinal growth was removed by shrinking cells, with hypertrophic cell number and density unchanged to three significant figures.** The two terms of G = n × h_max are independently manipulable, and *h_max* alone carries ~80% of the growth.
2. **The actuator is an osmotic pump with named components.** Not a vague "hypertrophy." Water follows actively imported inorganic osmolytes; **NKCC1 (SLC12A2) and AE3 (SLC4A3)** together account for ~80% of bone lengthening in this preparation. That converts "raise the volume set-point" from an aspiration into a specific transport problem.
3. **The trigger is membrane trafficking, not transcription.** NKCC1 mRNA rises only ~2–3-fold, but the protein **relocates from a perinuclear pool to the plasma membrane immediately before the cell swells.** The authors name the open questions precisely: "NKCC1 regulation through control of protein synthesis, translocation to the cytoplasmic membrane, and activation through phosphorylation/dephosphorylation events." **That is the lever, and it is a trafficking/phosphorylation lever, which is the class of target that small molecules reach well.** NKCC1's phospho-regulation is the WNK–SPAK/OSR1 axis, which is extensively drugged — in the inhibitory direction, which is the wrong way, but the axis is characterised and the chloride-sensing logic is known.
4. **It gives the adult articular deep zone a mechanistic reason for its halved volume set-point.** Decker's "chondrocytes set their volume by location" ([28438606](28438606.md)) now has a candidate mechanism: how much NKCC1 and AE3 is trafficked to the membrane, and how much of it is phosphorylated, at that location. A set-point implemented by transporter trafficking is addressable in a way a set-point implemented by, say, cell-cycle exit would not be.
5. **An immediate, actionable negative with human relevance: loop diuretics.** Bumetanide and furosemide inhibit exactly this transporter, cost ~35% of longitudinal growth in rudiment culture, and shortened tibiae in vivo in rats at normal electrolytes. Anyone on a loop diuretic is pharmacologically suppressing the one actuator that carries 80% of longitudinal growth. **This belongs on the anti-lever list alongside NSAIDs ([24449269](24449269.md)).**
6. **It also sets a ceiling caution.** NKCC1-null mice recover to normal size by maturity. The system is redundant, so single-transporter manipulation may be buffered — which is consistent with bumetanide capturing only 35% of the ~80%, and with needing DIDS on top to reach 80%.

## Levers
- **NKCC1 / SLC12A2** — the named transporter for hypertrophic swelling. Membrane trafficking and phosphorylation state are the regulated variables, not transcript level.
- **The WNK–SPAK/OSR1 → NKCC1 phospho-axis** (not studied here, named by the authors as the unknown): the regulated activation step, and the obvious place to look for an existing agent.
- **AE3 / SLC4A3** — ~14-fold up PZ→HZ, the second half of the osmotic pump, and explicitly flagged by the authors as having **no specific inhibitor**, hence no clean tool and no clean druggability read yet.
- **Coupled transporters that would have to co-operate**: NHE (sodium–hydrogen exchange), NCC, and the efflux side — K⁺-driven cotransporters (KCC family) and the Na⁺/K⁺ pump, which *reduce* volume. **Inhibiting the efflux arm is the untried direction with the right sign.**
- **Anti-levers: bumetanide, furosemide** (and DIDS, as a tool).

## Honest limits
- Rat, P7 metatarsal/metacarpal rudiments in organ culture for 24 h, plus tibiae for imaging and P49–P58 tibiae for expression. **Three different bones from three different ages**, which the authors acknowledge and explain.
- No adult tissue, no closed plate, no articular cartilage anywhere in it.
- **Pharmacological inhibition only.** Nobody raised the set-point; nobody showed growth can be *increased* through this axis. The entire finding is loss-of-function.
- Bumetanide at 100–200 µM is far above the ~20 µM needed for maximal NKCC inhibition in isolated cells; the authors argue in-situ exposure is lower, but off-target effects on bicarbonate exchange, chloride channels and K-Cl cotransport are a stated concern at the top of the range.
- **DIDS is nonspecific** — it also abolishes DTDST-mediated sulfate uptake needed for proteoglycan synthesis, and may act through intracellular pH. The AE3 attribution is a hypothesis the authors explicitly hedge.
- The microarray was internally inconsistent (NKCC1 up at P49 and P58, down at P53); only P49 cleared the 95% confidence limit. RT-PCR agreed with the microarray (r² = 0.986) but that is agreement between two measurements of the same pooled RNA, not independent replication.
- NKCC1-null mice reach normal adult size — so this transporter is not rate-limiting over a lifetime.
