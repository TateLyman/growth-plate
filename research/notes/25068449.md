# PMID 25068449 — Chau M, Lui JC, ... Baron J, Nilsson O. Gene expression profiling reveals similarities between the spatial architectures of postnatal articular and growth plate cartilage (2014, PLoS One)

Read in full (PMC, ~54k chars).

**Model.** 10-day-old rat proximal tibia, microdissected into articular superficial zone (SZ), articular intermediate/deep zone (IDZ) and growth-plate RZ. Microarrays (n=4), compared with an earlier RZ/PZ/HZ dataset (7-day rats). qPCR validation.

**Findings.**
- Contrary to the authors' hypothesis, articular **IDZ resembles growth-plate RZ**. SZ resembles PZ and HZ: 3/6 PZ markers and 27/126 HZ markers are up in SZ, including Mmp13, Mmp9, Col1a1/2, Ctsk.
- Shared IDZ/RZ genes: Bmp3, Grem1, Sfrp5, Sfrp1, Frzb (BMP and Wnt antagonists).
- IDZ vs RZ: IDZ is higher in Prg4 (25×), Postn, Wif1, Igf1 (IGF/GH signalling). RZ is higher in Hhip (13×), Bmp7, Sfrp5, and SHH/BMP pathway genes.
- Articular SZ has high Igf1 (13× over IDZ), Cd34, Tnmd.
- The authors infer that articular-cartilage progenitors may sit in the deeper layers, and that articular chondrocytes follow a program with "distinct similarities" to growth-plate differentiation, minus hypertrophy.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Articular cartilage is a cousin of the growth plate, from the same anlage, with an RZ-like progenitor compartment (IDZ).
- In juveniles, articular cartilage *grows the epiphysis* (cf. Kronenberg/Lui on articular growth).
- Articular cartilage persists for life in adults, i.e. it is the only hyaline cartilage at long-bone ends after fusion. Its deep zone sits on a calcified layer that is slowly endochondrally advanced (tidemark).

**Levers.**
- **The articular cartilage–subchondral bone unit is the only surviving, structurally "growth-plate-like" cartilage at bone ends in adults.**
- If its deep zone could be pushed to (a) proliferate, (b) undergo controlled hypertrophy and (c) be replaced by bone at its base, while the surface is maintained, the epiphysis would lengthen. This is the original juvenile mechanism of epiphyseal growth.
- Mechanical lever: articular cartilage biology is strongly load-dependent. The tidemark advances faster with *unloading* (Oegema 1997; need full text).
