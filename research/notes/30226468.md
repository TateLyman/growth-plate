# 30226468 — Palovarotene reduces heterotopic ossification in juvenile FOP mice but exhibits pronounced skeletal toxicity

**Citation.** eLife 2018;7:e40814. PMC6143342. Read in full (natural history, dosing, skeletal toxicity, FAP dynamics, Discussion).

## Model / method
*Acvr1^tnR206H/+; R26^NG/+; Pdgfrα-Cre* mice — ACVR1 R206H targeted to **PDGFRα+ fibro/adipogenic progenitors (FAPs)**, giving body-wide spontaneous HO. Palovarotene (RARγ agonist) 0.735 or 1.47 mg/kg i.p. **daily P14→P41**. Also: FACS-isolated bioluminescent R206H-FAP transplantation into SCID hosts with live 3-D bioluminescence tomography, ± palovarotene, ± anti-activin-A mAb.

## Exact numbers
**Disease natural history.** HO at ≥1 site: 40% at P14, 100% at P28, 100% at P42. Body weight −16% (P28) and −35% (P42) vs WT. Median survival 39 d; 43% survival at P42. Jaw HO in 100% of mice that failed to reach P42.

**Efficacy.** Body-wide HO/overgrowth burden reduced **~50%** at both doses (no dose-response → efficacy ceiling). Transplanted-FAP HO volume reduced ~70% by 1.47 mg/kg. Palovarotene did **not** improve survival (27% and 33% vs 47% vehicle), and 1.47 mg/kg reduced survival of **wild-type** juveniles.

**Skeletal toxicity — the part that matters here.**
- **3/4 mice at 0.735 mg/kg and 4/5 at 1.47 mg/kg completely lacked a proximal tibial growth plate at P42.** Same in palovarotene-treated wild-type mice.
- The same animals had **thickened articular cartilage** with thinner subchondral bone and an open, untrabeculated marrow space.
- **Severe osteochondral overgrowth at the knee in 3/4 and 4/5 mice, histologically emanating from the articular cartilages and associated connective tissues of the distal femur and proximal tibia.** Also abnormal costochondral junctions (5/5 at the high dose), stouter snouts (nasal cartilage).
- **Tibial length: −13% (0.735 mg/kg, p < 0.001) and −19% (1.47 mg/kg, p < 0.001)** vs vehicle. (FOP genotype alone: −5%.)
- In the two mice that retained a plate, **bony bridges were present within the growth plate** — the signature of prior physeal trauma.
- Zone measurement before ablation: normal proliferative zone width, **reduced hypertrophic zone width (p = 0.01)**.
- Window dependence (citing Inubushi 2018): dosing from **3–6 weeks** did not shorten tibia/femur or ablate the distal femoral plate, whereas **2–6 weeks** did. Toxicity tracks cumulative exposure and developmental window, not per-dose.

**FAP population dynamics.** WT FAPs roughly doubled d1→d3 then fell 73% by d5; only 9% of peak survived to d7. R206H-FAPs survived better and kept expanding d3→d5. **Anti-activin-A mAb abolished HO entirely and restored wild-type population dynamics.** Palovarotene only blunted expansion. **Peak FAP number predicted HO volume: R² = 0.85 (untreated), 0.83 (palovarotene).** Threshold for skeletogenic differentiation: **>20,000 transplanted cells**; above that, HO volume proportional to cell number. 7-day palovarotene pretreatment did **not** render FAPs refractory (contradicting the CD1530/NRX204647 reprogramming claim).

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
This is the most instructive negative result in the whole compound literature, and it cuts directly against the naive version of my own mechanical theory.

1. **A drug thickened articular cartilage in vivo and the limb got shorter.** Palovarotene produced exactly the tissue change PCAC Phase B/C aims for — thicker articular cartilage, proliferating joint-surface chondrocytes, osteochondral mass growing out of the joint surface — and the result was **−13% to −19% tibial length**. The new tissue grew as lumps at joint margins (osteochondromas, knee overgrowth, costochondral deformity), not as length. **Thickening a joint cartilage and lengthening a bone are different outcomes, and this experiment separates them.** This is the failure mode named in [THEORY.md](../THEORY.md) §6 shown happening, pharmacologically, in a live animal.
2. **The reason is the missing hypertrophic step.** Reduced hypertrophic zone width, blocked COL X-stage progression, blocked BMP/SMAD1-5-8 — retinoid signalling expands the cartilage pool while forbidding its conversion to bone. Cartilage accumulates; no bone is added in line. Confirms from the opposite direction what [37126720](37126720.md) shows positively: **conversion (hypertrophy → ossification), not proliferation, is the rate-limiting step for length.** Anything that only grows cartilage produces a swelling.
3. **Retinoid/RARγ agonism is therefore the wrong direction for height, and probably RARγ *antagonism* is the direction of interest.** Retinoids are a known cause of premature physeal closure in humans (cited: Noyes 2016, Vahlquist 1992, DiGiovanna 2001). The mirror-image hypothesis — that reducing RARγ signalling delays closure — is testable and is not something this paper did; it is a real gap.
4. **De novo endochondral bone formation in a postnatal animal has a cell-number threshold (>20,000 FAPs) and a linear dose-response above it (R² = 0.85).** That is quantitative, and it is the sort of number any "induce a new growth cartilage" proposal needs. It also says the process is not all-or-none biology but a mass-action one.
5. **Activin A blockade was complete where the small molecule was partial.** Ligand sequestration restored normal progenitor behaviour; the nuclear-receptor agonist only slowed it and poisoned the skeleton. Where a pathway has an obligate ligand, blocking the ligand is cleaner than tuning the transcriptional output. (This is the control/safety side of the FOP story in [28629737](28629737.md).)
6. **Everything about this pathway, as drugged today, exists to *stop* bone formation.** Palovarotene and garetosmab are brakes. There is no accelerator in the clinic, and this paper shows what happens when you press on the brake during growth.

## Levers (and anti-levers)
- **Anti-lever: RARγ agonism.** Ablates the plate, shortens the bone, overgrows joints. Unambiguous.
- **Possible lever, untested: RARγ antagonism / reduced retinoid tone** as a delay-closure strategy. The clinical observation that retinoids close plates early is the strongest circumstantial support; no one in this literature tried the reverse for height.
- **Activin A** as an obligate, blockable ligand for the FAP→cartilage→bone program — and therefore, read in reverse, as the ligand whose *presence* is required for de novo endochondral bone from a resident progenitor. Both directions are informative.
- **Progenitor number as the tunable quantity.** HO volume ∝ peak progenitor number, R² = 0.85. Whatever the signal, the output scales with how many competent cells are at the site.
- **Dosing window matters more than dose.** 3–6 wk safe, 2–6 wk ablates the plate, at the same per-dose amount. A general warning about any chronic skeletal pharmacology.

## Honest limits
- Juvenile mice with open plates; says nothing directly about adult closed plates.
- Mice never fuse their plates the way humans do, so "growth plate ablation" here is a drug-induced lesion, not a model of physiological closure.
- Semi-quantitative HO scoring (fusion to the skeleton prevented volumetric segmentation).
- Small n (4–7 per group).
- Disagrees with Chakkalakal 2016 on efficacy and on whether palovarotene rescues or harms skeletal growth; the authors attribute this to dosing schedule and window, which is plausible but unresolved.

## Chase
- Chakkalakal 2016 — the study that reported palovarotene *ameliorating* skeletal growth deficits in Prrx1-Cre FOP mice. Direct contradiction; needed in full.
- Inubushi 2018 — the 3–6 wk vs 2–6 wk window comparison.
- Shimono 2011, 2014 (CD1530, NRX204647) — the RARγ-agonist "reprogramming" claim this paper fails to reproduce.
- Lees-Shepard 2018 — the FAP cell-of-origin paper.
- Noyes 2016, Vahlquist 1992, DiGiovanna 2001 — human retinoid skeletal toxicity and premature physeal closure. **Directly relevant: human evidence that a drug can close plates. Needed in full for the mirror hypothesis.**
- Enomoto 1990, Kwon 2011 — pro-proliferative effects of retinoids on chondrocytes.
