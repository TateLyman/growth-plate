# PMID 35241664 — Tig1 regulates proximo-distal identity during salamander limb regeneration

Oliveira CR, Knapp D, Elewa A, Gerber T, Gonzalez Malagon SG, Gates PB, Walters HE, Petzold A, Arce H, Cordoba RC, Subramanian E, Chara O, Tanaka EM, Simon A, Yun MH. *Nat Commun* 2022;13:1141. PMC8894484. doi:10.1038/s41467-022-28755-1

**What I read:** the complete PMC full text — abstract, Introduction, all six Results sections, the full Discussion, and the Methods front matter, with all figure legends read in place. Not an abstract read.

## Model
Axolotl (and some *Notophthalmus viridescens*) limb regeneration. Single-cell RNA-seq mining of connective-tissue cells; qRT-PCR along the mature limb; baculovirus transduction and focal electroporation of blastemas; a **Hoxa13:Hoxa13-T2a-mCherry CRISPR reporter line** to label cells already specified distal, FACS-sorted for bulk RNA-seq; the classical **blastema engulfment assay** with custom neutralising antibodies.

## What Tig1 is
**Tig1 = Rarres1 = Retinoic Acid Receptor Responder 1 = Tazarotene-Induced Gene 1.** A conserved, membrane-bound cell-surface protein with a transmembrane latexin domain (carboxypeptidase-inhibitor family) and a **hyaluronic-acid-binding motif**; structurally resembles CD38. **57% sequence similarity between axolotl and human TIG1**, with all significant human protein features conserved. Human TIG1 is a known RA-responsive putative tumour suppressor. Its promoter carries RXRA/B/G, RARA, RARG elements and **MEIS1/MEIS2 binding sites**.

Contrast with Prod1, the previous sole candidate: Prod1 is **salamander-specific** and in the axolotl **lacks a membrane anchor**. Tig1 is conserved and membrane-bound. That is why this paper matters for anything mammalian.

## Exact findings
- **Graded proximally in the mature limb**: highest in upper arm, decreasing to the hand (p = 0.0491, 0.0068; n = 6). Same in newt.
- **RA-inducible in vivo in both tissue states**: 48 h after RA (100 µg/g), Tig1 rose in the **regenerating limb (p = 0.0014)** *and* in the **mature limb (p = 0.0025)**. Induction is connective-tissue-restricted — seen in AmAL1 cells, not in the myogenic NvA1 line.
- Upregulated over the **first 7 days post-amputation**, "coinciding with the establishment of proximal identities," then strongly downregulated for the rest of regeneration; restricted to proximal blastema and adjacent stump.
- **Reprogramming, in cells already specified distal** (Hoxa13:mCherry+ sorted): ~5,000 genes changed (p < 0.001). **Prod1 significantly up; Meis1 trending up; Hoxa11 and Hoxa13 down**, plus a network of 11 Hoxa13 co-regulated/target genes down — including **Bmp2, Bmp7, Lhx9, Lmo1, Spry1, Lhx2** and **Fgf8**. Cdkn1B and Rb up. Adhesion/ECM: Tnc, Fn1, Col3A1, Col8A1, Mmp18, N-cadherin down; **Sdc3 (syndecan-3, an HSPG)**, Cdh1, Mmp13 up — and Mmp13 up is a known signature of proximalising RA doses.
- **Positive feedback**: Tig1 overexpression raised endogenous Tig1 5'UTR, which is not in the vector. Tig1 drives its own transcription.
- **Cell-surface recognition**: anti-TIG1 antibodies against two non-overlapping extracellular epitopes **fully abolished** proximal-engulfs-distal blastema engulfment (as anti-Prod1 does); anti-TMEFF1 and a 1:200 dilution did not.
- **Proximal displacement**: focal electroporation into the distal third of a 4-day blastema sent cells that would have made digits and carpals into the lower and upper arm. Tig1P155A (latexin domain mutant) was *stronger*, so the latexin domain is dispensable.
- **Anti-proliferative**, but that is not the mechanism of displacement: Tig1, Prod1 and p53 all cut EdU incorporation, yet **p53 did not displace cells proximally.** Suppressing the cell cycle is not sufficient.
- Expression ordering suggests **Tig1 acts upstream of Prod1**, and Tig1 is itself a likely Meis target — so the chain reads RA → Meis → Tig1 → Prod1, with feedback.

## The direction correction — important, and it cuts against my framing
**Tig1 overexpression from mid-bud onward SHORTENED the regenerated limb** (p = 0.0014): fused and shortened digits (5/21), uneven or fused radius/ulna (8/21), carpal and metacarpal malformations (11/21), with the **lower arm and hand significantly shortened and the upper arm unchanged** (p = 0.0014, 0.0004). Same phenotype as Prod1 overexpression.

So **proximalization is not "more length" as a general rule.** It means "you are further up the limb than you thought." Whether that yields more or less structure depends entirely on timing and on whether there is unbuilt structure distal to the new value:
- RA or talarozole on an **early distal blastema** → the field believes it is proximal → it intercalates an entire extra segment → **tandem duplication, more total length** ([40494878](notes/40494878.md), [31026439](notes/31026439.md)).
- Tig1 **late, from mid-bud**, when distal elements are being built → suppresses the distal growth programme (Fgf8, Lhx2, Bmp2/7) → **those elements fail, shorter limb**.

This is not a contradiction between papers; it is the same rule read at two timepoints. But it means **§18 cannot be stated as "proximalize and gain length."** The length comes from *intercalation across a positional gap*, not from the proximal value itself.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The length-generating rule is intercalation, not proximalization.** Apposed cells of disparate positional value proliferate and generate the intervening values. The engulfment assay and the displacement assay are both read-outs of that disparity being sensed at the cell surface. What RA does usefully is *create a positional gap inside an otherwise uniform field*. That reframes the target: not "raise the positional value," but "install a disparity and let the tissue fill it."
2. **The sensor is a druggable surface protein conserved in humans.** TIG1/RARRES1 is 57% identical, membrane-bound, RA-inducible, antibody-neutralisable, and self-amplifying. Unlike Prod1 it is not an amphibian curiosity.
3. **RA moves Tig1 in mature tissue.** This is the one result I have found that cracks "mature tissue is refractory." [31026439](notes/31026439.md) found Alx4 and Shh unchanged in mature limb tissue after RA; here Tig1 rose significantly in the *mature* limb. The refractoriness may be about executing a pattern change, not about hearing the signal.
4. **Adult mammalian cells do carry positional identity.** The authors state it plainly: "The existence of cell-autonomously encoded spatial information in adult cells has been well-documented in human fibroblasts, where it is **epigenetically maintained**" (their refs 35, 36 — the Rinn HOX-code work). This materially softens §18.6: the heparan-sulfate positional code in *matrix* dies by mouse PN9 ([27499874](notes/27499874.md)), but the positional record in *cells* persists into adult humans as a HOX signature. Lock 2 may be a reading problem, not an erasure.
5. **Stable proximal identity may be intrinsically anti-proliferative.** Tig1 and Prod1 both suppress proliferation; proximal-most late blastema regions proliferate least. If proximal identity and growth are antagonistic, then "make everything maximally proximal" is self-defeating, and the gain has to come from the gap, not the level — which is the same conclusion as (1).

## Levers
- **RA → Meis → Tig1 → Prod1**, with Tig1 self-amplifying. CYP26 inhibition enters this chain upstream ([40494878](notes/40494878.md)).
- **Creating positional disparity** rather than raising positional value — the actual length-generating manipulation.
- **RARRES1 expression state in adult human tissue.** Human TIG1 is a tumour suppressor that is **frequently silenced by promoter methylation** in adult tissues and cancers (background knowledge, not from this paper — flagged as needing a primary). If RARRES1 is methylated off in adult human connective tissue, that is a named epigenetic lock with existing demethylating agents pointed at it. **This is a prediction, untested.**

## Anti-levers
- **Tig1 (or Prod1) overexpression at or after mid-bud** — shortens the limb by suppressing Fgf8, Lhx2 and Bmp2/7.
- Anti-TIG1 antibodies — abolish positional-disparity sensing entirely.
- Uniform proximalization of a whole field — no gap, no intercalation, and an anti-proliferative signature.

## Honest limits
1. Axolotl and newt; the human claim is sequence conservation plus a citation to the fibroblast HOX literature. **No mammalian functional experiment.**
2. Overexpression and antibody neutralisation only — **no loss-of-function genetics** for Tig1.
3. Tig1P155A behaved *more* strongly than wild type, so the latexin domain — the feature the authors expected to matter — is dispensable, and the actual mechanism of action is unknown. They speculate microtubule dynamics.
4. "Whether the prevention of distalisation is sufficient to proximalise cell identity is an important outstanding question" — their words. The reprogramming may be Hoxa13 suppression rather than positive proximal specification.
5. Whether Prod1 and Tig1 act in series or in parallel is unresolved; combining them gave a different result from either alone, and dose effects are not excluded.
6. Meis1 upregulation did not reach significance (high replicate variability), so the RA → Meis → Tig1 → Prod1 ordering is inferred from promoter motifs plus trends, not established.
7. How a surface protein produces ~5,000 gene changes is unknown.

## Chase list
- **Rinn JL et al. 2006 and 2008 — the HOX positional code in adult human fibroblasts, epigenetically maintained.** Highest priority from this paper: it is the only evidence that adult human cells retain positional identity, and it decides whether §18 has a mammalian substrate at all. Also cited by [27499874](notes/27499874.md).
- **Prod1 primaries** (da Silva 2002; Kumar 2007 nAG; Oliveira 2017) — in particular the **Prod1–nAG interaction**, which is the documented link between positional *level* and growth *permission*: nAG is a Schwann-cell-derived mitogen for blastema cells. This is the coupling between "how much should exist" and "build it."
- **Human RARRES1 promoter methylation status in normal adult connective tissue, cartilage and perichondrium.** If silenced, there is an epigenetic lock with existing drugs.
- Mercader/Meis requirement for RA proximalization (their ref 14).
- Whether anything is known about **TIG1/RARRES1 in human cartilage or growth plate**.
