# PMID 17968490 — Zhang P, Tanaka SM, Sun Q, Turner CH, Yokota H. Frequency-dependent enhancement of bone formation in murine tibiae and femora with knee loading (2007, J Bone Miner Metab)

Read in full (NIH manuscript PDF, 17 pp including tables).

**Model.** Female C57BL/6 mice, ~14 weeks old.
- Knee loading: 0.5 N, 5/10/15 Hz, 3 min/day for 3 days. Sham contralateral.
- Bone formation measured 75% of the length away from the knee (tibia ~12 mm from proximal end; femur ~12 mm from distal end).

**Results.**
- Tibia, best at 5 Hz:
  - Periosteal MS/BS 11.2 → 21.8%, MAR 0.20 → 0.27 µm/d, BFR 8.1 → 21.6 µm³/µm²/yr (×2.7).
  - Cortical area 0.56 → 0.63 mm²; cortical thickness 0.223 → 0.252 mm.
  - Endosteum also responded at 5 Hz.
- Femur, best at 15 Hz: periosteal BFR 8.6 → 15.5 (×1.8); cortical area 0.75 → 0.84 mm².
- Tibia is more porous (1.79% vs 1.18%) with lower BMD. The proposed explanation is that the poroelastic properties set the optimal frequency.

**What it establishes.**
- Effects reach far from the loading site (75% of bone length). The mechanism is likely hydraulic (IMP plus interstitial flow).
- Optimal frequency depends on the bone's poroelastic microstructure. Tuning is bone-specific.

**Levers for the theory.**
- Epiphyseal joint loading is a validated, non-invasive way to drive anabolic activity along a whole bone, but its products are *periosteal width* gains.
- For length, the signal must reach a cartilage-forming compartment at the bone end. A hydraulic stimulus by itself makes bone, not cartilage.
- Useful as a frequency-tuned *driver* component in a multi-part mechanism (e.g., to boost vascular/progenitor recruitment or perfusion near an engineered endochondral front).
