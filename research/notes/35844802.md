# 35844802 — Mechanosensitive Piezo1 is crucial for periosteal stem cell-mediated fracture healing

**Citation.** Int J Biol Sci 2022;18(10):3961–3980. PMC9274506. Read in full (Results §1–8, Discussion).

## Model / method
Mouse femoral fracture. scRNA-seq of callus (15 clusters). **Gli1-CreER;tdTomato** fate mapping of periosteal stem cells (PSCs). Intra-callus lentiviral Piezo1 knockdown. **Yoda1** (specific Piezo1 channel agonist) by injection. µCT, three-point bending, IHC, calcium imaging, transwell migration, osteogenic/chondrogenic differentiation, Co-IP, verteporfin (YAP inhibitor) rescue. Final arm: **tail-suspension delayed-union model** (mechanical unloading) ± Yoda1.

## Exact numbers
- **Piezo1 is enriched in PSCs and their derived osteoblastic lineage cells and chondrocytes** (scRNA-seq), and is upregulated during healing. Highly expressed at the plasma membrane; Yoda1 produces rapid Ca²⁺ influx.
- Piezo1 knockdown in callus → impaired healing. **Yoda1 → accelerated healing**: fracture gap indistinct at 14 dpf, line nearly invisible at 28 dpf; **BV, BV/TV and BMD all significantly raised at 7, 14, 21 and 28 dpf**; tissue volume declining by 21 dpf (faster remodelling).
- Histology: more cartilage in the callus at 7 dpf, then **cartilage almost gone by 14 dpf while still abundant in controls** — i.e. accelerated cartilage-to-bone conversion. **Total osseous tissue +42% at day 7 and +51% at day 14.** Lamellar cortical bone by 28 dpf vs still-woven bone in vehicle.
- **Maximum bending load 1.5-fold higher than control at 28 dpf.**
- Yoda1 raised Piezo1 expression in callus at 7 and 21 dpf (positive feedback).
- **No proliferation effect.** Ki-67+ cells unchanged in vivo; Yoda1 *reduced* PSC proliferation dose-dependently in vitro. What it increased was **migration** (transwell, dose-dependent) of Gli1-tdTomato+ PSCs into the callus.
- Differentiation: osteogenic markers Alp, Runx2, Osterix up; Alizarin Red nodules up; OCN+ cells up in callus at 14 and 21 dpf. **Chondrogenic markers Acan, Col2a1, Sox9 also up**, with **more GAG and more Col2a1 in pellets but no change in pellet size**. **BMP2 induced dose-dependently** at 24 h.
- Mechanism: Piezo1 activation → **YAP expression and nuclear localisation up → β-catenin expression and nuclear localisation up → nuclear YAP/β-catenin complex (Co-IP) → BMP2 and VEGFA**. Angiogenesis promoted indirectly via PSC-derived VEGFA.
- **Tail-suspension (unloading) delayed union**: reduced callus BV/TV/BMD, **47% lower peak load at 20 d**, more retained cartilage at 10 dpf, far fewer Gli1-Td+ cells, fewer OCN+ cells, fewer vessels, and **reduced Piezo1 protein in callus at 20 d**. **Yoda1 rescued all of it** — BV, BV/TV, BMD, Gli1-Td+ recruitment, OCN+ cells, vessel number, and the Piezo1 decline itself.
- Safety in this model: no change in body weight, no histological abnormality in heart, liver, spleen, lung or kidney, normal liver and kidney function.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
**This is the most important compound-level result in the whole project, because it is the one place where a small molecule substitutes for mechanical load in exactly the cell population my mechanical theory depends on.**

1. **Yoda1 chemically replaces mechanical loading.** The tail-suspension arm is the decisive experiment: remove load, healing fails and Piezo1 protein falls; add Yoda1, and bone volume, mineral density, progenitor recruitment, osteocalcin and vascularity all return. The authors state it as "chemical activation of the Piezo1 channel mimics mechanical stimuli." Everything PCAC asks a loading rig to do to a periarticular progenitor, this molecule does to the same progenitor from the inside.
2. **The target cell is Gli1+ periosteal stem cells** — the same population that forms cartilage at joint margins and stays cartilage only while movement continues ([21600197](21600197.md)), and the same lineage that makes de novo endochondral bone after injury in adults ([28645539](28645539.md)). Piezo1 is enriched in them and in their chondrocyte progeny, not merely in marrow MSCs (the authors make this distinction explicitly).
3. **It drives the step my theory is weakest on.** THEORY.md §6 names Phase D — converting thickened cartilage into longer bone — as the weak link, and notes that knee joint distraction thickened cartilage with no demonstrated bone-front advance ([21565898](21565898.md)). Yoda1 accelerates precisely that conversion: more cartilage at day 7, cartilage gone by day 14, osseous tissue +51%, mechanically stronger bone. It is a conversion-front accelerator.
4. **It supplies the two factors the front needs, from the resident cell: BMP2 and VEGFA.** BMP2 + injury is sufficient for adult de novo endochondral ossification ([28629737](28629737.md)); VEGFA is what lets the vascular front advance. Yoda1 induces both endogenously and locally, rather than requiring an exogenous growth factor implant.
5. **And again — it does not increase cell number.** Ki-67 unchanged in vivo, proliferation reduced in vitro, pellet size unchanged. It increases migration and throughput. So on its own it would consume a progenitor pool faster, not enlarge it. Combined with the identical finding for T3, OSK and dasatinib, the pattern is now unambiguous: **no existing compound in this literature enlarges the adult chondroprogenitor pool. They redistribute, re-specify or accelerate it.** A centimetre-scale scheme therefore needs a cell-supply solution (architecture, implanted progenitors, or niche expansion) that no compound provides.
6. **YAP again, opposite sign.** Piezo1 → YAP up → conversion to bone. Dasatinib → YAP down → cartilage maintained ([39402753](39402753.md)). FOP progenitors → YAP up → ectopic bone, and *Yap* deletion abolishes it ([38672135](38672135.md)). The YAP switch is now confirmed from four independent directions, with existing small molecules on both sides and verteporfin used as the blocker in this very paper. If anything in this corpus is the control variable for a staged cartilage→bone program, it is this.

## Levers
- **Yoda1** (Piezo1 agonist) — accelerates PSC migration, chondrogenesis, and cartilage-to-bone conversion; induces BMP2 and VEGFA; rescues unloading-induced failure. Existing research chemical, no clinical programme.
- **Sirt1 activators as an oral route to the same channel**: [40999873](40999873.md) shows Sirt1 binds, deacetylates and activates Piezo1 in chondrocytes; **SRT2104** and **resveratrol** both activate it, and Piezo1 loss in chondrocytes abolishes SRT2104's effect. Piezo1 deletion *in chondrocytes* largely impairs endochondral ossification and mechanically induced osteogenesis.
- **Gli1+ periosteal stem cells** as the addressable resident population.
- **YAP/β-catenin** as the transducer; **verteporfin** as the off-switch used here.
- **Piezo1 positive feedback** (Yoda1 raises Piezo1; unloading lowers it) — relevant to whether an effect would be self-sustaining or need continuous dosing.

## Honest limits
- Mouse, fracture. **A fracture is an injury with a pre-existing callus — a cartilage template already in place.** Nothing here shows Yoda1 creates a growth cartilage where none exists, which is the actual post-fusion problem. It accelerates a template the injury supplied.
- **Length was never measured.** The outcomes are bone volume, mineral density and bending strength — healing quality, not dimension. It is entirely possible for Yoda1 to accelerate conversion and produce no net length at all.
- Yoda1 has poor solubility and a short half-life, is given by local/intraperitoneal injection in rodents, and has no clinical development programme. Piezo1 is expressed in red cells, endothelium, lung, bladder, pancreas and baroreceptors; systemic Piezo1 agonism is not a benign proposition and this study's safety panel (28 days, mouse, 5 organs) does not address it.
- Resveratrol's oral bioavailability is poor enough that the companion paper had to build a yeast-microcapsule formulation; resveratrol also has a long history of effects that do not replicate.
- Accelerating cartilage-to-bone conversion at a joint margin is the mechanism of osteophyte formation. The same action that is useful at a fracture is the pathology at an articulation.
