# PMID 19619334 — Condylar growth after non-surgical advancement in adult subject: a case report (2009, Head Face Med)

Read in full (PMC, ~11k chars).

**Case.** 30-year-old man with temporomandibular disorder (TMD), bruxism and left condylar resorption on panoramic X-ray. Treatment: hard maxillary splint, 3 mm thick, with an inclined plane advancing the mandible 2.5 mm; worn consistently except mornings.
- 8 months: denser cortical layer on the left condyle; mouth opening 20→30 mm; pain VAS 80→20.
- 18 months: "new growth in the left condyle", morphology similar to the right side, symptoms resolved.
- Measured only on panoramic radiographs (low quality, no quantification).

**Background cited.**
- Mandibular condylar cartilage is fibrocartilage with a **pre-chondroblastic mesenchymal stem-cell layer** that rapidly differentiates into chondrocytes. Hyaline articular cartilage lacks this layer.
- Adult rhesus monkeys (McNamara): advancement causes adaptive condylar cartilage changes within 3 weeks; cartilage dimensions keep increasing, whereas untreated controls had a bony outer layer.
- Bone deposition occurs on the posterior and superior condylar surfaces.

**What it establishes.**
- Anecdotal human adult evidence that sustained altered joint loading (repositioning) can regenerate and grow bone at an adult secondary-cartilage articular surface.
- Low evidential weight: a single case, panoramic films, regrowth after resorption rather than true supra-normal growth.

**Levers.**
- In adult humans, a skeletal surface covered by a progenitor-bearing fibrocartilage (condyle) remains mechanoresponsive with endochondral output.
- Limb hyaline articular cartilage lacks that progenitor layer (per the authors). That makes the condyle a *privileged* adult growth site, and it explains why mechanics can grow the jaw but not the femur.
- A key question for a limb-bone theory: can a condyle-like progenitor surface be recreated (e.g., in the perichondrial/periosteal zone or the articular superficial zone)?

**Chase.** McNamara JA et al. 2003 (adult rhesus monkey advancement, Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop); Ruf & Pancherz; Rabie adult rat papers.
