# 42650460 — From FGFR3 hyperactivation to disease-modifying therapy in pediatric achondroplasia

**Citation.** Children (Basel) 2026. Narrative review. Read in full (§3 genetics, §4 pathophysiology, §5 therapeutics, §6–7).

## Why it is here
This is the complete current map of **every drug that has ever been shown to increase human height**. Its value to this project is almost entirely in one sentence and one pattern: the sentence is that these agents "can act only while functional growth plate cartilage is still present"; the pattern is that the best of them buys 1.5–1.8 cm/year *in a child with open plates*, and all of them are indicated, labelled or stopped on exactly that condition.

## The landscape, with numbers
**Approved**
- **Vosoritide** (modified CNP analogue, daily s.c.). Mechanism: CNP/NPR-B → cGMP → PKG2 → inhibition of FGFR3-MAPK/ERK. Phase 3: **+1.57 cm/year** vs placebo; ~**3.5 cm at 2 years**; benefit sustained to 7 years in phase 2 cohorts without accelerating skeletal maturation. Requires **documented open growth plates**; Australian guidance continues it only while there is no physeal closure, judged radiographically or by annual growth velocity >1.5 cm/year, and **stops it at documented epiphyseal closure**. AEs mainly injection-site reactions and mild hypotension. Effect on **final adult height still unproven**.
- **Navepegritide** (TransCon CNP, weekly s.c.). APPROACH trial, 84 children aged 2–11, 52 wk: **+1.49 cm/year** vs placebo, plus improved tibial–femoral angle, mechanical axis deviation and fibula:tibia ratio. **FDA accelerated approval February 2026 as Yuviwel**, for children ≥2 years with achondroplasia **and open epiphyses**; confirmation of final-height benefit still required.

**Late-stage**
- **Infigratinib** (oral FGFR1–3 TKI). PROPEL 3, 114 children aged 3–17, 52 wk: **+1.74 cm/year**; height z-score improved; segment ratio not significant; AE rates similar to placebo. Still investigational.
- **Dabogratinib (TYRA-300)**, oral FGFR3-selective, phase 2 BEACH301 (NCT06842355), children 3–10 with **open growth plates**. No efficacy data.
- **Umedaptanib pegol (RBM-007)**, anti-FGF2 RNA aptamer; phase 3 began recruiting 2026; no peer-reviewed phase 3 data.
- **BMN 333**, long-acting CNP, weekly, head-to-head vs daily vosoritide in ASPEN (NCT07441876); dose-finding update expected 2027.

**Combination**
- **COACH** phase 2, 21 children aged 2–11: navepegritide + lonapegsomatropin (TransCon hGH) weekly. **8.69 cm/year vs 5.95 cm/year** on navepegritide monotherapy — a difference of **+2.74 cm/year (p < 0.0001)**. The largest effect in the entire field, and it is two drugs on open plates in small children.

**Failed or negative**
- **Recifercept** (soluble FGFR3 decoy): paediatric programme **terminated for lack of efficacy at every dose tested**, not for safety. The review draws the right lesson: "the importance of distinguishing biological plausibility from clinically validated therapeutic benefit."
- **Meclizine** (repurposed, attenuates FGFR3–RAS–ERK): 26-wk open-label single-arm, 9 children, on concurrent GH. Height velocity **4.35 ± 1.36 → 4.46 ± 1.54 cm/year**. No efficacy.
- **Statins**: inconsistent; some studies show no FGFR inhibition in chondrocytes at all.

**Preclinical / other targets**
- **DKK1 inhibition with WAY262611** in *Fgfr3*^Y367C/+ mice: raised nuclear β-catenin, improved mandibular growth ex vivo, **increased condylar cartilage volume**, partially corrected hypertrophic differentiation and proliferation. Framed as craniofacial-specific.
- **(−)-Epicatechin**: improved bone elongation and **primary cilium defects** in mice.
- **Teriparatide**: partial improvement in animal models.
- **CRISPR-Cas9** correction and **cartilage-specific enhancer (−29E) targeting**; **SIS-102-ACH**, a systemic Bio-Courier siRNA selectively silencing mutant *FGFR3*. No clinical data.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The entire pharmacological armamentarium for height is plate-dependent by construction.** Two approved drugs, both labelled for open epiphyses; one stopped at documented closure; the phase 2/3 pipeline enrolling children with open plates as an inclusion criterion. Nobody in this field is attempting post-fusion growth, and the regulatory language makes the reason explicit. This is the clinical counterpart of the acromegaly finding ([22518118](22518118.md)): the drive exists, the substrate does not.
2. **The ceiling of pure pharmacological drive on an existing plate is ~1.5–1.8 cm/year, ~2.7 cm/year for two drugs combined.** That is the honest benchmark. It is achieved on the most responsive possible substrate — a child's open physis with pathologically suppressed proliferation that the drug de-suppresses. An adult has neither the plate nor the suppression to relieve.
3. **Recifercept is the single most useful negative result in the field.** A mechanistically impeccable ligand trap, strong preclinical support, and zero clinical effect at any dose. Any post-fusion proposal has to be read against it.
4. **Meclizine is the repurposing cautionary tale.** An existing, cheap, safe oral drug acting on the right pathway node produced 0.11 cm/year. Being on the correct pathway is not the same as being on the correct variable.
5. **The DKK1/WAY262611 result is the one entry in this review that is not plate-dependent** — it raised **condylar cartilage volume**, in the mandibular condyle, the one secondary cartilage that never fuses and that [THEORY.md](../THEORY.md) §2 builds on. Worth separating from the rest of the list for exactly that reason.
6. **(−)-Epicatechin's primary-cilium effect** connects to the mechanotransduction arm; cilia are the other candidate mechanosensor alongside Piezo1.

## Levers
- **CNP/NPR-B → cGMP → PKG2** as the validated anti-FGFR3 brake. Relevant only where a plate exists.
- **DKK1 inhibition (WAY262611)** → nuclear β-catenin → **increased condylar cartilage volume**. The only volume-increasing cartilage result in the review, in the only never-fusing growth cartilage. Highest-value item here.
- **Primary cilia** ((−)-epicatechin) as a second mechanosensory node.
- **Anti-lever: FGFR3 inhibition in an adult.** It relieves a pathological brake. There is no such brake to relieve in a normal closed skeleton, and the FGFR1/FGFR3 ratio literature ([39402753](39402753.md)) shows FGFR signalling has opposite signs in articular vs growth-plate chondrocytes.
- **Anti-lever: GH/IGF-1 as drive.** COACH shows GH adds 2.74 cm/year *on top of* a CNP drug *in children with open plates*. In adults the same axis produces acromegaly, which lengthens only the mandible ([22518118](22518118.md)).

## Honest limits
- A narrative review; every number is secondary. Several programmes (BMN 333, umedaptanib, dabogratinib, SIS-102-ACH) have no published efficacy data at all.
- No final-adult-height data for any agent, including both approved ones — the regulators have accepted growth-velocity surrogates.
- Entirely paediatric, entirely achondroplasia. Nothing in it concerns normal-stature individuals, adults, or closed plates.
- Small samples in the interesting arms (COACH n = 21, meclizine n = 9).
