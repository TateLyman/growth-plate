# PMID 36309308 — AcanCreER lacks specificity to chondrocytes and targets periosteal progenitors in the fractured callus (2023, Bone)

Read in full (PMC, ~21k chars).

**Model.** **Adult** mice (AcanCreER;Ai9). Pin-stabilised ("semi-stabilised") femur fracture; tibial-hole (intramembranous) injury.

**Findings.**
- Acan-traced cells are present in intact adult periosteum.
- After fracture, periosteal fibroblastic Acan+ CD90+ cells: 1.2% of CD45−Ter119− at d3, 9.33% at d5. They give rise to callus chondrocytes and fibroblastic cells.
- Labelled at d5 → 63±17% of Osx+ callus cells at d7 and 70±15% of Ocn+ osteoblasts at d12 are Acan-lineage.
- In tibial holes (no cartilage), Acan+ cells make osteoblasts directly.
- Periosteal SSPCs: cells near the fracture site differentiate into chondrocytes, distal ones into osteoblasts. Cartilage "is important for initial bridging and helps stabilize a fractured bone".

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Adult periosteum contains progenitors that readily make cartilage when the mechanical/biological situation demands it (fracture with motion).
- Cartilage forms where there is movement, near the gap. Bone forms where it is stable (distal), consistent with mechanobiology (Carter).

**Levers.**
- Adult periosteal progenitors are chondrocompetent.
- The decision cartilage vs bone is **mechanically biased**: interfragmentary motion/strain plus hypoxia gives cartilage; stability gives bone.
- A mechanical regime that mimics callus conditions *without fracture* might coax periosteal progenitors to chondrogenesis. Hard part: doing so in a polarised, longitudinal configuration.
