# 24485503 — Aromatase and estrogen receptor alpha deficiency

**Citation.** Fertil Steril 2014;101(2):317–323. PMC3939057. Review of the human aromatase-deficiency and estrogen-resistance literature. Read in full (all sections incl. animal models, clinical presentation, summary, management).

## Why it is here
**This is the strongest human evidence in the whole project that a growth plate can remain functional and actively lengthening bone into the fourth decade of life** — and that the thing which closes it is a single, pharmacologically accessible pathway rather than an inevitable cell-intrinsic countdown.

## Exact numbers and clinical facts
**The men.** Two **46,XY men with homozygous CYP19A1 (aromatase) missense mutations**, both with normal male sexual differentiation and unremarkable puberty, and heights "regarded as normal at adolescence":
- First evaluated at **24 and 38 years of age**, at **204 cm and 190 cm**.
- **"The linear growth continued into adulthood."**
- **"Diffuse bone demineralization and lack of epiphyseal fusion were detected by x-ray films in the knees and pelvis."**
- Severely retarded bone ages; eunuchoid body proportions.
- Markedly elevated LH, FSH and testosterone. Raised fasting insulin, raised total and LDL cholesterol, low HDL, with normal glucose. Estrogen administration lowered insulin and **raised bone density**.
- Both mutations lay in a highly conserved region thought to guard the substrate access channel; a third such mutation was found in a 38-year-old Italian man.

**The estrogen-resistant man.** One man with a **homozygous ESR1 (ERα) point mutation** — the 1994 case that first established estrogen as necessary for epiphyseal fusion in men — presents the same way: tall stature, eunuchoid proportions, continued linear growth, incomplete epiphyseal closure, osteoporosis. His insulin resistance and glucose intolerance **did not respond to very high doses of estrogen**, as expected for receptor loss. Normal sperm count with low motility. A second estrogen-resistance case (Quaynor 2013) was an 18-year-old girl.

**The women — the control that matters.** Aromatase-deficient 46,XX girls grew and developed normally through infancy and childhood, then at puberty **had no growth spurt** and no breast development, with bone age **8 months to 4 years behind** chronological age. Two were started on oral estrogen at pubertal onset: **breast development and a growth spurt followed, and both reached normal final height.** The authors' own inference: *"It may be predicted that linear bone growth would have continued in the absence of estrogen replacement, as is the case with the aromatase-deficient men."*

**In vitro:** transient expression of the mutant aromatase cDNAs gave only trace activity. Milder "nonclassic" alleles exist (Lin 2007: four 46,XX patients with residual activity and variable phenotypes), so the pathway is dose-graded, not all-or-none.

**Tissue specificity.** CYP19A1 is >123 kb and its expression is driven by **tissue-specific promoters with alternative splicing**, in ovary, testis, placenta, adipose, skin and brain. The authors note explicitly: *"mutations in any of these promoter regions or splice-junctions may result in loss of estrogen formation in one organ only… Analysis of such cases would provide insight into the relative role of estrogens produced in each of these sites."*

**Mouse models.** ArKO and αERKO reproduce the reproductive phenotypes. αERKO males infertile via failed epididymal fluid reabsorption; ArKO males initially fertile then progressively infertile with spermiogenic arrest from 4.5 months and Leydig hyperplasia.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The closure clock is estrogen-paced, not division-counted.** [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) rests on *n* (cell production) being "frozen" in adults, and my corpus attributes growth-plate senescence to progenitor exhaustion ([39563452](39563452.md), [19098525](19098525.md)). **These men were still growing at 38 with open epiphyses visible on plain radiographs of knee and pelvis.** Their resting-zone pools had not exhausted on any intrinsic schedule. So exhaustion is not an inevitable countdown that runs regardless — it is driven by, or at minimum gated by, ERα signalling. That materially strengthens the case that *n* is recoverable rather than spent.
2. **It is the best available human upper bound on what "keeping the plate open" is worth.** 204 cm at 24 and still growing. Not millimetres. This is the magnitude the user has been asking for, achieved in humans, by the absence of one enzyme.
3. **And it is the wrong lever for the actual problem, for one specific reason.** These men never closed. **Nothing here shows a closed plate reopening.** Aromatase inhibitors delay an event; they do not reverse one, and the entire clinical literature on them is paediatric with open plates. The relevance to ACTIVATION.md is therefore not "block estrogen" — it is the mechanistic point in (1), plus the cost accounting in (4).
4. **The price of systemic estrogen absence is specific and severe, and it is not hypothetical.** Diffuse demineralisation and osteoporosis, insulin resistance with an adverse lipid profile, reduced libido, probable fertility consequences, and in the women anovulation and large ovarian cysts. Aromatase inhibition in *normal* men lowers HDL. **Any scheme that contemplates reducing estrogen action in an adult for skeletal purposes is buying osteoporosis and metabolic syndrome, in a skeleton that has no open plate to benefit.** Bone density rose when these men were given estrogen back.
5. **The one genuinely new lever here is tissue-restricted aromatase.** Because CYP19A1 runs off tissue-specific promoters, local estrogen production in bone and cartilage is in principle separable from gonadal, adipose and brain production. The authors flag promoter-specific mutations as the natural experiment that would prove it. **If the closure clock is driven by locally synthesised estrogen acting on the plate's own ERα, then a promoter- or tissue-restricted intervention would decouple the skeletal effect from the systemic cost** — which is exactly the spatial-control problem that [COMPOUNDS.md](../COMPOUNDS.md) says sinks every systemic idea, solved by transcriptional geography rather than by delivery.
6. **The dose-graded "nonclassic" alleles matter for feasibility.** Partial loss of function gives partial phenotypes. So this is a rheostat, not a switch, and the relevant question becomes how much skeletal ERα signalling can be reduced before the osteoporosis cost exceeds the benefit — a question nobody appears to have asked in an adult.

## Levers
- **ERα signalling in the growth plate as the closure clock**, with human loss-of-function proof that removing it keeps plates open and bones lengthening into the fourth decade.
- **Tissue-specific CYP19A1 promoters** — the route to a skeletally-restricted intervention, named by the authors as untested.
- **Dose-graded alleles** — the pathway is a rheostat.
- **Anti-lever: systemic estrogen suppression in an adult.** Osteoporosis, insulin resistance, dyslipidaemia, low libido, and no open plate to act on. Estrogen replacement reversed the bone and insulin findings, which is the clearest statement of what the deficiency was costing.

## Honest limits
- **A review of a handful of cases.** Two aromatase-deficient men, one estrogen-resistant man, a small number of girls and women. No cohort, no controls, no systematic growth-velocity data — "continued linear growth into adulthood" is a clinical description, not a measured cm/year.
- **Open plates, never closed ones.** The entire phenotype is *failure to fuse*. There is no evidence here, or anywhere I have read, of a fused human plate reopening.
- Confounded by lifelong hypogonadotropic feedback disturbance, markedly raised testosterone and LH/FSH, and in one man a possible familial sperm disorder.
- Final heights in the treated women were normal, so their counterfactual (what would have happened untreated) is the authors' inference, not an observation.
- Nothing about articular cartilage, the tidemark, or the appositional route that ACTIVATION.md is built on. Its contribution is to the *n* argument and to the cost side, not to the mechanism.

## Chase
- **Smith et al. 1994 (NEJM) — the original estrogen-resistant man with the ESR1 mutation.** The founding case. Priority.
- **Morishima 1995 and Carani 1997 — the two aromatase-deficient men in full**, with whatever serial height and bone-age data exist. These are the only human records of adult linear growth; the growth *rate* is the number I want and this review does not give it.
- Quaynor 2013 — the second estrogen-resistance case.
- Lin 2007 — the four "nonclassic" aromatase alleles with residual activity.
- Ref 41 — estrogen administration raising bone density in aromatase-deficient men.
- Refs 25, 26 — the tissue-specific CYP19A1 promoter architecture. **Needed for the tissue-restricted lever.**
- 20164294 — "Tall stature without growth hormone: four male patients with aromatase deficiency" (J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2010). **Four more cases, and the title implies the growth is GH-independent.** Not open-access; on the needed list.
- 19707181 — "Aromatase deficiency in men: a clinical perspective" (Nat Rev Endocrinol 2009). On the needed list.
- Any report of **serial stature measurements** in an untreated aromatase-deficient or estrogen-resistant adult. That single number would set the ceiling for the whole closure-delay argument.
