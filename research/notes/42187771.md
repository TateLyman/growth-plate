# PMID 42187771 — Microscopic and geometric changes in the mandibular condylar head in response to subtle secondary overload: in search of a mechanical origin of condylar hyperplasia (2026, Biology Basel)

Read in full (PMC, ~33k chars).

**Model.** BALB/c mice, 8 weeks old ("adult", though still young).
- Right masseter paralysed with botulinum toxin (BoNTA). The left side receives a secondary "functional" overload (OL); the paralysed side is underloaded (UL). Untreated controls have symmetric load (SL).
- Harvested at 14 days. Small samples (~5–13 hemimandibles per group).

**Results.**
- Overloaded condyles trend toward thicker total and hypertrophic/hyperplastic (HPT) cartilage, especially centrally, with chondrocytes more vertically aligned.
- Geometry shifts are sexually dimorphic: males get broader and slightly taller; females get flatter.
- Trabecular bone: higher BV/TV and Tb.Th, lower connectivity (significant only for BV, BV/TV and Conn.D in males).
- **Most cartilage differences are not statistically significant**; inter-individual variation is large.
- Underloaded condyles have thinner cartilage and a more gracile bone structure.

**Key background claims.**
- Human condylar hyperplasia (CH) is non-neoplastic excess condylar growth; the "active" form has a thick, actively growing hypertrophic cartilage. It occurs in adults too (more often in the young), and mostly in women and on the preferred (right) chewing side.
- The authors propose CH is an *adaptive growth response to a mechanically more demanding environment* that stops once a stable mechanical state is reached.
- Mild changes in TMJ dynamics are adaptive; large changes are degenerative (Betti systematic review).
- Rabbits on a hard diet for 15 weeks had larger condyles with thicker HPT cartilage, but degenerative signs appeared with time (Ravosa).

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Human condylar hyperplasia is a natural, non-surgical example of **pathological but organised continued endochondral growth of an adult bone end**, plausibly driven by mechanical asymmetry. It is evidence that adult human bone ends with a persistent growth cartilage can be pushed into supra-normal elongation.
- The proposed rule is that growth stops when mechanical equilibrium is reached. That implies a **mechanostat-like set point**: growth reaches a target geometry and stops when loading normalises.
- So sustained growth would need a stimulus that keeps the tissue away from equilibrium (a ratchet).
- "Subtle" overload is adaptive; excessive overload is degenerative. The dosing window matters.

**Levers.**
- Loading asymmetry or overload of moderate magnitude on a progenitor-covered secondary cartilage gives thicker HPT cartilage and vertical column alignment.
- Avoid supraphysiological load (degeneration).

**Chase.** Betti systematic review (TMJ adaptive vs degenerative); Ravosa 2007 (hard diet rabbits); Balanta-Melo 2019 (same model); a human CH histopathology review (refs 1–3).
