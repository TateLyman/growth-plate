# PMID 31830758 — Chagin AS, Newton PT. Postnatal skeletal growth is driven by the epiphyseal stem cell niche: potential implications to pediatrics (2020, Pediatr Res) — review

Read in full (PMC, ~16k chars).

**Core claims.**
- Neonatal growth *consumes* chondro-progenitors. Once the secondary ossification centre (SOC) matures into a bony epiphysis, progenitors *self-renew* in an "epiphyseal stem cell niche" (Newton 2019; Mizuhashi 2018).
- The SOC's allocation of the cartilage into a thin plate "is needed to protect growth plate chondrocytes from mechanical demands associated with weight bearing" (ref 2: Xie et al.).
- **Elongation is directly related to hypertrophic chondrocyte size** (ref 9).
- Niche signals possibly include Shh from cells in the bony epiphysis.
  - Hh activation promotes niche proliferation; given before niche formation it blocks column formation.
  - **Hh inhibition abrogates stem-cell division and causes growth-plate fusion.**
  - Hypothesis: fusion = niche disruption, not total progenitor consumption. Fusion is abrupt and cell-death-free, consistent with bone rapidly eroding a plate that stops making chondrocytes.
- Absent estrogen or ERα, humans don't fuse and growth is "virtually unlimited".
- GH's growth effect appears at 2–3 weeks in mice, coinciding with niche formation, so GH may act on niche stem cells. mTORC1 activation drives symmetric stem divisions, increasing their number.
- Evolution: animals without SOCs (crocodilians, urodeles, teleosts) have fully cartilaginous epiphyses and *indeterminate growth* from a large progenitor pool. The SOC is an adaptation to land (load). Rodents and elephants also grow indeterminately despite SOCs.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Conceptually central. Growth capacity sits in a *niche*, a microenvironment that includes mechanical shielding by a bony cap, Hh from the epiphysis, and ECM.
- Fusion can follow loss of niche function even if progenitor-type cells remain.
- That suggests: if a niche-equivalent environment could be re-established in adult bone, local progenitors (periosteal/marrow SSCs) might be coaxed into a self-renewing chondro-progenitor state.

**Levers.**
1. **Mechanical shielding.** The niche needs protection from direct weight-bearing deformation. A growth zone must sit under a stiff cap, with loads transmitted around it or as hydrostatic pressure, not shear.
2. **Hh tone** from adjacent bone.
3. **Hypertrophic cell size** as the elongation actuator.
4. Indeterminate-growth animals keep a cartilage epiphysis. Persistence of an un-ossified cartilage domain is evolutionarily tied to continued growth.
