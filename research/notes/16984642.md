# PMID 16984642 — Zhang P, Su M, Tanaka SM, Yokota H. Knee loading stimulates cortical bone formation in murine femurs (2006, BMC Musculoskelet Disord)

Read in full (PMC, ~27k chars).

**Model.** Female C57BL/6 mice, ~14 weeks old (16 at loading).
- Lateral knee loading: 0.5 N peak-to-peak, 5/10/15/20 Hz, 3 min/day for 3 days. The contralateral knee was placed under the loader without oscillation (sham).
- Calcein on days 2 and 6 after the last loading; harvested at 13 days. Femoral mid-diaphysis.

**Results.**
- Strain at the mid-diaphysis was only ~12 µε (8–19), the same at every frequency. That is ~1/10 of the predicted osteogenic threshold.
- Periosteal bone formation:
  - 15 Hz: MS/BS ×1.7, MAR ×1.4, BFR ×2.4.
  - 10 Hz: ×1.4, ×1.3, ×1.8.
  - 5 and 20 Hz: not significant.
- Cortical area at 15 Hz: 0.66 → 0.74 mm² (+12%). Cortical thickness 0.188 → 0.213 mm (+13%).
- No significant endosteal effect. Tibia more porous than femur (different optimal frequency).
- Proposed mechanism: oscillatory **intramedullary pressure** modulation from lateral squeezing of the compliant trabecular epiphysis, which creates pressure gradients and interstitial flow.

**What it establishes.**
- Frequency-tuned, low-force epiphyseal loading is a strong, non-invasive anabolic signal transmitted *hydraulically* through the bone.
- **Frequency tuning matters (a resonance-like optimum).**
- Outcome is radial (periosteal) growth, not length.

**Levers.**
- The epiphysis is the most compliant part of the bone and is reachable from the skin. Lateral loading of it is the most efficient way to deliver mechanical energy into a bone end.
- That makes it a candidate delivery channel for any stimulus aimed at the subchondral/epiphyseal tissue of a fused bone.
- Chase: Zhang 2006/2007 tibia knee loading; Yokota & Tanaka 2005 (knee loading molecular transport); the human knee loading device (ref 38).
