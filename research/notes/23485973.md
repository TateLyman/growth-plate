# PMID 23485973 — Cooper KL, ... Tabin CJ. Multiple phases of chondrocyte enlargement underlie differences in skeletal proportions (2013, Nature)

Read in full (PMC, ~18k chars).

**Model.** Mouse (CD-1) and jerboa at P5: proximal tibia, proximal radius, distal metatarsal. Diffraction phase microscopy of dissociated hypertrophic chondrocytes; Igf1 conditional knockout (HoxB6-Cre).

**Findings.**
- "The greatest contribution to growth rate in mammals is due to the massive volume enlargement of hypertrophic chondrocytes expanding the skeletal tissue in the direction of longitudinal growth **within laterally restricting matrix channels**."
- **Three phases of enlargement.**
  1. ~600→2000 fl, true hypertrophy at constant density 0.183 pg/fl.
  2. ~2000→8000 fl, *swelling*: fluid uptake dilutes dry mass to ~0.07 pg/fl.
  3. >8000→~14,000 fl, proportional growth at low density. **Phase 3 is IGF1-dependent.**
- Slow radius: truncated phase 2, no phase 3, ~5000 fl. Mouse metatarsal ~8000 fl. Jerboa metatarsal ~23,000 fl (40× initial volume); its hypertrophic cell height is +58% vs mouse.
- Igf1-null: same hypertrophic cell number but cells 30% shorter; they never reach phase 3 (~7000 fl max).
- Kinetics: cells triple in height within ~12 h, then sit ~12 h before turnover (≈24 h hypertrophic lifespan).
- Swelling persists at 424 mOsm medium, so it is an intrinsic, regulated property.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- The **actuator of elongation is a hydraulic/osmotic process**: directed swelling of hypertrophic cells within longitudinally aligned matrix channels that constrain lateral expansion.
- This physical fact is directly relevant to any *mechanical* theory. Elongation is an anisotropic volumetric expansion converted to length by matrix anisotropy (columnar septa).

**Levers.**
1. Fluid uptake (phase 2) is a mechanically and osmotically coupled process. External hydrostatic/osmotic conditions and matrix stiffness could modulate it.
2. Lateral confinement (septa, perichondrial ring) converts swelling to length. An engineered or induced cartilage layer needs **anisotropic confinement** (stiff laterally, compliant axially) to produce longitudinal growth.
3. IGF1 governs phase 3, and local IGF1 is mechanosensitive (mechano-growth factor isoforms). Loading may modulate it.
