# 37483459 — Tissue engineering in growth plate cartilage regeneration: mechanisms to therapeutic strategies

**Citation.** J Tissue Eng 2023;14:20417314231187956. PMC10359656. Review. Read in full (injury phases, seed cells, cell-free therapy, biological factors, scaffolds, biomimetic structure, scaffold-free strategies, conclusions).

## Why it is here
The most complete existing account of **what has actually been tried to rebuild a growth plate**, and the only one organised around the real obstacle: after a physeal injury the default fate is a **bone bridge**, and every intervention in the field is an attempt to divert that fate back to cartilage. It is therefore the catalogue of levers for the hardest step in any post-fusion scheme.

## The four phases of physeal injury (the clock everything has to fit)
Inflammatory → fibrogenic (FGF-2 drives osteoprogenitor and MSC proliferation and migration, and *inhibits* chondrocyte differentiation) → osteogenic (PKD → Osterix; angiogenesis; bone bridge forms) → remodelling. The intervention window is before the osteogenic phase commits.

## Levers catalogued, with what each actually achieved
**Seed cells.** Chondrocytes; MSCs; **skeletal stem cells**; others. Also **cell-free therapy** (MSC-derived exosomes/EVs).

**Growth factors.**
- **IGF-1 from cell-free PLGA scaffolds** into a rabbit proximal tibial physeal injury → significant cartilage regeneration.
- **A cartilage-targeting single-chain human antibody fragment fused to IGF-1** → "superior therapeutic efficacy and minimizes adverse effects on other tissues compared to the direct use of IGF-1." **This is the delivery solution to the spatial-control problem, built and tested.**
- **FGF-2 + TGF-β1** > TGF-β1 alone for BMSC GAG output. **TGF-β1 in a gel-foam scaffold with autologous BMSCs** inhibited bone bridge formation but **failed to form a new cartilage structure**.
- **SDF-1α** for MSC homing; **TGF-β3** for chondrogenic commitment via SOX-9/Smad.
- **CTGF**: promotes growth-plate chondrocyte proliferation, maturation and hypertrophy; a **CTGF hydrogel + collagen sponge** in a 2 mm mouse articular defect produced new cartilage at 4 weeks; CTGF is a **direct SOX-9 target gene**.
- **PKD inhibition** after physeal injury: delayed MSC osteogenic differentiation, **increased MSC and cartilage amount, reduced bone formation at the injury site**, and significantly delayed bony healing.

**Anti-inflammatory.** Punicalin (maintains FOXO3 against IL-1β/TNF-α); TNF-α inhibition restores ECM in vitro but **TNF-α antagonism alone did not suppress the inflammatory response in vivo**; **celecoxib** (COX-2/iNOS) delayed both chondrogenesis and remodelling but **could not prevent the bony repair**.

**Anti-angiogenesis.**
- **Systemic bevacizumab** in rat physeal injury reduced angiogenesis and bone bridging — but by day 60 **tibial shortening was worse**, with **significant expansion of the hypertrophic zone of the adjacent intact growth plate**.
- **Locally injected alginate–chitosan hydrogel releasing VEGF-neutralising antibody** inhibited bone bridge formation **without interfering with normal longitudinal bone growth**. The clearest local-vs-systemic contrast in the whole corpus.
- **Perlecan** (binds VEGFR2 directly) and **FGF-18** as additional angiogenic/columnar regulators; with FGF-2, perlecan "promotes chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation into growth plate cartilage in a characteristic columnar arrangement."

**RNA regulators.** miR-410 (positive, targets Wnt3a); miR-145 and miR-495 (negative, target SOX-9); miR-143-3p and miR-100-5p (target BMPR2); **Dicer loss reduces proliferating chondrocyte number** by both reduced proliferation and accelerated hypertrophy; **miR-140 drives resting→columnar transition and inhibits hypertrophy, and columnar proliferation and survival require let-7**; the **PTHrP gradient induces miR-374-5p, miR-379-5p and miR-503-5p, which promote proliferation and inhibit hypertrophic differentiation**; miR-143 targets IHH.

**Zonal mechanics (quantified).** The **resting zone is the stiffest and least permeable** (dense collagen); the **proliferative zone is ~30% less stiff with ~4× higher permeability**. The **stiffness change from upper to lower hypertrophic zone may itself be the cue directing cells to endochondral ossification**. A gradient of mechanoregulatory signal (hydrostatic stress and maximum tensile strain) is high in the proliferative zone — maintaining cartilage phenotype, holding cells undifferentiated and favouring division — and **falls along the long axis through the hypertrophic zone, progressively favouring osteogenic differentiation**. Mechanical loading on the resting and proliferative zones "can induce the longitudinal arrangement of cells to maintain the columnar structure and drive the longitudinal growth." Unloading disrupts the actin cytoskeleton and destroys columnar organisation; excessive load also destroys zonal histology and metabolism.
> **Discrepancy to flag:** this review states "dynamic compressive forces stimulate growth plate cartilage growth, while shear and hydrostatic pressures are more likely to drive cartilage ossification," which **inverts the Carter/Stokes convention** used in [19540500](19540500.md) and [23009361](23009361.md) (cyclic hydrostatic pressure *maintains* cartilage; octahedral/deviatoric shear drives ossification). One of the two is wrong, or they are describing different loading regimes. Unresolved, and it matters: PCAC Phase B/C depends on which stress type does which.

**Biomimetic scaffolds — the two results that produced length.**
- **Yu et al.**: measured rabbit growth-plate moduli, then DLP-stereolithographed a **PEG-resin scaffold of vertical pillars** matched to those moduli, combined with a cartilage-mimetic hydrogel, and implanted it. In vitro it induced MSC chondrogenesis and ECM. In vivo it **allowed significantly greater limb growth than other treatment options — although only limited cartilage-like tissue was seen at the injury site.**
- **Li et al.**: an **ECM-derived oriented acellular scaffold** with a biomimetic columnar architecture and uniform transverse and longitudinal pores; oriented microtubules drew BMSCs into the core; it **promoted new cartilage, prevented bone bridge formation, and reduced angular deformity and length difference** in young rabbits, without fully preventing shortening.

**Scaffold-free.** Cell-sheet technology (preserves cell–cell contact, ECM and high uniform density): **MSC and chondrocyte sheets facilitated physeal cartilage regeneration and sufficiently prevented bone bridge formation**. Cartilage cell sheets from a thermoresponsive culture dish plus bone-marrow stimulation have been **tested in eight human patients** and were safe and effective for cartilage regeneration at defect sites. Limits: poor stability, low mechanical strength, surgical difficulty.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **Geometry produced length where biochemistry did not.** The one intervention that gave "significantly greater limb growth" was a scaffold whose **vertical pillars matched the native zonal modulus** — and it did so with only limited cartilage at the site. The one that produced the right histology (oriented columnar ECM scaffold) reduced but did not abolish shortening. Meanwhile TGF-β1 + BMSCs blocked the bone bridge and made no cartilage structure at all. **Architecture is doing the work.** Convergent with [40118845](40118845.md) from the opposite direction.
2. **Local delivery beats systemic by more than a margin — it reverses the sign.** Systemic anti-VEGF made the limb shorter and expanded the adjacent hypertrophic zone; the same antibody in a local hydrogel blocked bone bridging and left normal growth intact. And the cartilage-targeting scFv–IGF-1 fusion exists. For every compound in [COMPOUNDS.md](../COMPOUNDS.md), the delivery question is not a detail; it determines the direction of effect.
3. **The zonal stiffness map is the design specification.** Resting stiffest and least permeable; proliferative ~30% softer and ~4× more permeable; a declining mechanoregulatory gradient through the hypertrophic zone that progressively licenses ossification. That is a buildable gradient, and Yu et al. built a crude version of it and got limb growth.
4. **let-7 and miR-140 are the only named regulators in this corpus that increase *proliferating chondrocyte number*.** Dicer loss reduces it; miR-140 drives resting→columnar and blocks hypertrophy; let-7 is required for columnar proliferation and survival; the PTHrP-driven miRNAs promote proliferation and inhibit hypertrophy. Every *compound* I have found raises matrix and lowers proliferation. **The RNA layer is where the proliferation lever lives**, and it connects directly to the Lin28a lead ([41786976](41786976.md)) since Lin28a is the canonical let-7 suppressor — which, read naively, points the wrong way and is exactly why the Lin28a full texts are on the needed list.
5. **"More cartilage, less bone" recurs here too.** PKD inhibition: more MSCs and cartilage, less bone, delayed healing. Systemic anti-VEGF: expanded hypertrophic zone, shorter limb. Same dissociation as palovarotene ([30226468](30226468.md)) and chondrocyte Piezo1 cKO ([40999873](40999873.md)). Four independent interventions, one lesson: **blocking conversion accumulates cartilage and loses length.**

## Honest limits
- A review. Almost every claim is secondary and most primaries are unread.
- Entirely about **injured juvenile physes in rabbits, rats and mice**. The goal throughout is to prevent a bone bridge in a child, not to create growth in an adult. No experiment in it starts from a fused plate.
- "Significantly greater limb growth" (Yu et al.) is reported without a number in this text, and in a growing rabbit — i.e. rescue of growth that would otherwise have been lost, not new growth.
- The cell-sheet human data are 8 patients, for articular cartilage defects, not physes.
- The hydrostatic-vs-shear statement contradicts the mechanics literature I have read; unresolved.
