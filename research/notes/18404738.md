# 18404738 — Age and pattern of the onset of differential growth among growth plates in rats

**Citation.** J Orthop Res 2008;26(10):1395–1400. PMC2954232. Wilsman/Farnum group. Read in full (Introduction, Methods, all Results, Discussion).

## Model / method
Long Evans rats at **24 time points from gestational day 17 (before differential growth exists) to postnatal day 27 (when it is fully established)**. Four growth plates per animal: **proximal and distal radial, proximal and distal tibial**. Growth velocity by **fluorochrome labelling**. Full **stereological** measurement of chondrocytic kinetic parameters, including hypertrophic chondrocyte volumes, cell densities, cells produced per day and cells lost per day.

## Exact numbers — the decisive ones for this project
- **Growth plates at opposite ends of the same bone, in the same individual, at the same moment, elongate at rates that may differ by a factor of three to seven.**
- **Hypertrophic chondrocyte volume: ~4,000 µm³ in the slowly growing proximal radial plate at 24–28 days; ~20,000 µm³ in the rapidly growing proximal tibial plate at 7–13 days.** A **5-fold range within one animal**, same genome, same circulating hormones.
- **Postnatally, hypertrophic cell volume correlated with growth rate at r = 0.70 (0.01 > p > 0.001).**
- **The number of new chondrocytes produced per day was ~10,500 ± 1,500 per day in ALL FOUR growth plates** at day 13, when differential growth emerges. Cells lost per day "remained relatively constant." Within and between plates, new cells per day "varied but not significantly."
- **Postnatally, cell production kinetics correlated with growth velocity at only r = 0.35 — not statistically significant.**
- Prenatally and perinatally the picture inverts: velocity changes in all four plates track **cell production** (r = 0.79), and differential growth is not yet expressed.
- Quoting the prior literature: "**even over an eight-fold range of growth from approximately 50 µm a day to 400 µm a day, it has been demonstrated that the single biggest contribution to differential growth results from cellular swelling and associated shape change during hypertrophy.**"
- Differential growth emerges because the three faster plates **accelerated together but decelerated at different rates**; the proximal radial plate decelerates throughout, including prenatally. The separation seen at day 27 continues through days 35 and 42.
- Each plate has its own biological lifespan — primary centre, then secondary centre (distal before proximal in the radius, proximal before distal in the tibia), then closure (proximal radial closes before distal radial; distal tibial before proximal tibial).
- Authors' hypothesis for the onset of differential growth: **differential biomechanical forces — compressive, tensile and strain — originating prenatally from in-utero muscle contraction and primary-centre ossification, then "accentuated through gravity, especially as pups begin rigorous ambulation at two weeks of age."**
- Proposed cause of the sharp perinatal growth suppression in all four plates: the **fetal cortisol surge**, possibly acting by suppressing local chondrocyte IGF-1; human neonatal IGF-1 drops precipitously on day 1 and recovers by the end of week 1.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
**This is the keystone quantitative paper for [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md), and it both confirms the document's central argument and substantially reweights it.**

1. **In the postnatal mammal, growth rate is set by hypertrophic cell volume, and cell production is nearly invariant.** ~10,500 cells/day in four plates whose elongation rates differ by 3–7×. Volume correlates r = 0.70 with rate; production correlates r = 0.35, non-significantly. **The rate-setting variable postnatally is *h_max*, not *n*.**
2. **Therefore the "proliferation wall" of [COMPOUNDS.md](../COMPOUNDS.md) §2 is a much smaller obstacle than I presented it as.** That section's finding — that every safe compound raises matrix while leaving chondrocyte number flat or lower (T3, dasatinib, OSK, kartogenin, Yoda1, sprifermin) — is factually correct but strategically mis-weighted. Postnatally, cell number is not what distinguishes a plate growing at 400 µm/day from one growing at 50 µm/day. **Those compounds were being judged against the wrong variable.** Combined with the rat partition cited in [32852849](32852849.md) (enlargement 59%, matrix 32%, proliferation 9% of axial growth), and with bumetanide removing 35% of growth by shrinking cells with hypertrophic cell number unchanged to three figures ([20200963](20200963.md)), there are now **three independent lines showing the volume term carries the growth and the number term does not.**
3. **The headroom is measured, in mammals, within one animal: a 5-fold hypertrophic volume range (4,000 → 20,000 µm³).** Set that against the adult articular deep zone, whose cells are <50% the diameter of metaphyseal hypertrophic cells — i.e. **<12.5% of their volume** ([28438606](28438606.md)). If a terminal metaphyseal hypertrophic cell is ~14,000 µm³ ([23485973](23485973.md)), the adult articular deep-zone cell is around **1,750 µm³ — below even the slowest rat growth plate's 4,000 µm³, and sitting essentially at the phase-1/phase-2 boundary of Cooper's enlargement sequence (phase 1 ends ~2,000 fl).** **Adult articular deep-zone chondrocytes have completed true hypertrophy and never begun swelling. Phases 2 and 3 are entirely unused.** That is the precise statement of the headroom, and it names which phases to target: phase 2 (osmotic swelling, NKCC1/AE3) and phase 3 (IGF-1-dependent).
4. **It makes mechanics the proposed cause of differential *h_max*.** The authors' own hypothesis for why four plates diverge is differential compressive, tensile and strain loading, accentuated by gravity once the animal starts walking. **That is the mechanical lever acting on the volume term — exactly the coupling [THEORY.md](../THEORY.md) and ACTIVATION.md need, proposed independently by the people who measured the kinetics.** It is a hypothesis, explicitly untested in this paper.
5. **"All functions can be regulated independently."** The authors state it directly: growth velocity is cells produced × volume increase per cell, both present in every plate, both independently regulable, either able to dominate. That is G = n × h_max asserted by the people who measured it, with the postnatal weighting on *h_max*.
6. **Plates have individual lifespans and individual closure times.** Proximal radial closes before distal radial; distal tibial before proximal tibial. Combined with the spine closing last ([34362001](34362001.md)), this supports the "most recently closed is most reactivatable" prediction as a within-subject, multi-site experiment.
7. **A new candidate brake: glucocorticoids suppressing local chondrocyte IGF-1.** The perinatal growth arrest in all four plates is attributed to the fetal cortisol surge, possibly via suppression of chondrocyte IGF-1 — which is the phase-3 driver. Relevant both as an anti-lever (corticosteroids) and as evidence that IGF-1 availability is the systemically modulated input to *h_max*.

## Levers
- **Hypertrophic cell volume as the rate-setting variable postnatally** (r = 0.70 with velocity; 5-fold measured range; cell production invariant).
- **Phase 2 (osmotic swelling: NKCC1, AE3) and phase 3 (IGF-1-dependent)** as the specific unused capacity in adult articular cartilage.
- **Mechanical load — compressive, tensile, strain — as the proposed setter of differential volume.**
- **Local chondrocyte IGF-1**, suppressible by glucocorticoids.
- **Anti-lever: corticosteroids.** Dramatic deceleration or total cessation of elongation in experimental animals, and well known in children.

## Honest limits
- **Rat, gestational day 17 to postnatal day 27.** Juvenile, open plates, no adult, no fused plate, no articular cartilage.
- **Correlational.** Volume and velocity correlate at r = 0.70; nothing was manipulated. The mechanical-forces hypothesis for differential growth is explicitly not tested.
- The 59/32/9 partition and the 50–400 µm/day eight-fold statement are **cited from the group's earlier work** (their refs 4, 5, 7, 9), which I have not read. The primary of those — Wilsman et al., "Differential growth by growth plates as a function of multiple parameters of chondrocytic kinetics" (8982136, 1996) — is the single most load-bearing unread reference in the project.
- Hypertrophic volume by stereology on fixed tissue, with the usual shrinkage and section-angle caveats.
- Four plates in two bones; no spine, no articular surface.
