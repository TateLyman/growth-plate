# 37770652 — Garetosmab in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva: a randomized trial (LUMINA-1)

**Citation.** Nat Med 2023;29:2615–2624. PMC10579054. NCT03188666. Read in full (safety, period 1 and 2 efficacy, normotopic bone analysis).

## Model / method
Phase 2 RCT. **44 adult** FOP patients (mean age 27.6 y, 95.5% classic R206H, mean CAJIS 15.7), all with active HO at baseline. Period 1: garetosmab (fully human anti-activin-A mAb) 10 mg/kg i.v. Q4W (n = 20) vs placebo (n = 24), 28 wk, double blind. Period 2: all on garetosmab, 28 wk open label (n = 43). Period 3 open-label extension; **dosing suspended after a fatal AE**. Endpoints by ¹⁸F-NaF PET (total lesion activity) and CT (volume).

## Exact numbers
**Efficacy.**
- **Primary endpoint not met**: time-weighted % change in total lesion activity, garetosmab −8.1 vs placebo +16.6, difference −24.6% (95% CI −51.8 to +2.5), **P = 0.0741**. Hierarchical testing stopped there.
- HO volume by CT: +7.1% vs +32.0%, difference −24.9% (CI −80.8 to +30.9), n.s.
- **New lesions were almost abolished.** Period 1 new lesions: 27 (CT) / 29 (PET) on placebo vs **3 / 3** on garetosmab. Post hoc ~97% relative reduction in new lesion activity, ~90% in new lesion volume. Patients developing new lesions: 15% vs 45.8%.
- Period 2 crossover (n = 22 placebo→garetosmab): new HO lesions by CT **40.9% → 0%** (P = 0.0027); total new lesion volume LS mean 9.29 → 0.05 cm³ (P = 0.0039); PET new-lesion rate reduction 95%.
- **Pre-existing lesions did not expand in either arm**, and garetosmab had no effect on their volume or total activity. It only reduced ¹⁸F-NaF SUVmax within them (−33.2% vs −20.2% at wk 28).
- Pain: no difference (LS mean difference −0.34, CI −0.96 to 0.27).

**Safety — the reason this paper is in my corpus.**
- 100% of patients in both arms had ≥1 TEAE in period 1.
- **Five deaths among 44 patients (11.4%)** during the open-label periods: head/brain trauma from a fall, haemorrhagic stroke, fatal intestinal obstruction, traumatic splenic rupture + cardiac arrest from a fall, sudden cardiac death with pulmonary granulomatous inflammation from chronic aspiration. Investigators called them unrelated; the paper states plainly that **"causality cannot be ruled out."** Three of the five had CAJIS ≥24 (profound/end-of-life disease).
- Garetosmab vs placebo in period 1: **epistaxis 50% vs 16.7%**; **madarosis (loss of eyebrows and eyelashes) 30% vs 0%**; skin/soft-tissue infection composite incl. acne 60% vs 12.5%. One epistaxis SAE required hospitalisation and nasal packing.
- Period 2/extension: infections 86.0% (limb abscess 14.0%, anal abscess 9.3%, furuncle 9.3%, paronychia 9.3%, abdominal/groin/subcutaneous/vulval abscesses); madarosis 46.5%; alopecia 18.6%; hirsutism 9.3%; epistaxis 34.9%. **Six abscess SAEs in 5 patients requiring incision and drainage; five of the six judged drug-related.**
- Coagulation parameters and platelet function normal throughout; BMP9 fluctuations did not track epistaxis — the bleeding mechanism is unexplained.
- **Normotopic (normal) bone was unaffected**: no between-group difference in normal-bone PET SUV at week 8 or 28, and CTX-1 stayed in the reference range.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **Clean proof that the ligand drives *initiation*, not *growth*, of an endochondral lesion.** Blocking activin A abolished *new* lesions (40.9% → 0%) and did nothing to existing ones. The decision to start an endochondral unit and the subsequent elongation of that unit are pharmacologically separable. That is the same dissociation the architecture results show from the other side ([40118845](40118845.md)): the hard part is making the right structure in the right place; once it exists it runs.
2. **It sets the realistic safety price of modulating this pathway systemically in adults.** 11.4% mortality (confounded by severe baseline disease, but unexplained), recurrent abscesses requiring surgical drainage in 11.6%, loss of eyebrows and eyelashes in nearly half, epistaxis in a third with no identifiable coagulation defect. Activin A sits in immune, haematopoietic, gonadal, dermal and vascular biology, and blocking it body-wide shows it. Any proposal to drive the *opposite* direction — more BMP/ACVR1 signalling in a healthy adult, systemically, to grow cartilage — should be read against this.
3. **Normal bone was untouched.** Worth noting precisely because it is the mirror of my central finding: a systemic agent aimed at this axis reached the heterotopic lesions and left the normotopic skeleton alone. Systemic drive does not know where you want it.
4. **In wild-type tissue, activin A is an ACVR1 *antagonist*.** The whole trial rests on a neomorphic receptor. So this drug tells us nothing about activin A manipulation in a person without the mutation — and if anything implies that in a normal adult, activin A *blockade* would raise BMP-SMAD tone. Anyone reasoning from FOP to normal physiology has to invert the sign.

## Levers
- **Activin A** confirmed clinically as the obligate initiating ligand for ACVR1-R206H-driven endochondral HO, with a 100% relative reduction in new lesion incidence on crossover. The strongest human validation in the corpus that de novo endochondral ossification in an adult has a single blockable trigger.
- **Separability of initiation from elongation** — the useful design principle. A one-time, local initiation event plus a persistent architecture beats a chronic systemic drive.
- **Anti-lever: systemic antibody blockade of a pleiotropic TGF-β family ligand.** The AE profile is the argument.

## Honest limits
- n = 44, phase 2, primary endpoint missed (P = 0.07). The impressive numbers are a post-hoc and a prospectively-amended within-patient comparison, not the prespecified primary.
- All patients had active disease and high CAJIS; the deaths are badly confounded by baseline disability (two were falls).
- Period 2 has no concurrent control — the within-group period-1-vs-period-2 comparison cannot separate drug effect from the natural waning of HO accrual with age that [38672135](38672135.md) describes.
- COVID-19 forced a modified ITT population.
- Adults with a gain-of-function receptor. Nothing here is about growth, height, plates, or normal physiology.
