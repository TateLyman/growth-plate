# PMID 15308820 — Tanaka SM, Sun HB, Yokota H. Bone formation induced by a novel form of mechanical loading on joint tissue (2004, Biol Sci Space)

Read in full (PDF, 4 pp).

**Model.** Female C57BL/6 mice, 14 weeks old.
- Elbow loading: lateral–medial sinusoidal, 2 Hz, 0.5 N peak-to-peak, 3 min/day for 3 days, applied with a piezoelectric loader. The contralateral arm is the control.
- Calcein on days 2 and 6 after loading; harvested at 13 days. Section taken 2.5 mm distal to the elbow (ulnar diaphysis).
- Ex vivo femur streaming potentials: 5.8 N at 2 Hz on the distal epiphysis gives a 43 µV peak (~3.7 µV extrapolated for 0.5 N).

**Results.** Periosteal MS/BS ×3.2, MAR ×3.0, BFR ×7.9 vs control (n=10, p<0.05). Needed less force than ulna axial bending (>1 N).

**What it establishes.**
- First joint-loading paper. A small lateral squeeze of the epiphysis/joint generates intramedullary fluid flow (streaming potential) and drives strong diaphyseal periosteal bone formation *without diaphyseal strain*.
- **Epiphyseal compression works as a hydraulic pump.**
- What it increases is cortical apposition (width), not length.

**Levers.**
- The epiphysis can be used as a pressure/flow transducer for the whole bone.
- Lateral (transverse) cyclic compression of a bone end is a gentle, non-invasive way to create pressure gradients and fluid flow in marrow and cortex. Repurposing candidate: deliver strain or pressure into the epiphyseal region of a fused bone.
