# PMID 40494878 — Retinoic acid breakdown is required for proximodistal positional identity during axolotl limb regeneration

Suppah S, Arenas Gomez CM, Bothwell BE, et al. *Nat Commun* 2025;16:5247. PMC12152164. doi:10.1038/s41467-025-59497-5

**What I read:** the complete PMC full text — abstract, Introduction, all seven Results sections, the full Discussion, and the Methods (animal procedures and drug treatments, qRT-PCR) with figure legends read in place. Not an abstract read. (The 2024 bioRxiv preprint of the same work is PMID 39149228 / PMC11326211; not separately read.)

## Model
Axolotl limb regeneration. Paired amputations in the same animal: **proximal (upper humerus) on one limb, distal (through the carpals) on the other.** Drugs dissolved in housing water from 4 DPA, redosed every other day for **7 days**, then returned to clean water; **skeletal morphology scored 120 days post-treatment.** RARE:GFP RA-reporter and Hoxa13-T2A-mCHERRY reporter lines; CRISPR F0 crispants and F1 *Shox*−/− animals.

## The core mechanism
RA signalling is ~3.5× higher in proximal than distal blastemas. The paper shows that difference is set by **degradation, not synthesis or receptor level**:
- Rara, Rarg, Raldh1, Raldh2, Raldh3 — none match the patterning-gene expression pattern.
- **Cyp26b1** is graded along the PD axis: highest in autopod-level blastemas, decreasing proximally; mesenchymal, and spatiotemporally mirrors Hoxa11/Hoxa13 while anti-correlating with Meis1.
- Meis1/Meis2 decrease with progressively distal amputation; Hoxa13 is sharply highest in autopod amputations.

## Exact numbers — the pharmacology
**Talarozole (TAL, R115866), a CYP26 inhibitor, in the water for 7 days:**

| Dose | Distally amputated limbs | Proximally amputated limbs |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 µM | **92.3% duplicated a zeugopod** (61.2% whole, 30.1% partial); 7.7% none | normal |
| 1 µM | **100% duplicated a stylopod** (66.7% full, 33.3% partial) | normal |
| 5 µM | 92.3% failed to regenerate; 7.7% full humerus duplication | 84.6% regressed to the scapula; 15.4% normal |

- TAL raised RA signalling directly (GFP signal and *Gfp* transcript up in RARE:GFP reporters), lowered mesenchymal *Cyp26b1*, and **abolished HOXA13 reporter signal** at 1 µM.
- Epithelial *Cyp26a1* rose on TAL — the authors infer CYP26A1 clears excess RA in the epithelium and CYP26B1 sets mesenchymal levels. The phenotype is CYP26B1.
- **Dependency controls:** co-treatment with disulfiram (pan-RALDH inhibitor) blocked full proximalization; **AGN 193109 (pan-RAR antagonist) abolished duplication entirely (0% at 0.1 µM RAA; 83.3% no duplication at 1 µM).** So the effect runs through RA synthesis and RAR.
- Bulk RNA-seq: TAL-treated distal blastemas shift transcriptionally toward proximal blastemas (363 DEGs at 0.1 µM, 889 at 1 µM, vs 539 DEGs between untreated proximal and distal). Shared with the proximal-vs-distal comparison: 138 DEGs, including Pax9, Evx1, Alx4, **Shox and Shox2**.

## SHOX — the hinge to human height
- *Shox* and *Shox2* are **RA-responsive and graded proximally**; Shox is restricted to the Meis1+ proximal mesenchyme and is **mutually exclusive with Hoxa13+ cells**. The Shox promoter contains putative Meis1 binding sites. Shox is downstream of Meis1; Hoxa13 sets its distal limit.
- **Shox crispants and Shox−/− animals: significantly shortened stylopod and zeugopod, autopod length unaffected.**
- Histology: control stylopod chondrocytes progress proliferation → hypertrophy → calcification. **Shox-null stylopod chondrocytes "failed to proliferate, appearing to remain as reserve cartilage through adulthood"** and the elements lack calcification. Digit (autopod) chondrocytes ossify normally in the same animals.
- Shox is **dispensable for regeneration itself** — crispants form blastemas and palettes and regenerate on schedule, just short. Meis1 and Hoxa13 expression are unchanged in Shox−/−, so Shox does not set positional identity; it *executes* it.
- Shox crispant distal blastemas given 1 µM TAL still duplicated, but with **shortened** duplicated proximal elements — they read the goal change and cannot build it.
- The authors' own human framing: "Shortened limbs are frequently observed in humans with Shox haploinsufficiency, which is commonly linked to idiopathic short stature, Turner syndrome, and Leri-Weill dyschondrosteosis." Mice lack a functional *Shox* ortholog; *Shox2*-null mice have shortened humeri with chondrocytes that fail to proliferate and mature.

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **A clinical-stage oral small molecule changed how much skeleton an animal built, dose-dependently, from a 7-day exposure, scored 120 days later.** Talarozole has been through human Phase II (acne, psoriasis). This is the strongest "goal changer" datum in the project: not faster growth, but *an additional skeletal segment* because the tissue's positional target moved.
2. **The dial is a degradation enzyme, which is the druggable node.** RA level is set by CYP26B1, not by synthesis or receptor abundance — so you do not have to administer a retinoid at teratogenic systemic dose (cf. 150 µg/g in [31026439](31026439.md)); you block clearance and let endogenous RA rise locally where it is already being made.
3. **SHOX is the effector, and SHOX is a human height gene with monotonic dosage behaviour.** This is the first thing in the whole project that connects a positional-identity drug target to a gene whose dosage is known to set human stature.
4. **The Shox-null phenotype is precisely the state I argued adult cartilage is stuck in.** ACTIVATION.md §4 localises adult articular deep-zone cells at ~1,750 fl — the phase-1/phase-2 boundary, i.e. arrested before the volume programme. Shox-null chondrocytes "remain as reserve cartilage through adulthood" and never proliferate, enlarge or calcify. **SHOX is a named, RA-responsive permission signal for reserve cartilage to enter the growth programme, and it is segment-specific to the proximal (length-bearing) skeleton.** That is the gate I have been looking for, with a drug upstream of it.
5. **Segment specificity cuts both ways and is good news.** Shox governs stylopod/zeugopod and not autopod: there are separate transcriptional programmes for proximal vs distal endochondral ossification. A proximally-biased lever is exactly what a height strategy wants — long bones and (to be tested) axial elements, not hands and feet. Acromegaly's distal-dominant morphology is the natural contrast.

## Levers
- **CYP26 (specifically CYP26B1) inhibition** — talarozole (R115866, Phase II), liarozole (orphan designation), and the RAMBA class generally. Raises local RA without administering a retinoid.
- **Meis1 → Shox axis** — the proximalizing transcriptional output. Shox dosage is the quantitative knob.
- RAR-dependence is established, so RAR-selective agonism is an alternative entry; note the **RARγ sign mirror** already in COMPOUNDS.md Node E (palovarotene, an RARγ *agonist*, shortened rat tibiae 13–19%), which this paper does not resolve — see limits.

## Anti-levers
- **Overshoot destroys the tissue.** 5 µM TAL: 84.6% of proximal limbs *regressed to the scapula*; 92.3% of distal limbs failed to regenerate. The therapeutic window between "extra segment" and "no limb" is ~one log.
- Disulfiram (RALDH inhibition) and AGN 193109 (RAR antagonism) both block the gain — useful as proof, and as a warning that anything suppressing RA synthesis or RAR works against this.
- Shox loss — shortening, with reserve-arrested chondrocytes.

## Honest limits
1. **Axolotl, and a blastema.** Every result requires an amputation and a blastema. The paper's own model says dedifferentiated cells "modify Cyp26b1 expression depending on their positional memory" — the substrate is a dedifferentiated cell, not resting adult cartilage. Nothing here shows CYP26 inhibition does anything in an intact adult mammalian skeleton. This is the central unanswered question and the reason this is a mechanism, not a plan.
2. **Proximal blastemas did not respond at the doses that transformed distal ones.** "RA breakdown is crucial for positional identity in distally, not proximally, amputated limbs." The lever works by making a distal field believe it is proximal. There may be no headroom above "already proximal" — which matters enormously, because a human adult who wants to be taller is asking for *more proximal than proximal*, which this paper does not demonstrate exists.
3. **The sign conflict with palovarotene is unresolved.** Here, more RA → more proximal skeleton. In rats, an RARγ agonist shortened tibiae. Species, receptor selectivity, open vs closed plate, and development vs regeneration all differ. I cannot claim a clean direction for a mammalian growth plate from this paper, and COMPOUNDS.md Node E should continue to carry the mirror hypothesis as a hypothesis.
4. **Shox is required for proximal ossification, so the relationship to length is not simply "more Shox, more length."** The paper shows loss-of-function shortening; it does not show gain-of-function lengthening. Human *SHOX* duplication data are the place to test that and were not examined here.
5. Teratology is the obvious hazard class: this is retinoid signalling, and the paper's own 5 µM arm destroyed limbs.
6. RA synthesis along the PD axis is unresolved — Raldh3 was higher proximally by qRT-PCR but not by FISH or RNA-seq, and the authors decline to choose.
7. Upstream control of Cyp26b1 is unidentified; they infer 5' Hox regulation circumstantially.
8. TIG1 and PROD1 — the two known RA-responsive adhesion molecules — were *not* differentially expressed here, which the authors attribute to timepoint. See PMID 35241664 (Tig1, Nat Commun 2022), fetched, not yet read.
