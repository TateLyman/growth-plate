# PMID 35523895 — Muruganandan S, ... Sinha S, Dennis JE et al. A FoxA2+ long-term stem cell population is necessary for growth plate cartilage regeneration after injury (2022, Nat Commun)

Read in full (PMC, ~65k chars).

**Model.** Mice (FoxA2-CreERT2 tracing, ablation with DTA, serial transplantation, Salter–Harris type 1 (SH1)-like needle injury through the hypertrophic zone at P18). Rabbit histology also shows FoxA2+ cells.

**Findings.**
- FoxA2 marks the periarticular domain fated to become the SOC from P0, before Col10.
- Cells "left behind" from SOC formation populate the **top** of the resting zone, next to the SOC. They do not overlap with PTHrP+ cells at the bottom of the RZ (double-positive 0.017% = background). They can become PTHrP+ over time (0.1% double-positive at P40).
- **Self-renewal.**
  - FoxA2+: 38% of colonies form secondaries; 9% reach passage ≥9.
  - PTHrP+: 11% form secondaries; 1.4% reach passage 5.
  - Serial transplantation retains self-renewal and trilineage potential.
- **In vivo kinetics.** Columns from FoxA2+ cells *increase* over time (1% at 1 month → 26% of units at 9 months), whereas PTHrP+ columns decline. FoxA2+ cells are long-term SSCs. Before P28 they are osteo-chondro; after P28 mostly chondrogenic.
- **Injury (SH1-like, 30-gauge needle through the hypertrophic zone).**
  - Plate 42% intact at d1, 63% at d3, **96% regenerated by d7** with all three zones (physeal cartilage, not fibrocartilage or bone).
  - FoxA2+ cells expand 2.7-fold in the GP at d3, then transit to metaphyseal bone by d7.
  - DTA ablation (36% fewer cells) cut regeneration to 72%.
  - Rabbit literature: excision of proliferative + hypertrophic zones still regenerates the plate if the RZ is intact.
- Bony-bar formation follows SH 3–5 injuries that destroy the RZ.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Growth-plate regeneration depends on a quiescent long-term stem pool *adjacent to the bony SOC*. Once that pool is lost (fusion, or SH5 crush), the plate does not regenerate and a bone bar forms.
- In adults this pool is absent. It reiterates that a progenitor population with an SOC-like neighbour is the indispensable ingredient.

**Levers.**
- The SOC/cartilage interface is where long-term stem cells reside. Spatial architecture matters: a stiff, vascular bone plate above a cartilage layer.
- Injury *within* a niche-bearing plate expands the stem cells (regenerative response). Controlled micro-injury might activate a niche, but only if the niche exists.
