# PMID 23339896 — Kaneko H, ... Arikawa-Hirasawa E. Synovial perlecan is required for osteophyte formation in knee osteoarthritis (2013, Matrix Biol)

Read in full (PMC, ~26k chars).

**Model.** **Adult** female mice (11–12 weeks), Hspg2−/−-Tg lacking synovial perlecan. Two models:
- Surgical OA: MCL transection plus medial meniscectomy; analysed at 4 and 8 weeks.
- Intra-articular TGF-β1: 200 ng × 3, every other day.

**Findings.**
- Osteophytes form from periosteal/synovial MSCs at the cartilage–synovium junction. They proceed through chondrogenesis → hypertrophy → vascular invasion → bone with marrow, "similar to endochondral ossification in the growth plate".
- Scored size goes up to >3× adjacent cartilage thickness within 4–8 weeks.
- Synovial perlecan loss reduced osteophyte size and maturity, PCNA proliferation, Col II/Col X and p-Smad2. OA cartilage scores were unchanged.
- Triggers: "mechanical factors and cytokines".
- BMP-2 injections induce osteophytes near where growth plates meet the joint space. TGF-β1 induces them at cortical joint margins.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Adult mammals can build *new* endochondral bone from scratch, including a growth-plate-like sequence, at joint margins, within weeks.
- The trigger is joint instability (altered mechanics after MCL/meniscus loss) plus TGF-β.
- This is proof that **adult de novo endochondral growth happens in response to mechanics**, though in an uncontrolled, radial direction.

**Levers.**
- Joint instability / abnormal mechanics provokes chondrogenic recruitment of periosteal/synovial MSCs.
- The direction of osteophyte growth is the problem: outward (radial), not axial.
- A directed axial version would require the cartilage anlage to sit *between* bone ends in the load axis, i.e. at the articular surface or within the bone.
