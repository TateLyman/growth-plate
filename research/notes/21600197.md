# PMID 21600197 — Solem RC, Eames BF, Tokita M, Schneider RA. Mesenchymal and mechanical mechanisms of secondary cartilage induction (2011, Dev Biol)

Read in full (PMC, ~34k chars).

**Model.** Quail→duck neural crest mesenchyme chimeras ("quck"); in-ovo paralysis (decamethonium); stretch-activated channel block (gadolinium).

**Findings.**
- Secondary cartilage forms at articulations, sutures and entheses only under mechanical stimulation. Background: it is initiated and maintained by compressive loading (cyclic hydrostatic pressure).
- Duck mandibular-adductor enthesis cartilage depends on muscle insertion geometry. The geometry is set by neural crest mesenchyme; quail geometry gives no cartilage. The duck's lateral insertion puts the enthesis under **tension + shear**; quail's under tension only.
- **Paralysis abolished both enthesis and articular (quadratojugal) secondary cartilage. At the articulation, well-ossified bone formed instead** (Sox9/Col2a1 lost).
- **Gadolinium (stretch-activated channel block) converted articular secondary cartilage into bone** (Runx2 up in it), but spared enthesis cartilage.
- Articular secondary cartilage arises from **periosteal Sox9+Runx2+ pre-osteoblast progenitors under mechanical force**. Enthesis cartilage arises from aponeurosis via Fgfr2/Bmp4, independent of Runx2 and stretch channels.
- Hyperactivity (heat, 4-AP) increases bone length, muscle mass and joint cavity size.

**Relevance.**
- Direct developmental proof of the core assumption: **at an articulation, periosteal progenitors become cartilage only while movement continues, and turn to bone the moment movement stops** (or mechanosensing is blocked).
- **Movement is the switch that holds periosteum-derived tissue in a cartilage state at joint surfaces.** The mandibular condyle is exactly such a tissue, so this is why condylar growth is mechanically controllable.
- For PCAC: recruiting periosteal/perichondrial progenitors at the joint margin into cartilage requires sustained articulating motion with compression/shear (Phase A). Stretch-activated channel signalling is necessary at articulations.
