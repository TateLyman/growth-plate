# PMID 532591 — Houghton GR, Dekel S. The periosteal control of long bone growth: an experimental study in the rat (1979, Acta Orthop Scand)

Read in full (PDF, 3 pp).

**Model.** 6-day-old rat femurs explanted into sealed Millipore diffusion chambers inside adult host rat peritoneum (no blood supply, so no vascular effects; humoral factors shared). The right femur had a circumferential periosteal incision plus mid-third stripping; the left was the control in the same chamber.

**Results.**
- Growth (mm) control vs stripped: 7 d 0.78 vs 0.93; **14 d 0.95 vs 1.21 (p<0.05)**; 21 d 0.92 vs 1.22.
- Paired overgrowth 0.23 mm overall (p<0.0001), i.e. **~25% more growth**.

**Interpretation (authors, supporting Crilly 1972).**
- The periosteum is loosely attached along the shaft but strongly anchored at both ends at the perichondrial ring. **Periosteal tension acts as a mechanical restraint on growth-plate elongation (a tether).** Releasing it accelerates growth.
- The diffusion-chamber design excludes vascular (Trueta ischaemia/hyperaemia) and local hormone (Lacroix) explanations.

**Relevance for the theory (important).**
- The periosteum is a **tensioned sleeve that compresses the growth plates** via the perichondrial rings. That is a built-in mechanical brake on length.
- Corollary: the periosteum *stores* tension proportional to growth. In adults after fusion the sleeve is still anchored at the bone ends.
- Any engineered adult end-growth zone would be braked by an intact periosteal sleeve (Hueter–Volkmann compression). The sleeve's tension could be *modulated non-surgically* (e.g., by sustained traction that elongates the soft-tissue envelope, or by periosteal stretch).
- Inversely, *periosteal stretch* stimulates periosteal progenitors. Under longitudinal traction, the periosteal sleeve itself becomes the tissue under tension, and tension in periosteum promotes osteogenesis and, with motion, chondrogenesis.
- Stripping/division is surgery and excluded, but the *mechanical principle* (relieve compression on the growth front; put the sleeve in a different strain state) is usable.
