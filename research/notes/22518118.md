# PMID 22518118 — Morphological and histopathological changes in orofacial structures of acromegaly-like rats: an overview (2012, Int J Endocrinol)

Read in full (PMC, ~18k chars).

**Model.** Male Wistar rats, 10 weeks old (still growing). Human IGF-I 640 µg/day by subcutaneous minipump for 4 weeks, then 4 weeks off.
- Total circulating IGF-I rose ~1.7–1.9×; endogenous rat IGF-I was suppressed by negative feedback.

**Results.**
- Mandibular alveolar bone, maxillary alveolar bone and femur all elongated during IGF-I. **Elongation was greatest in the mandible.**
- The ascending ramus (condylar endochondral growth) was specifically lengthened.
- Condylar cartilage layer, bone matrix ratio and osteoblast number all increased.
- After IGF-I normalised, the tissue histology (cartilage width etc.) reverted, but **the gained skeletal length persisted**.

**Background claims.**
- Human acromegaly: significant skeletal changes after closure occur **only in the mandible** (ramus length, body length, chin), per Kunzler & Farmand in 31 patients.
- The secondary cartilage of the mandibular condyle is more IGF-I-sensitive than the femoral head (Delatte). Site-specific IGFBP-5 levels are high in mandibular and calvarial cells.
- **Suzuki et al.: local IGF-I injection into the condylar joint capsule increased endochondral ossification in adult rats.**

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- In adult humans with closed plates, chronic GH/IGF-I excess lengthens only the mandible (via the never-fusing condylar secondary cartilage), not limb bones. That is the cleanest human natural experiment:
  - **Systemic anabolic drive cannot elongate a fused long bone, because no responsive endochondral cartilage remains.**
  - It *can* elongate a bone whose end retains a progenitor-covered secondary cartilage.
- So the bottleneck after fusion is **the absence of a responsive cartilage template**, not lack of growth factor.
- Gains are retained after the stimulus stops: an endochondrally built increment is permanent.

**Levers.**
- Any post-fusion mechanism must first **recreate a responsive endochondral cartilage layer** at the bone end. Hormonal drive is only a gain on that template.
- Local IGF-I amplifies an existing secondary cartilage.

**Chase.** Suzuki 2004 (local IGF-I in adult rat condyle); Kunzler & Farmand 1991 (acromegaly skeletal changes); Delatte (condyle vs femoral head IGF sensitivity).
