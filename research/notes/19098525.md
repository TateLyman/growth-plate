# PMID 19098525 — Yokota H et al. Potential applications of pulsating joint loading in sports medicine (2009, Exerc Sport Sci Rev)

Read in full (PMC, ~14k chars; review).

**Content.**
- Pulsating joint loading (PJL): 1–20 Hz, 3–5 min/day, 0.5 N in mice. Bone formation and wound healing occur throughout ulna, tibia and femur.
- Working model: epiphyseal deformation → medullary pressure gradient → (a) lacunocanalicular interstitial flow (osteocyte shear) and (b) medullary flow toward a wound (pressure sink), recruiting marrow-derived MSCs, HSCs and EPCs.
- Candidate molecular pathways: MAPK/ERK (Osterix via p38), Wnt/β-catenin with Sost/Dkk1 suppression, PPARγ downregulation, and BMP/IGF/TGFβ/PI3K.
- Notes that **growth-plate chondrocytes** are among the cell types likely involved in the distance effects.
- No human data; a prototype device was in design.

**Relevance.** The review adds no new lengthening data. It contributes the concept that a pressure sink (a site of lower pressure, e.g. a lesion) directs medullary flow and progenitor migration toward it. For the theory, this is a possible *non-surgical progenitor-recruitment pump*. It does not on its own make cartilage.
