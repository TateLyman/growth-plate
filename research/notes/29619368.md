# PMID 29619368 — Javaheri B, ... Pitsillides AA. The Chondro-Osseous Continuum: Is It Possible to Unlock the Potential Assigned Within? (2018, Front Bioeng Biotechnol) — mini-review

Read in full (PMC, ~26k chars).

**Content.**
- Endochondral ossification is "the source of all longitudinal bone growth". It reappears in fracture repair and in osteoarthritis (OA).
- In OA, articular cartilage becomes vascularised, mineralised and replaced by bone, evident at the **joint margins (osteophytes)**, and possibly in bone-marrow lesions.
- Hypertrophic chondrocytes lack classical apoptosis; they transdifferentiate to osteoblasts.
  - Callus hypertrophic cells express Sox2/Oct4/Nanog near vessels; endothelial-conditioned medium induces this.
  - Hypertrophic cells express BCL2 and ALP, and some divide (BrdU).
- Cartilage ECM composition imprints the osteoblasts derived from it.
  - TIMP3 chondrocyte overexpression causes transient long-bone shortening, restored by adulthood.
  - Aggrecanase inhibition raises bone mass; collagenase inhibition lowers it.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Endochondral ossification is active in adults in two pathological settings at bone ends: osteophytes and subchondral advance in OA. The adult joint retains a chondro-osseous interface where cartilage → bone conversion proceeds.

**Levers.**
- The adult osteochondral junction under articular cartilage is a live chondro-osseous continuum, but it advances *pathologically* (OA) in an uncontrolled way.
- The engineering problem: make deep-zone cartilage *produce* more cartilage (and hypertrophy) at a rate matching or exceeding its ossification, so the joint keeps its cartilage thickness while the bone advances. That is net axial growth.
- Osteophytes show adult periosteal/synovial progenitors can form new endochondral bone, but outward, not axially.
