# PMID 39584870 — Unilateral "inactive" condylar hyperplasia: new histological data (2024, J Funct Morphol Kinesiol)

Read in full (PMC, ~32k chars).

**Material.** 15 surgical biopsies (proportional condylectomy) from **adults aged 28–36** with inactive unilateral condylar hyperplasia (UCH; negative SPECT, <10% discrepancy). No controls. Immunofluorescence intensities only (semi-quantitative).

**Findings.**
- All biopsies show 4 layers (fibrous articular → undifferentiated mesenchyme → transitional → hypertrophic) with a **markedly thickened hypertrophic layer**.
- The cartilage–bone front is irregular: chondrocyte groups in their matrix push down into the bone ("expansion processes").
- **Double osteochondral border**: a thin bone layer inside the hypertrophic layer plus a deeper front. The authors read this as the front moving down with cartilage growth, then up with ossification, i.e. **ongoing elongation in 28–36-year-olds**.
- Strong Col II (> Col I), MMP-2, osteocalcin in the deepest layer, and RANK+ osteoclasts in Howship's lacunae under the front.
- The authors attribute it to compression dominating over tension, which favours Col II and chondrocytes.
- Wolford describes a UCH variant with a vertical vector that "can occur at any age and is not self-limited".

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- **Direct human histological proof that a bone end in adults aged ~30 can still elongate endochondrally**, given a persistent progenitor-capped cartilage (the condyle).
- The elongating apparatus is the same 4-layer secondary-cartilage stack described in the mouse (Tsutsumi-Arai 2024).
- Compression-dominant loading with a hypertrophic layer, MMP-2 and osteoclast coupling is associated with growth.
- The "inactive" state is not zero. A low, sustained endochondral flux persists and can reactivate.

**Levers.**
- Adult human tissues tolerate and sustain an endochondral bone-end front for years without tumour behaviour.
- Mechanical bias (compression > tension) is the putative driver.
- The limiting factor in limbs is the lack of a persistent progenitor-capped cartilage, not adult biology per se.

**Chase.** Wolford 2014 (UCH classification; "not self-limited" vertical type); Slootweg & Müller 1986 (histological classification); Eslami 2003; Saridin 2010 (histology vs SPECT).
