# PMID 25883921 — Shim KS. Pubertal growth and epiphyseal fusion (2015, Ann Pediatr Endocrinol Metab) — review

Read in full (PMC text, ~15k chars).

**Content.** Narrative review. Elongation = chondrocyte proliferation + hypertrophy + matrix secretion in the physis. Growth velocity falls to ~0 "after" fusion, but the author states the cellular cause of senescence is unknown.

**Four candidate fusion mechanisms:**
1. Apoptosis of hypertrophic chondrocytes. Rat data support it; human fusing plates show no classical apoptosis (Emons 2009).
2. Autophagy. Seen in avian and neonatal mouse cartilage; absent in human fusing plates.
3. Hypoxia. Emons et al. saw a *dense border of thick bone surrounding growth-plate remnants* in fusing human plates. They propose it is a physical barrier cutting off oxygen and nutrients, killing cells non-classically.
4. Transdifferentiation of hypertrophic chondrocytes into osteoblasts. Supported by culture models, not by direct human data.

**Estrogen.** Indirect evidence that fusion follows exhaustion of proliferative capacity, which estrogen accelerates. Börjesson: cartilage ERα is needed for fusion in late puberty. Rodents do not fuse at sexual maturity and keep growing, which limits how far rodent results translate.

**Relevance to post-fusion growth.**
- Cessation of growth is framed as exhaustion of proliferative capacity, with fusion a downstream event.
- The "bone shell → hypoxia/nutrient barrier" idea implies that growth-plate remnants are sealed off by bone at the end.
- A mechanical or physical isolation step therefore takes part in the terminal phase. That is conceptually important: the last cartilage dies partly because it is entombed, not only because it is programmed.

**Levers.** Cartilage survival depends on diffusion access through bone. Any mechanism that keeps a cartilage layer in diffusive contact with marrow or synovial fluid avoids this death route. Synovial articular cartilage is the obvious adult example.
