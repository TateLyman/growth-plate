# PMID 42022344 — Severe enduring anorexia nervosa with significant height increase in adulthood (2026, PCN Rep) — case

Read in full (PMC, ~14k chars).

**Case.** Male; restrictive eating from age 11; BMI often ~10–15. Serial hand X-rays showed unfused plates.
- After keeping BMI >15 for ~1 year, he grew **~6 cm by age 27** (relative to the age-26 admission).
- A further **~10 cm** came in the following year.
- Puberty started (Tanner III; LH 1.9, testosterone 308.6 ng/dL, previously undetectable; IGF-1 212).

**Key points.**
- Undernutrition plus hypogonadism kept the plates open for 16 years past normal closure.
- Once sex steroids and nutrition returned, a normal-looking pubertal spurt (~16 cm) occurred at 26–28.
- So the growth plate's proliferative program was **not exhausted by chronological time**. It was preserved because it wasn't used (consistent with senescence being a function of cumulative growth, not age).
- Not post-fusion growth.

**Theory relevance.**
- Strong human evidence for the "cell-counting / use-dependent senescence" model: suppress growth and the remaining capacity is banked.
- Relevant only before fusion. Mechanically: it implies the cartilage progenitor pool, not systemic age, is the limiting resource, so an adult mechanism must regenerate that pool.
