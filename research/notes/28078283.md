# 28078283 — Periosteal distraction osteogenesis: an effective method for bone regeneration

**Citation.** Biomed Res Int 2016;2016:2075317. PMC5203878. Review. Read in full (all seven sections).

## Why it is here
**This is the non-destructive periosteal trigger that [ACTIVATION.md](../ACTIVATION.md) §17 named as the highest-value open question.** Periosteal distraction osteogenesis (PDO) "creates an artificial space between the bone surface and periosteum to generate new bone by gradually expanding the periosteum **with no need for corticotomy**." Schmidt et al. were the first to confirm histological new bone formation by **periosteal distraction without corticotomy**.

## Exact findings
**The principle.** Slow, sustained tension applied to intact periosteum over intact cortex, creating a subperiosteal space — Stevens' "**in vivo bioreactor**." The periosteum acts as a physical barrier excluding other soft tissue while supplying cells and factors. "**Not requiring seed cells and exogenous growth factors.**"
- **Periosteum matters more than the osteotomy.** Kojimoto: removing the periosteum markedly impaired callus formation in conventional distraction osteogenesis, "suggesting that periosteum is important for DO, **even more important than corticotomy**." Takeuchi: more new bone with periosteum retained than excised; periosteal preservation prevented external bone resorption and maintained mandibular vertical height.
- **The mechanotransduction.** Mechanical strain on human periosteal cells raises **RUNX2** and upregulates **TGF-β, bFGF, VEGF and PDGF**. "Slow and stable tension can activate the MSCs to differentiate into osteoblasts with high activity and even calcify to mature bone tissue." PDO itself "induces the release of **endogenous BMP-2**."
- **Periosteum–cortex communication is required.** Kostopoulos and Karring's PTFE capsules excluded surrounding soft tissue but **blocked periosteum–cortex contact**, and they concluded distraction devices must be **perforated** to maintain that communication.

**Quantities achieved.**
- Schmidt, rabbit mandible, U-shaped distractor: **new bone height 2.86 ± 0.56 mm**. Yamauchi with perforation + shape-memory alloy: **2.9 ± 0.5 mm**.
- PDO vs conventional DO head to head (Sencimen, rabbit): **14.4 mm² vs 25.4 mm²** of new bone; DO bone more compact, **PDO bone rich in adipose tissue**.
- **Cortical perforation (decortication) roughly doubles it**: 25.7 ± 5.1 mm² with vs **12.9 ± 3.2 mm² without**, at 8 weeks. With perforation, new bone formed under the whole mesh; without it, only near the screw. Saulacic: if the marrow cavity is not exposed the new bone depends on the **periosteum**; if exposed, on the **cortex**.

**Operational parameters.**
- **Latency 0–14 days** (conventional DO uses 5–7). One-day vs seven-day latency: 3.26 vs 2.62 cm², **not significantly different**. At least a week recommended for wound healing.
- **Rate: 0.2–1.0 mm/day used; optimum stated as lower than 0.33 mm/day.** Ilizarov's limb-lengthening law is 1 mm/day, but in PDO "cells and nutrition… can only come from the basal bone and periosteum," so 1 mm/day is too fast. Lower rate gave thicker trabeculae, less fat, less soft-tissue invasion.
- **Frequency:** 1 mm/day **in four steps once a day** is optimal in conventional DO; PDO studies used once or twice daily, never compared.
- **Dynamic beats static.** Dynamic distraction favoured early bone formation and produced bone "**similar to the rows of micropillars in conventional DO**," whereas immediate/static distraction "could just produce the woven bone."
- **Site:** calvarium best — flat, thick periosteum, large area. Keep the distraction site away from the incision or the wound dehisces.

**Adjuncts.** Subperiosteal **MSC injection** raised volume, height and mineralisation. **Exogenous VEGF** beneficial. **Platelet-rich fibrin** gave more mature bone. **Hyperbaric oxygen** beneficial. Simvastatin: insufficient evidence.
**And one striking negative control: in ovariectomised rabbits, bone formation was delayed and mineralisation insufficient, but with no significant histological difference — "osteoporosis caused by decreased estrogen did not affect the new bone formation in PDO."**

## What it establishes for post-fusion growth
1. **The periosteal programme can be switched on by tension alone, with no fracture, no osteotomy and no amputation.** That answers, in principle, the question §17 left open: every mammalian circumferential cartilaginous callus in the regeneration literature followed broken bone, and here the periosteum is activated by slow mechanical elevation of intact tissue over intact cortex. **The trigger is mechanical and non-destructive.**
2. **It is the periosteum, not the bone cut, that matters.** Stated outright: periosteum is "even more important than corticotomy" for distraction osteogenesis. That reorders the whole mental model of limb lengthening — the osteotomy creates the gap, but the periosteum builds the regenerate.
3. **It needs no cells and no exogenous factors**, and it recruits the endogenous cascade: RUNX2, TGF-β, bFGF, VEGF, PDGF and **endogenous BMP-2** — the same BMP-2 that substitutes for the missing partner tissue at an amputation stump ([37490254](37490254.md)). So tension on periosteum generates, locally and endogenously, the molecule that §17.3 identifies as the partner-substitute.
4. **Dynamic, slow, sub-0.33 mm/day distraction produces oriented "rows of micropillars."** Oriented columnar structure is what makes growth longitudinal rather than isotropic ([31787777](31787777.md)), and static distraction fails to produce it. **Rate and dynamism, not amplitude, set the architecture** — the same lesson as BMP2 working by extending signal duration rather than raising amplitude.
5. **Periosteum–cortex communication is a requirement, and a perforation doubles the output.** Another instance of the two-domain rule: the periosteal layer needs contact with the osseous layer, and the device must not seal them apart.
6. **It works in an estrogen-deficient animal.** A single sentence, but relevant given that every closure-delay idea in this corpus trades against osteoporosis.

## The honest limits — and they are decisive for the length question
- **PDO produces bone in a space on the *surface* of a bone.** It is appositional. Nothing in this review describes a **cartilaginous** intermediate, a circumferential cartilaginous callus, or endochondral ossification. **It is not the CCC programme; it is periosteal intramembranous bone formation under tension.** Whether the same input at a bone *end* would produce axial elongation is untested and unaddressed.
- **The magnitudes are ~3 mm of surface bone over weeks**, in rabbit mandible and pig calvarium, and PDO underperforms conventional DO roughly two-fold with more adipose tissue.
- Every study is a **device implanted surgically** — titanium mesh and screws, osmotic expanders, shape-memory alloys, resorbable meshes. "Non-destructive" means no osteotomy; it does not mean non-surgical.
- **Cortical perforation, which doubles the yield, is destructive** — and once the marrow is exposed the source shifts from periosteum to cortex, i.e. it becomes a conventional injury response.
- A review; all quantities are secondary, the optimal parameters are explicitly unsettled, and the device literature is heterogeneous (mandible, tibia, calvarium; rabbit, dog, pig, rat).
- No limb, no long-bone end, no joint, no length measurement anywhere.

## Chase
- **Schmidt et al. (ref 7) — the first demonstration of new bone by periosteal distraction without corticotomy.** The founding primary. Needed in full: histology, and specifically **whether any cartilage appears**.
- **Stevens et al. (ref 16) — the rabbit tibial "in vivo bioreactor," injectable gel, new bone repairing a contralateral tibial defect.** The cleanest version of the technique and the one in a long bone.
- Kojimoto (ref 8) — periosteum more important than corticotomy in DO.
- Zakaria (refs 27, 28) — the inclined device testing multiple distraction rates simultaneously; the <0.33 mm/day optimum.
- Kessler (ref 17) — dynamic vs static, and the "rows of micropillars."
- Oda (ref 23) and Yamauchi (ref 32) — decortication doubling new bone.
- Saulacic (refs 30, 36) — periosteum-dependent vs cortex-dependent sources; and PDO inducing endogenous BMP-2.
- Ref 40 — PDO in ovariectomised rabbits.
- **Anything applying periosteal distraction to the *end* of a long bone, or reporting a cartilaginous phase in PDO.** I found none, and it is the question that decides whether this is the §17 trigger or only an appositional technique.
