# PMID 28643917 — Estrogen receptor alpha mediates mandibular condylar cartilage growth in male mice (2017, Orthod Craniofac Res)

Read in full (PMC, ~14k chars).

**Model.** Male global ERα-knockout (ERαKO) vs wild-type mice at 49 days and 9 months. Small n (3–7 per group).

**Results.**
- 49 days: ERαKO has more cells and more proliferation (BrdU), fewer hypertrophic cells, and lower Sox9, Col10, Runx2 and DMP1. Maturation is delayed.
- 9 months: ERαKO has more cells and higher cell density, but the same cartilage thickness and OA scores.

**Key background statements.**
- The mandibular condylar cartilage (MCC) is **periosteum-derived fibrocartilage**.
- In long bones, **estrogen via ERα irreversibly depletes resting-zone progenitors and causes fusion in both sexes.**
- Cartilage-specific ERα deletion in females gave a thicker growth plate at 1 year; global ERαKO females had more femoral growth at 16–18 months.
- MCC does not fuse; growth may continue after appendicular maturity. Its progenitor pool may not be finite, and depletion is not seen until after 9 months in mice.
- Ovariectomy increases MCC thickness and proliferation (reversed by estrogen).

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Fusion is an estrogen/ERα-driven progenitor *exhaustion* program specific to primary growth plates. Periosteum-derived secondary cartilage is relatively exempt from that program.
- This supports the core idea that a post-fusion growth surface should be built from **periosteal-lineage (secondary) chondroprogenitors**, which escape the ERα-depletion clock, rather than from reviving the exhausted primary growth plate.

**Levers.**
- Periosteal lineage is the right source.
- The local estrogen environment modulates maturation pace, but this is a minor knob (and pharmacological, so outside the mechanical focus).
