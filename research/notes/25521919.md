# PMID 25521919 — Lui JC, ... Baron J. Spatial regulation of gene expression during growth of articular cartilage in juvenile mice (2015, Pediatr Res)

Read in full (PMC, ~28k chars).

**Model.** 1-week-old mouse tibial articular cartilage. Laser-capture microdissection into superficial (SZ), middle (MZ) and deep (DZ) zones (n=5); microarray, qPCR, ISH; comparison with the rat growth-plate dataset.

**Key statements and findings.**
- "In juvenile mammals, the epiphyses of long bones grow by chondrogenesis within the articular cartilage." Skeletal growth = physeal endochondral (metaphyseal elongation) + periosteal (widening) + **articular-surface chondrogenesis (epiphyseal expansion)**.
- Proliferation is sparse in adult articular cartilage, much more common in juveniles. Growth reportedly occurs appositionally from the articular surface.
- **SZ markers:** Cilp, Prg4, Fgf18, Dkk3, Bmp2/6. SZ has higher BMP ligands and receptors and Hh signalling at the surface.
- **DZ markers:** Sfrp5, Frzb, Pcp4, Efemp1 (shared with growth-plate RZ), plus Grem1.
- The DZ→SZ gradient mirrors RZ→HZ (r=−0.40 for SZ→DZ vs RZ→HZ). There is a BMP gradient: high at the surface, low deep.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- Defines a **second growth cartilage** at each bone end: articular cartilage, which in juveniles drives epiphyseal expansion by proliferation and endochondral replacement at its deep surface.
- In adults this cartilage persists but its growth is "sparse". Unlike the growth plate it never fuses; it is maintained for life.

**Levers (key for theory).**
- Articular cartilage is a never-fused, life-long growth-competent cartilage with an RZ-like deep compartment.
- Reactivating *juvenile-type articular growth* (proliferation plus endochondral conversion at the deep zone and tidemark) would lengthen the epiphysis axially.
- The mechanical environment of articular cartilage (cyclic hydrostatic pressure, surface shear) is a natural lever, since joint loading reaches this tissue directly and non-invasively.
- BMP/Wnt/Hh gradients are known mechanosensitive pathways.
