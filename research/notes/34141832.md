# PMID 34141832 — Matsumoto S, Nagata K, Yamaguchi H. Axial mechanical loading to ex vivo mouse long bone regulates endochondral ossification and endosteal mineralization through BMP-Smad (2021, Bone Rep)

Read in full (PMC, ~48k chars).

**Model.** Ex vivo 4th metacarpals from 3-week-old mice in collagen sponges. Cyclic axial load 0 / 7.8 / 78 mN at 0.5 Hz, 1 h/day for 4 days. Tetracycline/calcein pre-labels.

**Results.**
- Longitudinal growth rate (LGR, MF–TC/12 d): 2.48 ± 0.23 (0 mN) → **3.72 ± 0.62 (7.8 mN)** → 4.31 ± 0.41 µm/day (78 mN).
- The calcified zone thickened and the uncalcified zone thinned in a load-dependent way. So loading **accelerates the conversion of cartilage to calcified cartilage/bone**, i.e. it drives the ossification front faster.
- More hypertrophic chondrocytes.
- **Articular cartilage unchanged** (thickness, cell number) under the same loading.
- Upregulated genes: Ihh, Col10a1, Mmp13, Wnt4, Spp1, Dmp1, Mepe, Phex, Bmp6, Bmp2 (load-dependent). p-Smad1/5/8 up (not Smad2/3); Id1 up.
  - rhBMP-2 mimics the effect. Noggin + SB431542 block it.
- Downregulated: Car9 (carbonic anhydrase IX) and Acp5.
- Extracellular milieu: pH ↑ (alkalinisation), ALP ↑, PPi ↓, Pi ↑; Pi/PPi ×2.35 (7.8 mN) and ×3.0 (78 mN).

**Interpretation for the theory.**
- Loading in this ex vivo system raises the *calcification-front advance* (LGR) but by consuming cartilage (uncalcified zone thins). Net length gain depends on whether cartilage production keeps up. In a fused bone there is no cartilage to consume.
- **Articular cartilage is insensitive** to this axial cyclic loading regime with respect to calcification. That is good for joint safety, but it also means articular cartilage will not simply "ossify into length" under ordinary loading.
- BMP–Smad1/5/8 is the mechanical transducer for the ossification step.
- The local pH/Pi/PPi chemistry of the front is mechanically regulated. An adult articular tidemark advance would follow the same rules (Pi/PPi balance).

**Lever.** Loading speeds conversion; it does not create a cartilage template. Again: **template formation is the rate-limiting step after fusion.**
