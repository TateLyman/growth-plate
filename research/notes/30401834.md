# PMID 30401834 — Mizuhashi K, Ono W, ... Ono N. Resting zone of the growth plate houses a unique class of skeletal stem cells (2018, Nature)

Read in full (PMC author manuscript text incl. extended data legends, ~29k chars).

**Model.** Mouse distal femur. PTHrP-mCherry knock-in; PTHrP-creER BAC line; lineage tracing (tdTomato, Confetti); EdU label exclusion and retention; colony/serial passage; subcutaneous transplantation; DTA ablation; Hh agonist (SAG) and antagonist (LDE-225); micro-perforation injury.

**Key findings (with numbers).**
- **Timing.** PTHrP+ resting chondrocytes appear in the central plate at P3 and expand sharply between P6 and P9, *in close association with formation of the secondary ossification centre (SOC)*.
  - They are slow-cycling: EdU+ 6.1±2.3% vs 30.5±3.2% in the proliferating zone at P9.
  - creER-labelled cells: 7.7±2.0% vs 61.1±11.5%.
- **Phenotype.** About half of the mCherry+ cells are mSSC-immunophenotype (CD51+CD90−CD105−CD200+), 49.2±8.4%.
- **Lineage.** P6-labelled cells stay in the resting zone for about a week, form short columns (<10 cells) by ~P12–18, then long columns (>10 cells) by P18–36.
  - Each column is clonal (Confetti).
  - Labelled columns persist **≥1 year**, declining until 6 months then plateauing.
  - Descendants pass through hypertrophy to become osteoblasts and Cxcl12+ marrow stromal cells (not adipocytes in vivo).
- **Lateral restriction.** Labelled domains do not expand laterally: stem cells are dedicated to making columns *longitudinally*.
- **Proliferating-zone cells.** Dlx5-creER+ proliferating cells form only transient short columns.
- **Self-renewal in vitro.**
  - P9 colonies: 3.3% formed secondaries, none passaged further.
  - P12 colonies: 16.3% formed secondaries; 12.5% of those passaged ≥9 times.
  - So robust self-renewal is acquired "when the SOC actively develops".
  - Trilineage capable; in vivo transplants made cartilage and bone.
- **Ablation.** Partial DTA ablation shrinks the proliferating zone and expands the hypertrophic and resting zones (premature hypertrophy).
- **Hedgehog.** Both Hh agonist and antagonist reduce column number. Uninterrupted, *balanced* Hh signalling from transit-amplifying cells is required to keep stem-cell fate.
- **Injury.** Micro-perforation makes PTHrP+ cells differentiate *directly into osteoblasts*: without an intact proliferating zone the niche collapses.
- **Hierarchy.** Only ~2–3% of PTHrP+ cells acquire long-term self-renewal. The authors suggest PTHrP− short-term precursors drive most postnatal growth, with PTHrP+ SSCs maintaining long-term integrity.

**What it establishes for post-fusion growth.**
- The engine of longitudinal growth is a polarised stem-cell niche: PTHrP+ cells on the epiphyseal side, columns pointing metaphyseally, with Ihh feedback from hypertrophic cells.
- It forms *postnatally* together with the SOC, i.e. it is induced by a tissue-level reorganisation.
- It is fragile: injury converts the stem cells to bone.
- In an adult, the niche must be **re-created** (polarity + PTHrP/Ihh loop + protected position). It cannot simply be awakened, since in humans the cells are gone after fusion.

**Levers.**
1. The SOC appears to be an inductive event for niche formation. One hypothesis (Newton 2019; Xie 2020) is that the SOC mechanically shields the resting zone. That implicates a *mechanical boundary condition* (a stiff bony cap above a cartilage layer) as part of niche specification.
2. Niche maintenance needs an intact proliferating zone and Hh balance. Any de novo adult growth zone must be self-organising, with the PTHrP↔Ihh loop and enough thickness to separate the PTHrP source from the Ihh source.
3. Damage/perforation drives cartilage cells to bone. Mechanical overload or injury is therefore anti-niche, and any mechanical stimulus must be non-injurious.
