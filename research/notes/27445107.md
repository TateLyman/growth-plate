# PMID 27445107 — Gardner OFW, Fahy N, Alini M, Stoddart MJ. Joint mimicking mechanical load activates TGFβ1 in fibrin-poly(ester-urethane) scaffolds seeded with MSCs (2017, J Tissue Eng Regen Med)

Read in full (PMC, ~10k chars; short communication).

**Methods.**
- Human bone-marrow MSCs (4 donors, 18–49 y) in fibrin–PU scaffolds.
- Load: 10% cyclic compression on a 10% pre-strain + ±25° sliding shear at 1 Hz, 1 h/day, 5 days/week for 4 weeks.
- Seeding variants: uniform; uniform + surface layer; surface only.
- Cell-free scaffolds with exogenous latent TGF-β loaded for 6 h.

**Results.**
- Multiaxial load raised total TGF-β1 secretion (uniform group, weeks 2–4) and **raised the percentage of active TGF-β1 in every loaded group** vs all controls.
- **In cell-free scaffolds, loading alone activated latent TGF-β** to the same fraction. So the activation is physical (shear), not enzymatic.
- The authors suggest early joint mobilisation after microfracture/cell therapy would raise TGF-β activation and improve chondrogenic repair.

**Relevance.**
- Confirms Albro 2012 in a tissue-like construct: **articulating motion (compression + shear) both induces TGF-β1 production and physically activates it.** That is a closed mechanical loop for chondrogenic induction of adult human progenitors.
- It also explains why Schätti 2011 saw chondrogenesis without exogenous TGF-β.
- For the theory: motion/shear sessions at the articular surface are a non-drug TGF-β "dosing" mechanism that can recruit progenitors into cartilage.
